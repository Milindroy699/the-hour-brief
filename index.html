AI & Tech

Anthropic's shares begin trading on the Nasdaq under ticker ANT today after it priced the largest IPO on record at $83 a share, with Nvidia and Google each committing $10 billion to a concurrent $26 billion private placement, even as AWS sells a 75% stake in 13 data centers to Blackstone for about $5 billion in a sale-leaseback, Infoblox confirms a breach through a compromised SharePoint-migration vendor, Palo Alto Networks' Unit 42 details a China-linked AI-driven hacking campaign that breached 10 organizations, and UK chip startup Fractile raises $720 million at a $7 billion valuation

Takeaway Today’s tech story is Anthropic’s Nasdaq debut turning months of private AI financing into a public, real-time trade, even as its own chip and cloud suppliers keep bankrolling it off-exchange and the industry’s security exposure keeps compounding underneath.

Anthropic prices its IPO at $83 a share, above its range, and begins trading on the Nasdaq today

Anthropic priced its IPO at $83.00 per share, above the $70–$78 range it had marketed, selling 980 million shares — 956,351,354 from the company and 23,648,646 from existing stockholders — to raise about $81.3 billion, the company said. Shares are set to begin trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under ticker "ANT" after today's open, with the offering expected to close November 20.

Takeaway Pricing above its own marketed range, not just inside it, is Anthropic signalling the roadshow demand it saw was real rather than a number built to look good on a cover page.

Nvidia and Google each commit $10 billion to Anthropic's $26 billion private placement

Alongside its IPO, Anthropic is raising $26 billion in a concurrent private placement of non-voting Class N stock at the IPO price, per its amended SEC filing: Nvidia and Google are each buying about $10 billion, AMD $2.5 billion, Broadcom, Micron and Microsoft $1 billion each, and TSMC $500 million. The placement isn't contingent on the IPO closing and still requires stockholder approval.

Takeaway Two of Anthropic's own chip and cloud suppliers bankrolling it with non-voting stock buys them economics in the company without a board seat — circular AI financing on a $26 billion scale.

AWS sells a 75% stake in 13 new data centers to Blackstone for about $5 billion, then leases them all back

Blackstone Digital Infrastructure Trust agreed on November 16 to buy a 75.1% controlling stake in two joint ventures holding 13 newly built AWS data centers — nine in northern Indiana and four in Mississippi, with 647 MW of combined IT capacity — valued at about $5 billion at a 7.0% initial cap rate. AWS will lease back all 13 buildings for a weighted-average 12-year initial term with options to extend to 32 years, keeping a 24.9% stake itself; the deal, funded by about $1.9 billion of Blackstone's IPO cash and $2.5 billion of non-recourse mortgage debt, is expected to close in the fourth quarter.

Takeaway Selling majority control of brand-new data centers and immediately leasing them back lets Amazon keep the AI capacity while moving its cost off its own balance sheet.

FourWeekMBA →

Infoblox confirms a breach after hackers accessed company data through a compromised SharePoint-migration vendor

Networking and cybersecurity firm Infoblox confirmed unauthorized access to some corporate information through a third-party vendor that had trusted access to its environment and provided SharePoint data-migration services, the company said; it activated incident response, contained the intrusion and brought in outside cybersecurity specialists. Infoblox said it has found no indication its products, cloud services, or software build and release processes were affected, while unnamed sources told TechCrunch sensitive customer data may have been taken — a detail the company hasn't confirmed.

Takeaway A cybersecurity vendor getting breached through its own vendor's SharePoint access is a reminder that even security-focused companies inherit risk from whoever they grant trusted access to.

A China-linked actor used AI models to breach 10 of 19 targeted organizations, Unit 42 finds

Palo Alto Networks' Unit 42 disclosed on November 12 a campaign it tracks as CL-STA-1238, assessing with moderate confidence it is China-nexus intelligence collection: the actor paired the open-source Cairn AI penetration-testing framework with Chinese AI models Zhipu GLM and DeepSeek to autonomously run 25 operations against at least 19 organizations, including US semiconductor, defense and human-rights targets, achieving unauthorized access or data retrieval at 10 of them.

Takeaway Letting AI models plan and run most of an intrusion campaign on their own, not just assist a human operator, is the kind of automation security researchers have warned about for over a year — Unit 42 calls this one of the clearest documented cases yet.

UK AI-chip startup Fractile raises $720 million at a $7 billion valuation, before shipping a production chip

London-based Fractile closed a $720 million Series C on November 11 at a $7 billion post-money valuation, with Thrive Capital, Founders Fund, Lightspeed Venture Partners and Redpoint Ventures backing the round, according to reports on the raise. The company designs AI inference chips and has not yet shipped a production chip.

Takeaway A $7 billion valuation for a chip startup with nothing shipped yet is investors betting on Fractile's inference architecture well before there's real-world performance data to judge it by.

Takeaway Today’s business story is India's Q2 earnings season still throwing up one-off gains worth separating from the underlying numbers, while fresh IPOs, orders and funding talks keep the primary market busy and global layoffs grind on at a slightly slower November pace.

SBI's Q2 FY27 standalone profit rises 14.3% to ₹23,038 crore, helped by a mutual-fund stake sale

State Bank of India reported standalone net profit of ₹23,037.95 crore for the quarter ended September 30, up 14.27% year-on-year from ₹20,160 crore, boosted by a pre-tax exceptional gain of roughly ₹7,288 crore from divesting a 6.3% stake in SBI Funds Management and by lower provisions; core operating profit rose a more modest 7.3% to ₹34,226 crore. Consolidated profit, which also includes a gain from an NSE stake sale, rose about 20% to roughly ₹25,361 crore.

Takeaway Strip out the stake-sale gains and SBI's core operating profit growth looks far more ordinary than the 14-20% headline numbers suggest.

Jio Platforms posts its first results as a standalone-listed company: profit up 13.7% to ₹8,387 crore

Jio Platforms reported consolidated net profit of ₹8,387 crore for Q2 FY27, up 13.7% year-on-year from ₹7,379 crore and up 8.0% sequentially from ₹7,764 crore in Q1, on revenue from operations up 11.4% to ₹40,475 crore — its first quarterly disclosure since its October listing following a roughly ₹30,000 crore IPO.

Takeaway A steady double-digit profit rise in its very first results as a public company is Jio Platforms making the case to its new shareholders before anyone's had time to doubt it.

Kalpataru Projects wins a ₹2,610 crore South American order for transmission lines and substations

Kalpataru Projects International received a notification of award on November 16 for an EPC project worth about ₹2,610 crore covering power transmission lines and substations in South America, through its Power Transmission and Distribution business. PlatinumRx reportedly in talks to raise $32 million led by Sofina Ventures at up to a $200 million valuation

Bengaluru-based online pharmacy PlatinumRx is reportedly in talks to raise about $32 million in a round led by Belgium's Sofina Ventures, valuing the company at roughly $150–200 million, according to Inc42, which cited unnamed sources and said final terms could still change; PlatinumRx and Sofina did not respond to requests for comment. Global tech layoffs cross 206,000 for 2026, tracking a faster pace than last year

Tracking site TrueUp counted about 206,877 tech workers laid off across 715 events in 2026 as of its November 16 update, a pace of roughly 646 people a day versus 579 a day over all of 2025. November's pace has eased from October's, though: the tracker lists 32 layoff events so far this month, down from 51 in October.

Takeaway A faster year-on-year pace that's nonetheless cooling month-on-month suggests 2026's layoff wave hasn't let up, but it may be past its most intense stretch.

On sizing a position by your edge, via Zerodha Varsity: Varsity's chapter on the Kelly Criterion walks through the formula — Kelly% = W − [(1−W)/R], where W is your win rate and R your average-win-to-average-loss ratio — devised by John Kelly in the 1950s while he worked at AT&T's Bell Labs, long before traders adapted it for position sizing. With a 60% win rate and a 1.384 reward ratio, the formula suggests committing about 31% of capital to that trade. Varsity's own caution is the real point: professional traders typically risk only a small fraction of capital per trade, so the raw Kelly output is usually far too aggressive to use directly — it should inform position size, not dictate it.
