AI & Tech

Nvidia heads into tonight's first earnings call since Jensen Huang's resignation with interim CEO Colette Kress at the helm, as Anthropic closes out its IPO roadshow ahead of Wednesday's pricing and Thursday's Nasdaq debut, even as TrendForce forecasts another double-digit DRAM price jump for the fourth quarter, Sandisk's 8-for-1 stock split clears its final steps before Friday's split-adjusted trading, CISA's federal deadline for a four-flaw exploited-vulnerability batch has already passed, and AI-infrastructure orchestrator Edgevana signs over $700 million in Nvidia Blackwell deployment deals

Takeaway Today’s tech story is two overdue reckonings landing back to back — Nvidia’s first earnings since Huang’s ouster and Anthropic’s last day before pricing — while underneath them, memory prices, patch deadlines and fresh GPU-buildout money keep moving regardless.

Nvidia reports tonight in its first earnings call since Jensen Huang's resignation

Nvidia hosts its fiscal third-quarter earnings call Tuesday, November 17, after US markets close, per its investor relations site — the company's first report since CEO Jensen Huang resigned on November 12 following reports of extramarital affairs, one involving a woman flagged as a suspected Chinese intelligence contact. Interim CEO Colette Kress, Nvidia's CFO, leads the call; the company had guided to revenue of $108.0 billion, plus or minus 2%, before Huang's departure.

Takeaway A CFO delivering guidance she helped write, hours after a Monday session where chip stocks were already sold off hard, is as close to a live stress test as Nvidia's governance has faced in over a decade.

Anthropic's IPO roadshow closes out ahead of Wednesday's pricing and Thursday's Nasdaq debut

Anthropic set a $70–$78 per-share price range in a November 10 SEC filing for 825 million Class A shares on Nasdaq under ticker ANT, with pricing expected Wednesday, November 18, and trading set to start Thursday, November 19, CNBC reported. Up to 20% of the offering may go to retail investors, and cornerstone buyers including Google, Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft and TSMC have committed roughly $26 billion at the IPO price.

Takeaway Pricing a trillion-dollar-plus IPO one day after Wall Street's own AI-stock rout is Anthropic betting investor appetite survives a single bad Monday.

CNBC →

TrendForce forecasts PC DRAM contract prices to keep climbing into year-end, though the pace is slowing

TrendForce's November 11 update projects PC DRAM contract prices to rise 13–18% quarter-on-quarter in the fourth quarter, down from an 18–23% jump in the third quarter, as some PC makers pulled procurement forward into the first half of 2026 and finished-goods inventories build. The research firm's broader conventional-DRAM estimate points to 10–15% quarter-on-quarter growth over the same period.

Takeaway A slowing rate of increase is still an increase — TrendForce's numbers say the memory shortage squeezing PC and phone makers isn't over, it's just getting less dramatic.

TrendForce →

CISA's federal deadline for a four-flaw exploited-vulnerability batch — GPUStack to IBM Aspera — has passed

CISA added four actively exploited flaws to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on November 9 — covering GPUStack, SignalWire's FreeSWITCH, OpenSSH (the "SSHole" authentication-bypass bug affecting musl-libc Linux systems like Alpine) and IBM Aspera Faspex — giving federal agencies until November 12 to remediate, eSecurity Planet and The Hacker News reported. GPUStack's flaw could let an unauthenticated attacker gain admin rights; the FreeSWITCH bug scores 9.8 on the CVSS scale.

Takeaway Bundling an AI-infrastructure tool, a telephony platform, a file-transfer system and the SSH daemon itself into one three-day patch window is a reminder that exploited bugs don't wait for IT teams to finish last month's list.

eSecurity Planet →

Sandisk's 8-for-1 stock split clears its final steps before Friday's split-adjusted trading

Sandisk's board-approved eight-for-one forward split will see shareholders of record on November 9 receive seven additional shares for every share held, with the extra shares issued after markets close on November 19 and split-adjusted trading beginning November 20, per the company's SEC filing. The move raises authorized common shares from 450 million to 3.6 billion; the stock closed at $1,330.05 on October 29, equivalent to about $166 per share post-split.

Takeaway A memory maker splitting its stock eight ways to stay "accessible to employees" is a quieter marker of just how far AI-driven chip demand has pushed its share price in a year.

SEC Filing →

AI-infrastructure orchestrator Edgevana signs over $700 million in Nvidia Blackwell deployment deals

Edgevana said on November 9 it signed more than $700 million in multi-year, take-or-pay agreements to deploy Nvidia B300 GPUs and GB300 systems across North America and Europe, co-CEOs Ryan Fay and Subhan Jahromi announced. The company also reports about $7 billion in contracted customer demand and a pipeline above $20 billion, and says it can bring tens of megawatts of high-density AI compute online within 90 to 120 days across its 17 operational data-center sites.

Takeaway Not naming a single customer behind $700 million in contracts is standard practice in the AI-infrastructure boom right now — the backlog talks, the specifics stay private.

Flipkart Group's FY26 revenue nears ₹1.5 lakh crore as its loss narrows to ₹8,468 crore and Trent's Q2 profit rises 23% to ₹464 crore the same week Noel Tata hands over as its chairman, GAIL's Q2 profit jumps to ₹4,650 crore as India's Q2 earnings season nears its close, Tata Sons heads toward a November 26 board meeting over Noel Tata's restructuring push and Shapoorji Pallonji's ₹25,000 crore exit proposal, Reliance moves to dissolve its UK Hamleys franchising unit, Lenny's Podcast guest Elena Verna argues AI companies need a rewritten growth playbook, and the US jobs report shows hiring beating expectations in October as layoffs keep falling even as Omnicom prepares to cut 15,000 roles

Takeaway Today’s business story is India's Q2 season and the Tata succession saga converging in the same week, while globally a cooler labour market and one more AI-era layoff round sit alongside a case for throwing out the old growth playbook entirely.

Flipkart Group's FY26 revenue nears ₹1.5 lakh crore as its loss narrows to ₹8,468 crore

Flipkart Group's revenue for the year ended March 2026 grew toward ₹1.5 lakh crore with improving margins, while its consolidated loss narrowed to ₹8,468 crore, Inc42 reported. The group completed its reverse flip from Singapore to India in March 2026, ahead of a potential IPO.

Takeaway A narrowing loss alongside a completed reverse-flip is Flipkart visibly tidying its books and domicile at the same time — exactly what a pre-IPO company does in the year before it files.

Inc42 →

Trent's Q2 profit rises 23% to ₹464 crore the same week Noel Tata hands over as chairman

Trent reported consolidated Q2 FY27 profit of roughly ₹464 crore, up 23% year-on-year, on revenue of about ₹5,873 crore, up 22%, with operating margin improving 137 basis points, Upstox reported. Separately, Noel Tata stepped down as Trent's chairman effective November 11 after nearly three decades with the company, with Bahram Vakil taking over from November 12.

Takeaway Handing over a retailer that's still growing profit at double digits is Noel Tata choosing to step back from Trent while it's working, not after it stops.

GAIL's Q2 profit jumps to ₹4,650 crore as India's Q2 earnings season nears its close

GAIL (India) reported September-quarter net profit of about ₹4,650 crore, with first-half profit near ₹8,943 crore, as revenue from operations surged to ₹45,924 crore, PSU Connect reported, citing the board's October 30 approval of the results.

Takeaway A state gas utility posting a sharp profit jump on surging revenue is one more sign this earnings season has rewarded commodity and energy plays more consistently than almost any other sector.

PSU Connect →

Tata Sons heads toward a November 26 board meeting over Noel Tata's restructuring push

Noel Tata wrote to Tata Sons on October 29, in his capacity as a director, asking the board to take up a restructuring plan at its November 26 meeting that he says could help the company avoid a mandatory listing, after the RBI rejected its bid to deregister as a core investment company, Moneylife and YourStory reported. The same meeting is set to review the Shapoorji Pallonji Group's proposal to exit roughly ₹25,000 crore of its 18.38% Tata Sons stake through a selective capital reduction.

Takeaway Tying a board's own private-company preference to another shareholder's ₹25,000 crore exit ask, on the same agenda, is Noel Tata forcing two separate fights to be settled at once.

Moneylife →

Reliance moves to dissolve its UK Hamleys franchising unit

Reliance Industries told stock exchanges on October 29 that its UK-registered step-down subsidiary, Hamleys (Franchising) Limited, is undergoing a voluntary strike-off process, after a Companies House notice on October 19 and a Gazette notice on October 27, PSU Connect and other outlets reported. The entity recorded ₹56.08 crore in turnover in 2025 and nil net worth; dissolution isn't expected before roughly two months after the notice, and the filing doesn't affect Hamleys' Indian stores.

Takeaway Winding up a near-zero-net-worth UK shell while leaving the Indian Hamleys business untouched is routine corporate housekeeping, not Reliance stepping back from the toy brand.

PSU Connect →

Lenny's Podcast guest Elena Verna argues AI companies need a rewritten growth playbook

In a Lenny's Podcast episode, Lovable's head of growth Elena Verna said as much as 60–70% of traditional growth tactics no longer apply to AI products, pointing to tactics like building in public and giving the product away, and reframing the "minimum viable product" as a "minimum lovable product." She cited Lovable reaching $200 million in annual recurring revenue within a year with roughly 100 employees.

Takeaway Hitting $200 million ARR with about 100 people is the kind of ratio that makes a growth lead's claim that the old playbook mostly doesn't apply anymore hard to just wave off.

US hiring beats expectations in October as layoffs keep falling

US nonfarm payrolls rose by a stronger-than-expected 165,000 in October and the unemployment rate dipped to 4.1%, Anadolu Agency reported, citing government data. US employers announced 43,994 layoffs in October, down 71% year-on-year, according to Challenger, Gray & Christmas.

Takeaway A 71% year-on-year drop in announced layoffs alongside a hiring beat is a labour market that looks steadier than the AI-driven job-cut headlines of the past year would suggest.

Omnicom prepares to cut 15,000 roles after its Interpublic acquisition

Omnicom Group is cutting about 15,000 jobs following its acquisition of Interpublic Group, Anadolu Agency reported, as part of a wider wave of corporate job cuts that also includes Walt Disney eliminating thousands of roles.

Takeaway Fifteen thousand cuts after an ad-agency merger is the overlap math every holding-company acquisition eventually runs into, just on an unusually large scale.

Wall Street's AI and chip stocks lead a sharp Monday selloff into Nasdaq correction territory

The S&P 500 dropped 1.92% to 7,416.59 and the Nasdaq Composite fell 2.49% to 24,509.76 on Monday, with the Dow down 739 points, as a chip-sector gauge sank 5.3% and the VIX fear index surged 19%, CNN and Bloomberg reported. The Nasdaq remains more than 10% below its October 6 record close, putting it in correction territory, with investors positioning ahead of Nvidia's Tuesday earnings and Anthropic's expected Wednesday IPO pricing.

Takeaway Selling off this hard the night before the two events investors say they're waiting on is Wall Street admitting it isn't confident either result will come in clean.

GIFT Nifty signals a near-flat Tuesday open around 25,288 after Wall Street's overnight rout

GIFT Nifty futures traded around 25,288, down about 5 points or 0.02%, pointing to a largely flat Tuesday open for the Nifty 50 despite Monday's sharp Wall Street selloff, BusinessToday reported, with BSE, Bajaj Finance, Adani Enterprises, HDFC Life, Senco Gold and Cipla among stocks in focus.

Takeaway Barely budging after Wall Street's worst session in weeks is Dalal Street signalling it isn't ready to import Monday's AI-stock panic wholesale.

Just 1,099 of 2,821 NSE stocks trade above their December 2025 closing levels despite this month's rally

Even though the Sensex and Nifty have risen sharply in November, only 1,099 of 2,821 NSE-listed stocks were trading above their December 31, 2025 closing levels as of November 16, according to ACE Equity data cited by Business Standard, underscoring how narrow the rally's breadth has been.

Takeaway An index up double digits while most individual stocks are still underwater for the year is the clearest sign that this rally has been carried by a relatively small set of names.

Home Depot, Target and Walmart's earnings this week line up to set Wall Street's holiday-season read

Home Depot reports fiscal third-quarter results Tuesday before the opening bell, with Target following Wednesday and Walmart on Thursday, in a cluster of big US retail earnings that lands just before Black Friday, according to earnings-calendar previews. Analysts will be watching comparable-sales trends and holiday-quarter guidance across all three for signs of how resilient US consumer spending really is.

Takeaway Three of America's biggest retailers reporting in the same three days gives Wall Street an unusually concentrated read on consumer health, right as it's also digesting Nvidia and Anthropic.

With Nvidia reporting fiscal third-quarter results after Tuesday's close and Anthropic expected to price its IPO Wednesday ahead of a Thursday Nasdaq debut, traders are treating the two events as the week's real test of AI-stock sentiment after Monday's selloff, per market commentary accompanying the day's coverage.
