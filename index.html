Before the news

There’s a Japanese idea, mono no aware — the gentle awareness that things don’t last, and that this very impermanence is part of what gives them their beauty. It shows up today in a long-serving bank chief’s final fortnight at HDFC Bank, in a single trading session that handed pharma’s leadership of the market to IT almost as fast as Tuesday’s rally had taken shape, and in two chipmakers finally posting the AI-era profits their investors have been promised for years — a reminder that nothing, not even a boom, holds its shape for very long.

Takeaway OpenAI and Apple’s mutual accusations show how personal the AI hardware race has gotten, even as TSMC’s and Samsung’s own results show the AI buildout still cashing out in real revenue — right up until a single US inflation surprise was enough to send richly priced AI and chip stocks lower anyway.

OpenAI alleges a former employee took 100+ files on an unreleased device before rejoining Apple

In an October 14 court filing in the ongoing Apple Inc. v. Liu trade-secrets case, OpenAI alleges that a former employee — who had originally come from Apple, spent about six months at OpenAI, then returned to Apple — sent more than 100 confidential files about an unreleased device, including engineering and manufacturing data and supplier information, to personal accounts before leaving. OpenAI says the employee later presented related product-plan material to senior Apple executives, including Apple’s VP of operations in Asia, and is asking the court for room to investigate and potentially bring counterclaims; Apple says it has policies against this and has found no evidence supporting the allegations.

Takeaway Apple’s own trade-secrets suit against OpenAI just produced a mirror-image allegation, which is less a resolution than a sign of how porous the border between the two companies’ hardware teams already is.

Wall Street tumbles as a hotter-than-expected inflation reading drags the Nasdaq to its worst day since July

The S&P 500 fell 86.51 points (1.11%) to 7,718.94 on October 14 — its fifth loss in six sessions since its last all-time high — while the Nasdaq Composite dropped 511.51 points (1.88%) to 26,692.87 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average slipped 48.62 points (0.09%) to 51,621.48, Yahoo Finance and the Associated Press reported. The selloff followed September CPI data showing prices rose 3.7% year-on-year, hotter than the 3.6% economists polled by Dow Jones had expected, with AI-linked and chip stocks among the hardest hit.

Takeaway A single inflation surprise doing more damage to AI-linked stocks than that day’s actual AI news is a reminder of how much of this trade is still a bet on cheap money, not just on the technology.

TSMC posts a record Q3 on AI chip demand, with today’s earnings call set to address 2027 pricing

TSMC’s third-quarter 2026 revenue hit a record NT$1.49 trillion (about $46.71 billion), up 50% year-on-year and ahead of both the company’s own July guidance of $44.6-45.8 billion and the LSEG analyst consensus of NT$1.46 trillion, according to Kaohoon International. September revenue alone topped NT$500 billion for a second straight month, and TSMC has raised its full-year 2026 revenue-growth guidance to “slightly above 40%” in dollar terms; today’s earnings call, set for 2pm Taiwan time, is expected to address potential 2027 price increases and further capital-expenditure plans.

Takeaway Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom all lean on TSMC’s fabs, so a number this far ahead of guidance is the clearest sign yet that AI-chip demand is showing up as real revenue, not just backlog.

Samsung guides to a nearly ninefold jump in Q3 profit on AI memory demand

Samsung Electronics’ early-October guidance points to Q3 2026 operating profit of about 107.4 trillion won (roughly $80 billion), up about 782% year-on-year and the first time the company has guided above 100 trillion won in operating profit, on revenue of about 195 trillion won, up 127%, according to FourWeekMBA. Coverage ties the jump to tight AI-driven DRAM, NAND and high-bandwidth-memory supply, with Samsung’s HBM market share reportedly rising to about 33% in the second quarter of 2026 from 21% in the first; full audited results are due by the end of October.

Takeaway A single quarter nearly matching what Samsung used to post in profit over an entire year shows how completely AI memory has reordered the chip industry’s pecking order.

Indian AI startup funding surges 265% year-on-year to $438 million in the July-September quarter

Indian AI startups raised about $438 million across 35 deals in the July-September quarter, up 265% year-on-year from 26 deals in the same quarter last year, Inc42 reported, taking the sector’s nine-month 2026 total past $1.1 billion. That outpaced the broader Indian startup market, which raised about $2.2 billion across 210 deals in the same quarter — up just 5% year-on-year even as deal count fell 13% — with AI narrowly the most-funded sector ahead of cleantech’s $433 million.

Takeaway AI growing nearly nine times faster than the rest of Indian venture capital is a genuine signal, even if $438 million for an entire quarter is still smaller than some single funding rounds happening in Silicon Valley.

Takeaway A cluster of long-running uncertainties resolved today — Zetwerk’s IPO opened on schedule, the UPI fee survived the lobbying against it, and HDFC Bank’s succession is settled — even as two Wall Street banks’ earnings were each more complicated than their headline numbers suggested.

Zetwerk’s ₹5,012 crore IPO opens for public bidding today

Manufacturing platform Zetwerk’s ₹5,012 crore IPO — a ₹2,830 crore fresh issue plus an offer for sale of 14.45 crore shares worth about ₹2,182 crore — opens for public subscription today, October 15, after its anchor book closed October 14, the Free Press Journal reported. The issue is priced at ₹143-151 a share, implying a valuation of about ₹25,583 crore at the top end; bidding closes October 19, with listing targeted for October 23 on the BSE and NSE, and a minimum retail investment of ₹14,949 for the 99-share lot.

Takeaway Pricing a ₹5,012 crore issue to close in under a week is Zetwerk betting that a crowded earnings season won’t crowd out investor attention for its own listing.

NPCI’s 0.4% UPI merchant fee takes effect today, despite lobbying for a delay

The 0.4% merchant discount rate on UPI payments above ₹2,000 (capped at ₹300 per transaction) took effect today, October 15, as originally scheduled, NewsBytes reported — resolving the uncertainty left by last week’s stalled NPCI steering-committee meeting, despite fintechs and merchant bodies having pushed for a delay to January 2027. Person-to-person transfers stay free, small merchants receiving up to ₹1 lakh a month via UPI QR are exempt, and a flat ₹5 fee applies to railways, telecom, insurance and fuel payments; the Supreme Court declined to stay the rollout but asked the government to justify the ₹2,000 threshold.

Takeaway Holding the line on a fee the industry spent weeks lobbying against, in the middle of the festive season, suggests the government wanted this settled before Diwali shopping, not after.

HCLTech posts a record Q2 profit as its advanced-AI revenue grows 78%

HCLTech reported consolidated net profit of ₹5,132 crore for the quarter ended September 30, 2026 — its highest-ever quarterly net income, up 21% year-on-year and ahead of analyst estimates of around ₹4,859 crore — on revenue of ₹35,620 crore, up 11.5%, the Free Press Journal reported. New deal bookings came in at $3.26 billion, “advanced AI” revenue grew 78% year-on-year in constant currency to $219 million, and the board declared a ₹12-per-share interim dividend.

Takeaway A 78% jump in a single AI-revenue line next to an 11.5% jump in total revenue shows HCLTech’s AI bet is growing fast — just not yet fast enough to move the company’s overall numbers much.

RBI approves Anup Bagchi as HDFC Bank’s next MD & CEO

The Reserve Bank of India has approved Anup Bagchi, currently MD & CEO of ICICI Prudential Life Insurance, as HDFC Bank’s next MD & CEO for a three-year term effective October 27, Outlook Business reported, citing the bank’s own disclosure. Outgoing CEO Sashidhar Jagdishan, who decided not to seek reappointment, retires October 26 — a day before Bagchi’s term begins — with the board having fast-tracked the succession process since Jagdishan’s exit was announced in August.

Takeaway Settling a CEO succession just two days ahead of the actual handover, and days before the bank’s own results, is India’s largest private lender choosing continuity over suspense.

Wipro and Jio Financial Services both report Q2 results after today’s board meetings

Wipro’s board, meeting across October 14-15, approves its Q2 FY27 results after Indian market hours tonight, with management set to discuss them on a 7pm IST call, according to the company’s own newsroom release; its guidance had pointed to IT-services revenue of $2.574-2.627 billion, roughly flat in constant-currency terms. Jio Financial Services’ board also meets today to approve its Q2/H1 FY27 results, with an analyst call to follow, PSU Connect reported.

Takeaway Two unrelated companies’ boards landing on the same results date is a coincidence of the calendar, but it means tonight’s numbers will compete for the same evening’s attention.

Bank of America’s Q3 profit takes a $1.2 billion regulatory-settlement hit

Bank of America reported GAAP net income of $6.9 billion and diluted EPS of $0.91 for the third quarter of 2026, after a $1.2 billion regulatory-settlement charge cut earnings per share by $0.17, according to the bank’s own SEC filing. Excluding that charge, adjusted net income was about $8.1 billion and adjusted EPS $1.08; the reported efficiency ratio came in at 66% versus an adjusted 62%, and return on tangible common equity was 12.6% versus an adjusted 14.9%.

Takeaway A one-time settlement charge turning a solid banking quarter into a messy headline number is exactly the kind of result that rewards reading past the first line of the press release.

Morgan Stanley’s Q3 profit grows year-on-year, but still trails some analysts’ expectations

Morgan Stanley reported third-quarter 2026 net revenues of $20.3 billion, up from $18.2 billion a year earlier, with net income of $4.9 billion and diluted EPS of $3.03, versus $2.80 a year ago, according to the bank’s own earnings release. Return on tangible common equity came in at 22.6%, down from 23.5% in the same quarter last year; some analyst previews had put consensus EPS as high as $3.12, meaning the headline growth number still landed short of the market’s more bullish estimates.

Takeaway Healthy year-on-year growth reading as a disappointment is less about Morgan Stanley’s quarter and more about how high this earnings season has set the bar for Wall Street banks.

Sensex and Nifty slip as pharma drags even as IT cushions the fall

The Sensex fell 251.89 points (0.34%) to close at 72,781.89 on October 14, and the Nifty 50 dropped 99.25 points (0.44%) to 22,622.70, giving back part of Tuesday’s rally, the Free Press Journal reported. Nifty Pharma was the weakest sector, down about 1.3%, while Nifty IT was the best performer and limited the overall losses, with Infosys, TCS, Tech Mahindra, Hindustan Unilever and NTPC among the day’s biggest Sensex gainers and Shriram Finance, HDFC Life and Bajaj Finance the top losers.

Takeaway IT absorbing pharma’s losses almost sector-for-sector looks less like a reversal of Tuesday’s rally and more like money simply moving from one part of the market to another.

FIIs keep selling even as DIIs buy the dip

Foreign institutional investors were net sellers of about ₹1,857.40 crore in Indian cash equities on October 14, while domestic institutional investors were net buyers of about ₹2,670.40 crore, according to 5paisa’s FII-DII tracker — continuing a pattern that has held through much of this crude-and-inflation-driven stretch of October.

Takeaway DII buying is cushioning the market, not reversing the outflow, which is a different thing from FIIs actually coming back.

Wall Street tumbles on hot US inflation data, setting up a cautious Thursday for GIFT Nifty

The S&P 500 fell 1.11% to 7,718.94 and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 1.88% to 26,692.87 — its worst day since July — on October 14, after September CPI data showed prices rising 3.7% year-on-year against an expected 3.6%, TheStreet reported. Takeaway A hot inflation number in the US reaching all the way into GIFT Nifty’s Thursday-morning signal is a reminder of how tightly this month’s rally has been tied to rate expectations, not fundamentals.
