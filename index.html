Takeaway Global AI governance dominated the day — from the UN to the Trump-Xi summit — even as labs kept shipping faster, cheaper models and hardware, a reminder that speed and oversight are now being negotiated in the same week.

Trump and Xi meet in Washington today with AI chip controls off the table

Presidents Trump and Xi meet at the White House today, September 24, for a summit where expectations are low but AI looms large: US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said chip export controls are not formally on the agenda, though China could still push to have them eased, per CNBC and Karmactive. Ahead of the meeting, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent discussed a “US-China AI dialogue” with counterpart He Lifeng over the weekend, including a channel for AI incidents that reach “a national security level,” and analysts expect that hotline — not any deal to slow AI development, which CNBC calls “extremely unlikely” — to be the summit’s most concrete AI outcome.

Takeaway A superpower summit reduced to negotiating an AI incident hotline, rather than any real brake on the technology, shows how far apart Washington and Beijing still are on anything beyond crisis management.

Altman and Amodei ask the UN Security Council for shared global AI safety standards

Sam Altman and Dario Amodei addressed the UN Security Council on September 23, in the first session to directly host frontier US AI developers on shared safety concerns, per CNN. Amodei proposed three concrete steps: narrow bans such as prohibiting AI-assisted bioweapons design, cross-border verification systems, and common global testing standards with an incident-notification regime, telling the council “AI could be a risk to humanity as a whole.” Altman argued that whether the odds of catastrophe are put at “10% or 1% or 12% or 0.1%, none of these levels are remotely acceptable.” White House science adviser Michael Kratsios pushed back the same day, saying AI’s progress didn’t justify pausing development or creating new global governance structures.

Takeaway Two rival CEOs jointly lobbying the UN for binding rules, on the same day the White House waves off new oversight, is a clear sign the industry increasingly wants government-imposed guardrails it can’t credibly set for itself.

Anthropic launches Claude Opus 5.5, cutting typical costs 40% and removing usage caps

Anthropic released Claude Opus 5.5 on September 22, priced at $4/$20 per million input/output tokens — 20% below Opus 5 — with cache reads down 60% to $0.20 per million, adding up to roughly 40% lower cost on typical workloads, per Anthropic and Benzinga. The company says Opus 5.5 performs at the level of Claude Fable 5.1 on most work while running over 30% faster than Opus 5, and it beats Fable 5.1 on agentic coding (66.4% vs. 55.8% on Terminal-Bench 4.0). Anthropic is also removing five-hour usage caps for Pro, Max, Team and seat-based Enterprise subscribers.

Takeaway Cutting prices and lifting usage caps on the same day is Anthropic optimizing for developer lock-in ahead of its IPO, not just for benchmark bragging rights.

Claude AI agents help discover a CRISPR-like enzyme system

Anthropic’s life-sciences lab said on September 23 that roughly 950 Claude agents spent 21 hours and 210 million tokens scanning over 200,000 reverse transcriptase sequences, narrowing 3,500 candidates down to 20 — one of which, dubbed ART (array-associated reverse transcriptases), shows a CRISPR-like architecture with a reverse transcriptase, a partner gene, and evenly spaced DNA repeats. Anthropic says human involvement was limited to the initial prompt and wet-lab validation; CRISPR pioneer Feng Zhang called it “an exciting example of how AI agents can contribute to biological discovery.”

Takeaway This lands the same week Amodei is telling the UN that AI-enabled bioweapons design needs a global ban — a reminder that the tools accelerating genuine discovery and the tools raising dual-use alarm are the same tools.

Meta unveils $1,299 VR glasses and three new smart-glasses lines at Connect

At Meta Connect on September 23, Mark Zuckerberg introduced a roughly 100-gram VR headset called Meta VR Glasses, priced at $1,299 for a spring 2027 release, with a 5K Micro-OLED display and its computing, battery and storage split into a separate tethered module, per Tom’s Guide and The Next Web. Meta also refreshed Ray-Ban Meta to a third generation starting at $449, with a six-microphone array and more battery life than the previous generation, and launched a camera-free Ray-Ban Meta Audio line at $349, shipping October 13. Zuckerberg also revealed Muse Charm, a keychain pendant housing a personal AI agent, coming this fall with pricing not yet set.

Takeaway Seeding four different price points and form factors for AI-on-your-face hardware at once is Meta betting the winning wearable shape isn’t settled yet, unlike Apple’s single-SKU approach.

Critical flaw in the open-source Bifrost AI gateway exposed every connected provider’s API keys

Researchers disclosed CVE-2026-90898 (CVSS 9.8) on September 22 in Bifrost, an open-source gateway that routes requests to more than 20 LLM providers: because management-API authentication ships disabled by default, a single unauthenticated request to /api/mcp/client lets an attacker register a client that Bifrost executes immediately as the gateway’s own process user — and since Bifrost stores every connected provider’s API keys, that command execution hands the keys over too, per The Hacker News. The flaw affects HTTP transport releases 1.6.x through 1.6.11 and the transports/v2.0.0 line; version 2.1.0 fixes it.

Takeaway AI gateways have quietly become the keychain for an organization’s entire model stack, so one insecure default there is now a single point of failure for every LLM an org uses, not just one app.

Goa’s Hughes Precision Manufacturing raises over ₹250 crore to nearly triple ammunition capacity

Defence manufacturer Hughes Precision Manufacturing raised more than ₹250 crore from a group of ultra-high-net-worth individuals and family offices, announced September 23, to expand small-caliber ammunition capacity from roughly 80 million to 220 million rounds a year and build a new medium-caliber facility, per YourStory. Founded in 2016, the company says its order book has crossed ₹1,000 crore, with about 60% from Indian buyers including the Army, police and paramilitary forces and the rest from more than 20 countries; it says it is India’s only manufacturer-exporter of high-precision sniper ammunition for special forces.

Takeaway Private capital chasing India’s defence-manufacturing supply chain with the enthusiasm once reserved for consumer apps signals where growth-stage money now sees durable, policy-backed demand.

Prime Focus-backed Brahma AI raises $150 million at a $2 billion valuation

Brahma AI, an enterprise AI platform for audiovisual content spun out of Mumbai’s Prime Focus/DNEG group, raised $150 million via preferred shares — including $100 million from Multiples Alternate Asset Management — at a $2 billion post-money valuation, announced September 23, per Business Standard. The company counts Warner Bros., the NBA and Mayo Clinic among its customers across media, sports, healthcare and advertising, and says the funds will go toward R&D and a larger Silicon Valley presence; Prime Focus, through DNEG, will retain roughly 66% of Brahma AI’s economic ownership post-dilution.

Takeaway A visual-effects and production house spinning out a $2 billion AI subsidiary shows how “boring” services businesses can reinvent themselves as AI infrastructure plays when they own the right proprietary content pipelines.

ED names EaseMyTrip co-founder Nishant Pitti in Mahadev betting-app chargesheet

The Enforcement Directorate provisionally attached demat shares worth ₹59.60 crore belonging to EaseMyTrip co-founder Nishant Pitti under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, naming him in its fifth supplementary chargesheet filed before a special court in Raipur, reported September 22, per Business Today. The ED alleges Pitti helped route Mahadev betting-app proceeds into Indian equities disguised as foreign portfolio investment, and that he arranged with an associate of Dubai-based bookmaker Hari Shankar Tibrewal to artificially inflate Easy Trip Planners’ share price. Pitti said he was unaware of any chargesheet and had not been summoned; the company said it had received no formal legal intimation.

Takeaway For a listed travel-tech founder, an ED chargesheet alleging stock-price manipulation is a governance risk that booking-app growth metrics can’t offset.

FSSAI moves against Amazon, Flipkart, Zepto, Swiggy Instamart and BigBasket over food-safety lapses

India’s food safety regulator initiated penal action on September 23 against five e-commerce and quick-commerce platforms — Amazon, Flipkart, Zepto, Swiggy Instamart and BigBasket — over misleading claims, misbranding and prohibited listings, per Business Standard. Cited examples include misleading claims on a packaged dates product, product-information gaps on dairy items sold via Swiggy Instamart and BigBasket, and the sale of toxic Datura (dhatura) seeds on BigBasket, Amazon and Swiggy Instamart.

Takeaway As quick-commerce platforms race to add SKUs from thousands of unverified sellers, catalog hygiene is becoming a regulatory liability, not just a UX problem.

Ola Electric seeks board approval for a rights issue as its two-wheeler market share slips

Ola Electric said it will seek board approval on September 28 for a rights issue of equity shares, part of an already-approved plan to raise up to ₹1,500 crore through equity routes, per Business Standard. Size and pricing aren’t disclosed yet, but the company said the route would let retail, institutional and promoter shareholders all participate; it follows a ₹780 crore QIP just three months earlier and comes as Ola’s EV two-wheeler market share has slipped to around 7%, on sales of 88,910 units between January and August 24, 2026.

Takeaway Back-to-back capital raises within a single quarter, against a shrinking market share, suggest Ola Electric is now fundraising to survive a price war rather than to fund growth.

Telix agrees to buy Germany’s ITM for $1.65 billion to build a radiopharmaceutical powerhouse

Australia’s Telix Pharmaceuticals agreed to acquire all shares of Germany’s ITM Isotope Technologies for about $1.65 billion upfront, plus up to $700 million in milestone payments tied to FDA approvals, per BioSpace and MedCity News. The deal adds ITM’s radioisotope manufacturing infrastructure and a distribution network spanning more than 65 countries to Telix’s business, and folds in ITM’s lead asset, a lutetium-177-based therapy for neuroendocrine tumors that has completed one Phase 3 trial with a second fully enrolled. The companies expect to close by year-end and project combined unaudited revenue and income above $1.3 billion this year.

Takeaway A cancer-drug maker paying up for manufacturing capacity, not just a pipeline asset, shows how much of radiopharma’s bottleneck is now supply chain rather than science.

OpenAI in talks for a funding round above $1.2 trillion instead of a near-term IPO

OpenAI has held early discussions with large investors about a private round that could value the company near $1.2 trillion, a sharp step-up from the $852 billion valuation set in its March raise, as an alternative to going public soon, per The Motley Fool. The talks follow Sam Altman telling Fortune on September 12 that “given everything happening with safety, right now would be an ill-advised moment to go public,” effectively ruling out a 2026 listing. The maneuvering comes as rival Anthropic pushes toward its own roughly $2 trillion IPO as early as November.

Takeaway Citing AI safety to avoid public-market scrutiny while simultaneously seeking a trillion-dollar-plus private valuation shows the safety narrative and the fundraising narrative have become the same narrative.

NSE lists on the BSE today after its record ₹22,569 crore IPO closes 5.71x subscribed

National Stock Exchange shares are set to debut on the BSE today, September 24, after its IPO — priced at ₹1,785 per share — closed subscribed 5.71 times, drawing bids for 50.58 crore shares against 8.86 crore on offer, per Newsbytes. It’s India’s second-largest IPO ever after Hyundai Motor India’s ₹27,870 crore issue in 2024. Ahead of listing, the grey-market premium signaled a muted debut of roughly 2.4%, implying a listing price near ₹1,828, per Inkl.

Takeaway A sharply deflated grey-market premium heading into the listing of the exchange operator itself suggests even India’s most-anticipated IPO of the year is being priced for a modest pop, not a blockbuster one.

Wall Street sells off as the 10-year Treasury yield hits its highest level since 2007

US stocks fell Wednesday — the S&P 500 down 0.75%, the Nasdaq down 1.13%, the Dow down 0.68% — after the 10-year Treasury yield spiked to about 5.11%, its highest since July 2007, per TheStreet and Invezz. The trigger was a hotter-than-expected September PMI reading showing US business activity at its strongest in over five years, which revived fears the Fed could keep hiking rather than cut; Fed governor Michael Barr said the same day that additional rate hikes are still needed to bring down sticky inflation. Rate-sensitive, high-duration tech names were hit hardest, and the small-cap Russell 2000 fell even more, down 1.60%.

Takeaway Rising bond yields, not earnings, were the story on Wall Street — and that same yield story is what dragged India’s pricier IT stocks lower in the same session.

Brent crude climbs above $100 as the rupee slips to 95.74 against the dollar

Brent crude rose to $101.61 a barrel on September 23, up 2.37% on the day, as Saudi Arabia prepared to restart exports via its East-West pipeline and US-Iran envoy talks that Trump called “very productive” eased supply worries, even as US crude stockpiles rose by 1.8 million barrels, per The National and Trading Economics. The rupee fell 12 paise to close at 95.74 against the dollar, weighed by broad dollar strength on hawkish Fed commentary, though gains in domestic equities cushioned the fall, per Outlook Business.

Takeaway Oil back above $100 is now a structural feature of markets this year rather than a one-day spike, and it’s feeding the same inflation and yield anxiety that hit US and Indian equities alike.

Sensex and Nifty rebound from Tuesday’s dip as Bajaj Finance and Hindalco lead, IT lags

Sensex rose 299.17 points, or 0.40%, to close at 74,828.25 on Wednesday, while Nifty added 117.80 points to 23,446.80, rebounding from Tuesday’s slide, per India TV News and Business Standard. Shapoorji Pallonji Group’s hunt for ₹3,500 crore puts fresh focus on the Tata Sons listing fight

SP Group, which holds an 18.4% stake in Tata Sons as its largest minority shareholder, is seeking an additional ₹3,500 crore by exercising a greenshoe option on an existing ₹21,350 crore lender facility to meet a debt payment, with Deutsche Bank set to provide the bulk of the new funds, per Business Standard and Business Today. The financing squeeze puts a sharper spotlight on the unresolved fight over whether Tata Sons must list: a public listing could give the Mistry family a route to monetize its stake, which is why SP Group’s Shapoor Mistry has repeatedly pushed for one even as Tata Trusts, which control 66% of Tata Sons, contest the board’s September 17 vote to proceed toward an RBI-mandated listing.

Takeaway The Tata Sons listing fight isn’t just a governance dispute anymore — it’s now tangled up with a minority shareholder’s own balance-sheet stress, which raises the stakes on how quickly it gets resolved.

Zerodha Varsity principle for today: Wednesday’s Wall Street selloff traces back to one number — the 10-year Treasury yield spiking to its highest since 2007. Varsity’s chapter on valuation explains why that matters beyond bond markets: the risk-free rate, best proxied by that same 10-year yield, anchors the discount rate in every DCF and CAPM-based valuation, so a higher risk-free rate mechanically shrinks the present value of a company’s future cash flows. That’s exactly why the sell-off hit the highest-multiple, longest-duration names hardest — the Nasdaq fell more than twice as much as the Dow — and the same logic explains Wednesday’s sector split in Mumbai, where pricier IT names lagged while cheaper cyclicals like Hindalco rallied.
