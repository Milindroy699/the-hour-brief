AI & Tech

CoreWeave’s Q3 revenue surges 168% and sends Wall Street back into the AI trade, even as Anthropic’s IPO book runs several times oversubscribed and Google locks in an option on $10 billion of its stock, OpenAI opens a “Businesses in ChatGPT” hub with sponsored AI sales agents, Jensen Huang lets a congressional-briefing deadline pass without public comment, and Team Group’s own numbers show the DDR5 memory shortage still hasn’t peaked

Takeaway Today’s AI story is confidence returning to the trade — CoreWeave’s beat revived Wall Street’s appetite and Anthropic’s book is running oversubscribed, even as the memory shortage underneath it all keeps getting tighter, not looser.

CoreWeave’s Q3 revenue surges 168% to $3.66 billion as it raises full-year guidance

CoreWeave reported Q3 2026 revenue of about $3.66 billion for the quarter ended September 30, up 168% year-over-year from $1.365 billion and roughly 3% above the $3.55 billion analysts expected, with its revenue backlog reaching a record $132.5 billion, up 138% from a year earlier, the company said on November 10. CoreWeave raised its full-year 2026 revenue guidance to $13.0–$13.3 billion, even as its GAAP loss widened on higher interest expense from its debt-funded data-center buildout.

Takeaway A beat-and-raise quarter with a widening loss is becoming the standard shape of an AI-infrastructure earnings report — and investors keep rewarding it anyway.

OpenAI launches “Businesses in ChatGPT,” letting companies claim a profile and deploy AI sales agents

OpenAI said on November 10 that it is rolling out Businesses in ChatGPT in beta, letting companies claim a free profile in a new Business Hub, see click and mention counts, and — for eligible US ecommerce and online-service businesses at launch — deploy “Business Agents” that can complete tasks inside a ChatGPT conversation. OpenAI said sponsored placements will be clearly labelled and kept separate from ordinary ChatGPT responses, and that users’ chats with Business Agents won’t be shared with the businesses themselves.

Takeaway Turning ChatGPT into a business directory is OpenAI quietly building the ad business it has mostly avoided naming out loud.

Anthropic’s IPO book runs several times oversubscribed, with underwriters said to be weighing a higher price

Anthropic’s IPO roadshow, which opened November 10 with a proposed $70–$78-a-share range implying a valuation of up to $1.4 trillion, is already oversubscribed multiple times over, Bloomberg reported, while the Financial Times said underwriters are weighing raising the price band toward a valuation nearer $1.5 trillion. Vanda Research’s Viraj Patel said he expects record retail buying once the stock, ticker ANT, begins trading on Nasdaq.

Takeaway A book running several times oversubscribed before the roadshow even finishes is Wall Street’s clearest vote yet that it isn’t worried about an AI bubble, whatever it says in public.

Google locks in an option to buy up to $10 billion of Anthropic’s non-voting stock

Anthropic’s IPO filings show Alphabet holds an option to buy up to $10 billion of the company’s Class N common stock at the IPO price through a private placement tied to the listing; Class N shares carry no votes while Alphabet holds them. Alphabet and Amazon are both expected to hold stakes above 5% of Anthropic once existing convertible notes convert into stock at listing.

Takeaway Sizing a stake at $10 billion but stripping it of votes is Google’s way of betting big on Anthropic without inviting controlling-shareholder scrutiny.

Jensen Huang’s deadline to answer a congressional-briefing request passes without public word from Nvidia

Three House Republican committee chairs had given Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang until Wednesday, November 11, to accept a proposed November 18 closed-door briefing on his contacts with a Megaspeed executive reportedly alleged to have Chinese-intelligence ties, or to propose another date. As of Wednesday, Nvidia had not publicly said whether Huang accepted, declined or proposed an alternative, and the request remains voluntary rather than a subpoena.

Takeaway Letting a deadline pass without comment is its own kind of answer, even when nobody says anything at all.

The DDR5 memory shortage “hasn’t peaked,” Team Group tells investors, with prices up more than 450% in a year

Team Group’s own financial report tells investors the DDR5 shortage has not yet peaked, with the tightest stretch expected to run from the current quarter through the second quarter of 2027, as AI data centers keep pulling manufacturing capacity toward high-bandwidth memory and server DRAM. One tracked 16GB DDR5 chip price has risen more than 450% over the past year, from about $8.70 in October 2025 to roughly $48 in September 2026.

Takeaway Every AI earnings beat upstream keeps landing as a cost headache for anyone just trying to buy a laptop downstream.

Noel Tata steps down from six Tata Group boards on his 70th birthday while holding his ground at Tata Sons, even as Jio Platforms posts its first results as a standalone-listed company, Ather Energy crosses 300,000 scooters sold for the year, Clinics On Cloud and Peppermint Robotics lead a $290 million week for Indian startup funding, Myntra and Flipkart’s combined festive fashion share slides to 76.8% from 89%, and UPI crosses ₹1 lakh crore in daily transaction value for the first time

Takeaway Today’s business story is addition and subtraction happening at once — Noel Tata gives up ground to keep his strongest position and Ather quietly builds its lead, even as Jio, UPI and India’s funding week all notch records and Myntra and Flipkart lose theirs.

Noel Tata steps down from six Tata Group boards today, holding his ground only at Tata Sons

Noel Tata, chairman of Tata Trusts, retires on November 12 — his 70th birthday — from the boards of Trent, Voltas, Tata Investment Corporation, Tata International, Tata Steel and Titan, stepping away from four boards he chaired and two where he was vice-chairman, according to Storyboard18. He remains on the Tata Sons board as a Tata Trusts nominee director, the one seat that matters most given his unresolved dispute with chairman N. Chandrasekaran over the board’s move to reappoint Chandrasekaran for another five years.

Takeaway Giving up six boardrooms to hold one is only a retreat if you ignore which one actually decides anything.

Clinics On Cloud and Peppermint Robotics lead a $290 million week for Indian startup funding

Pune-based healthcare platform Clinics On Cloud raised ₹121.8 crore (about $12.75 million) in a Series A round from Dubai-based investors through the Model42 platform, valuing it at roughly ₹600 crore, while robotics startup Peppermint Robotics raised ₹101.4 crore (about $10.62 million) in a pre-Series B round led by pi Ventures and Crane Venture Partners. The two rounds were part of a week (November 2–7) in which Indian startups raised more than $290 million across 22 deals, led by jewellery brand GIVA’s $100 million-plus round.

Takeaway A healthcare platform and a robotics startup both raising comfortably in the same week suggests India’s funding slump has at least found a floor.

IndianStartupNews →

Ather Energy crosses 300,000 scooters sold for the year, growing 67% even as Ola Electric slides

Ather Energy delivered 301,133 electric scooters between January 1 and November 6, 2026, up 67% year-on-year, making it only the third Indian e-scooter maker — after TVS Motor and Bajaj Auto — to cross 300,000 units in a calendar year, according to Autocar Professional. Ola Electric, once the segment’s volume leader, has continued to lose market share over the same stretch.

Takeaway Two IITian-founded EV makers chose different paths out of the same market, and only one of them is still compounding.

Autocar Professional →

Myntra and Flipkart’s combined festive fashion order share slides to 76.8% from 89%

Fynd’s Festive Season Report 2026 found that Myntra and Flipkart’s combined share of festive fashion orders fell to 76.8% this year from 89% a year earlier, as Ajio, Amazon and Jiomart all gained ground, BusinessToday reported on November 6. The two Walmart-linked platforms still lead the category by a wide margin, but the erosion marks a clear shift from a near-duopoly toward a more contested field.

Takeaway Losing 12 points of share in a single festive season is the kind of number that looks small until a rival's quarterly results show where it went.

BusinessToday →

Jio Platforms posts its first results as a standalone-listed company, profit up 13.7%

Jio Platforms reported consolidated net profit of about ₹8,389 crore for the September 2026 quarter, up 13.7% year-on-year, on sales of ₹40,475 crore, up 11.4%, according to a Capital Market-sourced report dated November 5 — its first quarterly results since its October 28 Nasdaq-style NSE and BSE debut, where shares rose about 21% above their issue price. Akash Ambani told employees the listing marks a starting point rather than an endpoint, with the next decade focused on AI, cloud and space technology.

Takeaway A 13.7% profit rise is a thoroughly ordinary number for a company that just became India’s most-watched new listing in years — which may be exactly the point.

UPI crosses ₹1 lakh crore in daily transaction value for the first time

India’s Unified Payments Interface processed a record 25.47 billion transactions worth ₹31.53 lakh crore in October 2026, NPCI data reported on November 1 showed, with average daily value crossing ₹1 lakh crore for the first time at about ₹1.01 lakh crore a day. PhonePe led transaction volume with roughly 45.6% share in September, followed by Google Pay at 32.2% and Paytm at 8.1%.

Takeaway A payments network crossing ₹1 lakh crore a day is no longer a growth story so much as a description of how India now moves money, period.

Sensex and Nifty rally to their highest close since February, led by PSU banks and auto

The Sensex closed up 772.75 points (0.95%) at 81,879.40 and the Nifty 50 up 210.40 points (0.84%) at 25,255.05 on Wednesday, their highest close since February 2026, after an intraday high of 82,520.40 on the Sensex, with Nifty PSU Bank the top-performing sector at nearly 3% as banking and auto stocks led, according to Business Standard. The gains pared back somewhat from the day’s peak by the close.

Takeaway Giving back some of an intraday 1.7% gain by the close doesn’t take away from the fact that this is still Dalal Street’s best finish in nine months.

Wall Street logs its best session in weeks as CoreWeave’s earnings beat revives the AI trade

The S&P 500 rose about 1.3% to roughly 7,619.38 and the Nasdaq Composite gained about 1.6% to roughly 25,466 on Wednesday, with the Dow adding about 0.5%, as CoreWeave’s blowout Q3 report and raised guidance sparked a broad rally in chip and AI-infrastructure stocks, recovering much of the prior week’s AI-related sell-off, according to TheStreet and Yahoo Finance.

Takeaway One company’s earnings call reviving an entire sector’s stock prices says more about how jumpy this market is than about CoreWeave itself.

M&M, IndiGo and Trent lead Wednesday’s gainers, while Adani Ports and ICICI Bank lag

Mahindra & Mahindra led large-cap gainers on Wednesday, up 3.92%, with InterGlobe Aviation (IndiGo) up 3.76%, Trent up 2.59%, Power Grid up 2.13% and Tata Steel up 2.01%, according to Upstox. Adani Ports was the weakest large cap, down 1.84%, with ICICI Bank, Tech Mahindra, HCL Technologies and Nestle India also among the day’s losers.

Takeaway An auto-and-aviation-led gainers list next to an IT-and-FMCG-heavy losers list is a fairly clean read on which domestic sectors the rally actually trusted.
