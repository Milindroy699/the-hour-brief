Anthropic’s own prospective IPO investors reportedly push its valuation talk down toward $1 trillion from an earlier $2 trillion, even as AI-inference startup fal doubles its own valuation to $16 billion in a $1.3 billion round, OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Anthropic jointly back a shared opt-out token for publishers against AI training, and a federal November 13 deadline looms over an already-exploited, two-bug Ivanti zero-day chain

Takeaway Today’s AI story is confidence fraying at the public-market edge even as the private-money river keeps flowing underneath it — investors squeeze Anthropic’s IPO price while inference infrastructure, data-center power deals and industry-wide scraping rules keep moving forward regardless.

Anthropic’s own prospective IPO investors reportedly push its valuation talk down toward $1 trillion

Potential investors in Anthropic’s IPO are reportedly skeptical the company can hold its roadshow the week of November 9 and still list before Thanksgiving, according to a Reuters exclusive citing four people familiar with the talks; Anthropic declined to comment. Discussions have centered on a valuation closer to $1.5 trillion — down from earlier talk of $1.8–2 trillion — with one investor telling the Wall Street Journal pricing could land nearer $1 trillion, with Reuters tying the hesitation to the past month’s tech selloff and post-midterm AI-regulation worries.

Takeaway A company that was floating a $2 trillion number a month ago is now fielding investor pushback toward half that — the market, not the company, is setting the price here.

Reuters, via Kuwait Times →

AI-inference startup fal raises $1.3 billion, doubling its valuation to $16 billion in seven months

Fal, which runs inference infrastructure for image, video and audio AI models, closed a $1.3 billion Series F on November 3 led by Bessemer Venture Partners, with Nvidia, Sound Ventures and T. Rowe Price also participating, according to Bloomberg. The $16 billion valuation is double the $8 billion fal commanded in April, when Sequoia led its $350 million Series E.

Takeaway Nobody outside AI-infrastructure circles has heard of fal, and it just doubled in value in seven months — a reminder that money chasing AI’s plumbing layer hasn’t slowed even as bubble talk has picked up.

Bloomberg, via Four Week MBA →

A federal deadline on a chained, actively exploited Ivanti zero-day lands November 13

CISA added two Ivanti Connect Secure flaws, CVE-2026-111357 and CVE-2026-118527, to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on October 30, ordering federal agencies to patch the first by November 2 (already past) and the second by November 13. Researchers say a threat group tracked as UTA0576 chained both bugs with an earlier authentication-bypass flaw Ivanti disclosed October 13 to gain root access on unpatched systems; fixes are available in Connect Secure versions 25.1.1.3 and 22.8R2.6.

Takeaway One deadline already blown and a second four days out is exactly the kind of compounding risk that stays quiet until it’s a breach headline.

Help Net Security →

NTT turns to Bloom Energy fuel cells for 900 megawatts of AI data-center power

NTT Global Data Centers and Bloom Energy announced on November 5 that NTT will deploy Bloom’s solid-oxide fuel cells as primary on-site power across multiple global data-center projects totaling 900 megawatts, with first shipments in 2027 — Bloom’s largest international project to date. It follows Bloom’s financing partnership with Brookfield expanding to $25 billion, from an October $5 billion framework, to fund exactly this kind of on-site power buildout.

Takeaway When a data-center operator reaches for fuel cells instead of waiting on the grid, that’s a tell for just how power-constrained the AI buildout has become.

Bloom Energy/NTT, via Black Ridge Research →

OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Anthropic back a shared opt-out token for AI-training scraping

The four companies said on October 30 they will support a shared robots.txt signal, reportedly named “__usage_ai-training,” that publishers can pair with a Disallow rule to flag pages as off-limits for generative-AI training — a generic bot block alone won’t register as an opt-out. A November 4 analysis from the Knight-Georgetown Institute’s Alissa Cooper notes the mechanism doesn’t yet cover search use of the same content, and isn’t mandatory under the EU AI Act’s Code of Practice since it hasn’t been formally standardized.

Takeaway A shared opt-out mostly helps publishers who haven’t already been scraped — which, after several years of AI training runs, is close to nobody.

Bharti Airtel’s Q2 profit rises 31% to ₹8,888 crore even as it misses Street estimates and Nykaa’s profit triples to ₹101 crore, even as Tata Trusts’ Darius Khambata quits rather than seek reappointment, calling September’s board vote reappointing N. Chandrasekaran “a day that will live in infamy,” while India’s startup funding rebounds to $255 million this week on jewellery brand GIVA’s ₹975 crore round and Shiprocket swings to its first quarterly profit

Takeaway Today’s business story is steady corporate progress running alongside unfinished boardroom business — real profit growth at Nykaa, Shiprocket and Airtel, and a funding rebound for Indian startups, even as the Tata Sons succession fight claims its most senior outside voice yet.

India’s startup funding rebounds to $255 million this week, led by GIVA and EloElo Group

Indian startups raised about $255 million across 20 deals in the week of November 2–6, up from $130.3 million the previous week, according to Inc42’s weekly tracker. Jewellery brand GIVA’s roughly ₹975 crore round and microdrama app operator EloElo Group’s $80 million raise together accounted for most of the total.

Takeaway Two rounds carrying most of a week’s funding isn’t quite the broad recovery the headline number suggests.

Inc42 →

GIVA raises ₹975 crore led by Multiples PE, plans 160 new stores in FY27

D2C jewellery brand GIVA raised roughly ₹975 crore (over $100 million) led by Multiples PE, with Epiq Capital and Edelweiss Discovery Fund also participating, according to Inc42 — pushing its valuation above the roughly ₹4,500–4,600 crore it carried previously. The company plans to open 160 new stores in FY27 with the fresh capital.

Takeaway A round this size going straight into physical stores, not just marketing spend, says GIVA is betting its growth is real enough to show up in rent and footfall.

Inc42 →

Nykaa’s Q2 FY27 net profit triples to ₹101 crore

Nykaa’s standalone net profit rose 207% year-on-year to ₹101.19 crore for the quarter ended September 30, up from ₹32.98 crore a year earlier, as revenue grew 28% to ₹3,006 crore, according to Business Today.

Takeaway Tripling profit while still growing revenue at nearly 30% is the kind of quarter that quiets questions about whether e-commerce margins can ever really work.

Business Today →

Bharti Airtel’s Q2 profit rises 31% to ₹8,888 crore, just short of Street estimates

Bharti Airtel’s consolidated net profit rose 31% year-on-year to ₹8,888 crore for the quarter ended September 30, from ₹6,792 crore a year earlier, as revenue grew 16.7% to ₹60,878 crore and ARPU climbed to ₹271, according to Business Today. The result still missed analyst consensus of roughly ₹9,293 crore.

Takeaway A 31% profit jump that still disappoints the Street says more about how high expectations for India’s telecom leader have climbed than about Airtel’s quarter.

Business Today →

EloElo Group raises $80 million to take its microdrama business global

EloElo Group, which runs short-form “microdrama” video app Story TV, raised an $80 million Series C led by Hornbill Capital and NBCUniversal, with MIT Investment Co, Play Ventures, Kalaari Capital, Waterbridge and Griffin Gaming also participating, according to Inc42. The round takes the company’s total funding to around $130 million.

Takeaway An American media giant backing an Indian microdrama app is a small sign of how fast that format has gone from a niche bet to something worth global attention.

Inc42 →

Shiprocket swings to its first quarterly profit since listing

Logistics platform Shiprocket reported a net profit of ₹0.6 crore for the September quarter, reversing a ₹20.3 crore loss a year earlier, as revenue grew 35% year-on-year to ₹674 crore, according to Inc42. The company says it is now debt-free following its IPO.

Takeaway ₹0.6 crore is a thin profit by any measure, but turning positive at all, debt-free, is the signal that matters more than the number itself.

Inc42 →

Tata Trusts’ Darius Khambata declines reappointment, calls September’s board vote “a day that will live in infamy”

Senior advocate Darius Khambata, a longtime Tata Trusts trustee, told Noel Tata and CEO Siddharth Sharma in a November 2 letter that he will not seek another term at the Sir Dorabji Tata Trust and Sir Ratan Tata Trust, with his current term due to end November 9. Khambata called the September 17 Tata Sons board vote that reappointed N. Chandrasekaran as chairman — by a 4-1 margin, over Noel Tata’s objection — “a day that will live in infamy,” saying it has sullied Tata Sons’ reputation, according to Bar & Bench.

Takeaway When the person stepping down is a lawyer known for careful language, choosing a phrase like that is itself a message to the rest of the Tata Sons board.

Samvat 2083 opens in the red, snapping Sensex and Nifty’s eight-year Muhurat winning streak

The Sensex closed Sunday’s Muhurat session, marking the start of Samvat 2083, at 80,999.40, down 28.62 points or 0.04% from Friday’s 81,028.02, while the Nifty 50 slipped back below the 25,000 mark it had just reclaimed on Dhanteras — the first negative Muhurat close since 2017, according to Business Standard and Kotak Neo, even after the Sensex had been up as much as 0.49% intraday. Market breadth stayed healthy regardless, with 3,136 stocks advancing during the one-hour session.

Takeaway Breaking an eight-year pattern on the one day meant to be auspicious is the kind of coincidence investors will be talking about longer than the 0.04% move itself.

Kotak Neo →

The outgoing Samvat 2082 closes as benchmarks’ worst year in 15 years

Between the last Muhurat session on October 21, 2025 and November 4, 2026, the Nifty 50 fell about 7.4% and the Sensex about 8.4% over the Samvat 2082 year — its worst performance in 15 years, according to a Univest review — even as the Nifty Smallcap 100 gained roughly 4.5% and the Midcap 100 about 0.3% over the same stretch. Heavy foreign selling, a crude-oil shock and a rate-hike cycle were the main drags cited.

Takeaway A year where smaller stocks gained and the headline indices still fell is a reminder that “the market” and “the benchmark” aren’t always telling the same story.

Univest →

Brokerages split on Samvat 2083 targets, from Nifty 25,000 to 27,000 by next Diwali

A Business Standard poll of brokerages finds a central case near Sensex 87,000 and Nifty 27,000 by next Diwali, while Ventura’s Vinit Bolinjkar projects a narrower Nifty 26,500–27,000 range and SMC Global’s Seema Srivastava is more cautious at Nifty 25,000–26,500, according to Univest’s summary of the poll.

Takeaway When even the most cautious and most bullish brokerage targets both point up from here, the disagreement is about how much optimism is reasonable, not whether any is.
