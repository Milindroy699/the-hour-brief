AI & Tech

Wall Street's Nasdaq slides into correction as an AI-regulation scare wipes out Wednesday's post-midterm rally, even as Anthropic's amended IPO filing shows Q3 revenue surging to $17 billion while swinging to an operating loss, Microsoft offers buyouts to roughly 60,000 employees as AI spending takes priority, and Meta narrows its 2026 AI capex to $135–145 billion after a $9.89 billion legal charge

Takeaway Today’s AI story is the bill arriving — a chip-stock selloff erasing a rally in a single session, Anthropic's filing swinging between massive revenue growth and massive losses, and two of the industry's biggest spenders confirming that paying for this boom means layoffs and legal charges, not just data centers.

Nasdaq Composite enters correction as an AI-chip selloff wipes out the post-midterm rally

On Thursday, November 5, the Nasdaq Composite fell 1,421.51 points, or 5.52%, to close at 24,315.00 — about 12% below the record high it set a month earlier — while the S&P 500 dropped 4.36% to 7,353.73 and the Dow fell 1,833.06 points, or 3.44%, to 51,523.06, according to CNBC's live markets coverage. The rout erased Wednesday's rally, which had followed Democrats' sweep of the midterms; this time, investors sold chip and AI-infrastructure stocks on fears that a Democratic Congress and state officials could tighten rules on data-center buildouts.

Takeaway A single session wiping out the prior day's giant rally shows just how little conviction is behind either move right now — this market is trading on political headlines, not fundamentals.

Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom lead a chip-stock rout on AI-regulation fears

Nvidia fell more than 9% to a seven-month low — now down over 25% from its October 6 peak — while AMD plunged 17% and Broadcom dropped 10% in Thursday's session, according to Charles Schwab's market commentary. The selloff was concentrated in chipmakers and AI-infrastructure names tied most directly to the data-center buildout that Tuesday's election results put in political jeopardy. AMD CEO Lisa Su said this week that she does not see AI chip demand slowing anytime soon, per Yahoo Finance.

Takeaway Su's confidence and AMD's 17% one-day drop are both true at once — the structural demand story and the stock's near-term price can move in completely opposite directions.

Anthropic's amended S-1 shows Q3 revenue surged to $17 billion, even as it swung to an operating loss

Anthropic's amended IPO filing, reported November 2, shows third-quarter revenue of about $17.0 billion, up 1,127% year-over-year, according to StockAnalysis.com. The company swung to a $567 million operating loss, compared with $974 million in operating profit in the second quarter, which it attributed mainly to $5.0 billion of compute under short-term commitments; net loss for the quarter narrowed to about $23.3 billion from $66.3 billion in Q2, largely reflecting non-cash swings in the value of convertible notes and derivatives.

Takeaway Revenue up elevenfold and a swing into an operating loss in the same quarter is exactly the kind of mixed signal investors will be arguing over for the rest of this IPO roadshow.

Microsoft offers buyouts to around 60,000 employees as AI spending takes priority

Microsoft confirmed to TechCrunch that it is offering voluntary buyouts to roughly 60,000 employees at the vice-president level and below across the US, Canada, India and the UK, with eligible teams including Xbox, Windows, Copilot and Finance; data-center, sales and marketing staff are excluded. The company said the program is not a replacement for layoffs, which remain part of its approach depending on business conditions.

Takeaway Offering buyouts to 60,000 people while saying layoffs aren't off the table either is Microsoft trying to shrink its headcount costs without the blowback of mass firings.

Meta narrows its 2026 AI capex to $135–145 billion after a $9.89 billion legal charge

Meta reported Q3 2026 revenue of $64.6 billion, up 26% year-over-year, while narrowing its 2026 capital-expenditure guidance to a range of $135–145 billion, according to Yahoo Finance. A separate $9.89 billion litigation charge cut into operating income for the quarter, and one analysis put capex, including finance-lease principal, at $34.91 billion against $32.79 billion of operating cash flow — pushing free cash flow negative.

Takeaway Narrowing a capex range sounds like discipline, but paired with a legal charge this size and negative free cash flow, it reads more like managing optics around spending Meta can't actually slow down.

Yahoo Finance →

Google expands ads inside AI Mode search, including multiturn ads that follow up questions

Google said it is expanding ads within Search's AI Mode to more countries and launching multiturn ads in the US, letting sponsored content appear in follow-up queries within the same AI conversation, according to the company's own Ads & Commerce blog. Ads will continue to be labeled “Sponsored.”

Takeaway Putting ads inside the AI conversation itself, not just beside it, is Google testing how much commercial content a chat-style search experience can carry before people notice.

SBI's Q2 profit rises 14% to a record ₹23,038 crore as bad loans shrink and SBI Cards' profit jumps 75%, Hero MotoCorp's profit climbs 41% on a one-time gain and Tata Motors swings back into the black, even as Noel Tata prepares to step down from six Tata Group boards amid an unresolved fight over N. Takeaway Today’s business story is India's banks and automakers quietly delivering clean quarters while Tata's boardroom fight keeps simmering — a reminder that strong numbers and unresolved governance questions can sit inside the same company at the same time.

SBI's Q2 FY27 profit rises 14% to a record ₹23,038 crore as bad loans shrink

State Bank of India reported standalone net profit of about ₹23,038 crore for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, up 14.28% from ₹20,259 crore a year earlier, according to Business Today. Gross NPAs fell to 1.39% of advances from 1.73% a year ago, provisions dropped to ₹4,217 crore from ₹5,400 crore, and operating profit rose 7.28% to ₹34,226 crore.

Takeaway Profit growth matters less here than the NPA and provisioning numbers moving together in the same direction — that's a loan book actually getting healthier, not just bigger.

SBI Cards' Q2 FY27 profit jumps 75% to ₹777.60 crore

SBI Cards and Payment Services reported profit after tax of ₹777.60 crore for the quarter, up 75% year-over-year, according to PSUConnect.

Takeaway A 75% jump at the card-issuing arm, well ahead of the parent bank's own growth, suggests India's credit-card spending is recovering faster than lending overall.

Hero MotoCorp's Q2 FY27 profit rises 41% to ₹1,861 crore, helped by a one-time gain

Hero MotoCorp reported standalone revenue up 21% year-over-year to ₹14,687 crore and consolidated profit after tax up 41% to ₹1,861 crore, according to Multibagg; the jump included a one-time ₹397 crore gain from the equity consolidation of an associate company.

Takeaway Stripping out the one-time gain would put Hero's underlying profit growth well below the 41% headline — worth checking before reading too much into that number alone.

Tata Motors swings to a ₹1,257 crore profit as standalone revenue jumps 42%

Tata Motors reported a consolidated net profit of about ₹1,257 crore for the quarter, reversing an ₹867 crore loss a year earlier, with standalone revenue up roughly 42% to ₹23,900 crore; commodity inflation cut about 590 basis points from the standalone EBIT margin, according to the company's earnings call coverage carried by Yahoo Finance.

Takeaway A swing from loss to profit alongside a 590-basis-point margin hit from commodity costs shows the turnaround is real but still fighting serious cost pressure underneath.

Yahoo Finance →

Noel Tata set to step down from six Tata Group boards on November 12 amid unresolved Chandrasekaran dispute

Noel Tata will step down from six Tata Group company boards — including Trent, Voltas, Titan, Tata Steel, Tata Investment Corporation and Tata International — on November 12, 2026, when he turns 70, under the conglomerate's internal retirement-age policy for non-executive directors, according to People Matters. He will remain on the Tata Sons board as Tata Trusts' nominee director. The exits land amid an unresolved dispute over N. Chandrasekaran's reappointment as Tata Sons chairman for a further five years, which Noel Tata has publicly objected to.

Takeaway Stepping down from six operating-company boards while staying on at Tata Sons itself means Noel Tata keeps his seat at the one table where the Chandrasekaran fight actually gets decided.

Stratechery's “no AI bubble” thesis faces its sharpest test yet

In a March 16, 2026 essay titled “Agents Over Bubbles,” Stratechery's Ben Thompson argued that AI infrastructure spending is not a bubble, framing it as demand driven by agentic systems that make multiple model calls per task rather than speculative overbuilding. Thursday's chip-stock rout — which erased a day's rally and pushed the Nasdaq into correction territory — is the kind of move his argument will have to keep surviving through Anthropic's IPO and the next round of hyperscaler earnings.

Takeaway An eight-month-old argument isn't wrong just because the market had one bad day, but a thesis this confident needs to keep surviving days exactly like this one.

Sensex and Nifty surge over 2.5% as Democrats' midterm sweep and falling crude lift autos and banks

The Sensex closed Thursday, November 5, at 79,310.01, up 2,008.61 points or 2.60%, while the Nifty 50 rose 576 points, or 2.40%, to 24,536.60, according to Business Standard. Bajaj Finance led gainers, up 7.64%, followed by Maruti Suzuki (+5.46%) and Tata Motors (+5.44%); the Nifty Midcap index rose 1.8% and the Smallcap index 0.6%. The rupee strengthened to 94.47 per dollar from 94.64.

Takeaway A rally this broad, lifting autos, financials and midcaps together, suggests Indian investors read Tuesday's US election result as good news for easing oil prices rather than as any kind of AI-regulation threat.

Wall Street's post-election rally reverses into its worst day since April 2025

The Nasdaq Composite fell 1,421.51 points, or 5.52%, to 24,315.00 on Thursday, entering correction territory about 12% below its October high, while the S&P 500 dropped 4.36% to 7,353.73 and the Dow fell 1,833.06 points, or 3.44%, to 51,523.06, according to CNBC. The declines erased Wednesday's rally, which had followed Democrats' sweep of the midterms; this time the same political shift was read as a threat to AI data-center buildouts, dragging chip stocks down hardest.

Takeaway The exact same election result driving a historic rally one day and a correction the next shows just how unsettled Wall Street's read on AI-regulation risk really is.

Nvidia hits a seven-month low as the chip-stock selloff deepens

Nvidia fell more than 9% on Thursday to a seven-month low, now down over 25% from its October 6 peak, while AMD plunged 17% and Broadcom fell 10%, according to Charles Schwab's market commentary. Nvidia's own next earnings report isn't due until November 17, meaning its stock is moving almost entirely on other companies' fortunes and political headlines right now.

Takeaway A 25% slide with no company-specific news driving it is a reminder that Nvidia's stock price currently reflects sentiment about the whole AI buildout, not just its own numbers.
