AI & Tech

Apple’s first foldable iPhone goes on sale today with Foxconn’s assembly yield still stuck near 60%, as IBM beats expectations on accelerating Software and Infrastructure growth, Meta’s board approves a new class of stock to help fund its AI buildout, Anthropic and Nscale reportedly discuss a second $45 billion compute deal for a West Virginia data center barely two months after the first, and SoftBank’s Masayoshi Son seeks up to $100 billion from Gulf investors for a new AI and robotics fund even as his $65 billion OpenAI bet strains the balance sheet

Takeaway AI’s capital needs keep forcing structural change — a new Meta stock class, a second multi-billion-dollar compute deal before the first is finished, and a sovereign-wealth pitch — while Apple’s foldable shows even well-funded companies can’t out-spend a hard manufacturing yield problem.

Apple’s first foldable iPhone goes on sale today, with assembly yield still near 60%

Apple’s foldable iPhone, widely reported as the “iPhone Duo,” becomes available for purchase today, October 23, after preorders opened October 16, Free Press Journal reported, citing China’s Jiemian News. Foxconn’s final-assembly yield for the device stood at just above 60% as of mid-September — roughly four in ten units failing that production stage — with Samsung Display’s foldable OLED panels and the hinge cited as the main bottlenecks; Apple’s global stocking target for 2026 is about 6 to 8 million units.

Takeaway A yield barely above 60% on launch day means Apple is selling its most-hyped new hardware in years into supply it can’t yet make enough of.

IBM beats Q3 expectations and raises its free cash flow outlook on Software and Infrastructure growth

IBM reported October 21 that third-quarter revenue rose 5% year-on-year (6% at constant currency) to $17.1 billion, with Software up 8% and Infrastructure up 4%, while Consulting was flat, according to its SEC filing. The company kept its full-year constant-currency revenue growth outlook at 4-5% and now expects free cash flow to rise by more than $1 billion; CEO Arvind Krishna said clients are deploying the company’s AI tools to reshape their operating models.

Takeaway A hardware-and-consulting company growing fastest in Software and Infrastructure, not Consulting, is IBM’s AI pitch landing furthest from the part of its business that sells the advice.

SEC filing (IBM) →

Meta’s board approves a new non-voting share class to help fund its AI buildout

Meta’s board has approved creating a new, publicly listed, non-voting Class C common stock, effective January 19, 2027, according to an SEC filing, to help fund the scale of investment needed to expand AI infrastructure. The board declared a dividend of one Class C share for every existing Class A or Class B share, and the Chan Zuckerberg entities — which hold about 56.1% of Meta’s voting power — have already delivered written consent approving the change.

Takeaway Creating new stock that can’t vote lets Meta raise AI-infrastructure money without Mark Zuckerberg giving up an inch of control over how it’s spent.

SEC filing (Meta) →

Anthropic and Nscale reportedly discuss a second $45 billion compute deal

Nscale is in talks to sell Anthropic roughly $45 billion of computing power from a second building at its Monarch data center campus in West Virginia, Bloomberg reported October 21, with about 460 megawatts of capacity and a deal possibly finalized within days. The report comes barely two months after Anthropic’s first Nscale agreement — also roughly $45 billion over six years for about 460 megawatts from Monarch’s first building — was reported in August.

Takeaway Doubling down on the same campus for the same money twice in two months is Anthropic betting that compute, not cash, is the harder thing to secure.

The Star (Bloomberg) →

SoftBank’s Son seeks up to $100 billion from Gulf investors for a new AI fund

SoftBank founder Masayoshi Son is seeking to raise as much as $100 billion from Gulf investors for a new fund that would buy companies and use AI and robotics to improve their operations, the Financial Times reported, with talks described as preliminary. The push comes as SoftBank carries almost $65 billion invested in OpenAI, a $40 billion bridge loan due for refinancing by March 2027, and a separate $10 billion loan taken out in August against its OpenAI stake.

Takeaway Raising a fresh $100 billion while still refinancing debt taken against its own OpenAI stake is SoftBank treating its AI bet as too big to pause, not too big to keep feeding.

Product & Business

Jio Platforms’ record IPO closes today after a Day 2 close above five times overall subscription, as Infosys heads into today’s Q2 results and dividend call, even as food-delivery rivals Swiggy and Eternal report opposite Q2 trajectories — Swiggy’s loss narrowing to ₹922 crore as Eternal’s profit jump falls short of estimates — while Urban Company’s board clears a ₹2,500 crore raise after its own Q2 loss triples

Takeaway Capital keeps flowing into India’s new-economy companies even as their quarterly numbers diverge sharply — quick commerce and travel tech draw fresh money while food delivery’s two biggest names post opposite profit trends in the same week.

Jio Platforms’ record IPO closes today after crossing five times overall subscription on Day 2

Reliance’s Jio Platforms IPO closed its second of three bidding days above 5.4 times overall subscription as of the evening of October 22, with qualified institutional buyers bidding roughly 12.9 times their quota while the retail portion reached only about 1.5 times its own, per Chittorgarh’s live tracker. The ₹30,213 crore issue’s third and final bidding day is today, October 23, ahead of allotment on October 26 and a planned October 28 listing.

Takeaway Retail demand crept from 0.73 times on Day 1 to roughly 1.5 times by Day 2’s close — rising, but still a fraction of the enthusiasm institutions showed from the start.

Chittorgarh →

Swiggy’s Q2 loss narrows 15.6% to ₹922 crore as revenue jumps 36%

Swiggy reported a consolidated net loss of ₹922 crore for the quarter ended September 30, down 15.6% from ₹1,092 crore a year earlier, while revenue from operations rose 36% year-on-year to ₹7,571 crore, Business Standard reported October 22. The loss widened from the June quarter’s ₹791 crore as the company spent more on growing its Instamart quick-commerce arm and newer bets.

Takeaway A loss that shrinks from a year ago but grows from the quarter before is Swiggy trading a cleaner annual story for continued near-term spending on Instamart.

Business Standard →

Eternal’s profit jumps 92% to ₹125 crore, but still falls short of analyst estimates

Eternal, the parent of Zomato and Blinkit, reported October 21 that Q2 FY27 consolidated revenue rose 78% year-on-year to ₹24,138 crore and net profit climbed 92% to ₹125 crore, with Blinkit alone contributing ₹19,152 crore of revenue, Business Standard reported. The profit still missed CNBC-TV18’s ₹250 crore estimate, and shares fell as much as 3.5% on the result.

Takeaway Profit nearly doubling and still disappointing the street shows how far ahead of Eternal’s own numbers investor expectations for Blinkit have run.

Business Standard →

Urban Company’s board clears a ₹2,500 crore raise after its Q2 loss triples

Urban Company’s board approved on October 16 a plan to raise up to ₹2,500 crore through equity, warrants or convertible instruments, the same day it reported a Q2 FY27 net loss of ₹182.5 crore, up from ₹59.3 crore a year earlier, on revenue that rose 41% to ₹537.3 crore, Inc42 reported. The company said the money will help defend its InstaHelp quick-help business against rivals Snabbit and Pronto and fund a push into residential air conditioners under its Native brand; shares fell about 9.5% on the news.

Takeaway Raising capital the same day a loss triples is Urban Company betting that defending InstaHelp now is worth the market’s immediate verdict on its numbers.

Inc42 →

Flipkart takes a minority stake in B2B travel platform TravClan

Flipkart announced it has taken a minority stake in TravClan, a global B2B travel-tech platform used by travel agents, intended to strengthen its own Cleartrip and Flipkart Travel offerings, according to Inc42. Neither company disclosed the investment amount; TravClan said it would use the capital to expand its engineering and business-development teams.

Takeaway An undisclosed stake both sides still announced publicly suggests the strategic signal to Flipkart’s travel rivals mattered more than the dollar figure.

Inc42 →

India’s 2026 IPO fundraising hits a record pace for the first nine months despite fewer listings

India’s IPO fundraising reached about $12.5 billion in the January-September 2026 period, the largest total for that period since LSEG’s data began in 1980, even as the number of listings fell 17.9% year-on-year to 220, per LSEG data reported by 5Paisa. Average deal size rose to $57 million from $42 million a year earlier.

Takeaway Fewer, bigger IPOs raising a record sum says India’s primary market is consolidating around flagship listings like Jio’s rather than broadening out.

Infosys heads into today’s Q2 results and dividend decision

Infosys is scheduled to announce its Q2 FY27 results for the quarter ended September 30 around 3:45pm IST today, October 23, with its board also set to consider an interim dividend, Kotak Neo reported. Infosys paid a ₹23-per-share interim dividend alongside an ₹18,000 crore buyback when it last reported in October 2025, giving investors a reference point heading into today’s announcement.

Takeaway One of IT’s bellwethers reporting after three private banks already did this week means markets get a fuller picture of corporate India’s quarter only today.

Sensex and Nifty fall for a third straight session as a Houthi drone strike sends Brent to a five-month high

The Sensex fell 460.30 points, or 0.62%, to 73,249.39 on Thursday, October 22, while the Nifty 50 dropped 123.70 points, or 0.54%, to 22,687.65 — a third consecutive losing session — as Brent crude rose above $111 a barrel, its highest close since May, after a Houthi drone strike near Saudi Aramco’s Shaybah oilfield. Saudi Arabia’s Energy Ministry said the drone’s debris sparked a fire outside the plant and struck a contractor’s vehicle, but that production was unaffected; broader Indian indices, including the midcap and smallcap gauges, also declined.

Takeaway A drone strike that Saudi Arabia says left production untouched still moved Dalal Street — India’s market is pricing the campaign’s trajectory, not just today’s barrel count.

The National →

Nasdaq posts its worst session since June as Meta’s 11-day slide deepens on AI-spending worries

The Nasdaq Composite fell 2.6% to 26,008.86 and the S&P 500 dropped 1.21% to 7,663.43 on Thursday, October 22 — each posting its worst session in months — while the Dow Jones Industrial Average inched up 0.02% to 52,867.98, as Meta extended an 11-day losing streak after raising its full-year AI spending forecast to $137.5 billion and declining to commit to a 2027 capex figure, a slide that has cut roughly $18 billion from CEO Mark Zuckerberg’s net worth, Yahoo Finance and Forbes reported.

Takeaway A tech-heavy Nasdaq sinking while the Dow barely moves shows investors drawing a sharper line than ever between AI-spending stocks and everything else.

Zetwerk is set to list on the NSE and BSE today after closing 3.10 times subscribed

Zetwerk Manufacturing Business’s ₹5,012 crore IPO, which closed October 19 subscribed about 3.10 times overall — with qualified institutional buyers at 4.71 times and retail investors at just 0.77 times their quota — is scheduled to list on the NSE and BSE today, October 23, HDFC Sky reported. The company priced its issue at ₹143-151 a share after anchor investors committed roughly ₹2,251 crore ahead of the opening.

Takeaway A listing day arriving on retail’s weakest subscription number of the week will be the real test of whether institutional enthusiasm alone can support the stock.

HDFC Sky →

Jio Platforms’ record IPO closes for bidding today, its grey market premium still pointing to a rich debut

Reliance’s Jio Platforms IPO enters its third and final day of bidding today, October 23, with its grey market premium holding near ₹245-252 a share through the week — implying a listing roughly 22% above the ₹1,119 upper price band, according to trackers cited by Business Today. Allotment is expected October 26 ahead of a planned October 28 listing on the NSE and BSE.

Takeaway A premium that’s barely budged since Monday even as subscription numbers keep climbing is the grey market saying it already knows where this one lands.
