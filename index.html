AI & Tech

Independent evaluator METR finds Anthropic’s AI-assisted research running roughly 2x faster than a year ago and says Anthropic redacted most of its supporting evidence, even as the company’s first Wall Street stock analyst declines to rate it ahead of its IPO roadshow and Blackstone warns its post-IPO credit rating could land below investment grade

Takeaway Today’s AI story is about how little outsiders can verify — a safety evaluator, a credit-rating desk and a first-time stock analyst are all, in their own way, saying they can’t yet confirm how fast Anthropic is really moving or how much risk comes with its IPO.

METR finds Anthropic’s AI-assisted research running roughly 2x faster — then says Anthropic redacted most of its evidence

METR, an independent AI evaluation nonprofit, ran a high-access assessment of Anthropic’s internal R&D from August 12 to October 29, 2026, and published its findings on November 5. Researcher interviews broadly supported a roughly 2x acceleration in Anthropic’s AI-assisted research pace over the past year, which METR said it was confident wasn’t driven by scaled-up training compute — but Anthropic redacted “a majority of the evidence we found most informative for these conclusions” from the roughly 100-page report and cancelled a planned compute-allocation check partway through, according to METR. Anthropic co-founder Sam McCandlish responded that “AI is speeding up our research, but it’s hard to say exactly how much.”

Takeaway An outside group got rare access to measure how fast AI is improving AI at Anthropic — then had most of its best evidence redacted by the company it was evaluating.

Anthropic’s first Wall Street stock analyst declines to rate it, pending its IPO roadshow

Seaport Global Securities analyst Jay Goldberg initiated coverage of Anthropic without assigning a rating, citing the lack of a trading price and a preliminary S-1 with significant blanks, according to Seaport’s note as relayed by Investing.com. Goldberg called Anthropic’s model capabilities a genuine competitive advantage while flagging risk from its rising capital needs and complex governance structure.

Takeaway When the first analyst to cover a company won’t rate it yet, that says as much about the state of Anthropic’s IPO paperwork as about the company itself.

Investing.com →

Blackstone warns Anthropic may not secure an investment-grade credit rating after its IPO

Blackstone told investors it now expects Anthropic’s post-IPO credit rating to land in the “BB” range — below investment grade — reversing its earlier expectation of an investment-grade rating by year-end, according to the Financial Times as relayed by Investing Live. At stake is Anthropic’s roughly $63 billion “Project North Star” chip-financing plan, more than $42 billion of which carries a guarantee from investment-grade-rated Broadcom; Kroll Bond Rating Agency had already rated Anthropic BB+ in June.

Takeaway A below-investment-grade rating would shrink Anthropic’s pool of debt buyers and raise its borrowing costs right as it tries to finance a historic IPO.

Product & Business

India’s startup funding slumps to its weakest month in years at just $678 million in October with no single round crossing $100 million, even as LG Electronics India’s profit climbs 25.65% to ₹489 crore and the National Stock Exchange’s own Q2 profit rises 23% to ₹2,578 crore on the same trading boom reshaping Dalal Street

Takeaway Today’s business story is two speeds at once — steady double-digit profit growth at an established consumer brand and the exchange itself, right as the funding tap for new Indian startups runs drier than it has in years.

India’s startup funding falls 47.5% to $678 million in October — its weakest month in years

Indian startups raised about $678 million in October 2026, down 47.5% from September’s $1.29 billion and down 60.8% from October 2025, according to Entrackr’s monthly tracker as reported by NewsBytes. No startup raised $100 million or more during the month, with only Tessolve Semiconductor and Even Healthcare crossing $50 million; Bengaluru led all cities with $420 million raised across 52 deals, nearly 62% of the national total.

Takeaway A month with zero $100-million-plus rounds, in a year that’s already cooling, is the clearest sign yet that big-ticket investor appetite for Indian startups has gone quiet.

NewsBytes →

LG Electronics India’s Q2 FY27 net profit rises 25.65% to ₹489 crore

LG Electronics India reported a 25.65% year-on-year rise in net profit to ₹489.34 crore for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, up from ₹389.43 crore a year earlier, in results declared November 5, according to The Week. Revenue from operations grew 19.5% to ₹7,380.05 crore, compared with ₹6,174.03 crore in the year-ago quarter.

Takeaway A steady quarter for one of India’s most recognizable consumer-electronics brands, in a results season otherwise dominated by bigger names and bigger swings.

The Week →

NSE’s own Q2 FY27 consolidated profit rises 23% to ₹2,578 crore

The National Stock Exchange reported consolidated net profit of about ₹2,578 crore for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, up 23% year-on-year, as total income grew 9.5% to ₹4,554 crore, according to Business Today. Revenue from transaction charges rose 9% to ₹3,035 crore, with one analysis noting the exchange’s earnings base still leans heavily on weekly Nifty options; profit was down 17.4% sequentially from a stronger Q1 FY27.

Takeaway The exchange itself is profiting handsomely from the same trading boom that keeps filling this newsletter’s Stock Market lane — though a 17% sequential dip is a reminder that boom isn’t a straight line.

NSE and BSE hold a rare Sunday Muhurat trading session today, marking the start of Samvat 2083

NSE and BSE run their once-a-year Diwali Lakshmi Puja “Muhurat” session today, Sunday, November 8, 2026, with the main trading window set for 2:20–3:30 PM IST under an NSE circular dated October 19, 2026 — a departure from the normal Sunday market closure, according to Business Today. The ritual session marks the symbolic start of the Hindu calendar’s Samvat 2083, a tradition BSE has followed since 1957 and NSE since 1992, and every trade placed during the hour is real and settles normally, not merely ceremonial.

Takeaway A one-hour session that moves almost no money still matters symbolically — it’s the one Sunday a year India’s markets formally open.

Business Today →

The outgoing Samvat 2082 ends with the Nifty down 7.4% and Sensex down 8.4%, despite Friday’s rally

Between the last Muhurat session on October 21, 2025 and November 4, 2026, the Nifty 50 fell about 7.4% and the Sensex about 8.4% over the Samvat 2082 year, according to a Univest review, with heavy foreign selling, a crude-oil shock and a rate-hike cycle cited as drags; FPI net selling over the period totaled roughly ₹3.03 trillion. Smaller stocks fared better — the Nifty Smallcap 100 gained about 4.5% and the Midcap 100 about 0.3% — even as Friday’s seven-session Dhanteras rally pushed the Nifty back above 25,000 just before the year closed.

Takeaway A single blazing week can't undo a full year of foreign selling — Samvat 2082 still closes as a loss for the benchmarks that matter to most investors.
