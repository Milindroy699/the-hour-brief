Before the news

There’s a Danish idea called hygge — a feeling of coziness and contentment found in small, unhurried moments, often ones with no agenda at all. It fits a Sunday with no markets open and no single dominant headline: just Tata Motors and Eternal quietly turning real profits, Nestlé India doing the same only to watch its own stock dip anyway, and Anthropic’s and Apple’s much bigger, much louder weeks still a day or more away.

AI & Tech

Anthropic's IPO roadshow could open the week of November 9 as it races toward a Thanksgiving listing near a $2 trillion valuation, even as Apple heads into Monday's first earnings call under CEO John Ternus, Samsung edges past Apple in a Q3 smartphone market that shrank 20% to its weakest in 14 years, Qualcomm schedules its own fiscal Q4 report for November 4, Sarvam AI is reported to be raising up to $250 million from Nvidia, Accel and HCLTech, and Cisco begins selling Super Micro's AI server racks as part of its Nvidia-based data-center push

Takeaway Today’s AI story is everyone lining up for a crowded first week of November — Anthropic’s roadshow, Apple’s and Qualcomm’s earnings, and a fresh India AI funding round all converge on the same handful of days, even as the hardware underneath it keeps consolidating around fewer winners.

Anthropic's IPO roadshow could begin the week of November 9, aiming to list before Thanksgiving

Anthropic may start formal marketing for its IPO as soon as the week of November 9, putting it on track to begin trading before Thanksgiving, Bloomberg has reported, according to iTiger's coverage of the filing. The report puts Anthropic's targeted valuation near $2 trillion, with Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan leading the underwriting syndicate; the timeline is described as subject to change, and at least one other report has pointed to marketing instead starting closer to mid-November.

Takeaway A roadshow date still described as tentative this close to it shows how much Anthropic's own IPO calendar depends on market conditions it can't control in the two weeks before Thanksgiving.

Apple heads into Monday's Q4 call as analysts weigh memory costs against a tariff-refund boost

Apple reports fiscal fourth-quarter 2026 results Monday, November 2, its first earnings call under CEO John Ternus and CFO Kevan Parekh, according to a preview from iThinkDiff. Apple had guided to 9–11% revenue growth and 47–48% gross margin, with one report citing close to a point of that margin coming from tariff refunds, while one aggregator's consensus points to roughly $113.38 billion in revenue; Apple has separately flagged rising memory costs as a pressure on the quarter ahead.

Takeaway Guiding margin higher on tariff refunds while warning about memory costs in the same breath means this call will be read as much on cost pressures still to come as on Monday's actual numbers.

Samsung edges out Apple as the global smartphone market posts its weakest Q3 in 14 years

Samsung held about 23% of global smartphone shipments in the third quarter against Apple's 22%, with the two combining for 45% of a market that shrank roughly 20% year-over-year to its weakest third quarter in 14 years, according to Counterpoint Research and Omdia data reported by Android Headlines. IDC's separate count put Apple just 500,000 units behind Samsung, with Apple's 22% share the first time it has crossed 20% in any third quarter.

Takeaway Two companies gaining share while the overall market shrinks by a fifth means the growth every other smartphone maker needed simply evaporated, rather than shifting to a rival.

Sarvam AI is reported to be raising up to $250 million from Nvidia, Accel and HCLTech

Indian AI startup Sarvam AI is in talks to raise as much as $250 million from Nvidia, Accel and HCLTech at a valuation near $1.5 billion, according to Outlook Business's reporting; other accounts put the round closer to $234 million already closed. The company is one of India's highest-profile bets on building foundation-model infrastructure domestically.

Takeaway Having Nvidia itself in the round, rather than just a financial investor, is the detail that signals this is as much about securing compute access as it is about raising capital.

Qualcomm schedules its fiscal Q4 report for November 4, after a narrow EPS miss last quarter

Qualcomm will report fourth-quarter and full fiscal-year 2026 results on Wednesday, November 4, after market close, the company said in its own release. The report follows a fiscal third quarter in which revenue of $9.95 billion beat estimates but adjusted EPS of $2.21 missed the $2.23 analysts expected, with CEO Cristiano Amon citing a "challenging memory and supply environment."

Takeaway Beating on revenue while missing on EPS because of memory and supply costs is the same pressure Apple is flagging ahead of its own report — a sign this is an industry-wide cost problem, not a company-specific one.

Cisco starts selling Super Micro's AI server racks as part of its Nvidia-based data-center push

Cisco began commercially selling Super Micro's liquid-cooled, rack-scale AI server systems this past month as part of its Nvidia-aligned "Secure AI Factory" offering, with Super Micro's stock rising about 9% on the news, according to CryptoBriefing's report.

Takeaway A networking company now reselling another vendor's server racks shows how much of the AI buildout is being assembled through partnerships rather than any one company controlling the whole stack.

Product & Business

Eternal's Q2 FY27 profit jumps 92% even as it falls short of analyst estimates, as Swiggy's narrower loss still sends its stock to a 52-week low, Tata Motors swings back to a ₹1,254-crore profit on 36% revenue growth, Nestlé India's profit rises 36% only for the stock to fall anyway, PhonePe and Google Pay's combined UPI lead keeps softening as Navi cracks the top four, and Flipkart's own food-delivery pilot still hasn't gone public past its October-end target

Takeaway Today’s business story is India’s biggest consumer names all reporting real improvement — profit up at Eternal, Tata Motors and Nestlé alike — with the market reacting to each one differently anyway, a reminder that a good quarter and a rising stock aren’t the same promise.

Eternal's Q2 FY27 profit jumps 92% but still falls short of analyst estimates

Eternal (formerly Zomato), the parent of Zomato and Blinkit, reported consolidated net profit of ₹125 crore for Q2 FY27, up 92.3% year-over-year from ₹65 crore, with revenue rising about 78% to roughly ₹24,138 crore, according to YourStory's report on the October 21 results. The profit figure still missed the ₹271 crore analysts polled by LSEG had expected.

Takeaway Profit growth this large still landing short of consensus shows how much more the market now expects from Eternal after several quarters of improving numbers.

YourStory →

Swiggy's loss narrows 16% but its stock still falls to a 52-week low

Swiggy reported a consolidated net loss of ₹922 crore for Q2 FY27, down 16% year-over-year, with revenue up 36%, according to Inc42's report on the October 22 results. Swiggy said Instamart's adjusted EBITDA losses peaked during the quarter, but the stock still fell as much as 14.68% days later to a fresh 52-week low, with analysts divided and several cutting price targets.

Takeaway A narrower loss triggering a 52-week low shows the market judging Swiggy against its own prior trajectory, not against Eternal's bigger headline numbers.

Inc42 →

Tata Motors swings back to a ₹1,254-crore profit on 36% revenue growth

Tata Motors reported consolidated Q2 FY27 revenue of about ₹25,338 crore, up roughly 36% year-over-year, and a net profit of about ₹1,254 crore versus a loss of ₹867 crore a year earlier, according to Kotak Neo's report on the October 23 results. Consolidated EBITDA rose about 31% to ₹2,770 crore on wholesale volumes up 42%, even as the stock closed down 4.41% the same day.

Takeaway A profit swing this clean failing to lift the stock on the same day shows the market had already priced in the recovery and was looking past it to what comes next.

Nestlé India's profit rises 36% even as the stock falls on the news

Nestlé India reported standalone profit after tax of ₹1,026.77 crore for Q2 FY27, up 36.3% year-over-year from ₹753.20 crore, with revenue up 20.3%, according to Free Press Journal's report on the October 15 results. The stock fell about 5.46% to ₹1,308 after the results despite the headline beat.

Takeaway A profit jump this size still sending the stock lower says investors found something in the details — likely margin or volume quality — that the headline number didn't capture.

Free Press Journal →

PhonePe and Google Pay's combined UPI lead keeps softening as Navi cracks the top four

PhonePe processed 10.91 billion UPI transactions in September 2026 for a 45.57% share, with Google Pay at 7.71 billion (32.23%) and Paytm at 1.94 billion (8.1%), while Navi's 1.14 billion transactions pushed it past CRED into fourth place, according to Entrackr's report on NPCI data. NPCI's rule capping any single app at 30% of UPI volume is due to take effect by the end of 2026, a threshold PhonePe remains well above.

Takeaway PhonePe sitting 15 points above a cap that's supposed to apply within months is the real story here — the rule and the market reality are still pointed in opposite directions.

Entrackr →

Flipkart's food-delivery pilot still hasn't gone public, past its own October-end target

Flipkart had targeted a public launch by the end of October for its in-app food-delivery service, "Eat-in by Minutes," but as November begins the company has not announced that launch, according to Curly Tales' tracking of the rollout. The pilot remains limited to more than 8,000 employees in Bengaluru, with no separate app yet and its roughly 10% restaurant commission still unconfirmed.

Takeaway Missing a self-set deadline this specific, without comment, suggests Flipkart found something in the pilot worth fixing before taking on Zomato and Swiggy for real.

India's markets enter a second shut day with Friday's gains as the only data on the board

The Sensex and Nifty 50 remain closed through the weekend after Friday's second straight gain — the Sensex up 308.49 points (0.42%) to 74,459.53 and the Nifty 50 up 116.35 points (0.51%) to 23,053.10 — with trading set to resume Monday, November 2, according to Kotak Neo's report on Friday's close. IT and healthcare led Friday's gains while midcap and smallcap indices fell even as the benchmarks rose.

Takeaway Two full days without a single new trade is a reminder that a "close" isn't a snapshot of sentiment right now — it's just the last moment the market was open to express one.

Monday's reopen lands the same day as Apple's first earnings call under CEO John Ternus

Indian markets reopen Monday, November 2 — the same day Apple reports fiscal fourth-quarter results in its first call under new CEO John Ternus, according to iThinkDiff's earnings preview. Qualcomm follows two days later with its own fiscal Q4 report on November 4, making the first week of November a busy one for markets on both sides of the world at once.

Takeaway Reopening into someone else's earnings day, rather than a quiet Monday, means India's market will take its first cue of the week from a report it had no hand in.

NSE's rare Sunday Muhurat-trading session is now just a week away, on November 8

The NSE has scheduled a special live trading session for Sunday, November 8, 2026, to mark Diwali Muhurat trading, with the main window running from roughly 2:20pm to 3:30pm alongside separate pre-open and post-close windows, according to Outlook Money's report on the exchange's circular. It is one of the only sessions all year that falls on a weekend.

Takeaway Scheduling a trading session specifically on a day markets are normally shut shows how much symbolic weight Muhurat trading carries over its actual volume.
