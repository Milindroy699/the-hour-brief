AI & Tech

Nvidia unveils a modular AI-factory design that cuts deployment from six months to 14 days as Greenpeace protesters confront Jensen Huang at his Berlin keynote over chips found in Russian weapons, even as Micron’s Taiwan union stages the island’s first-ever chip-industry strike over AI profit-sharing, Anthropic launches a proactive Claude Duet agent, AI-agent cloud startup Daytona hits a $1 billion valuation, Sam Altman reportedly lays out three possible endgames for the AI race to OpenAI staff, and Arista patches a third actively-exploited VeloCloud Orchestrator zero-day in four months

Takeaway Today’s AI stories split between ambition and strain — faster-to-deploy AI factories and a billion-dollar agent-cloud valuation show the build-out accelerating, while a Taiwan factory strike, a weapons-linked protest and a third exploited zero-day in four months show the costs piling up alongside it.

Nvidia says its new DSX POD design can cut AI-factory deployment to 14 days

At his GTC Berlin keynote on October 21, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang said the company’s next-generation Vera Rubin computing platform is now in full production, and unveiled DSX POD, a reference design for complete Vera Rubin AI factories built by partners inside modular containers with liquid cooling, networking and cabling pre-installed. Nvidia said the design lets partners cut on-site deployment from roughly six months to as little as 14 days once site utilities are ready, according to the company’s own blog.

Takeaway Turning a data center into a shipped, pre-wired container is Nvidia racing to remove the one bottleneck its own chips can’t fix — how fast a building gets ready for them.

Greenpeace protesters interrupt Huang’s Berlin keynote over chips found in Russian weapons

Greenpeace activists disrupted Jensen Huang’s October 21 GTC Berlin keynote, pressing him on reports that Nvidia’s Jetson Orin chips have been recovered from Russian weapons systems, according to Tom’s Hardware. Huang responded, “All important issues, but let me get through my keynote,” before resuming his presentation on Nvidia’s new AI infrastructure.

Takeaway A chipmaker can control who it sells to, but not where components end up once embedded in someone else’s hardware — which is exactly the gap the protest was aimed at.

Micron’s Taiwan union stages the island’s first-ever chip-industry strike over AI profit-sharing

Micron’s union at its Taoyuan, Taiwan plant staged a 24-hour warning strike on October 22 — Taiwan’s first-ever semiconductor-industry strike — after talks over sharing AI-driven memory profits broke down, Nikkei Asia reported. The roughly 2,300-member union is seeking a permanent program paying 15% of operating profit as quarterly bonuses; Micron has instead offered one-time payouts and, separately, a new program paying quarterly bonuses half in stock and half in cash, which the union had not accepted as of the strike. A sister union at Micron’s Taichung plant reached a separate agreement a day earlier.

Takeaway Record AI-driven memory profits are now creating their own labor fights — Samsung and SK Hynix workers already get profit-linked bonuses, and Micron’s Taiwan staff want the same deal.

AI-agent cloud startup Daytona hits a $1 billion valuation on a $60 million round

Daytona, which sells sandboxed cloud environments where AI agents can write code, browse the web and run software, raised a $60 million Series B led by FirstMark at a $1 billion valuation, the company said October 20, according to tech.eu. CEO Ivan Burazin said revenue run-rate grew 25-fold between January and September; reported customers include Anthropic, OpenAI, Nvidia, Cursor, Cognition and Meta, and total funding now stands at $91 million.

Takeaway When Anthropic, OpenAI and Nvidia are all paying one startup to host the sandboxes their own agents run in, that startup has found a toll booth everyone needs to pass through.

tech.eu →

Anthropic launches Claude Duet, a proactive Opus 5.5 agent, in waitlisted preview

Anthropic began rolling out Claude Duet on October 21 as a waitlisted research preview for US Pro and Max subscribers, powered by the newly released Claude Opus 5.5, according to MacRumors. Duet runs in its own cloud environment, sends a daily morning review of email and calendar, and keeps working on tasks after the app is closed — but asks before sending messages, making payments, signing documents or deleting anything; there is no extra charge during the preview, though background work counts against existing plan limits.

Takeaway An agent that works while you sleep only earns trust by asking permission before anything irreversible — which is exactly the line Anthropic built Duet around.

MacRumors →

Sam Altman reportedly tells OpenAI staff there are three possible endgames for the AI race

Altman told OpenAI staff in a recent internal briefing that AI’s trajectory could end one of three ways, Forbes reported October 22, citing original reporting by The Information: an international watchdog body modeled on the IAEA, a CERN-style merger of major labs that share models with partner countries, or one company that out-competes every rival so thoroughly it “melts” their GPUs. Altman reportedly told staff OpenAI has about a year to help decide which path the industry takes.

Takeaway Laying out a scenario where OpenAI simply out-computes every rival, alongside two cooperative alternatives, is Altman keeping the aggressive option on the table even while describing the diplomatic ones.

Arista patches a third actively-exploited VeloCloud Orchestrator zero-day in four months

Arista disclosed on October 22 that attackers are actively exploiting CVE-2026-114625, a critical (CVSS 9.9) SQL-injection flaw in its on-premises VeloCloud Orchestrator software that lets any authenticated user gain “superuser operator” privileges, SecurityWeek reported. It is the third VeloCloud Orchestrator zero-day Arista has had to patch since July, after separate flaws in July and September; customers who already patched for the earlier bugs still need to update again, since the new fixed versions are higher than September’s.

Takeaway Patching the same product against three different zero-days in four months suggests attackers found a soft target, not a one-off bug.

Product & Business

Infosys cuts its own full-year revenue guidance even as Q2 profit rises 6%, as Jio Platforms’ record IPO closes with a historic 22.19-times subscription and over 1 crore applications, HDFC Bank’s incoming CEO Anup Bagchi prepares to take over Monday, IndiGo moves to end its London Heathrow service as it retires its leased Boeing 787s, enterprise-AI startup AuxoAI raises $20.7 million, and Ather Energy extends its lead as Ola Electric’s September e-scooter market share slips to 6.5%

Takeaway Results season is turning more cautious than celebratory — Infosys grew profit but still cut its own outlook — even as India’s IPO market keeps setting records and fresh capital keeps flowing to AI-native startups like AuxoAI.

Jio Platforms’ IPO closes as India’s most-subscribed IPO ever, crossing 1 crore applications

Reliance’s Jio Platforms IPO closed its three-day bidding window on October 23 subscribed 22.19 times overall — with qualified institutional buyers at about 70.55 times, non-institutional investors at 14 times and retail at 2.72 times — after crossing over 1 crore applications during the bidding period, according to Dainik Jagran, citing cumulative NSE data. The ₹30,213 crore issue is India’s largest-ever IPO; allotment is expected October 26 ahead of an October 28 listing.

Takeaway Over 1 crore people applying for one IPO is Reliance turning India’s largest-ever listing into a retail moment, not just an institutional one.

Infosys profit rises 6% but the company cuts its own full-year revenue guidance

Infosys reported October 23 that Q2 FY27 consolidated net profit rose about 6% year-on-year to ₹7,823 crore on revenue up 11% to ₹49,244 crore, and declared a ₹26-per-share interim dividend, according to Analytics Insight. The company cut its FY27 constant-currency revenue growth guidance to 1-2% from 1.5-3%, citing a challenging demand environment, while keeping its 20-22% operating-margin guidance unchanged; AI revenue rose 29% sequentially to about 10.5% of total quarterly revenue.

Takeaway Growing profit and revenue while cutting your own outlook is Infosys saying this quarter was fine but the one ahead looks tougher.

HDFC Bank’s incoming CEO Anup Bagchi takes over Monday, succeeding Sashidhar Jagdishan

Anup Bagchi, currently MD and CEO of ICICI Prudential Life Insurance, is set to begin his three-year term as HDFC Bank’s MD and CEO on October 27, succeeding Sashidhar Jagdishan, who told the board in August he would not seek reappointment, according to Free Press Journal. The RBI has approved the appointment and Bagchi joined HDFC Bank’s board as an additional director on October 2; shareholder approval under the Companies Act is still pending.

Takeaway Hiring a sitting insurance-company CEO to run India’s largest private bank is HDFC Bank betting on outside leadership rather than promoting from within.

IndiGo moves to end its London Heathrow service as it retires its leased Boeing 787s

IndiGo will discontinue its Boeing 787-9 widebody operations from October 25 and wind up the damp lease under which it has flown six 787-9s from Norse Atlantic Airways, ending its London Heathrow route, according to Aerocorner. The airline cited geopolitical tension, airspace constraints, fuel costs and currency pressure, saying the operating environment had “changed considerably” since the route launched; Heathrow service stays paused until its own Airbus A350-900s arrive, starting in 2027.

Takeaway Walking away from a marquee long-haul route to a global financial hub is IndiGo prioritizing cost discipline over prestige, rather than absorbing a leased widebody it can’t make work economically.

Enterprise-AI startup AuxoAI raises a $20.7 million Series A led by NTTVC

AuxoAI, a San Francisco startup that embeds engineering teams inside large enterprises to build production AI systems, raised a $20.7 million Series A led by NTTVC on October 20, with Norwest’s George Still Jr. and ServiceNow executive Amit Zavery among the individual investors, Inc42 reported. The company says its Damia platform has supported more than 40 clients across manufacturing, healthcare, consumer goods and telecom, and the new funding will go toward hiring more engineers and expanding into new markets.

Takeaway Getting a ServiceNow executive to personally invest alongside a VC round is AuxoAI converting an enterprise customer relationship into a stake in its own success.

Ather Energy holds third place as Ola Electric’s e-scooter market share slips to 6.5%

India registered about 2.03 lakh electric two-wheelers in September, up 85% year-on-year, with TVS Motor leading at 53,989 units and Bajaj Auto second at 48,383, according to Vahan registration data cited by EVFY. Ather Energy came third with roughly 30,477 units, a 57% year-on-year gain, while Ola Electric fell to about 13,449 units and a 6.5% share — the only top-six maker to shrink year-on-year. Ather separately crossed 8 lakh cumulative retail sales on September 28.

Takeaway Being the only top-six e-scooter maker whose sales shrank while the overall market grew 85% is Ola Electric losing the race even as the race gets bigger.

Sensex and Nifty fall for a fourth straight session as crude surges and volatility jumps

The Sensex fell 125.56 points, or 0.17%, to close at 73,123.83 on Friday, October 23 — a fourth consecutive losing session — while the Nifty 50 dropped 87.65 points, or 0.39%, to 22,600.00, as Brent crude surged to around $114 a barrel and India’s VIX volatility index rose about 6%, ANI reported. All sectors closed in the red.

Takeaway Four straight losing sessions with crude still climbing is Dalal Street pricing in more Middle East risk, not treating it as a one-session scare.

Wall Street slides Friday to cap a volatile week, though the Dow still ends the week higher

The Dow Jones Industrial Average sank roughly 600 points, or about 1.2%, to close near 52,215 on Friday, October 23, while the S&P 500 slipped about 0.5% to roughly 7,624 and the Nasdaq Composite eased 0.2% to roughly 25,955, as rising crude prices weighed on sentiment, Yahoo Finance reported. For the week as a whole, the S&P 500 and Nasdaq both still finished lower, but the Dow ended higher, after Thursday’s sharp AI-spending-driven selloff.

Takeaway The Dow ending the week up while the S&P and Nasdaq ended it down is the same split seen all week — investors still treating AI-heavy tech as the riskier trade.

Zetwerk lists at a 5.5% premium on its stock-market debut

Zetwerk Manufacturing Business shares listed on the NSE at ₹159.25, a 5.46% premium over its ₹151 issue price, and at a similar level on the BSE, on October 23, Inc42 reported. The ₹5,012 crore IPO had closed subscribed about 3.10 times overall, with retail demand lagging at 0.73 times even as qualified institutional buyers subscribed 4.73 times their quota.

Takeaway A premium debut on retail’s weakest subscription number of the week shows institutional demand alone was enough to support Zetwerk’s stock.

Tonbo Imaging shares list at a 66% premium in a blowout debut

Tonbo Imaging India shares listed on the BSE at ₹1,162, a 66% premium over its ₹700 issue price, and at ₹1,160 (65.71%) on the NSE, on October 23, Business Standard reported. The defense-electronics maker’s ₹852 crore IPO — entirely an offer for sale — had been subscribed over 130 times; shares touched a high of ₹1,209.85 during the session before closing around 62% above issue price.

Takeaway A 130-times-subscribed book turning into a 66% listing pop shows how much demand India’s market still has for defense-linked manufacturing stories.

Jio Platforms’ IPO closes at a record 22.19 times subscription ahead of Wednesday’s listing

Reliance’s Jio Platforms IPO closed its bidding window on October 23 subscribed 22.19 times overall, per cumulative NSE data reported by Upstox — with qualified institutional buyers at roughly 70 times their quota, far ahead of retail’s 2.72 times — after an opening day that saw the issue fully subscribed within about 100 minutes. Allotment is expected October 26 ahead of listing on the NSE and BSE on October 28.

Takeaway QIBs bidding roughly 70 times their quota against retail’s 2.72 times is institutions pricing in a much bigger listing pop than ordinary investors are counting on.

Trading concept for today, via Zerodha Varsity: When an IPO is oversubscribed, retail, sHNI and bHNI shares are allotted by lottery — every valid application in a category has an equal chance regardless of how many lots it asks for — while QIB shares are allotted pro-rata to bid size, which is why institutions can lock in near-certain allotment on a hot IPO like Jio’s while retail applicants are left to chance.
