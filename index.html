Before the news

There’s a Japanese idea called wabi-sabi — finding beauty in imperfection and in things still becoming, rather than waiting for something flawless before it counts. It shows up throughout today’s edition: Swiggy’s quarterly loss narrowing but still running past ₹900 crore, AMD’s record quarter still carrying a $1.07 billion charge, and OpenAI quietly trimming its own fundraising target rather than holding out for the number it first wanted.

AI & Tech

OpenAI reportedly cuts its fundraising target to $20 billion at a $1.25 trillion valuation as Anthropic's amended IPO filing warns its models may be nearing a self-improvement threshold while disclosing nearly $900 billion in compute commitments, even as AMD's results reveal a $2.9 billion chip-startup deal and a $1.07 billion charge tied to OpenAI and Meta warrants, and the Xen Project credits Claude with finding two critical hypervisor flaws

Takeaway Today’s AI story is big numbers getting a reality check — OpenAI trimming its own ask, Anthropic flagging real risk in its own IPO filing, and AMD's record quarter still carrying a billion-dollar charge all show the boom now arriving with asterisks attached.

OpenAI reportedly cuts its funding round to $20 billion at a $1.25 trillion valuation

The Financial Times reported on October 30 that OpenAI has cut its in-progress funding round from roughly $30 billion to about $20 billion and lowered its target valuation from $1.4 trillion to $1.25 trillion, according to Investing.com's coverage of the FT story. Reporting ties the cut to a sell-off in AI-linked tech stocks and to some investors wanting to see Anthropic's IPO pricing before committing further capital; the initial close is expected to land below even the revised $20 billion target, with wealthy individual investors potentially topping it up later at the same valuation.

Takeaway Trimming the target by a third rather than insisting on the original number suggests OpenAI would rather close a smaller round on schedule than hold out and risk it stalling entirely.

Anthropic's amended S-1 warns Claude may be nearing a recursive self-improvement threshold

Anthropic's amended IPO filing, submitted November 2, discloses spending commitments that have surged to nearly $900 billion in total, according to Axios. The amendment also adds a warning, under the company's Responsible Scaling Policy, that its internal estimates indicate it may "imminently" approach a capability threshold tied to recursive self-improvement — a point where a model could meaningfully accelerate its own further development — though Anthropic says no current model has been designated as having crossed it.

Takeaway Putting a risk this specific into an IPO filing, rather than a blog post, is Anthropic telling investors directly that the thing it says it's racing toward is also the thing it's most worried about.

Anthropic signs its first publisher licensing deal, with the AP, for election data

Adweek reported that Anthropic struck a content and data licensing deal with the Associated Press just before the US midterm elections, citing two people familiar with the matter; Claude users who asked about election-night polling updates saw information attributed to the AP. Neither company commented on the record or disclosed financial terms, and Adweek notes it's Anthropic's first deal with a digital news publisher after it had previously treated scraping as fair use rather than pursuing the licensing deals OpenAI and Google have signed.

Takeaway One quiet licensing deal, landing days after Anthropic's own public S-1 filing, looks less like a policy reversal and more like a company managing its legal exposure right as it prepares to answer to public shareholders.

Xen credits Claude with finding two critical VM-escape flaws in its hypervisor

The Xen Project disclosed two critical vulnerabilities on November 3 under advisory XSA-523, tracked as CVE-2026-102703 and CVE-2026-102704, according to The Hacker News. The flaws sit in Xen's grant-copy code and could let a malicious guest virtual machine read and write arbitrary memory belonging to other domains, including Dom0; all Xen versions from 4.6 onward are affected, and patches exist for the current unstable branch and the 4.19.x, 4.18.x and 4.17.x release lines. Xen credited Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.6 with the discovery, with a human researcher validating the findings.

Takeaway A model finding flaws that let one virtual machine read another's memory is exactly the dual-use case the industry keeps warning about — the same capability that patches a hypervisor could just as easily have found the bug for an attacker first.

AMD's Q3 filing discloses $2.9 billion in terms for its Taalas inference-chip deal

AMD's quarterly SEC filing, disclosed around November 4, values its pending acquisition of Toronto-based AI inference-chip startup Taalas at approximately $2.9 billion in cash and stock, with up to $1.4 billion of that contingent on undisclosed conditions, according to Securities.io. AMD first announced the deal in August 2026 without disclosing financial terms; the acquisition is expected to close by the end of 2026 subject to regulatory approval, and AMD plans to fold Taalas's inference silicon into systems built around its own CPUs and Instinct GPUs.

Takeaway Waiting until a routine quarterly filing to reveal the price tag, rather than disclosing it at announcement, suggests AMD wanted the deal's strategic story told well before anyone could pick apart the math.

Securities.io →

AMD recorded a $1.07 billion charge tied to warrants it issued to OpenAI and Meta

AMD disclosed a $1.07 billion non-cash charge, or 66 cents a share, in the third quarter ended September 26, tied to the fair-value accounting of warrants it granted OpenAI and Meta as part of its chip-supply agreements with them, according to Reuters' report carried by Investing.com. The charge came even as AMD forecast faster-than-expected growth for 2027, and the warrants reflect a now-common structure where chipmakers hand equity-like stakes to their biggest AI customers in exchange for locking in multi-year compute commitments.

Takeaway A billion-dollar accounting charge tied to its own customers' warrants shows how deeply AMD has had to tie its balance sheet to OpenAI and Meta's futures just to win their business.

Product & Business

Reliance Industries posts a record ₹25,548 crore Q2 profit in Jio Platforms' first quarter reporting as a separately listed entity as Adani Enterprises' revenue jumps 75% even as its profit slides, while Swiggy's loss narrows and Eternal's profit surges, GIVA reportedly lines up a $100 million private-equity raise, and Democrats' sweep of the US midterms — built partly on a backlash against AI data centers — complicates Big Tech's buildout plans

Takeaway Today’s business story is strong headline numbers sitting next to real strain — record profit at Reliance and Adani Enterprises alongside Swiggy's still-sizeable loss and a political backlash against AI data centers that could slow the very buildout funding this sector's growth.

Reliance Industries posts a record Q2 FY27 profit of ₹25,548 crore, up 41%

Reliance Industries reported consolidated net profit of about ₹25,548 crore for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, up roughly 41% year-over-year from ₹18,165 crore, on gross revenue up 27.8% to a record ₹3,62,268 crore, according to Upstox. The results mark the first quarter in which Jio Platforms reported separately as its own listed entity following its recent spin-off.

Takeaway A record quarter arriving in the same period Reliance splits out Jio as its own listed company makes it harder to tell how much of the growth is the core business and how much is simply new reporting lines.

Jio Platforms' first standalone results show revenue up 11.3% to ₹47,485 crore

Jio Platforms reported its first results as a separately tracked entity for Q2 FY27: revenue rose 11.3% year-over-year to about ₹47,485 crore, while EBITDA grew 16.6% to roughly ₹21,879 crore, expanding its EBITDA margin by about 250 basis points to 54.1%, according to DSIJ.

Takeaway EBITDA growing faster than revenue and margins expanding by 250 basis points shows Jio squeezing more profit out of its existing subscriber base rather than relying on user growth alone.

Adani Enterprises' revenue jumps 75% to ₹37,216 crore even as profit slides

Adani Enterprises reported Q2 FY27 operational revenue up 75% year-over-year to ₹37,216 crore, while consolidated net profit came in at ₹1,492 crore, down from the year-ago quarter, according to Inkl's report of the results.

Takeaway Revenue surging 75% while profit falls points to margin pressure or heavy reinvestment somewhere in the group's sprawling incubating businesses, not a simple slowdown.

Swiggy's Q2 loss narrows 16% to ₹922 crore as revenue jumps 36%

Swiggy reported a consolidated net loss of ₹922 crore for Q2 FY27, narrowing about 16% from ₹1,092 crore a year earlier, on revenue up 36% year-over-year to roughly ₹7,571 crore, according to Upstox. Instamart's losses widened even as its revenue grew, and shares fell sharply on the results.

Takeaway A loss narrowing at the same time Instamart's losses widen shows food delivery is subsidizing quick commerce's growth, not that the overall business is actually getting healthier.

Upstox →

Eternal's Q2 profit surges 92% to ₹125 crore

Eternal, Zomato's parent company, reported Q2 FY27 net profit up 92% year-over-year to about ₹125 crore from ₹65 crore a year earlier, according to Business Today; shares slipped despite the beat as investors weighed the pace of margin recovery against continued quick-commerce investment.

Takeaway Shares falling on a 92% profit jump is the clearest sign yet that India's food-delivery investors are now pricing in a specific margin trajectory, not just rewarding any improvement.

GIVA reportedly in talks to raise about $100 million from private equity firms

Jewellery brand GIVA is in talks with private-equity investors including Multiples, Warburg Pincus and KKR to raise roughly $100 million (about ₹934 crore), which would value the company at over ₹6,000 crore, according to reporting cited by Venture Intelligence and Pulse.bot. The deal has not closed and terms could still change.

Takeaway Three large PE firms competing for a stake, rather than one investor quietly closing the round, suggests GIVA has leverage to negotiate terms most Indian D2C brands its size don't get.

Voters reject AI data centers in nearly every local ballot measure as Democrats sweep the midterms

Preliminary results from Tuesday's US midterm elections show roughly 40 of 41 local ballot measures across ten states went against data-center buildout, with over 80% of voters in Asheville rejecting a data center already under construction there, according to Forbes. CNBC linked the Democrats' broader midterm gains to the same backlash, noting Ohio's governor-elect had campaigned on a conditional moratorium on new data centers.

Takeaway A near-unanimous rejection across 41 separate local votes is a far stronger signal than any single poll that communities, not just activists, are turning against the AI buildout in their own backyards.

Sensex and Nifty rally for a fifth straight session as media, metal and FMCG lead

The Sensex closed Wednesday, November 4, at 77,301.40, up 383.55 points or 0.50%, while the Nifty 50 rose 149.75 points, or 0.63%, to 23,960.60, extending their winning streak to a fifth session, according to Business Standard. Media, metal, FMCG and realty stocks led the gains, while Nifty IT was the only sectoral loser, down over 1%.

Takeaway A rally broadening into media and metal rather than staying confined to banks suggests Indian investors are now chasing gains across sectors rather than sticking to one earnings-driven trade.

FIIs turn net buyers of ₹7,284 crore as DIIs book profits

Foreign institutional investors were net buyers of about ₹7,284 crore of Indian equities in the cash segment on November 4, while domestic institutions were net sellers of about ₹3,712 crore, according to 5paisa's FII/DII data.

Takeaway Foreign money returning in size just as domestic funds lock in profits is the same handoff pattern seen earlier this week — worth watching for whether it's becoming a trend rather than a one-off.

5paisa →

Wall Street logs its biggest post-midterm rally in 44 years as Democrats sweep Congress

The Dow Jones Industrial Average surged 1,569.40 points, or 3.03%, to 53,356.12 on November 4, its best single-day gain since April 2025, as the S&P 500 rose 1.58% to 7,688.62 and the Nasdaq Composite climbed 1.44% to 25,736.51, according to Fool.com. The rally followed Democrats winning control of Congress in the midterms; Morgan Stanley's Andrew Slimmon said markets simply "like gridlock," since it makes new regulation and sweeping policy changes less likely. Oil fell sharply as energy stocks took the hardest hit.

Takeaway A 3% single-day surge driven purely by the expectation of legislative gridlock, not any new economic data, shows how much of this market's current mood is about certainty rather than growth.

Fool.com →

Nvidia's stock closes nearly 16% below its October high

Nvidia shares closed at $204.64 on November 2, nearly 16% below their October record, as AI-valuation jitters tied to OpenAI's fundraising cut and Anthropic's shifting valuation weighed on chip stocks broadly, according to KuCoin's markets desk. Nvidia carries direct exposure to both companies through multibillion-dollar supply partnerships, and its own next earnings report isn't due until November 17.

Takeaway Nvidia's stock sliding on its customers' fundraising troubles, weeks before it even reports its own numbers, shows how tightly its valuation is now tied to OpenAI and Anthropic's financial health rather than its own results.

Hindsight bias, via smallcase: Windmill Capital's explainer describes investors' tendency to look back at outcomes like today's Wall Street surge and convince themselves it was predictable all along, when no forecaster actually called a precise 3% gridlock rally the day before it happened. The discipline is resisting that false certainty — writing down what you actually expected before an event, not after, so you don't quietly overwrite the memory of how uncertain it really was.
