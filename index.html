There’s a Korean idea called nunchi — the subtle art of reading a room: sensing the mood, timing and unspoken signals around you well enough to act well without being told. AI & Tech

Nvidia reportedly tells cloud and data-center clients to cap their China business at 49% as AMD posts a record Q3 with Data Center revenue nearly doubling, even as Arista Networks logs its fastest growth since its IPO on AI-networking demand, Palantir posts its first-ever $2 billion quarter with commercial revenue overtaking government contracts, and a Claude-based tool cuts Enveda's drug-manufacturing document review time by 89%

Takeaway Today’s AI story is capital discipline hiding behind blowout numbers — AMD, Arista and Palantir's results and Nvidia's quiet China guardrails show the industry managing exposure as carefully as it chases growth, not just racing to scale.

Nvidia reportedly tells cloud and data-center clients to cap their China business at 49%

The Financial Times reported on November 3, citing five people familiar with the matter, that Nvidia is privately instructing cloud and data-center partners to keep at least 51% of their business tied to non-Chinese companies going forward, according to Tom's Hardware. The policy reportedly applies globally to new and future contracts and was communicated through Nvidia's sales force rather than in any official documentation; Nvidia didn't confirm or deny the report when asked by AFP, instead pointing to longstanding US policy encouraging clouds to build on American infrastructure.

Takeaway A policy delivered verbally through sales channels rather than in writing suggests Nvidia wants the restriction enforced without creating a paper trail that invites regulatory or diplomatic scrutiny.

AMD posts a record Q3 2026 as Data Center revenue nearly doubles

AMD reported fiscal third-quarter 2026 results on November 3: revenue of $13.4 billion, up 45% year-over-year, with Data Center segment revenue growing 97%, according to the company's SEC Form 8-K filing. Non-GAAP diluted EPS came in at $2.09 on a 56% gross margin; CEO Lisa Su said demand for its Instinct and EPYC products kept strengthening heading into the fourth quarter.

Takeaway Data Center revenue nearly doubling in a single quarter shows AMD closing the gap with Nvidia in the AI-chip race faster than the market had priced in.

Arista Networks posts its highest revenue growth since its IPO on AI-networking demand

Arista Networks reported Q3 2026 revenue of about $3.66 billion on November 3, well above its own roughly $3.3 billion guidance and up 58.5% year-over-year — the company's highest year-over-year growth since going public, according to its SEC Form 8-K earnings release. The quarter included record profitability, and Arista disclosed new AI-networking rack-scale architectures developed with AMD, Arm, Broadcom, Meta and Microsoft.

Takeaway A networking-equipment maker growing faster than at any point since its IPO shows how much AI-buildout spending is flowing into the plumbing around the chips, not just the chips themselves.

Arista SEC filing →

Palantir posts its first-ever $2 billion quarter as US commercial revenue overtakes government revenue

Palantir's Q3 2026 results, filed via SEC Form 8-K on November 2, showed revenue of $2.313 billion, up 96% year-over-year and above the company's own guidance of $2.16–2.164 billion. GAAP net income was $1.128 billion, a 49% margin; US commercial revenue reached $984 million, surpassing US government revenue for the first time in company history.

Takeaway A commercial segment overtaking government revenue for the first time marks a real shift for a company long defined by defense and intelligence contracts, not just a strong quarter.

A Claude-based tool cuts Enveda's drug-manufacturing document review time by 89%

Biotech Enveda and Anthropic jointly reported around October 27-29 that a Claude-powered "CMC Document Processor" reviewed 1,061 pages of manufacturing batch records in about 2.8 hours, versus roughly 25.9 hours previously including human adjudication, according to HPCwire/AIwire. The tool reportedly flagged all 11 issues human reviewers had caught plus 7 more, though Enveda's quality team still performed final sign-off on every record, and testing found it misread at least one struck-through handwritten correction.

Takeaway An 89% time cut is real, but keeping a human quality team as the final checkpoint shows even Anthropic's own customers aren't yet trusting the model to sign off alone.

Product & Business

Larsen & Toubro's Q2 profit jumps 33% to a record order book as Noel Tata prepares to step down from six Tata Group boards on turning 70 amid his unresolved dispute with N. Chandrasekaran, even as Paytm's parent swings to a ₹225 crore profit and Hero MotoCorp's shares fall despite a reported 41% profit jump, while Tech Mahindra pairs a 33% profit rise with a 1:1 bonus issue and SBI Cards and SBI Life round out a busy Q2 earnings week

Takeaway Today’s business story is India’s earnings season cutting every way at once — record profit and orders at L&T sit beside Hero MotoCorp's profit that still spooked investors and Noel Tata quietly stepping back from six boards without giving up his fight.

Paytm parent One97 Communications swings to a ₹225 crore Q2 FY27 profit

One97 Communications, Paytm's parent, reported consolidated net profit of about ₹225 crore for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, up sharply from roughly ₹21 crore a year earlier, on revenue of about ₹2,648 crore versus ₹2,061 crore, according to Free Press Journal, reported October 26. Shares traded lower the next morning despite the beat.

Takeaway Paytm's profit scaling far faster than its revenue is a sign the cost-cutting since its RBI troubles is now showing up directly in the bottom line.

Free Press Journal →

Larsen & Toubro's Q2 FY27 profit rises 33% on a record order book

Larsen & Toubro's board approved Q2 FY27 results on October 27: consolidated net profit rose 33% year-over-year to about ₹5,202 crore on revenue up roughly 16% to ₹78,750 crore, according to Free Press Journal and Investywise. Order inflow for the quarter came in around ₹1.48 lakh crore, up 28%, lifting the order book to about ₹8.54 lakh crore; shares rose about 4% the next session.

Takeaway A record order book alongside a 33% profit jump gives L&T a multi-quarter revenue pipeline that's harder to fake than one good quarter.

Free Press Journal →

Hero MotoCorp's shares fall despite a reported 41% Q2 profit jump

Hero MotoCorp's board approved Q2 FY27 results on October 28: consolidated profit after tax rose 41% year-over-year to about ₹1,861 crore on revenue up 23% to ₹14,971 crore, according to Free Press Journal — a figure that included a one-time ₹397 crore gain tied to an associate company's capital raise. Standalone net profit, which strips out that one-time item, rose a far more modest 4.5% to about ₹1,456 crore, and shares fell roughly 3% as investors focused on that weaker underlying number, according to Upstox.

Takeaway A 41% headline profit jump that shares still sold off on shows investors increasingly look past one-time gains to the standalone number underneath.

Tech Mahindra's board approves a 1:1 bonus issue as Q2 profit rises 33%

Tech Mahindra reported consolidated net profit of about ₹1,586 crore for Q2 FY27, up 32.7% year-over-year, on revenue up 15.6% to roughly ₹16,180 crore, according to Kotak Neo. Its board also approved a 1:1 bonus share issue alongside an interim dividend of ₹15 per share.

Takeaway Pairing a profit beat with a bonus issue is Tech Mahindra signaling confidence that its turnaround under newer leadership has staying power, not just a one-quarter bounce.

Kotak Neo →

SBI Cards' Q2 profit jumps 75% on lower costs

SBI Cards and Payment Services reported profit after tax of about ₹778 crore for Q2 FY27, up 75% year-over-year, driven largely by lower costs rather than revenue growth, according to Univest.

Takeaway A 75% profit jump built on cost control suggests India's largest standalone card issuer is banking efficiency gains before betting on a consumer-credit rebound.

Univest →

SBI Life's Q2 profit rises a steadier 11%

SBI Life Insurance reported net profit of about ₹549 crore for Q2 FY27, up 11% year-over-year, according to Univest.

Takeaway A modest double-digit gain, far more muted than its banking-group peers' results this earnings season, shows India's insurance arm growing in line with premiums rather than any one-off boost.

Noel Tata will step down from six Tata Group boards on November 12, turning 70

Noel Tata is set to leave the boards of Trent, Voltas, Tata Investment Corporation, Tata International, Tata Steel and Titan Company on November 12, 2026, when he turns 70, under the group's retirement policy for non-executive directors, according to People Matters and National Herald; he's expected to keep his Tata Sons board seat as a Tata Trusts nominee. The exits land amid his continuing dispute with Tata Sons over N. Chandrasekaran's reappointment — an October 26 letter from Noel Tata reiterated his objections and asked for the board meeting's minutes and voting records, according to the same reports.

Takeaway Stepping back from six operating boards while keeping his Tata Sons seat and his objections to Chandrasekaran's reappointment shows Noel Tata is picking which fight to keep fighting, not withdrawing from the group's governance entirely.

Sensex and Nifty rally for a fourth straight session as banking and financial stocks lead

The Sensex closed Tuesday, November 3, at 76,917.85, up 1,225.07 points or 1.62%, while the Nifty 50 rose 370.05 points, or 1.58%, to 23,810.85, their fourth straight gain, according to Trading Economics and Trade Brains. Financial services and private banks led, helped by strong Q2 earnings, easing crude prices and renewed foreign buying, while FMCG and realty also outperformed.

Takeaway A rally now led by banks rather than oil-sensitive sectors suggests Monday's crude-driven bounce is broadening into a more durable, earnings-backed move.

Trade Brains →

FIIs turn net buyers as DIIs book profits into the rally

Foreign institutional investors bought a net ₹6,837 crore of Indian equities on November 3 while domestic institutions sold a net ₹3,679 crore, for a combined net inflow of about ₹3,158 crore, according to NiftyTrader's FII/DII data.

Takeaway Foreign money returning just as domestic funds take profits is a classic handoff — and worth watching for whether it holds past one session.

NiftyTrader →

Infosys, SBI and Adani Enterprises buck the rally as session laggards

Even as the broader market surged, Infosys, State Bank of India, ONGC, Power Grid Corporation and Adani Enterprises were among the session's notable laggards on November 3, according to Trade Brains.

Takeaway A rally this broad still leaving five large names behind is a reminder that index-level gains don't mean every stock is participating.

Trade Brains →

Wall Street's S&P 500 snaps a three-day losing streak as chip stocks rebound

The S&P 500 rose roughly 0.36% on Tuesday, November 3, snapping a three-day losing streak, while the Nasdaq Composite gained about 0.83% as semiconductor stocks rebounded; AMD and Arista Networks both topped their own sales forecasts after the close. Apple shares fell and weighed on the Dow, which slipped about 0.38%, according to Yahoo Finance and Fool.com.

Takeaway A chip-led bounce pulling the S&P 500 back into the green even as Apple dragged the Dow shows how unevenly "the market" is actually moving right now.

Fool.com →

Apple's Q4 beat gets its first full Indian trading-session test today

Apple's fiscal Q4 2026 results — a 12% revenue beat under new CEO John Ternus, reported Monday after Indian markets had closed — got priced into Wall Street on Tuesday even as Apple's own shares fell and dragged on the Dow, according to Fool.com. Indian IT and supply-chain-linked stocks get their first full session to react to that mixed reaction today.

Takeaway A headline earnings beat that still drags on its own stock is exactly the kind of mixed signal Indian markets now have to price in with a day's lag.

The disposition effect, via smallcase: Windmill Capital's explainer describes investors' tendency to sell winning stocks too early to lock in gains, while holding on to losers far longer than they should out of fear of "admitting" the call was wrong. With Infosys, SBI and Adani Enterprises lagging today's broad rally, the discipline is resisting the urge to hang on to them purely to avoid that admission, while letting the banks that are actually working get the benefit of the doubt.
