AI & Tech

Anthropic’s own books reach the public only through a leaked draft S-1 showing an $80.7 billion first-half loss and $518 billion of locked-in compute commitments, even as the company quietly pitches Congress a narrowly-scoped AI safety bill, OpenAI acquires biotech startup Orchestra Bio to push AI agents into drug discovery while separately chasing a $1.4 trillion valuation, and meeting-notes startup Otter.ai raises $320 million to fund its own enterprise push

Takeaway AI’s spending and its guardrails are both still being negotiated out of public view — Anthropic’s real finances surfacing only through a leaked filing, its own safety bill written to a narrow catastrophe threshold, and OpenAI racing to lock in fresh capital before any of its numbers become public record.

Anthropic’s draft IPO filing shows an $80.7 billion loss for the first half of 2026

Anthropic’s confidential draft S-1, reviewed by the Wall Street Journal, shows a net loss of about $80.7 billion for the six months ended June 30, 2026, against revenue of $16.3 billion — up from a $13 billion loss on $1.1 billion of revenue in the same period a year earlier, Reuters reported. Spending on computing capacity and infrastructure climbed to roughly $9 billion in the half, from $2.3 billion a year earlier, and about 20% of the period’s revenue came from just two customers; the company is said to be targeting a November IPO.

Takeaway Revenue growing nearly 15-fold while the loss balloons just as fast is Anthropic betting the spending curve bends before the cash does.

Anthropic’s draft filing discloses $518 billion in locked-in compute commitments

The same draft prospectus, reviewed by Reuters and the Financial Times, discloses at least $518 billion in infrastructure commitments over roughly the next decade, with about 80% of that payable regardless of whether Anthropic actually uses the capacity. The filing reportedly puts Anthropic’s obligations at $111.1 billion to Google, $110 billion to Amazon and $31.4 billion to Microsoft under long-term cloud deals running into the early 2030s.

Takeaway Locking four-fifths of a $518 billion bill in as non-cancellable is Anthropic betting demand keeps climbing faster than it could ever renegotiate out of these contracts.

Anthropic pitches Congress a draft AI safety bill with a 50-death threshold

Anthropic has privately circulated a draft federal AI safety bill to lawmakers, Politico first reported, that would let the Commerce Department seek a court order blocking a model only over an imminent risk of more than $1 billion in damage or more than 50 deaths or serious injuries from a bioweapon, loss of control, or a cyberattack. Independent evaluators registered with Commerce — which labs could pay — would review the companies and their models; critics note the thresholds mean a model causing $999 million in damage or 49 deaths would fall just short of triggering a block.

Takeaway Writing the trigger at 50 deaths rather than one is Anthropic proposing a bill built to stop a catastrophe, not an everyday harm.

OpenAI acquires biotech startup Orchestra Bio to push AI agents into drug discovery

OpenAI announced on October 20 that it has acquired Orchestra Bio, a startup whose AI agents help biopharma teams track research projects and flag delays — for instance identifying which clinical trials a manufacturing shortfall would affect. Orchestra’s team and technology will fold into Rosalind, OpenAI’s life-sciences suite that already works with Novo Nordisk and Moderna; financial terms were not disclosed.

Takeaway Buying a tool that flags delays, rather than one that invents drugs, is OpenAI betting the bottleneck in a 10-to-15-year drug-approval timeline is logistics, not discovery.

OpenAI reportedly seeks $30 billion at a $1.4 trillion valuation

OpenAI is pushing to lock in investor commitments for a new round of at least $30 billion at a valuation around $1.4 trillion, Reuters reported, up from the roughly $1.2 trillion figure discussed in earlier reporting. The round is reportedly meant to function as bridge financing in place of a near-term IPO, after CEO Sam Altman ruled out going public in 2026.

Takeaway Raising private capital at a higher price than its last round, instead of filing to go public, lets OpenAI's valuation keep climbing without having to defend it to public-market analysts yet.

Pulse2 (Reuters) →

Otter.ai raises $320 million from General Catalyst’s Customer Value Fund

Meeting-notetaking startup Otter.ai said on October 20 it has closed $320 million in growth financing from General Catalyst’s Customer Value Fund, a non-dilutive vehicle its CEO says is repayable with interest rather than structured as equity. Otter says it now has more than 40 million users, up from 25 million a year ago, and that teams at over 86% of the Fortune 500 use it; the new money is earmarked for enterprise sales, international expansion and product work including a live, in-meeting Q&A feature.

Takeaway Taking repayable growth capital instead of diluting further is Otter betting its own revenue, not a fresh equity round, can carry its enterprise push.

Product & Business

Reliance’s Jio Platforms opens India’s largest-ever IPO to the public today as the India-New Zealand free trade deal takes effect, TCS moves to fold Best Buy’s India operation into an AI-native hub, Netflix beats on margin and raises its full-year outlook, India’s startup funding climbs even as deal count keeps shrinking, and Meta reports Q3 earnings today with its AI spending under the microscope

Takeaway Capital is still flowing toward scale today — Jio’s record IPO, TCS’s AI-hub bet, a freshly inked trade deal — but toward fewer, larger bets: startup deal count keeps falling even as total funding rises, and Meta’s results tonight will test whether that same concentration is paying off in AI spending.

Jio Platforms’ record ₹30,213 crore IPO opens for public subscription

Reliance’s Jio Platforms opened its IPO to public bidding today, October 21, at a price band of ₹1,065-1,119 a share, seeking to raise about ₹30,213 crore in what would be India’s largest-ever public issue; the offer is entirely fresh shares, with ₹27,500 crore of the proceeds earmarked to prepay borrowings at subsidiary Reliance Jio Infocomm. Anchor bidding on October 19 drew more than $25 billion in demand, over 30 times the anchor quota; the issue closes October 23, with listing targeted around October 28.

Takeaway Earmarking most of the fresh capital to pay down Jio Infocomm’s own debt is Reliance using this IPO to delever the business it’s selling a piece of, not just to cash out.

India-New Zealand free trade agreement takes effect

The India-New Zealand free trade agreement came into force on October 20, Dussehra, after both countries ratified it on September 21, giving 100% of Indian exports duty-free access to New Zealand from day one; 57% of New Zealand’s exports to India, including wool and coal, become tariff-free immediately, with a second round of cuts following on January 1, 2027. New Zealand has also committed to facilitating $20 billion of investment into India over the next 15 years, and wine tariffs will fall from 150% to 25-50% over a decade.

Takeaway New Zealand getting immediate access on only 57% of its exports while India’s goods clear duty-free on day one shows which side pushed harder to close this deal.

TCS moves to turn Best Buy’s India operation into an AI-native capability center

Tata Consultancy Services signed an agreement on October 1 to take over Best Buy’s India global capability center, acquiring 100% of BBY Services India LLP for a nominal ₹1 lakh, with completion expected within three to four weeks of signing. TCS plans to use its Global Value & Innovation Centres framework to redesign the unit’s workflows into what it calls an AI-native capability center; the companies’ release describes no job cuts, with staff moving into TCS’s global organization.

Takeaway Paying a token ₹1 lakh for the entity while inheriting its whole workforce means the real price of this deal is the multi-year AI transformation TCS is signing up to deliver, not the cash changing hands.

Netflix posts 12% revenue growth and a wider margin, raises its full-year outlook

Netflix’s Q3 2026 revenue rose 12% year-on-year (11% on a currency-neutral basis) to $12.9 billion, with operating margin expanding to 34.6% from 28.2% a year earlier, the company’s shareholder letter shows. Netflix now expects full-year 2026 revenue of $51.1 billion, up 13%, and a 31.5% operating margin; the letter points to its India slate and a new content-licensing partnership with France’s TF1 as part of the quarter’s strength.

Takeaway A margin that jumped more than six points in a single year is the clearer signal in these numbers than the revenue growth — Netflix is extracting more profit from the same subscriber base, not just adding more of it.

India’s startup funding climbs to $10.9 billion even as deal count keeps falling

Indian startups raised about $10.9 billion across 1,253 rounds between January and mid-October 2026, Tracxn data shows — up 6.8% year-on-year even as the number of rounds fell sharply from 1,982, pushing the average investment per round from roughly $5.1 million to $8.6 million; the top 10 startups by funds raised accounted for nearly 36% of all capital. Chip-services firm Tessolve Semiconductor’s $75 million round from first-time India investor Vector Line Capital, aimed at funding acquisitions, was last week’s largest disclosed check.

Takeaway More money chasing barely two-thirds as many rounds is India’s investors concentrating bets on fewer companies, not actually investing more broadly across the ecosystem.

Meta reports Q3 earnings today with its AI spending under scrutiny

Meta reports third-quarter 2026 results after the market close today, October 21, with analysts’ consensus around $63.4 billion in revenue; the bigger focus is expected to be capital spending, after Meta guided to $130-145 billion of capex for the full year. Evercore ISI’s Mark Mahaney has said he expects both capex and expense guidance to come in above Wall Street’s estimates, with a knock-on hit to 2027 profit forecasts.

Takeaway When analysts are watching the capex line more closely than the revenue line, the market has already stopped asking whether Meta’s AI bet is big — it’s asking whether it ever pays off.

Procter & Gamble to report fiscal Q1 earnings Thursday

Procter & Gamble confirmed it will webcast a discussion of its fiscal first-quarter 2027 results on Thursday, October 22, at 8:30 a.m. ET; analysts’ consensus points to earnings of about $1.89 a share. The report is one of the first reads on US consumer spending heading into the holiday season, after P&G’s prior quarter was released in July.

Takeaway A consumer-staples giant’s results landing the same week as Meta’s AI-spending test gives markets two very different reads on demand within 48 hours of each other.

Indian markets reopen today after sitting out Tuesday’s Dussehra holiday

Trading in equities, derivatives and other segments was shut across the NSE and BSE on Tuesday, October 20, for Dussehra, leaving Monday’s close — Sensex down 876.37 points (1.17%) to 73,852.90, Nifty down 257.20 points (1.11%) to 22,888.95 — as the most recent session heading into today’s reopen. The day’s main event is the public opening of Jio Platforms’ ₹30,213 crore IPO, India’s largest-ever, after an anchor book that drew more than $25 billion in demand on Monday.

Takeaway A holiday landing between Jio’s anchor bidding and its public opening gives Monday’s anchor frenzy an extra day to cool before retail investors get their turn.

Wall Street’s Dow jumps over 500 points as earnings optimism steadies the market

The Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 529.85 points, or 1.02%, to close at 52,495.88 on Tuesday, with 3M the top gainer at up 5.78%, while the S&P 500 added 0.28% to 7,712.57 and the Nasdaq Composite slipped 0.02% to 26,424.82 as megacap tech lagged, according to CNBC’s market coverage.

Takeaway Industrials carrying the Dow higher while megacap tech barely moves is a rotation, not a broad rally — the AI trade took Tuesday off.

Jio Platforms’ grey market premium points to a roughly 20% listing gain

Jio Platforms’ shares were commanding a grey market premium of about ₹225 as of October 16 — implying a listing price near ₹1,344 against the ₹1,119 upper price band, or about 20% above it — up from ₹148 on October 4, according to IPO trackers. The premium trades in an unregulated, informal market and has likely moved since, so it’s read as a sentiment signal rather than a forecast of where the stock actually lists.

Takeaway A premium that climbed by more than half in under two weeks says demand had been building well before today’s public opening, not starting with it.

Zetwerk’s IPO allotment is finalized today ahead of Friday’s listing

Zetwerk’s ₹5,012 crore manufacturing-platform IPO, which closed Monday subscribed 3.10 times overall — institutional buyers at 4.71 times but retail investors at just 0.77 times — finalizes share allotment today, October 21, ahead of a tentative listing on October 23.

Takeaway Retail investors failing to even fill their own quota, in an IPO window otherwise dominated by Jio’s frenzy, is a reminder that oversubscription headlines don’t apply evenly across every issue open at once.

Trading concept for today, via Chittorgarh: Grey market premium (GMP) is the unofficial premium at which IPO shares change hands before they list — trades that happen entirely outside SEBI’s purview, in cash, through informal broker networks, with no circuit filters to limit swings. A ₹150 issue trading at an ₹80 GMP implies a listing near ₹230, but because the market is thin and unregulated, GMP can move sharply on nothing more than sentiment and shouldn’t be the sole basis for an investment decision — exactly the gap today’s two overlapping IPOs, Jio’s richly priced and Zetwerk’s barely moving, are both testing.
