Adobe says Black Friday online spending hit a fresh US record above $9.7 billion by Friday evening, as Meta and OpenAI each move to buy up AI coding-agent startups on the same day and TSMC approves a $1 billion investment split between Anthropic and OpenAI, even as New York’s RAISE Act opens an AI registration portal pulling both labs into state oversight, CISA sets a patch deadline for four newly exploited flaws, and hearing-aid retailer Amplifon discloses a breach exposing Social Security numbers for a subset of 1.3 million affected customers

Black Friday online spending sets a fresh US record, Adobe says

Adobe Analytics reported Black Friday 2026 (November 27) US online spending reached $9.7 billion by 6:30pm ET, up 12.8% year-over-year, with the full day expected to land between $13 billion and $13.4 billion — beating Adobe's own pre-season forecast of $12.9 billion. Thanksgiving Day spending alone hit $7.1 billion, up 10.4% and above forecast; Adobe expects Cyber Monday, November 30, to remain the season's single biggest shopping day at $15.1 billion.

Takeaway Consumer spending is proving resilient even as AI-driven stock-market jitters have rattled tech investors elsewhere this fall.

TSMC approves up to $1 billion split between Anthropic and OpenAI

At a November 10 board meeting, TSMC approved up to $1 billion in total investment — $500 million to buy Anthropic shares via a private placement tied to Anthropic's IPO price, and $500 million into OpenAI preferred stock. The same board session authorized $40.93 billion in capital appropriations and a separate $500 million vehicle for strategic semiconductor investments.

Takeaway The world's dominant chipmaker is now taking direct equity stakes in the two AI labs most dependent on its chips, tying chip supply and AI-model economics more tightly together.

Meta and OpenAI both move to buy AI coding-agent startups, same day

Meta acquired Conductor, whose app lets engineers run multiple AI coding agents at once, on November 4 (terms undisclosed); its hosted "Conductor Cloud" was wound down November 20, though the local Mac app keeps receiving updates. The same day, OpenAI announced it will acquire Pierre Computer Company, maker of the Trees and Diffs developer-tools libraries already used in Codex, plus a Git-based "Code Storage" layer it plans to fold into Codex; OpenAI says Pierre's open-source projects will stay open.

Takeaway Meta and OpenAI moving on the same day to buy coding-agent infrastructure shows that owning the AI coding stack has become a genuine platform fight.

CISA sets a November 12 deadline for four newly exploited flaws

CISA added four actively exploited vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on November 9 — flaws in GPUStack, FreeSWITCH, OpenSSH and IBM's Aspera Faspex — giving federal civilian agencies until November 12 to patch them.

Takeaway A fresh four-flaw batch every few days is the normal cadence now — the real story is which products keep showing up on it.

eSecurity Planet →

New York's RAISE Act opens its AI registration portal

New York's DIGIT office, within the state's Department of Financial Services, opened the RAISE Act registration portal on November 10. Large frontier AI developers generating over $500 million in annual revenue — including OpenAI and Anthropic — must register by January 1, 2027, when the law takes effect.

Takeaway New York becomes the second state after California to impose binding frontier-AI safety obligations, adding another seam to AI labs' fragmenting US regulatory map.

TechCrunch →

Amplifon Americas breach, disclosed this month, affects 1.3 million people

Amplifon Americas disclosed that hackers broke into its internal systems and copied files from certain systems between January 18 and February 8, 2026, filing notices with Vermont's and Texas's Attorneys General in early November. A Texas filing puts the total affected at roughly 1.3 million people nationwide; the company says exposed data includes names, contact details, dates of birth and government ID numbers, with Social Security numbers and financial information taken for a smaller subset of those affected.

Takeaway Monthslong gaps between a breach and its public disclosure remain the norm, even for incidents eventually affecting over a million people.

Tata Sons' long-delayed board meeting takes up Noel Tata's restructuring plan

Tata Sons' board met on November 26 to take up, at Noel Tata's request, an RBI-linked reorganisation plan addressing the regulator's mandatory-listing requirement for upper-layer NBFCs, alongside a ₹25,000 crore funding request from the Shapoorji Pallonji Group — an agenda reportedly postponed from an earlier scheduled date. Tata Trusts, which holds roughly 66% of Tata Sons, has opposed a listing, while Shapoorji Pallonji wants partial stake monetisation.

Takeaway A boardroom standoff between a regulator's listing mandate and a major shareholder's cash need remains unresolved even after the meeting everyone was waiting for finally happened.

Indian startups raise $195 million this week, led by Ripplr's $45 million Series C

Per Inc42's weekly tracker, 24 Indian startups cumulatively raised about $195.2 million in the week of November 24-28, up 14% from $171.4 million the prior week. Ripplr's $45 million Series C was the week's largest round, followed by a $35 million investment in Square Yards from Smilegate VC and a $25 million follow-on round for Neo Group led by Crystal Investment Advisors.

Takeaway A second straight week of rising deal flow is a tentative sign India's funding winter may be thawing, even if full-year totals still trail the 2024-25 peak.

PB Fintech co-founder Alok Bansal to step down from his executive role

PB Fintech, the parent of Policybazaar, saw its shares fall as much as 6.26% intraday to ₹970.40 on November 19 after co-founder Alok Bansal announced he will give up his executive role effective March 31, 2027, subject to shareholder approval. No successor has been named; CEO Yashish Dahiya credited Bansal's role in building the company and its 2021 listing, saying Bansal had first raised wanting to step back five years earlier.

Takeaway A founding-team exit at one of India's marquee new-age listed companies is a reminder that even strong IPO stocks aren't immune to leadership-transition risk.

Palo Alto Networks pays $4.5 billion for AI startup Applied Compute

Palo Alto Networks announced on November 18 an all-stock deal to acquire AI startup Applied Compute for $4.5 billion, expected to close in its fiscal Q3 (by April 30, 2027) pending regulatory approval. Applied Compute, founded in May 2025 with about 50 employees and a roughly $100 million annualized revenue run-rate, builds post-training and inference infrastructure for open-weight AI models; its CEO, Yash Patil, becomes Palo Alto's co-chief AI officer. The announcement came alongside Palo Alto's fiscal Q1 earnings — revenue of $3.44 billion, up 39% year-over-year — and raised full-year guidance.

Takeaway A cybersecurity vendor buying AI infrastructure outright rather than partnering signals that securing the AI stack is becoming existential, not optional.

2026's tech layoffs keep piling up, running ahead of last year's pace

Fast Company's running tracker shows Canva cutting 22% of its staff (1,271 people) via relocation or termination, announced November 3; a Washington state WARN filing covering 95 Amazon employees as part of previously disclosed retail cuts; fresh cuts to Block's Cash App and Square teams the day after its Q3 earnings; and roughly 300 roles cut at Squarespace. A separate tracker put 2026's year-to-date tech layoffs at about 206,877 people across 715 events as of November 16, running about 21% ahead of 2025's pace.

Takeaway The headline AI story is investment and acquisitions, but the quieter one — job cuts running ahead of last year — keeps compounding underneath it.

Retailers brace for a record $1.013 trillion US holiday season

The National Retail Federation forecasts 2026 US holiday sales will grow 4.2% to $1.013 trillion — the fastest pace since 2021's 12.6% jump — building on 2025's $972 billion season, which itself topped $1 trillion for the first time. NRF cites resilient consumer spending despite persistent inflation and uneven confidence in the economy.

Takeaway A second straight trillion-dollar holiday season, growing faster than last year's, is the clearest sign yet that higher prices haven't dented American shopping habits.

Sensex, Nifty tumble nearly 1% as crude surge rattles Dalal Street

Indian benchmarks fell sharply on Friday, November 27, with the Sensex down 989.33 points (1.17%) to 83,339.26 and the Nifty 50 down 277.30 points (1.07%) to 25,607.20, as Brent crude crossed $97 a barrel on escalating West Asia attacks. Nifty IT, Private Bank, Auto, Realty and Financial Services were the weakest sectors, while metal and chemical stocks bucked the trend. For the week, the Sensex fell 0.69% and the Nifty fell 0.75%.

Takeaway A geopolitically-driven oil spike, not domestic fundamentals, snapped India's two-day recovery heading into the weekend.

Coal India unit Mahanadi Coalfields makes a muted stock market debut

Mahanadi Coalfields listed on November 27 after its ₹8,981 crore IPO (price band ₹129-136), debuting at a discount rather than a premium — ₹133.15 on the BSE, down 2.10% versus the ₹136 issue price, and around ₹134 on the NSE. The IPO was entirely an offer-for-sale by parent Coal India, which has been trimming its stake through a series of subsidiary listings.

Takeaway A rare discount listing for a PSU coal IPO signals investor caution even as broader sentiment weakened on the same trading day.

Wall Street rallies in shortened Black Friday session; S&P 500 posts best week in 19 months

In Friday's Thanksgiving-shortened session, which ended at 1pm ET, the S&P 500 rose 1.6% to 7,297.49 and the Nasdaq Composite jumped 2.3% to 24,129.50, led by a rebound in AI and chip stocks after a rough November for tech. For the week, the S&P 500 gained 5.6% — its best week since April 2025 — and the Dow posted its biggest weekly gain since October 2022.

Takeaway US tech's sharp reversal gives global markets a firmer footing heading into next week — a sharp contrast to Friday's crude-driven selloff in India.

India's Q2 FY27 GDP print, due Monday, is the next test for markets

India's Ministry of Statistics is scheduled to release July-September FY27 GDP data on Monday, November 30. The Finance Ministry's nowcast pegs growth near 7.3%, SBI Research is more bullish at around 7.8%, while Brickwork Ratings expects a slowdown to about 7% — against Q1 FY27's 7.8% and a tough year-ago base of 8.2% in Q2 FY26.

Takeaway Monday's GDP print, alongside crude's trajectory, will be the first real test of whether Friday's selloff was a blip or the start of a trend.
