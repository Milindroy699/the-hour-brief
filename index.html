AI & Tech

Nvidia heads into Tuesday's first earnings call since Jensen Huang's resignation with interim CEO Colette Kress at the helm, as Anthropic's IPO roadshow enters its final two days with up to 20% of shares reserved for retail investors ahead of Wednesday's pricing and Thursday's Nasdaq debut, even as Google, Samsung, Warby Parker and Gentle Monster all open preorders for Gemini-powered smart eyewear, Microsoft's November Patch Tuesday fixes roughly 1,090 flaws including an actively exploited Outlook zero-day, CISA adds an actively exploited Cisco BroadWorks flaw to its federal must-patch list, and OpenAI strikes a deal with PayPal to let ChatGPT agents complete purchases

Takeaway Today’s tech story is an industry holding its breath before two reckonings — Nvidia’s first earnings since Huang’s ouster and Anthropic’s final IPO sprint — even as everyday security debt, from Outlook to Cisco gear, keeps piling up underneath.

Nvidia reports Tuesday in its first earnings call since Jensen Huang's resignation

Nvidia will report fiscal third-quarter results and host its conference call on Tuesday, November 17, after markets close, per its investor relations site — the company's first earnings report since CEO Jensen Huang resigned on November 12 following reports of extramarital affairs, one involving a woman flagged as a suspected Chinese intelligence contact. CFO Colette Kress, named interim CEO, will lead the call; Nvidia had guided to revenue of $108.0 billion, plus or minus 2%, before Huang's departure.

Takeaway A CFO stepping in to deliver guidance she helped write, days after her boss's abrupt exit, is the closest thing to a stress test Nvidia's governance has faced in over a decade.

Anthropic's IPO roadshow enters its final two days, with up to 20% of shares reserved for retail investors

Anthropic set a price range of $70 to $78 per share in a November 10 SEC filing, planning to sell 825 million Class A shares on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT, CNBC reported, with pricing expected Wednesday, November 18, and trading set to start Thursday, November 19. Up to 20% of the offering may go to retail investors, and cornerstone buyers including Google, Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft and TSMC have already committed roughly $26 billion at the IPO price.

Takeaway Carving out a fifth of a trillion-dollar-plus IPO for retail investors is Anthropic betting that public enthusiasm, not just institutional demand, is what gets it across the pricing line.

CNBC →

Google, Samsung, Warby Parker and Gentle Monster open preorders for Gemini-powered smart eyewear

Warby Parker confirmed in its third-quarter earnings release that it would begin taking preorders for its first Gemini-powered smart glasses on November 12, with delivery promised for the holiday season, VisionMonday reported. Samsung's "Intelligent Eyewear" and Gentle Monster's own Gemini-powered frames opened for preorder the same day, though Google itself hasn't confirmed pricing or an on-sale date for any of the three partners' hardware.

Takeaway Letting three different eyewear brands launch the same day, with no price from Google itself, is a company testing demand for an assistant before it commits to selling a device.

VisionMonday →

Microsoft's November Patch Tuesday fixes roughly 1,090 flaws, including an actively exploited Outlook zero-day

Microsoft's November 10 Patch Tuesday addressed about 1,090 vulnerabilities, including 63 rated critical, per CSO Online — among them CVE-2026-124194, an Outlook spoofing flaw already under active attack that lets hackers steal hashed NTLM credentials. Patching the Outlook flaw requires a separate Office update beyond the standard Windows cumulative patch, researchers noted.

Takeaway An email client bug that needs its own separate patch, on top of the one everyone already installs, is exactly the kind of gap attackers are counting on IT teams to miss.

CSO Online →

CISA adds an actively exploited Cisco BroadWorks flaw to its must-patch list, setting a November 24 deadline

CISA added CVE-2026-20320, an unauthenticated XXE flaw in Cisco BroadWorks' OCI XML parser, to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, BleepingComputer reported, giving federal agencies until November 24 to remediate it. The flaw affects a platform widely used by telecom carriers to deliver hosted unified-communications services.

Takeaway A two-week patch window for a flaw already being exploited against carrier-grade communications software is CISA betting disclosure alone will move faster than the attackers already inside.

BleepingComputer →

OpenAI and PayPal agree to embed a payments wallet into ChatGPT for AI agents

OpenAI and PayPal confirmed a deal to build a digital wallet into ChatGPT, letting the assistant complete purchases on a user's behalf with approval required on each transaction, CBS News reported. The companies didn't disclose a launch date, and OpenAI hasn't said which merchants or regions would be covered first.

Takeaway Requiring a human nod on every single purchase is OpenAI's admission that letting an agent spend money unsupervised is still a trust problem it hasn't solved.

Product & Business

Oil India's Q2 profit rises 53% to ₹2,521 crore and Godrej Industries' more than doubles to ₹560 crore as India's Q2 earnings season winds down, Sun Pharma's profit holds near ₹1,990 crore on an 11.8% sales rise, Adani Enterprises' headline profit falls 55% even as its core business books record half-year EBITDA, and Shipping Corporation of India's profit more than triples to ₹577.8 crore, even as Indian startups raise just $162.9 million this week — down 32% — Coforge joins SpaceX's Grok enterprise network, and Stratechery's Ben Thompson argues the real risk in Jensen Huang's unraveling is governance, not national security

Takeaway Today’s business story is India's Q2 season closing out on uneven footing — clean compounding at some firms, one-time-adjusted numbers at others — while global commentary keeps circling back to governance as the real cost of Nvidia's scandal.

Oil India's Q2 net profit rises 53% to ₹2,521 crore on higher realisations

Oil India reported net profit of ₹2,521 crore for the September quarter, up 53% year-on-year, on higher crude output and stronger realisations, Business Standard reported. The state-run explorer's results were among a cluster of public-sector energy firms posting double-digit growth this earnings season.

Takeaway A state oil explorer posting its best quarter in years is a reminder that even in an AI-dominated news cycle, commodity cycles still move the needle for plenty of India's biggest companies.

Business Standard →

Godrej Industries' Q2 profit jumps 131% to ₹560 crore

Godrej Industries reported consolidated net profit of ₹560 crore for the September quarter, up from ₹242.5 crore a year earlier, as revenue rose 45%, Business Upturn reported. The company didn't break out how much of the gain came from its chemicals, agri or finance businesses individually.

Takeaway More than doubling profit without disclosing which division drove it is the kind of headline number that invites a closer look at the segment-level filing before taking it at face value.

Business Upturn →

Sun Pharma's Q2 sales rise 11.8% to ₹16,100 crore as net profit holds near ₹1,990 crore

Sun Pharmaceutical Industries reported September-quarter sales of about ₹16,100 crore, up 11.8% year-on-year, with net profit near ₹1,990 crore, Investywise reported. First-half FY27 consolidated sales reached ₹31,284 crore, up 11% from the same period last year.

Takeaway Sales and profit growing at roughly the same single-digit-to-low-double-digit pace is Sun Pharma showing steady compounding rather than the one-off jumps driving some of this earnings season's other headline numbers.

Investywise →

Adani Enterprises' headline profit falls 55% even as its core business books record half-year EBITDA

Adani Enterprises posted half-year EBITDA of ₹11,958 crore, up 56% and a record for the period, on revenue up 62% to ₹71,569 crore, Oneindia reported. But Q2 profit attributable to owners fell 55% to ₹1,492 crore from ₹3,304 crore a year earlier; continuing profit before tax, excluding a ₹2,644 crore one-time OFAC settlement booked in the June quarter, actually rose 45% to ₹3,297 crore.

Takeaway Flagging the one-time settlement that explains the headline profit drop, rather than letting a 55% decline stand unexplained, is the kind of disclosure that holds up to scrutiny.

Oneindia →

Shipping Corporation of India's Q2 profit more than triples to ₹577.8 crore

Shipping Corporation of India reported net profit of ₹577.8 crore for the September quarter, up from ₹189.2 crore a year earlier, as revenue rose 71.1%, MENAFN reported, citing the company's results.

Takeaway A state shipping line tripling profit on a 71% revenue jump is a quieter sign that India's trade volumes are still growing faster than the headlines about global shipping give it credit for.

MENAFN →

Indian startups raise $162.9 million this week, down 32% from the week before

Twenty-two Indian startups raised a combined $162.9 million between November 10 and 14, Inc42 reported, down from $237.8 million raised by 20 startups the previous week. Finnable and Bombay Shaving Company led the week's deals, per the outlet's roundup.

Takeaway A third straight week of declining deal value is India's funding market cooling even as the IPO pipeline stays unusually busy.

Inc42 →

Coforge joins SpaceX's Super Intelligence partner network to bring Grok into enterprise production

Coforge said it has joined SpaceXSI's partner network as a Transformation Partner, building industry-specific applications on Grok and embedding them into clients' existing systems through dedicated engineering pods, CXOToday and AiThority reported. The company didn't disclose financial terms or name which clients would deploy first; rival Tech Mahindra struck a similar SpaceXSI partnership on October 22.

Takeaway A second major Indian IT services firm signing with SpaceX's AI arm within weeks is the clearest sign yet that Grok is trying to buy enterprise credibility through the outsourcing giants that already run Fortune 500 back offices.

CXOToday →

Stratechery's Ben Thompson argues the real risk in Jensen Huang's unraveling is governance, not national security

In a November 10 Stratechery Update titled "The Jensen Huang Story Gets Worse," Ben Thompson wrote that the scandal's deepest damage to Nvidia lies in what it reveals about board oversight, not the national-security questions raised by Huang's alleged contact with a suspected Chinese intelligence operative.

Takeaway Reframing a tabloid-style scandal as a governance failure, rather than a security one, is Thompson arguing the board — not just the CEO — has questions to answer.

GIFT Nifty futures traded around 25,242, up 71 points, pointing to a positive start for the Nifty 50 on Monday even as Asian markets broadly declined and Brent crude eased toward $95 a barrel, Business Standard reported in its morning market live blog.

Wall Street's week of recovery sputters to a halt on Friday as major indices close mostly lower

US stocks closed mostly lower on Friday, November 13, with the Dow the lone gainer among the three major indices, as a week that had looked like a recovery lost momentum into the close, per ABC News' wire coverage. Nvidia shares cooled again after the prior week's swings tied to Jensen Huang's resignation.

Qualcomm's post-earnings guidance wobble still weighs on chip sentiment

Qualcomm's fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of $10.32 billion and EPS of $2.17 both edged past estimates on November 4, but shares fell about 2% after its outlook came in light, with margins in its chip business shrinking even as data-center and automotive revenue grew, SiliconANGLE reported.

Takeaway A beat-and-raise quarter that still sends the stock down on margin worries is exactly the kind of result Nvidia's own investors are bracing to avoid on Tuesday.
