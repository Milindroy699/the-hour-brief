AI & Tech

Anthropic’s amended S-1/A warns Claude may be nearing a self-improvement threshold even as its own public-filing clock sets November 16 as the earliest possible Nasdaq trading day, while chip stocks claw back Thursday’s rout with AMD leading a Friday rebound and an AI-driven memory shortage leaves Western Digital sold out of hard-drive capacity through 2026

Takeaway Today’s AI story is confidence and caution side by side — Anthropic flagging its own models’ self-improvement risk in a filing meant to sell investors, while chip stocks recover and a memory shortage keeps tightening around the industry.

Anthropic’s amended S-1/A warns Claude may be nearing a self-improvement threshold

Anthropic’s amended S-1/A, filed with the SEC on November 2, discloses that the company’s own internal estimates suggest future models may soon meet its Responsible Scaling Policy’s threshold for “dramatically accelerated progress attributable to AI” — the definition it ties to recursive self-improvement, according to AI Weekly’s coverage of the filing. No current Claude model has crossed that line, the filing says, and Anthropic adds that it does not plan to let Claude independently design and iterate future frontier models without meaningful human oversight, even if that becomes technically possible.

Takeaway Putting an “imminent” self-improvement warning in a filing meant to sell investors on the company is either remarkable candor or remarkable marketing — probably some of both.

Chip stocks claw back Thursday’s rout as the Nasdaq rebounds 1.09%

The Nasdaq Composite rose 1.09% to close at 24,579.42 on Friday, November 6, while the S&P 500 added 0.31% to 7,376.86, according to CNBC’s live markets coverage. AMD led chipmakers higher, closing at $449.04, up roughly 11% on the day, while Nvidia rose to $186.64 and Broadcom added a more modest 0.74% to $293.69 — all three still recovering from Thursday’s rout, which had left the Nasdaq more than 10% below its October high and technically in correction territory.

Takeaway A one-day bounce this uneven across three chip stocks that fell together on Thursday shows investors are already picking winners and losers inside what was, a day earlier, a single panic.

An AI-driven memory shortage leaves Western Digital sold out of hard-drive capacity through 2026

Western Digital says it is effectively sold out of hard-disk-drive capacity for all of 2026, with most of that output committed to its largest customers — overwhelmingly AI companies — and firm orders already extending into 2028, according to PC World. Cloud customers now account for about 89% of Western Digital’s revenue, and its most recent quarterly revenue rose 45% year-over-year to $3.34 billion; separately, SanDisk has reportedly raised its NAND flash contract prices by around 50% for November as AI data centers compete with consumer-electronics makers for the same chips, according to Tom’s Hardware.

Takeaway When a hard-drive maker’s order book stretches to 2028, the AI buildout isn’t just buying GPUs anymore — it’s quietly reshaping the market for the boring storage hardware everyone else also needs.

Anthropic’s public S-1 clock sets November 16 as the earliest possible Nasdaq trading day

Because Anthropic filed its S-1 publicly, rather than confidentially, on October 26, SEC rules require its roadshow to wait at least 15 days, pushing the earliest start to around November 10 — with November 16 the earliest possible first trading day under those same timing rules, according to market-analysis site XTB. Anthropic is reportedly targeting the Nasdaq ticker ANT and a listing before Thanksgiving, though the company has not confirmed a date, and the price range in its filing remains blank.

Takeaway Every date in Anthropic’s IPO timeline right now is a calculation from SEC rules, not a confirmation from Anthropic — worth remembering before treating any specific day as locked in.

Product & Business

Stratechery’s Ben Thompson frames Apple’s earnings-day memory-cost warning as “The Hardware Company Regression,” even as India’s Q2 FY27 earnings season crosses its halfway mark with median profit growth near 21% and Cholamandalam Investment and Finance’s profit jumps 41% to ₹1,630 crore alongside a ₹5,000 crore fundraise

Takeaway Today’s business story is steady, unglamorous progress — a close read of Apple’s earnings, India’s earnings season tracking ahead of last year, and a clean profit beat all point the same way without one single headline-grabbing deal.

Stratechery’s Ben Thompson revisits Apple’s memory-cost warning as “The Hardware Company Regression”

In a November 3 column titled “Apple Earnings, Memory Problems, The Hardware Company Regression,” Stratechery’s Ben Thompson argues that Apple’s hardware business looks stronger than ever even as memory-chip costs weigh on it, and asks whether Apple’s software ambitions help or hurt that picture. The piece follows Apple’s own warning, on its most recent earnings call, that it expects “significantly higher memory costs” to keep affecting its business.

Takeaway Thompson’s framing is a useful reminder that a company’s hardware can be thriving and squeezed by the same supply chain at once — the AI-driven memory shortage doesn’t pick sides.

India’s Q2 FY27 earnings season crosses its halfway mark with median profit growth near 21%

With 558 of roughly 1,023 expected companies having reported — about 54% — median profit growth across those reporters stands at 20.8% year-on-year, and 72 of the 146 companies with analyst estimates have beaten them, according to a Tapetide earnings tracker. Chemicals companies lead all sectors on median profit growth, up 50.8%, while realty is the weakest, down 16.6%; the reporting window runs to November 14.

Takeaway A season where seven in ten reporting companies are growing profit, not just the headline names everyone already covered, says more about India’s underlying corporate health than any single blockbuster result.

Cholamandalam Investment and Finance’s Q2 FY27 profit rises 41% to ₹1,630 crore

Cholamandalam Investment and Finance Company reported standalone net profit of ₹1,630 crore for the quarter, up 41% year-on-year, with six-month profit at ₹3,283.66 crore, according to InvestyWise. The company’s board also approved a ₹5,000 crore qualified institutional placement (QIP) to raise fresh capital.

Takeaway Raising ₹5,000 crore right alongside a 41% profit jump suggests Cholamandalam is funding growth from a position of strength, not patching a gap.

Sensex and Nifty extend their rally to a seventh straight session as Nifty reclaims 25,000 on Dhanteras

The Sensex closed Friday, November 6 (Dhanteras), at 81,028.02, up 1,718.01 points or 2.17% from the previous close of 79,310.01, while the Nifty 50 gained 2.01% to 25,030.35 — crossing the 25,000 mark for the first time since February 27, 2026 — according to Business Standard and ETV Bharat. Banking stocks led the rally, though broader markets lagged: the Nifty Midcap 100 slipped 0.02% and the Smallcap 100 fell 0.82%.

Takeaway Seven straight gaining sessions taking the index back to a nine-month level is a genuinely strong run, but broader markets quietly sitting out the last leg is worth watching.

Wall Street’s Nasdaq rebounds 1.09% Friday, still short of undoing Thursday’s correction

The S&P 500 rose 0.31% to 7,376.86 and the Nasdaq Composite gained 1.09% to 24,579.42 on Friday, November 6, according to CNBC, with chip stocks leading the recovery after Thursday’s rout had pushed the Nasdaq more than 10% below its October high. Strong US jobs data was cited as a factor in Friday’s bounce, even as the same data also raised the odds that the Federal Reserve holds off on a December rate cut.

Takeaway A 1% bounce the day after a 5.5% drop barely moves the needle — Wall Street is still net lower on the week, whatever Friday’s headline says.

A rare Sunday Muhurat trading session arrives November 8 as India’s holiday-shortened week continues

NSE circular NSE/CMTR/71775 sets Sunday, November 8, 2026, as this year’s Muhurat trading date — the special Diwali Lakshmi Puja session exchanges hold once a year — with Monday, November 9, a normal trading day and Tuesday, November 10, a holiday for Diwali-Balipratipada, according to AliceBlue. It’s an unusual calendar quirk: India’s markets go from Friday’s Dhanteras rally, through a dead Saturday, into a one-hour symbolic session on a Sunday most weeks never trade at all.

Takeaway Muhurat trading moves little real money by design — it’s a ritual, not a forecast — but it does mean this holiday-thinned week isn’t done delivering sessions yet.
