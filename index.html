Takeaway Today’s AI story is a board reversing itself in full public view — eleven days after ousting Huang over a conduct scandal, Nvidia decided the cost of being without him outweighed the cost of looking indecisive, and Wall Street rewarded the flip-flop anyway.

Nvidia's board reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO, eleven days after forcing him out

Nvidia's board voted unanimously on November 23 to reinstate Jensen Huang as President, CEO and a director, effective immediately, reversing its November 17 position that it would not bring him back. The board said it was "satisfied with respect to Mr. Huang's candor and ability to lead NVIDIA" and said it had received input from "senior levels of the United States government" in reaching its decision; Colette Kress, who had served as interim CEO, returns to her role as CFO.

Takeaway Reversing a public, on-the-record stance within six days is a costlier signal about board unity than either decision would have been on its own.

Nvidia's stock has its best day in over two decades, igniting a $1 trillion Wall Street rally

Nvidia shares closed up 31.48% at $165.05 on Monday after the reinstatement news, adding close to $1 trillion in market value. The S&P 500 jumped 4.73% to 7,237.17 and the Nasdaq Composite surged 6.51% to 24,089.56 — both their best single-day gains since April 2025 — while the Dow rose roughly 1,400 points; the rally pulled the S&P 500 and Dow out of correction territory they had entered the week before.

Takeaway A single leadership reversal erasing two major indexes' corrections in one session says more about how concentrated this market's AI bet has become than about Nvidia's underlying business.

CNBC →

Trump claims a role in the reversal as Nvidia's investigation into Huang grinds on

Trump said the board "made the correct and proper decision" and claimed he was "represented in the board meeting," a claim Nvidia has not confirmed; Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent had separately urged the board to look outside the company for its next chief. Nvidia says the independent-director committee investigating the conduct that led to Huang's November 12 resignation — including his contact with a woman U.S. officials suspect worked for Chinese intelligence — will continue, with closed-door congressional questioning of Huang and general counsel Tim Teter still scheduled for December 1-2.

Takeaway Reinstating Huang while the investigation into what he actually did stays open is the board betting it can have its CEO back and still look like it's taking the scandal seriously.

Amazon reportedly nears a $7.5 billion deal for Israeli AI video startup Decart

Amazon is reportedly close to paying more than $7 billion — potentially nearing $7.5 billion with earnout targets included — in cash, stock and deferred payments for Decart, an Israeli AI video startup valued at $4 billion in a May funding round; Anthropic had explored a roughly $6 billion deal for the company before walking away in September. Amazon already gives Decart early access to its Trainium chips.

Takeaway Paying nearly double a company's May valuation for a startup it already supplies chips to is Amazon betting on owning the AI video stack, not just backing it.

Microsoft's AI chief signals the company is preparing to lean less on OpenAI's models

Microsoft AI chief Mustafa Suleyman has reportedly prepared staff for the possibility of losing preferential access to OpenAI's technology, even though Microsoft's license runs through 2032 under a non-exclusive deal the two companies revised in April. Microsoft is said to be targeting a frontier general-purpose model of its own.

Takeaway A company that invested tens of billions in OpenAI building its own alternative isn't abandoning the partnership — it's hedging against needing it.

Dataconomy →

CISA adds four more actively exploited flaws to its must-patch list

On November 18, CISA added vulnerabilities in Synology's DiskStation Manager, Apache Axis, Vanderbilt's REDCap and BerriAI's LiteLLM to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, citing evidence of active exploitation including ransomware attacks on NAS devices. Federal agencies face a November 21 deadline for the Synology, Axis and REDCap flaws and a December 2 deadline for the LiteLLM flaw.

Takeaway An open-source LLM gateway sitting on the same must-patch list as decade-old enterprise software is a reminder that AI infrastructure inherits all of software's usual security debt.

Takeaway Today’s business story is unfinished fights everywhere — Tata's governance battle, a builder's profit squeeze — even as startup funding and US holiday spending both show real momentum.

Tata Sons heads toward Thursday's board meeting with two fights still unresolved

Noel Tata has asked Tata Sons' board to take up, at its November 26 meeting, a restructuring tied to the RBI's listing directive for upper-layer NBFCs and a funding request from the Shapoorji Pallonji Group worth ₹25,000 crore, seeking partial stake monetisation. Tata Trusts opposes a listing, and the RBI reiterated on November 16 that Tata Sons must comply with listing rules.

Takeaway The group still hasn't settled who gets the final word on Tata Sons' structure — not the regulator, not the trusts, and not Noel Tata alone.

Fintech InPrime Finserv raised a ₹101 crore Series B round, doubling its valuation to ₹360 crore, with its founders retaining a 28.5% stake after the round.

Target posts its strongest holiday-quarter sales growth in years

Target's net sales rose 7.1% to about $27.1 billion and comparable sales grew 5.6% in the quarter reported November 18-19, beating analyst estimates and marking its third straight quarter of sales growth; the company raised its full-year outlook, crediting price cuts, even as apparel sales fell 3.8%.

Walmart's US e-commerce sales jump 28% this holiday season

Walmart's US e-commerce sales rose 28% as grocery comparable sales grew low single digits and general merchandise — led by fashion, home and automotive — also grew low single digits; the company's CFO described the start of the holiday season as being on solid footing.

Takeaway Grocery and general merchandise growing only modestly while e-commerce grows 28% shows where Walmart's real holiday fight is happening — online, not in stores.

Dilip Buildcon's profit nearly halves despite rising revenue

Dilip Buildcon's consolidated net profit fell about 53% to ₹101 crore in the quarter ended September, even as revenue rose and its order book hit a record ₹33,081 crore; the company says the drop partly reflects a high year-ago base that included ₹122 crore of one-off exceptional gains versus just ₹2 crore this quarter.

Takeaway A record order book doesn't help this quarter's profit — it's a bet on revenue the company hasn't booked yet.
