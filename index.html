Nvidia still has no named successor to CEO Jensen Huang, a month into open scrutiny of his conduct

Axios and Fortune reported November 9 that Nvidia has named no successor to CEO Jensen Huang even as scrutiny continues over his conduct and an alleged Chinese-intelligence contact, with the company saying Huang remains committed to leading Nvidia “without interruption.” Names floated as possible candidates include CFO Colette Kress, CUDA architect Ian Buck and outside executives such as AMD's Lisa Su, though none has been confirmed.

Takeaway Naming no successor a month into open scrutiny of its CEO is Nvidia betting that Jensen Huang remains the one risk the market won't actually price in.

Anthropic launches Claude Meetings in beta, letting Claude join live Google Meet calls

Anthropic said November 10 that Claude Meetings, now in open beta for Team and Enterprise customers, lets Claude join a Google Meet call as a visible participant, answer questions in real time, and turn the discussion into whiteboards, timelines and follow-up tasks, Axios and TechCrunch reported. Microsoft Teams and Zoom aren't supported at launch, and admins control whether Claude can join calls that include external guests.

Takeaway Shipping a meeting bot for Google Meet only, not Zoom or Teams, is Anthropic picking its fights carefully rather than trying to win every conference-room integration at once.

China's public-comment window on AI export curbs covering 23 technologies closes today

Beijing's commerce and science ministries opened public comment on October 16 on a draft list of 40 controlled technologies, 23 of them AI-related, including model weights and source code above a two-trillion-parameter threshold, per regulations tracker regulations.ai. The submission window closes November 15, though pre-export registration for covered items already took effect the day the draft was announced, and no final catalogue has yet been published.

Takeaway Putting the registration requirement into force before the comment period even closes shows Beijing treating this less as an open consultation than paperwork before a decision it's already made.

Zyxel patches a zero-day exploited to compromise nearly 1,000 network switches across 48 countries

Zyxel fixed CVE-2026-107441, an out-of-bounds write in its GS1900 switches' web interface that let an unauthenticated attacker run OS commands, BleepingComputer and CSO Online reported. Researchers at Binarly found attackers had compromised 996 devices across 48 countries to harvest configs and hashed root credentials; the flaw had hidden behind an already-patched bug, so an earlier June fix didn't stop it.

Takeaway A patch that quietly fails to close the real hole is worse than no patch at all — it tells defenders the problem is solved months before anyone finds out it isn't.

BleepingComputer →

A second actively exploited Ivanti Connect Secure flaw's federal patch deadline passes

CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog set a November 13 deadline for federal agencies to fix CVE-2026-118527, the second of two Ivanti Connect Secure flaws added October 30 after Ivanti shipped fixed versions 25.1.1.3 and 22.8R2.6 on October 29, Help Net Security reported. A related flaw in the same chain had its own November 2 deadline pass two weeks earlier.

Takeaway Two deadlines on the same vulnerability chain, two weeks apart, is CISA admitting that patching a VPN gateway everyone depends on can't happen all at once, even under an active-exploitation order.

Help Net Security →

A decade-old OpenSSH flaw gets exploited within two days of its fix

GreyNoise recorded the first in-the-wild exploitation of CVE-2026-123158 on November 7, about 40 hours after a fix shipped in OpenSSH 10.6p2, hitting Linux systems that run musl with PAM disabled — Alpine Linux's default configuration — The Hacker News reported in its November 9 weekly recap. Other researchers tied the activity to botnet and cryptomining campaigns.

Takeaway A flaw hiding in OpenSSH for a decade, then weaponized within two days of its fix, is the clearest sign yet that patch day and exploit day are converging.

Product & Business

China blocks SoftBank's $5.4 billion bid for ABB's robotics division as Revolut confirms a $115 billion valuation after completing a secondary share sale, even as India's Q2 earnings season rolls on with Coal India's profit up 53% to ₹6,516 crore, Nykaa's more than tripling to ₹101 crore, PB Fintech's rising 43% to ₹192 crore, Bajaj Finance's climbing 29% to ₹6,367 crore, and Hero MotoCorp's up 41% to ₹1,861 crore

Takeaway Today’s business story is capital turning selective — China blocked a $5.4B robotics deal and Revolut's jump came from existing shares, not new money, even as India's Q2 season keeps delivering outsized profit growth across lending, beauty and two-wheelers.

China blocks SoftBank's roughly $5.4 billion bid for ABB's robotics division

ABB confirmed on November 5 that China's foreign-investment security review office has prohibited SoftBank's planned acquisition of its robotics division, a deal valued at about $5.4 billion, the South China Morning Post reported. China accounts for roughly 30% of ABB Robotics' revenue, and the two companies said they remain in “close dialogue” on the transaction even after the block — the second major deal China has derailed this year, after it ordered Meta to unwind its acquisition of AI startup Manus.

Takeaway Losing approval in your target's biggest market isn't a technicality — it's the deal's whole rationale disappearing.

Revolut confirms a $115 billion valuation after completing a secondary share sale

Fintech Revolut said November 4 it has completed a secondary share sale valuing the company at $115 billion, up from $75 billion a year earlier, Tech.eu reported. Shares in the sale priced at $2,017 each; the company didn't disclose how much was raised, and CEO Nik Storonsky's roughly one-third stake would now be worth more than $36 billion.

Takeaway A 53% valuation jump in under a year, delivered via existing shares rather than new capital, is Revolut's investors cashing in confidence, not funding growth.

Tech.eu →

Coal India's Q2 net profit rises 53% to ₹6,516 crore

Coal India reported attributable net profit of ₹6,516 crore for the quarter ended September 30, up 53% year-on-year, as revenue rose 21% to ₹44,179 crore, according to its October 29 results covered by Upstox. The board also declared a second interim dividend of ₹10.25 per share, with a record date of November 4.

Takeaway A state coal miner growing profit faster than revenue, in the same year renewables keep grabbing headlines, is a reminder fossil-fuel demand hasn't gotten the memo yet.

Nykaa's Q2 profit more than triples to ₹101 crore

FSN E-Commerce Ventures, which runs Nykaa, reported November 4 that consolidated net profit rose 207% year-on-year to ₹101 crore for the September quarter, as revenue grew 28% to ₹3,006 crore and EBITDA margin expanded by about 196 basis points to 8.7%. The board separately approved acquiring an 82% stake in Simply Nam Beauty for up to ₹33 crore.

Takeaway Profit tripling on 28% revenue growth is Nykaa's operating leverage finally doing more work than its topline.

PB Fintech's Q2 profit rises 43% to ₹192 crore

PB Fintech, which owns Policybazaar, reported November 3 that consolidated net profit rose about 43% year-on-year to ₹192 crore for the September quarter, as revenue from operations grew 35% to ₹2,178 crore on a 38% jump in insurance premiums, YourStory reported. Profit was also up 18% sequentially from ₹163 crore in the June quarter.

Takeaway A fintech aggregator growing profit faster than premiums is PB Fintech proving it can take a cut without taking on more risk.

Bajaj Finance's Q2 profit rises 29% to ₹6,367 crore as assets under management grow 26%

Bajaj Finance reported consolidated profit after tax of ₹6,367 crore for the September quarter, up 29% year-on-year, as its assets under management grew 26% to ₹5.85 lakh crore, according to its Q2 FY27 results covered by Kotak Neo.

Takeaway Profit and loan book growing at nearly the same pace is Bajaj Finance showing it can scale without the two drifting apart.

Hero MotoCorp's Q2 profit rises 41% to ₹1,861 crore, aided by a one-time gain

Hero MotoCorp reported consolidated revenue of ₹14,971 crore for the September quarter, up 23% year-on-year, with profit after tax rising 41% to ₹1,861 crore, PSU Connect reported. The results, approved October 28, included a one-time gain of about ₹397 crore from an associate's equity consolidation.

Takeaway Flagging the one-time gain inside your own profit number, rather than letting the headline growth rate stand alone, is the kind of disclosure that holds up to scrutiny.

India's October retail inflation breaches the RBI's 6% ceiling for the first time in two years, at 6.09%

Retail inflation climbed to 6.09% in October from 5.54% in September, crossing the RBI's upper tolerance limit of 6% for the first time in two years, MoSPI data released November 12 showed, Business Standard and Forbes India reported. Food inflation was the main driver, rising to 8.94% from 7.74% the month before, with rural inflation at 6.63% against urban inflation of 5.41%.

Takeaway A ceiling breach after two clean years is the kind of number that outlasts a single bad session — it changes what the RBI is allowed to do next, not just how Monday opens.

Brent crude logs its sharpest weekly drop since the pandemic, settling near $92

Brent crude lost about 16% over the week to November 9 — its steepest weekly decline since the start of the Covid-19 pandemic — before steadying around $92 a barrel by November 11 on signs of easing US-Iran tensions, Trading Economics reported. The benchmark had traded above $107 a barrel earlier in the month.

Takeaway A benchmark losing 16% in a week after spiking past $107 is crude pricing in a Gulf conflict's worst case, then quickly pricing it back out.

Trading Economics →

Bitcoin falls to a three-month low near $73,100 on hot US inflation data and rising Fed rate-hike bets

Bitcoin dropped as much as 3% to a three-month low of about $73,100 on November 11, Trading Economics reported, as hotter-than-expected US inflation data hardened expectations of further Federal Reserve rate hikes and dented demand for riskier assets. The token was down roughly 17% for the year to date.

Takeaway Crypto hitting a three-month low on the same inflation data moving India's markets shows how tightly digital assets are now wired into the same macro lever as everything else.
