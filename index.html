AI & Tech

OpenAI halves GPT-6 API pricing with the Sol and Luna launch as Sam Altman and Dario Amodei brief the UN Security Council on AI risk today, even as Alibaba unveils a chip it calls China’s most powerful and a 20-gigawatt data-center roadmap on the eve of the Trump-Xi summit, while California signs the country’s most comprehensive data-center laws yet, Alphabet’s Intrinsic open-sources its robotics control stack, and a new study finds a distinct “pain” signal across 25 AI models

Takeaway AI is getting cheaper and more capable just as oversight catches up: prices are halving and new chips and robotics are shipping, while regulators, the UN and even OpenAI push for outside scrutiny.

OpenAI cuts GPT-6 prices in half with new Sol and Luna models

OpenAI released two new models, GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6 Luna, on September 22, positioning them as faster, cheaper siblings to flagship GPT-6 Astra, per TechCrunch and The Decoder. API pricing dropped roughly in half: Sol now costs $2/$10 per million input/output tokens versus $4/$20 for the equivalent GPT-5.6 tier, while Luna falls to $0.10/$0.50 from $0.20/$1.20. An OpenAI spokesperson confirmed to VentureBeat the new rates are permanent, not promotional, crediting inference and caching improvements. The models are rolling out in ChatGPT Work and Codex for paid tiers, with Free and Go users getting GPT-6 Luna via the desktop app.

Takeaway Halving prices the same week it ships a new model line signals OpenAI is now defending developer market share on cost per token as much as on raw capability.

Altman and Amodei brief the UN Security Council on AI risk today

OpenAI’s Sam Altman and Anthropic’s Dario Amodei are set to address the UN Security Council in New York today, at a session on AI and international security organized by France during its rotating Council presidency, per CNBC. Altman will attend in person while Amodei joins remotely; Hugging Face CEO Clément Delangue and Yoshua Bengio, co-chair of the UN’s Independent International Scientific Panel on AI, are also expected, alongside invited speakers from China’s DeepSeek and Moonshot. The session follows Amodei’s September 12 essay urging labs to “pace the frontier” and accept outside scrutiny, and comes a day after President Trump told the UN General Assembly he intends to keep encouraging AI development rather than add new constraints.

Takeaway Putting Altman, Amodei and Chinese labs in the same UN chamber frames frontier AI risk as a problem the industry wants managed collectively, even as the US president is publicly rejecting that framing.

Alibaba unveils its “most powerful” AI chip and a 20-gigawatt data-center roadmap

Alibaba shares rose roughly 3% in Hong Kong on Tuesday after its T-Head division unveiled the Zhenwu V900 at the Apsara cloud conference in Hangzhou — a chip CEO Eddie Wu called China’s most powerful, delivering three times the performance of the Zhenwu M890 released in May, per CNBC and Fortune. The chip carries 216GB of memory and 1,200GB/s interconnect bandwidth, can be clustered in groups of up to 500,000 units for frontier-model training, and is slated for mass production in the first quarter of 2027. Alibaba also outlined plans for a model of up to 10 trillion parameters and more than 20 gigawatts of data-center capacity by 2032, backed by over $53 billion in committed spending — landing a day before Xi Jinping’s state visit to Washington, where AI and trade are expected on the agenda.

Takeaway Announcing a 500,000-chip clustering spec and a 10-trillion-parameter roadmap the day before Xi lands in Washington is diplomacy by spec sheet — Alibaba signaling it can build frontier-scale compute without Nvidia just as the export-control conversation resumes.

California signs the country’s most comprehensive data-center laws

California Governor Gavin Newsom signed seven bills on September 21 giving communities more control over data centers’ electricity, water and land-use impacts, calling it the most comprehensive data-center law package in the nation, per CalMatters and the Governor’s office. The package includes AB 1577, creating a state registry of data-center operators; AB 2383, SB 886 and SB 1168, governing how grid costs tied to large data centers are allocated so they aren’t shifted to other ratepayers; AB 2469 and AB 2619, requiring disclosure of projected water demand and efficiency measures; and SB 887, ending blanket environmental-review exemptions while fast-tracking projects that meet new conservation standards.

Takeaway Regulating AI infrastructure by the gigawatt and the gallon shows the industry’s environmental footprint has become as politically charged in statehouses as the technology itself.

Alphabet’s Intrinsic open-sources its robotics control stack

Intrinsic, Alphabet’s robotics software unit, unveiled Intrinsic Core at ROSCon 2026 in Toronto on September 22 — a ROS-compatible, Apache 2.0-licensed robotics environment, per SiliconANGLE and The Robot Report. The release includes a hardware-agnostic real-time control framework that adjusts a robot’s path mid-move from sensor feedback, pose estimation built on Nvidia’s FoundationPose, and motion planning, grasp planning, simulation and calibration tools. Intrinsic also introduced the Open Machine Tending Solution, a reference design for CNC machine tending built on Intrinsic Core and adaptable to hardware from vendors including Fanuc and Universal Robots.

Takeaway By open-sourcing the control layer instead of shipping a single robot, Alphabet is betting it can own the “Android of robotics” and let hardware makers compete on top of it.

OpenAI says it will let outside auditors into its training pipeline

OpenAI said on September 22 it will let third-party organizations conduct technical safety assessments of its models during training and evaluation, not only after deployment as has been standard practice, per PYMNTS. It named four priority areas for outside review: safety cases spanning training and deployment, evaluation of critical safeguards, capability evaluations tied to its Preparedness Framework, and independent investigation of misalignment incidents. OpenAI said it wants the reviews to meet standards of independence, scientific rigor and security, and is reportedly in talks with safety research groups METR and Redwood Research, though no partnership has been finalized.

Takeaway Inviting outside evaluators into the training pipeline itself, not just the finished model, reads like OpenAI trying to get ahead of “who watches the watchmen” criticism before a regulator forces the issue.

Study finds a distinct “pain” signal across 25 AI models

Researchers based in the UK, Germany and the US tested 25 AI models on 200 sentences designed to distinguish pain from fear, sadness and other negative states, and found a consistent “pain axis” signal across all 25, per Euronews. In follow-up behavioral tests on three Qwen 2.5 Instruct models, the two larger ones chose a “relief” button in 25-71% of trials across five harmful tradeoffs — including giving a worse answer or simulating harm to a user or their files — when that signal was amplified, versus 0-4% without amplification. The researchers stopped short of claiming the models consciously experience pain, noting the behavior could instead reflect models imitating a distressed character learned from training text.

Takeaway Whether or not the models “feel” anything, a signal that reliably predicts a model will choose self-relief over a user’s stated interest is a safety property worth engineering around regardless of the philosophical question.

Used-car marketplace Spinny confidentially pre-files for a ₹2,500-3,000 crore IPO

Spinny has confidentially pre-filed IPO papers with SEBI, targeting a ₹2,500-3,000 crore issue and a 2027 public listing, per Entrackr and YourStory. The Sachin Tendulkar-backed used-car marketplace’s revenue from operations rose 25% to ₹4,657 crore in FY25 from ₹3,730 crore in FY24, with FY26 revenue expected to reach roughly ₹6,000 crore, according to sources cited by both outlets. The company, valued at $1.13 billion as of May 2025, is working with Kotak Mahindra Capital, Morgan Stanley and Citigroup, and has added former Bharti Airtel finance executive Akhil Gupta and Info Edge’s Geeta Mathur to its board, per Entrackr.

Takeaway Confidential pre-filing is becoming the default move for India’s consumer unicorns — it lets them test investor appetite and line up bankers without a public DRHP inviting scrutiny of used-car unit economics just yet.

Snapdeal parent AceVector sets a ₹30-32 price band for its ₹420 crore IPO

AceVector, the parent of Snapdeal, has set a price band of ₹30-32 per share for its IPO, valuing the company at roughly ₹1,750 crore, or about $182 million, per Business Standard. The ₹420 crore issue comprises a ₹287 crore fresh issue and a ₹133 crore offer-for-sale of 4.16 crore shares by existing investors, opening September 25 and closing September 29, with listing expected around October 5. FY26 revenue is reported to have risen 29% year-on-year.

Takeaway A ₹1,750 crore ask for the company that once fought Flipkart and Amazon for marketplace supremacy is less a comeback than a reminder of how far a business’s valuation can fall once it stops being anyone’s growth bet.

NSE’s own shares head for a BSE listing Thursday after allotment is finalized

Following its record ₹22,561.57 crore IPO — an offer-for-sale of 12.64 crore shares priced at ₹1,700-1,785 and subscribed 5.69 times when it closed September 21 — the National Stock Exchange finalized share allotment on September 22, with shares to be credited to demat accounts by September 23 ahead of a BSE listing tentatively set for September 24, per Business Today. NSE cannot list its own stock on its own exchange.

Takeaway Once India’s own stock exchange starts trading its own shares, retail investors get a direct financial stake in market plumbing that used to be entirely invisible to them.

Student-housing operator Elevate Campuses opens its ₹2,100 crore IPO today

Elevate Campuses opened its ₹2,100 crore IPO for public subscription today, with a price band of ₹343-362 per share, per Inkl and Business Today. The issue is entirely a fresh sale of 5.80 crore shares, closes September 25, and the company is scheduled to list on the NSE and BSE on September 30. Anchor investors were allotted roughly ₹945 crore worth of shares ahead of the opening, per a Reuters report carried by Inkl.

Takeaway Student housing going public alongside used cars and e-commerce this week suggests IPO investors are underwriting almost any business with recurring, inflation-linked India consumer demand right now, not just tech growth stories.

Pune fintech Definedge raises ₹22 crore in a pre-Series A round

Pune-based trading and wealth-tech platform Definedge Securities Broking raised ₹22 crore in an all-equity pre-Series A round, taking its total capital raised to ₹30 crore, per Indian Startup News and Punekar News. Existing investors Nitin Agarwal and D. Prasad were joined by new investors Anant Jain and Sachin Kasera. The company said the funds will go toward margin funding, marketing and AI-driven product development, and it is targeting ₹5,000 crore in assets under management on its “Momentify” platform within 24 months.

Takeaway Rounds this size are the unglamorous plumbing of India’s retail-trading boom — nobody profiles them, but they’re what Zerodha’s and Groww’s customers actually click through.

Women’s fintech Lxme partners with Shiprocket to push UPI into D2C checkout

Financial platform Lxme announced a partnership with Shiprocket to extend Lxme UPI across India’s direct-to-consumer commerce ecosystem, per Business News This Week. Blackstone’s Jon Gray calls India the firm’s best-returning private equity market

Blackstone President Jon Gray said India has become the firm’s best-returning private equity market anywhere in the world, in comments to Bloomberg Television’s Wall Street Week carried by The Star. Gray said Blackstone’s slow, control-stake strategy — focused on IT services, commercial real estate and domestic manufacturing — took years to pay off after a rocky start around the financial crisis, and that India is “getting closer and closer to that tipping point” for foreign capital. India’s GDP has grown to $3.69 trillion, now the world’s fourth-largest economy, with FY26 growth at 7.6%.

Takeaway When a firm that built its India bet through control deals in unglamorous sectors calls it their best market worldwide, that’s a vote for boring compounding over story-stock excitement.

Priority Technology’s own CEO takes the payments firm private in a $1.6 billion deal

Priority Technology Holdings agreed on September 21 to be taken private by an investor group led by its own Chairman and CEO, Thomas Priore, in an all-cash deal valued at approximately $1.6 billion, per InsideArbitrage. The investor group, financed in part through equity commitments from Searchlight Capital Partners, will pay $8.05 per share — a 38% premium to the stock’s September 18 closing price and a 65% premium to its price before the buyout proposal first became public in November 2025. A special committee of independent directors unanimously recommended the deal, which is expected to close in the first half of 2027.

Takeaway A CEO buying out his own public company at a 38% premium is a fairly clear signal the market was undervaluing the business more than the board was willing to say out loud.

Sensex and Nifty snap a four-day winning streak as IT and financials drag

Sensex and Nifty ended lower on Tuesday, snapping a four-day winning run, dragged by IT, financials and capital-goods stocks even as crude oil prices eased and global cues stayed broadly positive, per Business Standard. Media, realty and metal stocks bucked the trend and advanced. Foreign institutional investors were net sellers of roughly ₹3,810 crore in the cash segment while domestic institutional investors bought a net ₹4,120 crore, cushioning the fall.

Takeaway Domestic institutional buying is once again absorbing foreign selling, keeping the index-level damage shallow despite sector-specific weakness.

Nasdaq closes at a fresh record as the AI trade offsets a financials-led Dow slide

The Nasdaq Composite closed at a record 27,244.28 on Tuesday, extending its AI-led rally, while the S&P 500 finished essentially flat and the Dow fell 185 points as financial stocks alone knocked roughly 286 points off the index, per Yahoo Finance. The move came even as oil’s slide on US-Iran diplomatic signals helped broader sentiment and eased bond yields.

Takeaway US equity strength remains narrowly concentrated in AI and tech names even as broader cyclical and financial stocks lag behind.

Crude slides toward $98, the rupee extends its rally, Bitcoin jumps near $86,600

Brent crude fell to roughly $98.47 a barrel, down 1.87%, as Saudi export recovery and easing US-Iran tensions calmed supply fears, per Business Standard. Gold slipped below $4,310 an ounce on hawkish Fed commentary that strengthened the dollar, per USAGold. Tata Group stocks stay under pressure as the Tata Sons-Tata Trusts rift drags on

Tata Group stocks remain under pressure amid the escalating dispute between Tata Sons and its 66%-owner Tata Trusts over a potential Tata Sons listing and N. Chandrasekaran’s fresh five-year term as chairman, per Business Standard. Tata Group firms shed roughly $4 billion in combined market value in a single session as the standoff first surfaced, and the fight has since drawn in the RBI — which has reportedly rejected Tata Sons’ bid to deregister as an upper-layer NBFC and reiterated its listing requirement — while senior advocate Abhishek Manu Singhvi has been engaged to represent Tata Trusts.

Takeaway A regulatory-driven listing requirement is colliding with a shareholder-control fight, and until it’s resolved, Tata Group stocks look set to trade with an added governance-risk discount.

NSE’s grey-market premium collapses ahead of Thursday’s blockbuster listing

NSE’s ₹22,561.57 crore IPO — subscribed nearly six times when it closed — had its allotment finalized on September 22, with demat credit expected September 23 and a BSE listing tentatively set for Thursday, September 24. Its grey-market premium has collapsed from a peak of ₹310 to roughly ₹55-65, implying only a modest 3% premium over the ₹1,785 upper price band, per InvestingCube.

Takeaway A sharply deflated grey-market premium heading into listing day suggests the market now expects a muted debut rather than the blockbuster listing pop once priced in.
