Before the news

There’s a Greek idea called meraki — doing something with soul, creativity or love, leaving a piece of yourself in the work rather than just going through the motions. AI & Tech

Amazon's Q3 2026 revenue and profit both miss Wall Street estimates even as AWS grows 39% and operating income hits a record margin, sending its stock lower for a second straight session, as Intel beats its own guidance on “extraordinary” Xeon demand, OpenAI closes in on a $30 billion round at a $1.25 trillion valuation, Apple's foldable iPhone Duo expands to 28 more countries today, Super Micro's preliminary results blow past its own guidance on AI server demand, and Samsung tracks toward retaking the global smartphone lead from Apple in 2026

Takeaway Today’s AI story is the market getting pickier about spending versus proof: Amazon and Intel both posted genuine growth, but only the one that cleanly beat its own numbers was rewarded — scale alone isn’t settling the AI-capex argument anymore.

Amazon's stock falls for a second day as Q3 revenue and profit miss despite AWS growing 39%

Amazon reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of $201.9 billion, up 12% year-over-year, and diluted EPS of $1.89, both missing Wall Street's roughly $202.3 billion and $1.96–$1.97 estimates, according to the Motley Fool's October 29 market-close report. AWS revenue grew 39% to about $46 billion and operating income reached a record $28.1 billion — Amazon's highest-ever operating margin — but the stock, which had already closed down 4.25% at $243.79 in Thursday's regular session on capex jitters, fell further after hours as Amazon held its 2026 AI infrastructure spending plan at roughly $220 billion.

Takeaway Beating on AWS growth and operating income wasn’t enough once investors zeroed in on the size of the capex bill behind it — the same pattern that sank Alphabet two sessions earlier.

Intel beats its own Q3 guidance as its CEO cites “extraordinary demand” for Xeon chips

Intel reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of $16.5 billion, up 21% year-over-year, with non-GAAP EPS of $0.52 against a roughly $0.40 estimate heading into the October 29 report, according to the company's SEC filing. The release said revenue, gross margin and earnings per share all exceeded guidance, and CEO Lip-Bu Tan described extraordinary demand for the Xeon family, adding that supply is now the priority. Intel guided fourth-quarter revenue to $15.0–$16.0 billion with non-GAAP EPS of $0.25.

Takeaway A chipmaker beating its own guidance on data-center demand, in the same week Amazon and Alphabet got punished for spending on exactly that demand, shows investors drawing a line between supplying the AI boom and bankrolling it.

SEC filing →

OpenAI closes in on a $30 billion round that would value it at $1.25 trillion

OpenAI is nearing a funding round of up to $30 billion led by investors from the United Arab Emirates and Coatue, at a pre-money valuation of about $1.25 trillion, CNBC reported on October 22; the round had not been confirmed closed as of this week. A separate Bloomberg report put the target valuation closer to $1.4 trillion, a gap that may come down to pre- versus post-money framing.

Takeaway A round this size still being described in “nears” and “reportedly” more than a week after it first surfaced says as much about how guarded OpenAI is being as it does about the price tag itself.

Apple's foldable iPhone Duo expands to 28 more countries today

Apple's first foldable iPhone, the iPhone Duo, launched in more than 70 countries and regions on October 23 and reaches 28 additional countries and regions today, October 30, according to MacTrast's rollout coverage. Apple has said walk-in stock at its stores will be limited on launch days, and some markets' online delivery windows are already stretching into early 2027.

Takeaway Staggering one device's rollout across a full week, rather than launching everywhere at once, is Apple managing a supply constraint in public instead of hiding one.

Super Micro's preliminary results blow past its own guidance on AI server demand

Super Micro said preliminary revenue for its fiscal first quarter of 2027 (ended September 30) will come in between $18.5 billion and $19.5 billion, far above the $14.5–$15.5 billion it had guided, with gross margin now estimated at 12.0%–12.5% versus 10.4%–10.8% guided, according to an October 20 disclosure covered by LetsDataScience. The company credited AI platform demand for the gap and will hold its full earnings call on November 3; the figures remain unaudited and subject to revision.

Takeaway Pre-announcing numbers this far above its own guidance, three weeks before the actual earnings call, is Super Micro trying to get ahead of a market that's turned skeptical of AI-spending stories by backing this one with hard numbers early.

Samsung tracks toward retaking the global smartphone lead from Apple in 2026

Counterpoint Research forecasts Samsung's 2026 smartphone shipments rising about 0.8% while Apple's fall 2.1%, which would hand Samsung back the global shipment lead, according to the Korea JoongAng Daily. The same coverage notes a separate outlook suggesting Apple could retake the top spot again in 2027.

Takeaway A one-year swing over fractions of a percent in a two-horse race shows how close to a coin flip the smartphone market has become at the very top.

Product & Business

Reliance heads into today's Q2 FY27 results and a debenture-fundraising decision as Caterpillar and Mastercard both beat Wall Street's Q3 estimates, even as JSW Steel's profit surges on record production, Ather Energy posts its first quarterly profit, Vedanta Iron and Steel narrows its loss, and ExxonMobil heads into its own Q3 release today already flagging a one-time hit to its headline earnings

Takeaway Today’s business story is genuine operating strength turning up across very different companies — from Caterpillar’s record backlog to Ather’s first profit — even as the day’s two biggest results, Reliance’s and Exxon’s, are still to come.

Reliance heads into today's Q2 FY27 results and a debenture-fundraising decision

Reliance Industries' board meets today, October 30, to consider its September-quarter and first-half FY27 results alongside a possible fundraise through non-convertible debentures, according to a Kotak Neo preview. The results follow a week in which Jio Platforms' stock-market debut lifted Reliance's group value past the Tata Group's to become India's most valuable business house.

Takeaway Weighing a debenture raise in the same board meeting as results, right after Jio's listing lifted the group's standing, suggests Reliance wants fresh financing options on the table while its valuation story is working in its favour.

Caterpillar's Q3 profit surges on a record order backlog

Caterpillar reported third-quarter 2026 sales and revenues of $20.7 billion, up from $17.6 billion a year earlier, with profit per share of $7.13 ($7.20 adjusted) against $4.88 a year ago, according to the company's SEC filing. CEO Joe Creed credited strong order rates and a record backlog, and the company returned $2.0 billion of cash to shareholders through buybacks and dividends during the quarter.

Takeaway A record backlog, not a one-off order, behind this jump is Caterpillar's strongest signal yet that the industrial side of this year's spending boom has real staying power.

SEC filing →

Mastercard's Q3 profit climbs on revenue as consumer spending stays resilient

Mastercard reported third-quarter 2026 net income of $4.6 billion and diluted EPS of $5.28 ($5.31 adjusted), on net revenue of $9.8 billion, up 14%, with gross dollar volume up 7% in local currency, according to the company's October 29 SEC filing. CEO Michael Miebach called it "another strong quarter."

Takeaway Double-digit revenue growth holding steady through a week when Big Tech's spending plans spooked markets is a reminder that consumer spending itself hasn't flinched yet.

SEC filing →

JSW Steel's profit surges on record steel production

JSW Steel reported Q2 FY27 consolidated net profit of about ₹5,359 crore, up sharply from ₹1,646 crore a year earlier, as revenue rose to roughly ₹54,486 crore from ₹45,152 crore, on crude steel production of 7.27 million tonnes — the company's highest-ever quarterly output, according to Kotak Neo's October 23 report. Higher coking coal and other input costs continued to press on margins at its Indian operations even as volumes hit the record.

Takeaway Record output delivering a record profit while input costs keep rising underneath it shows JSW growing past a cost problem rather than solving it.

Kotak Neo →

Ather Energy posts its first quarterly profit

Ather Energy reported a Q2 FY27 profit of about ₹1.26 crore, compared with a loss of roughly ₹154 crore a year earlier, as revenue rose about 63% to roughly ₹1,527 crore and volumes climbed 42% to 93,211 units, according to Business Today's October 26 report. Its Hosur plant ran at 98% peak capacity utilisation, shares rose as much as 15% on the news, and the company plans to add about 2.25 lakh units of annual capacity.

Takeaway One profitable quarter after years of losses is a real milestone for Ather, but a single quarter — however welcome — is not yet proof the business can stay profitable through a slower one.

Vedanta Iron and Steel narrows its loss as operations improve

Vedanta Iron and Steel's consolidated loss after tax narrowed to about ₹324 crore in Q2 FY27 from ₹477 crore a year earlier, with EBITDA up 56% as steel operations improved, even as quarterly revenue of about ₹2,788 crore slipped roughly 2.4% year-over-year, according to Kotak Neo's October 27 report.

Takeaway A shrinking loss built on efficiency gains rather than higher revenue shows this business still finding its footing, not yet growing out of its problems.

Kotak Neo →

ExxonMobil heads into today's Q3 report already flagging a one-time earnings hit

ExxonMobil is scheduled to release third-quarter 2026 results today, October 30, with an October 16 regulatory filing flagging an estimated $2.5 billion negative timing effect on GAAP earnings for the quarter — a measure the company said would not affect its non-GAAP adjusted earnings, according to its own investor-relations announcement. The report follows a strong second quarter, when Exxon posted $14.5 billion in GAAP earnings.

Takeaway Disclosing a one-time hit to the headline number two weeks before reporting it is Exxon trying to make sure the real story — the adjusted figure — isn't lost in a confusing GAAP number on the day.

Sensex and Nifty rebound sharply, snapping a losing run

The Sensex closed up 905.01 points (1.24%) at 74,151.04 and the Nifty 50 gained 237.90 points (1.05%) to settle at 22,936.75 on Thursday, October 29, after opening lower and reversing through the session, according to Business Standard's market-close report. Realty led the sectoral rally; the gains came despite elevated crude prices and lingering geopolitical worries, with the Federal Reserve's rate hold and a run of upbeat earnings cited as the main support.

Takeaway Opening lower and closing sharply higher on the same day shows this was a session where sentiment genuinely turned, not just a market drifting up on calm.

Business Standard →

Alphabet's stock posts its biggest one-day dollar loss ever as Wall Street's tech selloff deepens

The Nasdaq Composite fell about 1.4%, the S&P 500 slipped to roughly 7,720, and the Dow eased to around 52,340 on Thursday, October 29, as Alphabet's stock closed down 12.64% — wiping out close to $500 billion in market value in a single session, the largest one-day dollar loss by any company in history, according to Yahoo Finance's market-close coverage. The slide came two sessions after Alphabet's own earnings miss and extended into Thursday as investors kept reassessing how much cash Big Tech is burning on AI infrastructure.

Takeaway Wiping out this much value in one session, days after the earnings that triggered it, shows the market is still repricing Alphabet downward rather than having already found its new level.

Yahoo Finance →

Amazon's shares extend their slide after hours following Thursday's Q3 miss

Amazon's stock, which had already closed down 4.25% at $243.79 in Thursday's regular session on capex concerns ahead of its report, fell further after its earnings release — estimates of the after-hours drop ranged from about 6% to as much as 9% across different trackers, according to Investing.com's coverage of the October 29 report. The declines came even though AWS revenue grew 39% in the quarter, underlining how closely investors are now watching Amazon's roughly $220 billion 2026 AI capital-spending plan.

Takeaway A stock falling before earnings on capex fears, then falling again after earnings confirm the capex, shows the market had already made up its mind about what it didn't want to hear.

India's markets open today with Reliance Industries' board set to consider its September-quarter results and a debenture fundraise, while US markets await ExxonMobil's third-quarter report after a week dominated by hyperscaler earnings reactions. Both are among the last major results of a week that has moved sharply on earnings news in both directions.

Takeaway Closing out an earnings-heavy week with two more big reports due is a reminder that today's calm open can still turn on a single number this afternoon or tonight.
