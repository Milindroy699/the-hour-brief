Before the news

There’s a Japanese word, ganbaru — to push through with steady, unglamorous effort rather than wait for a single breakthrough, doing one’s best even when the outcome isn’t guaranteed. It shows up today in a manufacturing-platform IPO grinding through a soft subscription book right to its final bidding day, in a record anchor book being worked through one bid at a time ahead of India’s largest-ever listing, and in regulators now asking two AI labs to document, page by page, that their safety promises hold up under pressure.

FTC fires 50-page subpoenas at OpenAI and Anthropic over AI safety incidents

The FTC sent civil investigative demands — each reportedly running more than 50 pages — to OpenAI and Anthropic on October 14, seeking records on security incidents, safeguards, marketing and privacy practices, according to Axios, which multiple outlets including Engadget and Gizmodo subsequently confirmed. The demands reportedly reference July’s rogue-agent breach of Hugging Face servers among the incidents under review; OpenAI told Axios it would “engage constructively with the FTC.”

Takeaway Naming specific incidents rather than a general safety concern suggests the FTC is building a consumer-harm case, not just fishing.

Anthropic tells investors Q3 revenue jumped 45% to $16.8 billion as its IPO target reportedly cools

At an October 14 San Francisco investor meeting, Anthropic told prospective investors its Q3 2026 revenue reached $16.8 billion, up 45% from Q2’s $11.6 billion, and that the company was profitable on an adjusted basis, according to multiple outlets including Devdiscourse and the Khaleej Times. Most reports still cite Anthropic’s IPO target near $2 trillion, though one Bloomberg-attributed report said some investors are now discussing a lower figure closer to $1.5 trillion for a planned November share sale; neither the revenue figures nor the valuation talk have been independently audited or confirmed by the company.

Takeaway A 45% jump sounds huge until you note Q2’s own growth was far steeper — deceleration, not just scale, is what investors are now pricing in.

TypeSafe AI raises $870 million at a $7.5 billion valuation, weeks after its seed round

TypeSafe AI, maker of an AI model called Jev that returns typed values directly into application code rather than chat responses, closed an $870 million Series A at a $7.5 billion valuation, according to Pulse2 and CryptoBriefing. Andreessen Horowitz led the round, with Sequoia Capital and existing investor DCVC also participating and a16z general partner Martin Casado joining TypeSafe’s board — a jump from the roughly $200 million valuation set by the company’s seed round just weeks earlier.

Takeaway Going from a $200 million seed to a $7.5 billion Series A within weeks says more about how little VCs are willing to wait to find out than about anything Jev has proven yet.

Oratomic raises $475 million in the largest-ever US quantum-computing Series B

Pasadena-based neutral-atom quantum computing startup Oratomic closed a $475 million Series B on October 8, lifting its valuation to $5.4 billion just three months after a Series A priced it at $1.5 billion, according to Dealroom and Wilson Sonsini’s deal announcement. ARCH Venture Partners, Spark Capital, Khosla Ventures, Index Ventures, General Catalyst and Bezos Expeditions co-led the round, which the company says is the largest Series B ever raised in US quantum technology; CEO Dolev Bluvstein says a useful machine needs only 10,000 qubits and expects it ready by 2030, a timeline analysts have called aggressive.

Takeaway Tripling a quantum-computing startup’s valuation in three months with no hardware yet shipped says more about how much capital is chasing the next frontier bet than about anything Oratomic has actually built.

Attackers chain three Ivanti zero-days to breach fully patched VPN appliances

Volexity disclosed that an actor it tracks as UTA0576 has been exploiting a zero-day authentication-bypass chain in Ivanti Connect Secure VPN appliances since at least October 1, chaining three vulnerabilities to gain unauthenticated, root-level code execution on fully patched devices, according to The Hacker News and SecurityWeek. CISA added the lead flaw, CVE-2026-104935, to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog and ordered federal agencies to patch by October 16; the attackers have been going after Active Directory credentials, including LAPS passwords and BitLocker recovery keys.

Takeaway A zero-day chain that still breaks into a “fully patched” device is the clearest reminder that patching alone can’t keep up with attackers who already have the next exploit queued up.

Microsoft’s October Patch Tuesday fixes a record 1,411 flaws, including an active zero-day

Microsoft’s October 13 Patch Tuesday addressed 1,411 vulnerabilities — a record by BleepingComputer’s count — across Windows, Office, Exchange, SharePoint and Azure, including three zero-days. One, CVE-2026-96951, a Windows Shell command-injection flaw that bypasses Mark-of-the-Web protections, is confirmed under active exploitation; seventy of the fixes are rated Critical, and the release also marks the end of support for Windows 11 24H2 Home/Pro and Windows 10 Enterprise LTSB 2016.

Takeaway A record patch count landing the same month two systems run out of support is exactly the gap attackers are counting on users not to notice.

Tata Trusts’ deadline to answer governance complaints lands today

Maharashtra’s Charity Commissioner extended Tata Trusts’ deadline to formally respond to governance complaints filed by trustees Venu Srinivasan and Vijay Singh to today, October 19, according to Free Press Journal, citing NDTV Profit. Srinivasan and Singh have questioned Noel Tata’s position as a perpetual trustee and the appointment of his son Neville as a trustee, while trusts affiliated with the group have separately filed 84 caveats before the Commissioner to protect their right to be heard in the dispute.

Takeaway Filing 84 caveats just to preserve a seat at the table shows how many different Tata-affiliated parties now expect this fight to be decided by a regulator, not a boardroom.

Jio Platforms scales back its IPO valuation target to about $108 billion

Reliance’s Jio Platforms is seeking a valuation of up to ₹10.3 lakh crore (roughly $108 billion) in its IPO at a price band of ₹1,065-1,119 a share, according to Bloomberg-sourced reporting carried by Kotak Neo — well below the roughly $170 billion some earlier reports had floated for India’s largest-ever listing. The fresh-issue-only offer still aims to raise about ₹36,000 crore ($3.8 billion), with anchor bidding opening today and a listing targeted for around October 28.

Takeaway Trimming the valuation target by a third from earlier chatter, even as anchor demand already tops $20 billion, is Reliance pricing this IPO to be oversubscribed rather than merely sold.

Clueso raises $10 million to automate corporate video with AI agents

Bengaluru-based Clueso, founded by IIT Madras alumni, raised a $10 million Series A for its agentic video platform, which learns a company’s products and brand to create and maintain corporate video automatically, according to Inc42 and TheNextWeb. Dharana Capital led the round, with continuing backing from Y Combinator and f7 Ventures and new investors Founders Future and Neotribe Ventures; more than 80% of the new capital came from existing seed-stage backers.

Takeaway Having its own seed investors supply most of a Series A is Clueso’s quiet way of saying its earliest backers like what they’re seeing enough to double down rather than wait for a new lead.

Amazon’s Great Indian Festival opens to its highest-ever visit count

Amazon’s Great Indian Festival logged more than 440 million visits in its opening phase this festive season — the platform’s highest volume ever — with products ordered up 100% year-on-year and daily app downloads up roughly fivefold, according to YourStory. The surge lands in the same week as Flipkart’s own Big Billion Days, which separately reported 6.3 million concurrent users and 34% growth in new customers at its opening.

Takeaway Both platforms posting record openings in the same week says festive demand itself is growing, not just that shoppers are choosing one app over the other.

Zetwerk’s ₹5,012 crore IPO closes today after a soft, sub-1x book

Manufacturing platform Zetwerk’s ₹5,012 crore IPO — priced at ₹143-151 a share — closes for public bidding today after a tepid start: the issue was subscribed just 0.23 times on Day 1 and 0.31 times by Day 2, with retail and QIB portions each near 0.37 times, according to Inc42’s subscription tracker. Allotment is expected October 21 ahead of a tentative October 23 listing.

Takeaway A sub-1x book through two of three bidding days on a ₹5,000-crore-plus issue is the market testing how much appetite is left after a run of recent IPOs.

Infosys heads toward Friday’s Q2 results and an interim-dividend call

Infosys is set to report its Q2 FY27 results on October 23, with its board also considering an interim dividend at the same meeting, according to Upstox and Kotak Neo’s earnings-season previews. The results land in the final stretch of a Q2 season that has already brought HCLTech’s record quarter and Wipro’s and Tech Mahindra’s results, among more than 70 companies reporting this cycle.

Kroger moves to cut 3,000 corporate jobs over three years as it leans into AI

Kroger told employees on October 12 that it will cut about 3,000 corporate positions over the next three years — trimming its corporate headcount from roughly 8,200 to about 5,200 — as part of a push to adopt AI and reduce duplicative work, according to the Associated Press and the Atlanta Journal-Constitution. CEO Greg Foran’s memo said store and distribution-center jobs are not affected, and the cuts follow an earnings call a day later that flagged further cost-cutting and streamlining.

Takeaway Cutting corporate headcount by more than a third while leaving stores untouched is Kroger betting AI can replace office work faster than it can replace a checkout line.

Enterprise-AI startup RapidCanvas raises $29 million to expand its Fortune 100 sales push

RapidCanvas, which pairs an AI agent platform with field engineers to build and run custom enterprise AI apps, raised a $29 million Series B led by Vertex Ventures SEA & India with Hitachi Ventures joining, according to Inc42 and TheNextWeb. The round — which also drew existing backers Accel and Peak XV Partners — lifts the Austin-based company’s total funding to $52.5 million and comes as it says annual recurring revenue grew eightfold over six quarters.

Takeaway An eightfold revenue jump in six quarters, not a flashier demo, is what got Vertex Ventures and Hitachi to write this check — enterprise AI’s venture pitch is shifting back toward proof over promise.

Wall Street’s chip-led selloff carries its longest streak since 2008 into the new week

Wall Street heads into the new week still digesting an eighth straight session of chip-stock declines through Friday, October 16 — the sector’s longest losing streak since the 2008 financial crisis, according to Trading Economics. The S&P 500 closed Friday at 7,659.44 (-1.1%) and the Nasdaq Composite at 26,140.03 (-2.1%), both posting their worst week since June, with Micron down 5.6% and AMD down 5.9% that session.

Takeaway An eight-session streak that’s still intact heading into Monday shows Wall Street hasn’t found a reason yet to stop pricing in doubt about the AI buildout’s pace.

FIIs’ rare Friday buying meets a record week of fresh IPO supply

Foreign institutional investors turned net buyers of about ₹1,165.92 crore in Indian cash equities on October 16 — a reversal after weeks of selling — while domestic institutional investors turned net sellers of about ₹3,661.28 crore the same day, according to Trendlyne’s daily tracker. Takeaway One day of FIIs buying while DIIs sell doesn’t undo weeks of the opposite pattern, but it’s an odd moment for foreign money to show up just as the market has to absorb its biggest-ever IPO.

Trading concept for today, via Zerodha Varsity: A moving average smooths out a stock’s daily noise by averaging its closing price over a fixed window — 50 days, 100 days, 200 days — so a trader can read the underlying trend instead of each day’s jump. When price trades above its moving average, buyers are paying more than the recent average suggests they should, a sign of an uptrend; when it’s below, the opposite. Many traders combine a short-term and a long-term average and watch for a “crossover” — when the shorter one crosses above the longer one — as a signal to enter a trade, though the approach works worse in sideways, range-bound markets.
