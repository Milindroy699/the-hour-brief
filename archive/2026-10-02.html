AI & Tech

Anthropic targets a November IPO at close to a $2 trillion valuation as Broadcom offers to lend it up to $42 billion to lease its own chips, even as OpenAI seeks $30 billion in bridge financing at a $1.4 trillion valuation to put off going public, Google ships its most powerful model yet in Gemini 4 Argon, AMD pays $8.2 billion for Fei-Fei Li’s World Labs to push into physical AI, and a New York Times investigation finds Meta’s “experimental” AI data centers have cut its tax bill by $3.9 billion

Takeaway Anthropic borrowing against its own IPO, OpenAI raising bridge capital instead of going public, and Meta gaming tax law all point the same way — the industry’s capital needs have outgrown ordinary financing, even as the model race keeps accelerating underneath it.

Anthropic targets a November IPO at a potential $2 trillion valuation

Anthropic is pushing to go public as soon as mid-November, with formal IPO marketing potentially starting the week of November 9 and a listing targeted on the Nasdaq, per Bloomberg. An offering could value the company at roughly $2 trillion and raise as much as $100 billion, which would top SpaceX’s record debut from earlier this year; Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan and Citigroup are serving as underwriters.

Takeaway A $2 trillion target for a company that didn’t exist a decade ago says less about Anthropic’s revenue today than about how much investors are willing to pay for a seat at the frontier.

Broadcom offers to lend Anthropic up to $42 billion to lease its own chips

Broadcom has offered to lend Anthropic up to $42 billion to finance its infrastructure buildout, disclosed in Anthropic’s IPO prospectus and reported by CNBC and Reuters. The convertible notes would cover roughly a third of the $125.2 billion Anthropic has committed to a five-year deal for Broadcom-supplied TPU compute, and Anthropic is set to become Broadcom’s largest customer in its core chip-design business next year; the prospectus itself flags Broadcom’s dual role as supplier and financier as a “potential conflict of interest.”

Takeaway Broadcom financing the chips Anthropic then leases back from Broadcom is circular even by this industry’s standards, and Anthropic’s own prospectus is the one flagging it.

CNBC →

OpenAI seeks $30 billion in bridge financing at a $1.4 trillion valuation, putting off an IPO

OpenAI is in early talks to raise at least $30 billion at roughly a $1.4 trillion valuation, up about 64% from the $852 billion mark set in its March 2026 round, per Bloomberg. The round would function as bridge financing rather than a public offering, letting OpenAI stay private for now; CEO Sam Altman has called an IPO this year “ill-advised” while the company continues its safety work, and terms could still change.

Takeaway Raising $30 billion specifically to avoid an IPO shows OpenAI can get public-market money without public-market disclosure — for now.

Google releases Gemini 4 Argon, calling it its most powerful model yet

Google released Gemini 4 Argon on September 30, built for sustained reasoning on tasks like real-world software engineering, legal and financial work, and cybersecurity defense, per TechCrunch and Google’s own blog. The model raises Google’s output token limit to one million, up from 64,000, giving it more room to reason in a single pass; it’s rolling out first to trusted cyber defenders through Google’s Fairwind Program, with introductory pricing of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, and no broad public release date yet.

Takeaway Gating a flagship model behind a cyber-defense program before a public launch is Google hedging against the exact kind of misuse rivals have been getting caught on this year.

TechCrunch →

AMD pays $8.2 billion for Fei-Fei Li’s World Labs; she becomes chief scientist

AMD agreed on September 28 to acquire spatial-intelligence startup World Labs for about $8.2 billion in an all-stock deal, per Tom’s Hardware and Yahoo Finance. World Labs builds models that generate and simulate interactive 3D environments from text, image and video, aimed at robotics learning and physical AI; cofounder and CEO Fei-Fei Li, the ImageNet pioneer, will become AMD’s executive vice president and chief scientist reporting to CEO Lisa Su, in what ranks as AMD’s second-biggest acquisition ever after Xilinx.

Takeaway AMD buying a spatial-AI lab outright, rather than just licensing its models, is a bet that the next computing cycle is about chips understanding physical space, not just language.

Tom’s Hardware →

Meta’s “experimental” AI data centers have cut its tax bill by $3.9 billion, NYT finds

A New York Times investigation found Meta classifies its AI data centers as “pilot models” — experimental facilities that could fail — to qualify for a federal research tax credit, per coverage picked up by Yahoo Finance and Benzinga. The savings climbed from $700 million in 2023 to $2 billion in 2024 and $3.9 billion in 2025, making Meta the credit’s largest beneficiary among public companies; Meta’s own securities filings warn the IRS could claw the savings back, and one tax specialist told the Times the framing is “kind of wild and out there.”

Takeaway Calling multibillion-dollar data centers an experiment to get a tax break, then telling investors the IRS might disagree, is Meta pricing in the risk of its own argument failing.

Moneyview lists at a roughly 62% premium on its NSE and BSE debut as Accenture’s earnings beat sends its stock up 22% in its best day ever, even as Nike warns of fresh layoffs inside a new restructuring plan that also expands its India technology hub, Seeds Fincap raises ₹100 crore from the Michael & Susan Dell Foundation, Bessemer Venture Partners adds a new India partner, a Jaipur healthtech startup’s ₹4 crore seed round aims at 100,000 autism screenings, and Reliance keeps Jio Platforms’ record IPO on track for a Navratri-to-Diwali listing window

Takeaway A strong IPO debut and a record earnings day sit next to a fresh layoffs warning from the same global company — growth and cost-cutting are showing up in the same breath across both Indian and global business today.

Moneyview lists at a roughly 62% premium on its NSE and BSE debut

Moneyview shares debuted at ₹55 on the NSE — a 61.76% premium over its ₹34 issue price — and at ₹55.61 on the BSE, a 63.56% premium, per Angel One and 5paisa. The stock extended its gains through the session, at one point trading as much as 82% above the issue price, after the ₹1,091.68 crore IPO closed subscribed roughly 102 times overall.

Takeaway A near-102x book translating into an 82% intraday pop shows just how much demand was still left on the table at a ₹34 issue price.

5paisa →

Accenture’s earnings beat sends its stock up 22% — its best day ever

Accenture reported fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of $18.68 billion, up 6% in dollar terms and above its own guided range of $17.75-18.40 billion and analyst estimates of $18.03 billion, with earnings of $3.29 a share beating the $3.18 consensus, per CNBC. Bookings came in at $22.17 billion with a 1.2 book-to-bill ratio, and full-year revenue reached $74.18 billion; shares jumped more than 22% on the results, their best single-day performance on record.

Takeaway A 22% single-day move on a consulting stock is Wall Street deciding Accenture’s AI-services pivot is actually translating into bookings, not just slide-deck language.

CNBC →

Nike warns of 2027 layoffs inside a new restructuring plan that also builds up its India tech hub

Nike reported fiscal first-quarter revenue of $11.2 billion, down 4% on a reported basis, with earnings of $0.48 a share beating the $0.44 estimate, per CNBC and Nike’s own filings. The company launched “Pace,” a multi-year operating-model overhaul targeting $2.5 billion in cumulative savings through fiscal 2031 against roughly $1 billion in pre-tax charges; CEO Elliott Hill warned the plan “will result in fewer roles across Nike” starting in calendar 2027, while the restructuring also builds out the Nike India Technology Center as one of just two strategic tech hubs alongside its Oregon campus.

Takeaway Nike cutting roles globally while naming its India center one of only two strategic tech hubs shows where it thinks the next phase of its cost structure actually gets built.

Seeds Fincap raises ₹100 crore Series B led by the Michael & Susan Dell Foundation

Gurugram-based NBFC Seeds Fincap raised more than ₹100 crore ($10.4 million) in a Series B round led by the Michael & Susan Dell Foundation, with existing backers Z47 and Lok Capital joined by new investors Norinchukin Capital and Alteria Capital, per Inc42 and Entrackr. The MSME lender disbursed around ₹620 crore in FY26 and had an AUM of ₹722 crore as of August, and is targeting ₹1,000 crore in AUM by March 2027.

Takeaway A philanthropic foundation leading a commercial Series B, alongside existing VC backers, is an unusual capital mix for an NBFC chasing India’s underserved MSME credit gap.

Bessemer Venture Partners adds Mayank Khanduja as a partner on its India team

Mayank Khanduja has joined Bessemer Venture Partners as a partner focused on early-stage investing in India, per YourStory’s daily startup roundup. Khanduja spent 15 years at Elevation Capital before stepping down from the firm in February 2026, and will focus on backing founders from their first institutional round onward.

Takeaway A 15-year Elevation veteran switching firms rather than starting his own fund is a vote that India’s early-stage game still rewards an established platform over going solo.

Jaipur healthtech Aignosis raises ₹4 crore seed, targets 100,000 autism screenings

Aignosis, a Jaipur-based healthtech startup building AI-assisted autism screening technology, raised ₹4 crore in a seed round led by Antler and Nikhil Kamath, with angel participation from Varun Dua and IIMA Ventures, per YourStory. The company plans to move from validating its screening technology to expanding across India’s healthcare system, aiming for at least 100,000 total screenings by the end of FY26-27.

Takeaway A ₹4 crore seed round is small money, but a 100,000-screening target is the kind of scale ambition that only makes sense if AI genuinely cuts the cost of each screening.

Reliance keeps Jio Platforms’ record IPO on track for a Navratri-to-Diwali window

Reliance is still targeting the Navratri-to-Diwali 2026 period to launch Jio Platforms’ IPO, India’s largest-ever, structured as a 100% fresh issue of roughly ₹37,700 crore with no existing shareholders selling down, per multiple IPO-tracking desks including Chittorgarh and Rupeezy. SEBI approved the offer documents on August 28, but the RHP — which would fix the price band, lot size and bidding dates — has yet to be filed.

Takeaway Every rupee of this issue is new money into the business rather than an exit for existing holders, which is unusual for a listing this size and says Reliance is funding growth, not cashing out.

Nifty and Sensex confirm an eighth straight weekly decline, their longest losing streak since 2001

The Nifty 50 closed at 22,421.95 on Thursday, October 1, down 198.50 points (0.88%), and the Sensex closed at 71,909.70, down 570.59 points (0.79%), confirming an eighth consecutive weekly decline — the longest such streak in 25 years, since a nine-week slide in 2001, per widely syndicated market reports. The Nifty lost 3.1% and the Sensex 2.7% this week alone, their steepest weekly declines in more than six and four months respectively, taking the cumulative eight-week fall to 8.7% and 8.4%; market breadth stayed firmly negative, with 2,916 stocks declining against 1,306 advancing, and Auto, Media, FMCG, Metal and Realty each fell 2-3%.

Takeaway A losing streak this long hasn’t happened in 25 years, but at an 8.7% cumulative fall it’s a fraction of 2001’s 27% drop over nine weeks — the record here is about duration, not depth.

Bajaj Auto and Maruti Suzuki slide on a weaker-than-it-looked festive-season sales mix

Nifty Auto fell more than 3% on Thursday as Bajaj Auto shares dropped as much as 9% (closing down 7.62%) and Maruti Suzuki fell 4.86%, per Whalesbook and IndMoney. September headline sales looked solid, but the mix disappointed: Bajaj Auto’s domestic two-wheeler sales fell 12% year-on-year even as total sales grew 5% on export strength, raising concern about demand heading into the festive season that automakers typically count on for a sales spike.

Takeaway A 5% headline sales gain hiding a 12% domestic decline is exactly the kind of number that looks fine until you check what’s actually driving it.

Brent crude jumps past $102 as the US sends a third aircraft carrier to the Middle East

Brent crude jumped 4.4% to close at $102.31 a barrel on Thursday and WTI climbed 2.7% to $92.87, per CNBC and BusinessToday, after the US deployed a third aircraft carrier strike group — the USS Theodore Roosevelt, due in the region by late November — alongside the USS George H.W. Bush and USS George Washington already on station, plus thousands of additional troops, following multiple tanker attacks in the Strait of Hormuz. GIFT Nifty futures were down about 135.50 points early Friday, signaling a negative open for the Nifty 50 amid the escalation.

Takeaway Sending a third carrier strike group, not just talking tough, is the kind of military signal that oil markets treat as a real supply risk rather than posturing.

DIIs keep absorbing FII selling, buying slightly more than foreign investors sold on Thursday

FIIs were net sellers of ₹9,484.20 crore in the cash segment on Thursday, October 1, while domestic institutional investors net-bought ₹10,041.80 crore — DII buying exceeding FII selling by roughly ₹558 crore on the day, per NSE and BSE cash-segment data.

Takeaway DIIs didn’t just offset Thursday’s FII selling, they slightly outbought it — on a day the index still fell, which says the selling pressure is larger than either side’s cash flows alone.

Wall Street edges higher as Treasury yields retreat ahead of Friday’s jobs report

The S&P 500 rose 0.19% to 7,666.45, the Nasdaq Composite added 0.04% to 26,871.60 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average gained 0.04% to 50,926.56 on Thursday, clawing back earlier declines as Treasury yields retreated from their highest levels in more than 20 years, per Yahoo Finance. Initial unemployment claims fell to 197,000 for the week, slightly better than the 200,000 expected, ahead of Friday’s closely watched September jobs report.

Takeaway A flat-to-slightly-up Wall Street session the day before a major jobs print is the market holding its breath, not making a call.

Zerodha Varsity principle for today: Varsity’s module on trading costs introduces impact cost — the gap between a stock’s last traded price and the actual price you get filled at, caused by your own order moving the market. It’s invisible on a brokerage statement but very real: a large order in a thin, low-liquidity stock can move the price against you before the full quantity executes, quietly eating into returns that look fine on paper. The lesson is to size orders to the stock’s actual liquidity, not just to how much you want to buy or sell.
