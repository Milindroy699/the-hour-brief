AI & Tech

Nvidia reportedly discusses guaranteeing billions of Nscale's debt to keep building Anthropic's West Virginia data center as CoreWeave's default-insurance costs hit record highs, even as Microsoft's Azure growth accelerates to 48% and Apple heads into its first earnings call under CEO John Ternus, while OpenAI reverses a ChatGPT Pro usage cut, Google ships a cybersecurity-focused Gemini 4 Carbon and Mistral open-sources a trillion-parameter model built for the same fight

Takeaway Today’s AI story is the financial plumbing starting to show beneath the model headlines — Nvidia underwriting its own customers’ debt and CoreWeave’s rising default risk say as much about this buildout’s health as any new release does.

Nvidia is reportedly in talks to guarantee billions in debt for Nscale's West Virginia buildout for Anthropic

Nvidia is discussing guaranteeing billions of dollars of debt that UK-based cloud provider Nscale would raise to build a second, 460-megawatt phase of its Monarch data-center campus in West Virginia, The Information reported October 29, with Reuters picking up the report the same day. Anthropic agreed in August to pay roughly $45 billion to use the site; nothing has been signed, the guarantee size hasn't been disclosed, and terms could still change. Nvidia is already an investor in Nscale and reportedly guaranteed about $860 million of its Texas lease obligations back in October 2025.

Takeaway Nvidia increasingly underwriting the debt of the cloud providers that buy its own chips blurs the line between customer and balance-sheet risk in the AI buildout.

CoreWeave's cost of insuring against default hits record levels amid AI debt worries

The cost of insuring CoreWeave's debt hit record highs in mid-October, with five-year credit-default-swap spreads implying roughly a 60%-plus probability of default within five years, up from about 30% a year earlier, Reuters reported. An October 27 update found the spread had narrowed somewhat from its peak but remained elevated, with analysts describing CoreWeave as facing expensive credit rather than an imminent liquidity crisis; its total debt load is estimated at $35–51 billion depending on the source.

Takeaway Credit markets, not just stock prices, are now where the first real cracks in AI infrastructure economics would show up.

Microsoft discloses Azure's dollar revenue for the first time as its growth hits 48%

Microsoft's fiscal Q1 2027 results, covering the quarter ended September 30 and reported October 28, showed Azure revenue growth accelerating to 48% from 43% the prior quarter, beating the company's own roughly 45% guidance, with Microsoft disclosing Azure's standalone revenue — about $33.2 billion — for the first time, according to Windows Report. Commercial bookings backlog rose 73% year-over-year to $677 billion, and capex hit $39.4 billion for the quarter, roughly double a year earlier.

Takeaway Breaking out Azure's actual dollar figure right as growth hits a multi-year high looks like a deliberate move to show cloud strength that percentages alone could no longer fully convey.

Apple heads into today's Q4 call as John Ternus's first earnings report as CEO

Apple reports fiscal fourth-quarter 2026 results today, Monday November 2, at 2pm PT/5pm ET — its first earnings call under CEO John Ternus, who took over from Tim Cook on September 1 after an April board-approved transition, according to 9to5Mac. Apple's own guidance points to 9–11% revenue growth and 47–48% gross margin; as of this writing, the call has not yet happened and no results are available.

Takeaway This is Apple's real leadership-transition test — the market's first chance to judge Ternus, not just the quarter's numbers.

OpenAI reverses planned ChatGPT Pro usage cuts after subscriber backlash

OpenAI had planned to shrink included usage allowances for existing ChatGPT Pro 200 subscribers after October 29 while keeping the $200-a-month price, but reversed course after backlash, instead doubling usage limits across its Pro 100, Pro 200, Pro 500 and Business Premium tiers at the same prices, according to Android Headlines. The changes primarily affect ChatGPT Work and Codex; OpenAI has not published exact numerical allowances per tier.

Takeaway OpenAI burning through user goodwill while it searches for a pricing model that covers inference costs without alienating its highest-paying customers shows how unresolved AI's unit economics still are, even at the top of the market.

Google launches Gemini 4 Carbon, a frontier model built for cybersecurity

Google announced Gemini 4 Carbon on October 28, pitching it as its strongest model yet for finding, exploiting in controlled testing, and patching software vulnerabilities, citing self-reported scores of 88.2% on SEC-bench Pro and 54.8% on ExploitGym, according to 9to5Google. Rollout is staggered, with cyber defenders getting access first ahead of developers and consumers, and Google says it's running voluntary pre-release testing with the US government.

Takeaway Marketing a frontier model explicitly on its ability to find and exploit vulnerabilities is a notable shift in how labs are now framing their biggest releases.

9to5Google →

Mistral open-sources a trillion-parameter model built for cybersecurity defense

Mistral AI released open weights for Mistral Large 4, a sparse mixture-of-experts model with roughly 1 trillion total parameters (about 49 billion active during inference), positioned for cybersecurity use cases, according to CSO Online. The company moved to a custom license rather than the Apache 2.0 license used for Large 3, saying it red-teamed the model with cybersecurity specialists and state authorities before release — while acknowledging the open release could also help attackers, since published weights can't be revoked.

Takeaway Betting that open weights nobody can revoke beat safety-through-control as a pitch to European enterprises is a direct contrast with how Google and OpenAI are gating their own cyber-capable models.

Product & Business

Meesho's loss narrows 68% and Delhivery swings back to profit in their first quarters testing public-market patience, as Ather Energy notches its first-ever profit and PB Fintech's net profit rises 43%, even as Urban Company's loss triples on a new bet and Alphabet, Meta and Amazon's Q3 earnings show Big Tech's AI capex now visibly outrunning free cash flow

Takeaway Today’s business story is profitability becoming the test everywhere at once — India’s newly public consumer-internet names are narrowing losses just as Alphabet, Meta and Amazon get punished for AI spending outrunning their own cash flow.

Meesho's loss narrows 68% to ₹132 crore in its first quarter as a listed company

Meesho reported Q2 FY27 results on October 28: consolidated revenue rose about 34% year-over-year to roughly ₹4,132 crore, while net loss narrowed 68% to ₹132.4 crore from ₹411 crore a year earlier, according to Inc42 and YourStory. Net merchandise value grew 23% to ₹12,911 crore and annual transacting users hit 284 million, though some brokerage commentary flagged slowing order growth as a watch-item.

Takeaway The market rewarding margin discipline over raw growth in Meesho's first results as a public company sets the bar other loss-making consumer-internet IPO hopefuls will now be measured against.

Delhivery swings back to profit as revenue crosses ₹3,000 crore for the first time

Delhivery's unaudited Q2 FY27 results, announced October 30, showed revenue from services up 19.8% year-over-year to ₹3,066.4 crore, with consolidated net profit of ₹40.6 crore versus a ₹50.5 crore loss a year earlier, according to Free Press Journal and YourStory. EBITDA nearly tripled to about ₹205 crore from ₹68 crore, with express parcel volumes up 32% to 325 million shipments.

Takeaway Delhivery's return to profit, a year after a surprise loss, suggests the logistics price war with Amazon- and Flipkart-backed rivals has eased enough for scale to finally show up in margins.

Ather Energy posts its first-ever quarterly profit as scooter sales jump 42%

Ather Energy's board approved Q2 FY27 results on October 26: consolidated net profit came in at ₹1.26 crore, versus a ₹154.07 crore loss a year earlier — the company's first profitable quarter since listing, according to Business Today. Revenue rose about 63% to ₹1,464 crore on scooter sales of 93,211 units, up 42% year-over-year; shares jumped roughly 14-15% on the news, with HSBC and Nomura raising price targets.

Takeaway Ather turning profitable before larger rival Ola Electric reframes the EV-scooter race as being won on unit economics, not just market share.

PB Fintech's net profit rises 43% as insurance premiums grow 38%

PB Fintech, the parent of Policybazaar and Paisabazaar, reported Q2 FY27 consolidated net profit of about ₹192 crore, up 43% year-over-year from ₹135 crore, on revenue of roughly ₹2,178 crore, according to YourStory's coverage of the October 30 results. Total insurance premiums processed grew 38% to about ₹9,385 crore, with active partners up 55% to roughly 131,000.

Takeaway Insurance distribution is proving to be PB Fintech's most durable profit engine, even as its smaller lending arm stays the choppier piece of the business.

Urban Company's loss triples to ₹182.5 crore even as revenue grows 41%; board clears ₹2,500-crore fundraise

Urban Company's board approved Q2 FY27 results on October 16: revenue from operations rose 41% to ₹537 crore, but consolidated net loss widened to ₹182.5 crore from ₹59.3 crore a year earlier, according to Inc42. The company said the swing was largely a ₹174 crore investment in its InstaHelp vertical; excluding that, adjusted EBITDA was positive at ₹30 crore. The board also cleared a fundraise of up to ₹2,500 crore, and shares fell about 9.5% on the news.

Takeaway Urban Company deliberately trading near-term profitability for a new bet, and topping up its war chest at the same time, suggests it sees its home-services core as mature enough to fund an adjacent expansion.

Alphabet's cloud revenue more than doubles to $30.7 billion, but a profit miss sends shares down 8%

Alphabet's Q3 2026 results, reported October 28, showed total revenue up 25% to about $127.6 billion, with Google Cloud revenue up 103% to $30.7 billion, according to NewsBytes and AI Weekly. Net income still fell about 25% to roughly $26.2 billion, with diluted EPS of $2.12 well below the roughly $3.00 expected, which Alphabet attributed to unspecified equity-investment losses; capex hit $51.4 billion, pushing free cash flow negative. Shares fell about 8% in after-hours trading despite the cloud beat.

Takeaway Even Google's best-ever cloud quarter wasn't enough to offset investor nerves about capex outrunning cash flow — a sign the market's patience with "spend now, profit later" AI narratives is thinning.

Meta's AI capex tips its quarterly free cash flow negative despite a 26% revenue beat

Meta's Q3 2026 revenue, reported October 21, rose 26% to $64.6 billion, above its own guidance range, but capex of $34.9 billion tipped quarterly free cash flow negative by about $2.1 billion, according to Fourweekmba's breakdown. Meta narrowed its 2026 capex guidance to $135–145 billion, signaling the spending pace isn't easing even as it tightens the range.

Takeaway A revenue beat this clean still burning more cash than it brings in shows how far ahead of returns the AI capex cycle has gotten, even at a company with Meta's core-ad-business margins.

Amazon's AWS grows 39% — its fastest in nearly five years — but shares fall on capex guidance

Amazon's Q3 2026 results, reported October 29, showed total revenue of $201.9 billion, up 12%, with AWS revenue up 39% to roughly $46 billion and AWS operating income up 55% to $17.8 billion, according to AI Weekly. CEO Andy Jassy projected AWS growth above 40% in both 2027 and 2028, but Amazon still guided to about $220 billion in 2026 capex with 2027 spending even higher, and shares fell about 7% after hours.

Takeaway AWS's fastest growth in five years is the strongest proof yet that enterprise AI demand is real, not just hype — but Wall Street is now pricing in the multi-year capex bill that comes with capturing it.

The Fed holds rates in a hawkish 10-2 vote, with both dissents pushing for a hike

The Federal Reserve kept its target rate at 3.75–4.00% on October 28, with two policymakers — Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack — dissenting in favor of a 25-basis-point hike rather than a cut, according to Admiral Markets' coverage of the decision. Commentary widely framed it as a "hawkish hold," with markets now pricing meaningful odds of a hike, not a cut, at the Fed's December 8-9 meeting.

Takeaway A central bank holding steady while its loudest dissent pushes for higher, not lower, rates is the kind of split that keeps bond-yield pressure on richly priced stocks elevated longer than a simple hold would suggest.

Wall Street logs its worst day since June as an Intel-led tech selloff deepens

The S&P 500 fell 1.82% to 7,580.07 and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 2.86% to 25,437.00 on October 30, their worst session since June, as a selloff triggered by Thursday's Amazon and Intel earnings widened, according to BNN Bloomberg's report. Intel shares plunged roughly 28% on weak guidance, with Nvidia cited as the single heaviest drag on the S&P 500 even as the broader market briefly pared losses.

Takeaway Two of this earnings season's most-watched AI suppliers having their worst days for very different reasons — Intel on demand, Nvidia on sheer index weight — shows how concentrated the market's AI bet has become.

Monday's Indian reopening lands hours before Apple's first earnings call under CEO John Ternus

Indian markets reopen at 9:15am IST Monday, hours ahead of Apple's fiscal Q4 earnings call at 5pm ET — its first under new CEO John Ternus, who took over from Tim Cook on September 1, according to 9to5Mac's preview. Apple had guided to 9–11% revenue growth and 47–48% gross margin; any surprise, good or bad, lands on Indian IT and supply-chain sentiment only after Tuesday's open.

Takeaway India's market gets a full trading day before the one earnings call most likely to move global tech sentiment this week even reaches the tape — Tuesday, not Monday, is when that reaction actually shows up here.

Brent crude holds near $115 a barrel after Houthi strikes on Saudi oil infrastructure

Brent crude was quoted near $115.11 a barrel and WTI near $101.65 in the most recent verified reading, from October 28, after Yemen's Houthis claimed missile strikes on Saudi Aramco's Ras Tanura refinery and other Aramco sites in late October, according to 5paisa's price tracker and The Print's report on the strikes. Saudi authorities haven't confirmed damage, and no settlement figure more recent than October 28 could be independently verified.

Takeaway With crude still this elevated days after the last confirmed reading, India's oil-import bill and the rupee are carrying a live risk that has nothing to do with anything on an earnings calendar.

A heavy week of India Inc results opens with Bajaj Finserv, Apollo Hospitals and Dabur all due by Wednesday

Bajaj Finserv reports Q2 FY27 results on November 3, with Apollo Hospitals and Dabur India following on November 4, Hindalco Industries on November 6, and ITC, Marico, BSE and Berger Paints also on the week's calendar, according to Kotak Neo's earnings-week preview. Anchoring bias, via Zerodha Varsity: Varsity's trading-biases module describes anchoring (or "focalism") as fixating on an initial reference point — a price first seen, an old high — and judging everything after it against that anchor rather than on its own merits; its own conclusion is blunt: there is no real cure for anchoring bias, only awareness of it. After Friday's selloff left the Sensex and Nifty off their recent highs, the risk is anchoring to last week's levels as "normal" when this week's real anchor should be Wednesday's wave of earnings and Apple's own guidance update, not wherever the index happened to sit a few sessions ago.
