Before the news

There’s a Japanese idea called wabi-sabi — finding beauty in things that are flawed, incomplete or impermanent, rather than insisting on a tidy, perfect version of them. It fits today’s edition: Anthropic emailing bankers the wrong IPO numbers and retracting them hours later, Titan’s doubled profit carrying a one-time asterisk, and Adani Enterprises’ headline profit falling even as its underlying business actually grew — in each case, the real story is in the flaw, not despite it.

AI & Tech

Anthropic emails bankers IPO pricing figures it retracts hours later as inaccurate, even as a November 12 deadline cuts off Cursor’s access to OpenAI’s models, Jensen Huang faces a November 11 deadline to even schedule a congressional briefing over an alleged Chinese-intelligence contact, OpenAI pledges to cover its own data centers’ electricity costs after its side lost nearly every November 3 ballot measure, and Beijing’s draft AI export curbs near a November 15 comment deadline

Takeaway Today’s AI story is oversight catching up with momentum — a botched IPO filing, a CEO facing a congressional deadline, and new export and community-cost promises all land the same week, even as one chipmaker’s guidance shows real caution creeping into next quarter’s numbers.

Anthropic emails bankers IPO pricing figures, then retracts them hours later as “inaccurate”

Anthropic sent banks a filing Monday showing roughly 867 million shares priced at $70–$80 each — implying proceeds of up to $70 billion — before retracting the figures within hours and saying a corrected filing would follow, according to Reuters and The Information; Anthropic declined to comment, and no official price range was public as of Monday.

Takeaway Sending bankers the wrong numbers on IPO week is the kind of mistake that invites more scrutiny of the real numbers, not less.

Reuters, via InvestingLive →

OpenAI’s November 12 cutoff of Cursor’s model access edges closer

OpenAI told SpaceX on August 28 it would end Cursor-maker Anysphere’s access to its models, including GPT, effective November 12 — triggered by a change-of-control clause after SpaceX’s acquisition of Anysphere, with OpenAI citing an inability to trust SpaceX and xAI with its terms of service. Cursor keeps its current access until the deadline but won’t receive future OpenAI models, and still offers Anthropic’s and Google’s models alongside Grok.

Takeaway A contract clause written for an ordinary vendor dispute is now the thing deciding which AI models millions of developers can reach.

The Next Web →

Jensen Huang faces a November 11 deadline to even schedule a congressional briefing over an alleged Chinese-intelligence contact

Congressional committee chairmen have given Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang until November 11 to set a date for a requested briefing, after the New York Times and Financial Times reported that Alice Huang, an executive at Singapore-based chip firm Megaspeed, was a Chinese intelligence contact recruited to target him; a reported romantic relationship between the two remains disputed. Huang has said his dealings with Megaspeed were “normal,” Nvidia says earlier diversion allegations were “thoroughly investigated and debunked last year,” and Singapore’s Ministry of Home Affairs has denied investigating any Chinese-intelligence link to Alice Huang.

Takeaway A deadline to merely schedule a meeting, not to answer for anything specific yet, is still enough to keep a story alive for another news cycle.

Axios →

OpenAI pledges to cover full electricity costs at its own data centers, after its side lost nearly every data-center ballot measure

OpenAI published a “National Community Compact” and a “Compute Campus Policy Blueprint” on November 5, committing to pay the full electricity and infrastructure cost of any data center it owns and operates, stop signing NDAs with public officials, and involve local residents and regulators in site planning. The move follows the November 3 midterms, in which data-center-friendly ballot measures lost in nearly all of 41 tracked statewide contests, including a notable defeat in Ohio.

Takeaway Companies don’t usually publish their own losing-ballot-measure count unless they’re trying to get ahead of the next vote, not just the last one.

TechRadar →

China’s draft AI export-control rules near a November 15 public-comment deadline

China’s Ministry of Commerce and Ministry of Science and Technology proposed export controls on 40 technologies on October 16 — 23 of them AI-related, covering large models, AI agents and compute clusters — with the public-comment window closing November 15 and an interim pre-export registration requirement already in effect. Open-source and already-published technologies are excluded from the controls.

Takeaway A government drafting export controls on its own AI models and agents is as much a signal of how strategic it considers them as it is a restriction on anyone else.

ITV News →

Microchip Technology beats Q2 estimates but gives weaker December-quarter guidance, and shares fall

Microchip Technology reported fiscal Q2 FY27 non-GAAP earnings of $0.97 a share on revenue of $1.622 billion, up 42.2% year-on-year and ahead of consensus, on November 5 — but its December-quarter revenue guidance of roughly $1.61 billion came in about 1.4% below analyst estimates, and the stock fell on the news. Data centers made up 16.7% of the company’s sales in the quarter, and executives flagged caution about the pace of further growth in that segment on the earnings call.

Takeaway A beat-and-fall reaction is the market telling a chipmaker that this quarter’s numbers matter less than next quarter’s guidance.

Product & Business

Titan’s net profit more than doubles to ₹2,393 crore on a one-off gain as Noel N. Tata prepares to retire as its Vice Chairman, even as Bank of Baroda posts a record ₹6,532 crore quarterly profit and Adani Ports raises its full-year guidance while Adani Enterprises’ headline profit falls 55% on the same quarter’s 75% revenue surge, while Mahindra & Mahindra, MapmyIndia and Saregama all post double-digit profit growth and Bengaluru cleantech startup altM raises a $10.3 million Series A

Takeaway Today’s business story is a strong Q2 earnings season complicating its own good news — real profit growth across autos, banking, ports, mapping and music lands alongside a one-off gain at Titan, a steep headline profit fall at Adani Enterprises, and a Tata family board exit, proof this season’s numbers need a second look before they’re taken at face value.

Titan’s Q2 net profit more than doubles to ₹2,393 crore, boosted by a one-time gain, as Noel N. Tata prepares to retire as Vice Chairman

Titan Company’s net profit rose to ₹2,393 crore for the quarter ended September 30, more than double a year earlier, though the figure includes a ₹671 crore one-time revaluation gain on a financial liability; excluding that, underlying profit was about ₹1,722 crore, still up 54% year-on-year, on revenue from operations of roughly ₹20,337 crore, according to The Week. The company’s board also announced that Noel N. Tata will retire as Vice Chairman and director effective November 12.

Takeaway A profit number that needs an asterisk is still a real number — just not quite as dramatic as the headline makes it look.

The Week →

Bank of Baroda posts a record ₹6,532 crore quarterly profit, up 35.8%

Bank of Baroda’s standalone net profit rose 35.8% year-on-year to a record ₹6,531.54 crore for the quarter, from ₹4,809 crore a year earlier, as operating profit jumped 51% to ₹11,430 crore and gross NPA improved to 1.78% from 2.16%, according to PSU Connect. Treasury income was a major driver, rising to ₹4,093 crore from ₹1,086 crore, and the bank’s H1 FY27 profit actually fell 16.5% to ₹7,810 crore because of a heavy Q1 provision.

Takeaway A record quarter leaning heavily on treasury gains and a weak first half sitting right next to it is a more complicated story than “record profit” alone suggests.

PSU Connect →

Adani Ports’ Q2 profit rises 18% to ₹3,682 crore, and it raises its full-year guidance

Adani Ports and Special Economic Zone’s net profit rose 18% year-on-year to ₹3,682 crore for the quarter, on revenue up 24% to ₹11,340 crore, and the company raised its FY27 revenue guidance by ₹1,000 crore to ₹44,000–46,000 crore and its EBITDA guidance by the same amount to ₹26,000–27,000 crore, according to Republic World.

Takeaway Raising full-year guidance off a single quarter’s strength is a bet that this growth rate holds, not just a description of what already happened.

Republic World →

Adani Enterprises’ headline profit falls 55% to ₹1,492 crore even as revenue jumps 75%

Adani Enterprises’ consolidated net profit fell 55% year-on-year to ₹1,492 crore for the quarter, from ₹3,304 crore a year earlier, even as revenue rose 75% to ₹37,216 crore, according to Free Press Journal. The drop is mostly a base effect: last year’s quarter included a large one-off gain from selling its AWL Agri Business stake, and excluding exceptional items, profit before tax from continuing operations actually rose 146% to ₹2,002 crore.

Takeaway A 55% profit fall sitting on top of 146% underlying growth is the clearest case this earnings season for reading past the headline number.

Free Press Journal →

Mahindra & Mahindra’s Q2 consolidated profit rises 25% to ₹4,588 crore, even as margins slip

Mahindra & Mahindra’s consolidated net profit rose 25% year-on-year to ₹4,588 crore for the quarter, from ₹3,673 crore a year earlier, as consolidated revenue grew 25% to ₹57,553 crore and auto revenue climbed 39%, according to Upstox. Operating margin slipped to about 13.4% from 14.6%, and the stock still gained over 6% on results day.

Takeaway Growth and margin compression landing in the same quarter is a reminder that strong volume numbers don’t automatically mean stronger profitability per unit.

Upstox →

MapmyIndia’s Q2 profit jumps 90.6% to ₹35.3 crore — but falls 29% from the quarter before

C.E. Info Systems (MapmyIndia) reported consolidated net profit up 90.6% year-on-year to ₹35.3 crore, from ₹18.5 crore a year earlier, as revenue from operations grew 15.8% to ₹131.7 crore and its Automotive and Enterprise segments grew 51.9% and 44.1% respectively, according to Inc42. The profit figure is still 29.1% lower than the previous quarter’s ₹49.7 crore.

Takeaway A 90% year-on-year jump reads very differently once you notice it’s also a 29% drop from the quarter before — momentum, not a new baseline.

Inc42 →

Saregama’s Q2 profit rises 46% to ₹64 crore as its fastest-growing segment, live events, still loses money

Saregama India’s consolidated net profit rose about 46% year-on-year to ₹64.17 crore, from ₹43.76 crore a year earlier, as revenue grew 36.6% to ₹314.30 crore, according to The Week. Live-events revenue surged 101% to ₹44.7 crore but posted a segment loss, while video revenue fell 16%; the stock jumped about 13% on results day.

Takeaway The market cheered the headline profit jump and mostly looked past the fact that the fastest-growing new segment is still losing money.

The Week →

Bengaluru cleantech startup altM raises $10.3 million to turn farm residue into specialty chemicals

altM, founded in 2022 by two former Tesla engineers, raised a $10.3 million Series A led by Rainmatter (Zerodha’s venture arm) and Mela Ventures, with existing investors Omnivore, Theia Ventures, Aureolis Ventures and Spectrum Impact also participating, according to Inc42. The company converts crop residue like sugarcane bagasse and rice straw into cellulose, lignin and other specialty chemicals, and plans a 1,000-tonne-a-year commercial plant by 2028.

Takeaway Turning farm waste that would otherwise be burned into industrial chemicals is the kind of unglamorous business that quietly solves two problems at once.

NSE and BSE stay shut today for Diwali-Balipratipada, a day after Sensex and Nifty closed at an 8-month high

Indian exchanges are closed for regular equity and derivatives trading on November 10 for Diwali-Balipratipada, a day after the Sensex added 107.25 points (0.13%) to close at 81,106.65 and the Nifty 50 added 58.25 points (0.23%) to 25,044.65 on Monday — both benchmarks’ highest close in eight months, according to Kotak Neo and HDFC Sky. MCX’s evening commodity session runs as usual.

Takeaway After a week of 2%-plus daily swings, a 0.13% gain to an eight-month high is itself the headline — this market is pausing to catch its breath, not reversing, right before a holiday pause of its own.

Wall Street logs its best day since April as crude oil slides and chip stocks rebound

The S&P 500 jumped 2.35% to 7,549.94 and the Nasdaq rose 2.74% on Monday, their best session since April 8, as Brent crude slid 5.8% and chipmakers broadly rebounded — AMD gained 13% — while the Dow rose 2.15% to 52,389.75, according to the Associated Press. The CBOE Volatility Index fell about 22% to near 19.9, its first sub-20 close since the recent selloff began.

Takeaway A single day erasing weeks of chip-stock jitters says more about how fast sentiment can swing than about anything that actually changed overnight.

Foreign investors keep adding to Indian government bonds as the rupee strengthens

Foreign portfolio investor holdings of Indian government securities under the Fully Accessible Route rose from ₹3.57 lakh crore to ₹3.65 lakh crore between November 2 and 9, even as the rupee strengthened to 93.74 against the dollar, according to Kotak Neo.

Takeaway Debt inflows climbing quietly alongside a strengthening rupee is the kind of steady vote of confidence that doesn’t make headlines the way a one-day equity swing does.

Kotak Neo →

IREDA extends its winning streak to seven sessions as Whirlpool of India edges up on a Q2 beat

IREDA shares closed up 7.47% on Monday, their seventh consecutive gaining session, while Whirlpool of India rose 0.3% after reporting Q2 FY27 revenue up 14% to ₹1,873 crore, though its EBITDA margin contracted to 2.1%, according to Kotak Neo.

Takeaway A seven-session win streak and a thin-margin earnings beat landing in the same ticker roundup is a reminder that this market’s gains are coming from very different kinds of stories.
