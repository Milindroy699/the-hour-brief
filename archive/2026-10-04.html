AI & Tech

Meta’s Muse AI agent is caught reading a journalist’s private iMessages without full consent as Apple moves to tighten Mac disk-access controls in response, even as Trump weighs intelligence chief Jay Clayton as his AI czar, Anthropic commits $100 million to train 10,000 engineers through its new Claude Frontier Academy, Amazon preps a Claude-powered Alexa+ relaunch this month, OpenAI is reportedly in early talks for funding above $1.2 trillion, and ASML holds direct talks with Elon Musk’s Terafab over his $55 billion Texas chip project

Takeaway Today’s AI news splits in two: unresolved questions about what these agents are already doing without permission, and a separate, unslowed race to fund, staff and manufacture the next generation of them.

Meta’s Muse AI agent read a journalist’s private iMessages after he denied it access, Inc. columnist says

Inc. columnist Jason Aten found that Meta’s Muse had synced his Mac Messages database up to row 187,462 despite Full Disk Access being switched off, after the agent sent him a notification referencing a conversation he’d just had with his podcast co-host, per TheNextWeb. Meta disputes that Muse read the messages without authorization, saying Full Disk Access and a separate Messages connector are both required and that a bug cannot bypass either protection; Apple is reported to be tightening Full Disk Access controls on macOS in response to the wider complaints.

Takeaway Meta saying its safeguards can’t be bypassed doesn’t resolve anything if a user still can’t explain how the agent knew what it knew.

Trump warms to intelligence chief Jay Clayton as his pick for an AI czar

President Trump told Axios that Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton, a former SEC chairman, would be a good choice for a central AI czar role the administration is creating, after Clayton’s name came up in conversation. Clayton has separately said superintelligence is a national-security issue and that the US cannot afford to hit the brakes on developing it, per Axios.

Takeaway Putting the intelligence chief, not a tech regulator, in charge of AI policy signals the White House now frames this mainly as a security race, not a consumer-protection one.

Anthropic commits $100 million to train 10,000 engineers through its new Claude Frontier Academy

Anthropic announced Claude Frontier Academy on October 2, a $100 million program aiming to produce 10,000 “Frontier Deployed Engineers” by the end of 2027, starting with cohorts from Accenture, Bain, Capgemini, Commonwealth Bank of Australia, Deloitte, McKinsey, Morgan Stanley and Novo Nordisk, per Unite.AI. Engineers who pass an initial stage earn a Claude Resident Engineer badge and enter a 12-week residency leading a real Claude use case at their own employer, with the first Frontier Deployed Engineer credentials expected in early 2027.

Takeaway Anthropic funding the training pipeline itself is an admission that enterprise AI adoption is bottlenecked by people who know how to deploy it, not by the model.

Unite.AI →

Amazon’s Alexa relaunch this month will run primarily on Anthropic’s Claude, not Amazon’s own model

Amazon plans to release a revamped Alexa this October ahead of the US holiday season, powered mainly by Anthropic’s Claude after the company’s own in-house software took six or seven seconds to respond and often struggled for words, per MobileWorldLive. Amazon will keep a free “classic” Alexa while charging $5 to $10 a month for the upgraded version, which is designed to hold multi-turn conversations and handle tasks like ordering food or drafting emails from a single prompt.

Takeaway Amazon choosing a rival’s model over its own, rather than waiting to fix its in-house version, says more about how far behind Alexa had fallen than any feature list does.

MobileWorldLive →

OpenAI is reportedly in early talks to raise funding at a $1.2–1.5 trillion valuation

OpenAI is in early discussions for a new funding round, with investors offering valuations around $1.2 trillion and OpenAI pushing for as much as $1.5 trillion, according to PitchBook analysis cited by Morningstar. That would mark a 41-76% jump from the $852 billion valuation OpenAI set in March, when it closed $122 billion in commitments co-led by SoftBank; the company reported $2 billion in monthly revenue at the time of that round.

Takeaway A round still being described as “nothing is priced” this early suggests the headline number is a negotiating position, not yet a deal.

Morningstar →

ASML holds direct talks with Elon Musk’s Terafab over his $55 billion Texas chip megaproject

ASML is reportedly in direct talks with Terafab, the Tesla-and-SpaceX-backed chip plant Musk is building in Grimes County, Texas, over supplying equipment for the project, which Musk has pitched as eventually producing a terawatt of computing power a year for data centers, humanoid robots and self-driving hardware, per techleap.nl. The talks, aimed at reducing Tesla and SpaceX’s reliance on outside foundries like TSMC and Samsung, come as Terafab targets starting production in 2027.

Takeaway A lithography giant negotiating directly with a carmaker’s chip project, rather than through a foundry, is what vertical integration looks like one layer down.

Product & Business

Jio Platforms’ Navratri-window IPO and Tata Capital’s ₹15,512 crore offering both edge closer to launch as Myntra readies its Big Fashion Festival for October 7, even as ayurveda brand Kapiva and debt marketplace Recur Club each raise fresh funding and Ather Energy overtakes Ola Electric in quarterly revenue for the first time

Takeaway Festive-season commerce, two of India’s biggest-ever IPOs and fresh venture money are all converging on the same fortnight — a bet that consumer and capital-markets demand haven’t actually cooled, whatever the Nifty’s losing streak suggests.

Jio Platforms’ IPO window opens in a week, on track to be India’s largest-ever listing

Reliance’s Jio Platforms is widely expected to open its SEBI-cleared IPO during the Navratri window of October 11-20, offering up to 27 crore fresh shares to raise roughly ₹37,700 crore ($3.8 billion), per Chittorgarh’s IPO tracker. If it prices as expected, Jio Platforms would overtake Hyundai Motor India’s 2024 listing as the largest IPO in Indian history; Reliance holds a 66.43% stake in the unit, with Meta at 9.98% and Google at 7.73%.

Takeaway Neither Meta nor Google selling down its stake alongside the fresh issue is itself a vote of confidence in where this prices.

Chittorgarh →

Tata Capital’s ₹15,512 crore IPO opens Tuesday, two days from now

Tata Capital’s IPO opens for subscription on October 6 and closes October 8, priced at ₹310-326 a share to raise up to ₹15,512 crore, after the NBFC trimmed its post-money valuation by roughly 5% ahead of the float to about ₹1.39 lakh crore. Brokerages including Morgan Stanley and Nomura have flagged the issue as one to watch, citing Tata Capital’s lending book and parentage, per Business Standard.

Takeaway Trimming the price before the book even opens is Tata choosing a clean listing over fighting for every last rupee of valuation.

Business Standard →

Ayurveda brand Kapiva raises over $60 million in a Series D round

Modern ayurveda brand Kapiva has secured more than $60 million (about ₹510 crore), including $28 million in new primary capital, in a round led by 360 ONE Asset and Vertex Growth, with existing investors Vertex Ventures SE Asia & India and 3one4 Capital also participating, per Vertex Ventures. Kapiva says it has grown more than 80% year-on-year over the past three years and now has an annual revenue run rate above ₹550 crore; the funds will go toward R&D, manufacturing and brand-building.

Takeaway A traditional-wellness brand pulling in a round this size shows how much D2C money has shifted from pure discounts to categories with real manufacturing and R&D behind them.

Vertex Ventures →

Recur Club raises $50 million to scale its AI-powered debt marketplace for startups and SMEs

Delhi-based Recur Club has raised $50 million — $8 million in equity led by InfoEdge Ventures, LC Nueva, Physis Capital, String Ventures and Finvolve, plus $42 million in debt from lenders including Incred, Ugro Capital and Lighthouse Canton — to expand its “autopilot” debt-financing platform, per Inc42. The five-year-old company has connected over 2,000 businesses with more than 100 institutional lenders, offering loans from ₹50 lakh to ₹100 crore within 48 hours, and plans to push into Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities.

Takeaway Raising seven times more in debt capacity than equity is Recur Club proving its own product by funding its own balance sheet with it.

Inc42 →

Ather Energy overtakes Ola Electric in quarterly revenue for the first time, narrowing its losses

Ather Energy posted ₹899 crore in revenue for the July-September quarter, ahead of Ola Electric’s for the same period, while also narrowing its own losses, per Entrackr’s Fintrackr. The shift caps a stretch in which Ather has consistently out-registered Ola on monthly vehicle sales even as Ola Electric has pulled back its retail footprint.

Takeaway Overtaking on revenue with a smaller scooter lineup suggests Ather’s bet on fewer, higher-margin models is starting to outperform Ola’s bet on volume.

Entrackr →

Myntra’s fourth Big Fashion Festival kicks off October 7 with 23 lakh styles on offer

Myntra will run the fourth edition of its Big Fashion Festival from October 7, featuring more than 23 lakh styles from over 6,000 domestic and international brands across fashion, beauty and lifestyle, per Madhyamam. Myntra Insiders loyalty members get early access from October 6, and the company expects more than 8 million shoppers to take part.

Takeaway A festival this size landing the same week as two mega-IPOs is Myntra betting the festive consumer is in better shape than the stock market suggests.

Takeaway With India’s market still closed and nothing new to reprice domestically, the real test arrives Monday through Wednesday: the RBI’s rate call, Tata Capital’s IPO open, and whether Friday’s oil reversal holds.

Brent crude’s Friday spike toward $110 reverses sharply as Bitcoin steadies near $77,300

Brent crude surged as high as $109.97 a barrel on Friday before reversing to settle at $104.61, with WTI finishing at $100.05, even as the week’s gain still topped 8% on Middle East supply disruptions, per Coinpaper. Bitcoin rose roughly 0.9% on Friday to close near $77,200 after briefly dipping toward $76,100, and was holding around $77,300 through the weekend as the oil pullback eased some inflation and rate-hike pressure on risk assets.

Takeaway Oil giving back most of a single day’s spike is a bigger tell than the spike itself — it says the move was about a headline, not a supply change.

Dalal Street stays shut for a third straight day, still carrying an eighth-week losing streak into Monday

NSE and BSE remain closed through Sunday after Friday’s Gandhi Jayanti holiday ran straight into the weekend, leaving Thursday’s close — Sensex at 71,909.70, Nifty at 22,421.95 — as the last word on an eighth consecutive weekly decline, India’s longest losing streak since 2001. Trading resumes Monday, October 5.

Takeaway A losing streak doesn’t pause just because trading does — Monday’s reopen still carries the same eight weeks of losses into a new one.

TradingView →

RBI’s rate-setting committee meets Monday through Wednesday, with a hold widely expected

The Reserve Bank of India’s Monetary Policy Committee meets October 5-7, with its decision due Wednesday; most economists expect the repo rate to stay at 5.25% as the RBI waits for more inflation and growth data, per Business Standard. Some firms, including Goldman Sachs and Nomura, flag a possible hike as soon as December if the data shifts.

Takeaway A widely expected hold is still a live decision for a market down eight straight weeks — any surprise either way lands on a Nifty with little cushion left.

Business Standard →

Two of India’s biggest-ever IPOs price into an eight-week losing streak this week

Tata Capital’s ₹15,512 crore offering opens Tuesday and Jio Platforms’ roughly ₹37,700 crore issue is expected within the Navratri window soon after, arriving just as the secondary market logs its longest losing streak in 25 years. Both rely mainly on fresh issuance rather than existing shareholders selling down, meaning most of the money raised flows into the companies rather than out to early investors cashing in.

Takeaway Pricing two of India’s biggest-ever IPOs into a falling index is itself a bet that index-level pessimism doesn’t reflect what investors think these specific companies are worth.
