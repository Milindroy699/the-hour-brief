A federal judge rules the Pentagon’s blacklisting of Anthropic unlawful as Nvidia quietly pauses its AI Compute Partnership cloud-financing deals over antitrust worries, while Marvell’s stock slides despite record revenue on a delayed Google chip payoff and Meta closes another loophole in its smart glasses’ recording light

A federal judge rules the Pentagon's blacklisting of Anthropic unlawful

US District Judge Rita Lin ruled on 28 August that the Department of Defense acted illegally when Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth designated Anthropic a national-security “supply chain risk,” finding the move violated the company's First Amendment rights and its due-process rights under the Fifth Amendment, TechCrunch and Bloomberg reported. In a 59-page order, Lin called the designation “arbitrary and capricious” and wrote that “the empty invocation of national security is not a blank check to punish and retaliate against government critics”; the dispute traces back to Anthropic's refusal to let the Pentagon use Claude for autonomous weapons or domestic mass surveillance. The government is expected to appeal, and the ruling does not force the Pentagon to adopt Anthropic's technology.

Takeaway A federal court just told the government it can't use an obscure supply-chain statute to punish an AI company for the safety limits it chooses to enforce on its own model — a precedent that matters far beyond this one contract dispute.

Nvidia pauses parts of its AI Compute Partnership cloud-financing program over antitrust worries

Nvidia has paused some deals under its AI Compute Partnership, a financing program launched less than two months earlier that offered credit support to smaller AI cloud companies in exchange for a cut of their cloud revenue, the Wall Street Journal reported on 27 August. Some Nvidia employees grew concerned the arrangement could draw antitrust scrutiny after the company told certain customers they could only rent capacity to Nvidia-approved buyers and pushed for compute to be spread across multiple smaller firms rather than one large customer; a recent quarterly filing disclosed the program had already reached $36 billion in commitments with typical six-year terms.

Takeaway A financing program designed to help smaller AI clouds afford Nvidia's chips ended up giving Nvidia so much say over how those same clouds could sell their capacity that its own staff started flagging it as a monopoly risk.

Marvell's stock slides despite a revenue beat as Google's $120 billion chip payoff waits until 2029

Marvell Technology shares fell as much as 8–10% on 28 August even after fiscal second-quarter revenue rose 37% year-on-year to $2.74 billion and non-GAAP earnings of $0.94 a share beat estimates, with the company also raising its FY2027 and FY2028 revenue guidance, CNBC and Bloomberg reported. Investors instead focused on comments from CEO Matt Murphy that the custom-chip agreement with Google — which could generate up to $120 billion in revenue through fiscal 2033 and includes a warrant for Google to buy a $12.2 billion stake in Marvell — won't become a major revenue contributor until fiscal 2029, later than markets had been pricing in.

Takeaway Beating on every number that matters today wasn't enough once investors realised the one number that matters most — when Google's money actually shows up — had just moved two years further out.

CNBC →

Meta closes another loophole that let people record with its smart glasses' privacy light covered

Meta is rolling out a software update to Ray-Ban Meta, Oakley Meta and its own $300 glasses line that stops the camera from recording if the privacy indicator light is covered mid-recording, 9to5Google reported on 28 August, closing a gap after users found they could start recording and then cover the LED. The update follows an earlier fix for tape-covered lights and comes after a hobbyist began charging customers to physically disable the LED hardware entirely; Meta says it will also pursue takedowns and legal action against people selling such modifications.

Takeaway Every fix Meta ships for this loophole has been reactive — discovered by users first, patched after the fact — which isn't reassuring for a device whose entire privacy promise rests on one small light.

Nicola Coughlan, Hugh Bonneville and dozens of UK performers demand a law against AI voice cloning

Actors including Nicola Coughlan, Hugh Bonneville, Matt Lucas, Luke Evans and Siobhan McSweeney have backed “Save Our Voices Now,” a campaign with around 80 signatories to an open letter and a UK government petition asking that every person's voice be recognised as their own statutory property, ITV News reported on 28 August. Audiobook narrator Alice Sockett, who co-founded the campaign, called unauthorised AI voice cloning “an existential threat to our entire industry,” as the campaign pushes for legal protection against AI systems replicating a real person's voice without consent.

Takeaway A petition asking lawmakers to formally define voice as property is a sign that “it sounds like me” has become a legal question the UK hasn't answered yet, and performers don't want to wait for a court case to find out the hard way.

Stanford data shows young workers in AI-exposed jobs are now 19% below where hiring trends predicted

Employment for workers aged 22–25 in occupations most exposed to AI automation is now 19% below where it would be had it tracked less-exposed peers, up from a 13% gap a year earlier and 15% in mid-2025, according to Stanford Digital Economy Lab analysis of ADP payroll data covering millions of US workers, TIME reported. The decline is concentrated in roles built on “codified” knowledge — tasks like writing code or handling routine customer queries that can be fully automated — while employment holds steady or grows in jobs where AI mainly augments rather than replaces the work.

Takeaway A gap that's widened every time researchers have checked it — 13%, then 15%, now 19% — looks less like a temporary hiring freeze and more like a structural shift in which entry-level jobs simply stop existing.

Product & Business

Mukesh Ambani’s Jio Platforms gets SEBI’s nod for what could be India’s biggest-ever IPO as Tata Motors unifies its lineup under a new TATA.CARS identity, while Bengaluru’s InstaAstro and Micromax’s new deeptech family office keep funding flowing, Bengaluru hoteliers defer their Swiggy-Zomato boycott, and Gap’s stock jumps on an Old Navy leadership shake-up

Jio Platforms gets SEBI's nod for what could be India's biggest-ever IPO

The Securities and Exchange Board of India issued its observations on Jio Platforms' draft prospectus during the week ended 28 August, clearing the way for Mukesh Ambani's telecom and digital-services arm to proceed with an IPO that could raise close to ₹37,700 crore ($4 billion), Deccan Herald and Bloomberg reported. The issue, a fresh sale of up to roughly 270 million equity shares with no offer-for-sale component, would surpass Hyundai Motor India's $3.3 billion 2024 listing to become India's largest-ever public offering; Jio Platforms serves more than 526 million subscribers, close to half of India's wired and wireless internet market.

Takeaway Structuring the entire float as fresh shares rather than existing investors cashing out signals Reliance wants this IPO read as a growth-capital story for Jio, not an exit event for its early backers.

Tata Motors unifies its passenger-vehicle lineup under a new “TATA.CARS” identity

Tata Motors Passenger Vehicles unveiled Tata.Cars as its new customer-facing master brand, adopting a phrase Indian consumers already use informally, under the tagline “Nothing's Too Far,” Business Standard and BusinessToday reported on 27–28 August. The new identity brings a “Parallel Pathways” visual system, an “Ambition Blue” colour and a dedicated sound identity across dealerships, digital touchpoints and marketing, though existing model names, badges and the legal entity name remain unchanged; Tata.ev continues as a separate EV sub-brand.

Takeaway Rebranding around a name customers already use is a low-risk way to look modern without actually asking buyers to relearn what they call the cars they already own.

Purple Style Labs raises ₹306 crore from anchor investors ahead of its Monday IPO opening

Purple Style Labs, the parent of luxury fashion platform Pernia's Pop-Up Shop, allotted shares to ten anchor investors — including ICICI Prudential Mutual Fund, Jupiter India Fund and Aditya Birla Sun Life Insurance — raising nearly ₹306 crore at the top of its ₹546–575 price band, Business Standard reported on 28 August. The ₹680 crore public issue opens for subscription on 31 August and closes 2 September.

Takeaway Anchor demand landing right at the top of the price band gives the company a clean signal to point to when it opens to retail investors on Monday, whatever the broader market does between now and then.

Micromax launches a ₹250 crore family office to back Indian deeptech startups

Micromax Informatics has set up a family office with a ₹250 crore corpus dedicated to deeptech startups, writing average cheques of ₹10–20 crore into pre-Series A to Series B companies building “scalable intellectual property” across AI, semiconductors, defence tech, spacetech and robotics, Inc42 reported. The company plans to pair capital with its own manufacturing ecosystem and industrial support for portfolio companies, and says the office will invest across India and select global markets.

Takeaway A phone maker turned manufacturer offering factory access alongside capital is a more differentiated pitch to deeptech founders than another cheque from a fund with no hardware supply chain behind it.

Astrology app InstaAstro raises $12 million as faith-tech goes AI-first

InstaAstro, an online astrology and spiritual-wellness platform, raised $12 million in a Series A led by Singularity AMC and Artha Venture Fund, Entrackr and Inc42 reported on 27 August. The five-year-old company, which has 12 million registered users and over 5,000 verified experts across 183 countries, says revenue more than doubled to ₹111 crore in FY26 and has since crossed a ₹200 crore annualised run rate; the new capital will fund AI-led sentiment analysis and multilingual consultation tools alongside international expansion.

Takeaway An astrology platform doubling revenue and layering on AI sentiment analysis is a reminder that “faith-tech” in India is being built and funded with the same product playbook as any other consumer app.

Bengaluru hoteliers defer their Swiggy-Zomato boycott to 31 August as commission fight drags on

More than 1,000 Bengaluru restaurants and hotels that had threatened to delist from Swiggy and Zomato over commissions ranging from 8–28%, undisclosed discounting and delayed refunds agreed to postpone the boycott to 31 August after talks with Swiggy CEO Rohit Kapoor and Zomato's Aditya Mangla, Deccan Herald reported. Both platforms have agreed to some demands — including capping commissions and requiring written approval before running promotions on a restaurant's behalf — while asking more time on others; restaurants say they'll switch off the apps from 1 September if the remaining demands aren't met.

Takeaway The threat is working precisely because Swiggy and Zomato now face real low-commission competition from Flipkart Minutes and Rapido's Ownly — restaurants have leverage this time because they finally have somewhere else to list.

Lovable's Elena Verna says the AI growth playbook only reuses a third of what worked before

Elena Verna, head of growth at AI app-builder Lovable, told Lenny's Newsletter that only 30–40% of her fifteen-plus years of growth experience transfers to building an AI-native company, in an episode detailing how Lovable reached $200 million in annual recurring revenue in under a year with roughly 100 employees — among the fastest climbs to that mark on record. Verna said Lovable prioritises shipping new features over optimising existing ones and treats giving the product away for free as its strongest growth lever, arguing AI forces companies to rebuild growth strategy from first principles rather than porting over pre-AI playbooks.

Takeaway If a growth executive with two decades of pattern-matching says most of her old patterns don't transfer, that's a stronger signal that AI products need genuinely different playbooks than any amount of think-piece speculation about it.

Gap's stock jumps 12–14% after Old Navy gets a new CEO to fix its sales slide

Gap Inc. shares surged as much as 14% in premarket trading on 28 August after the company named longtime Target and Walmart marketing veteran Michael Francis as Old Navy's president and CEO, effective 2 November, alongside second-quarter results that beat estimates and a raised full-year profit outlook, Reuters and CNBC reported. Old Navy, Gap's largest brand, posted its first sales decline in twelve quarters — down 4% — even as Gap's namesake brand grew comparable sales 10%, making the fix at Old Navy central to the retailer's broader turnaround.

Takeaway A single management change driving a double-digit stock pop shows how much of Gap's turnaround story now hinges on one brand and one hire, rather than the company-wide recovery the headline earnings beat suggests.

Nifty IT surges up to 5% as Nvidia's earnings glow spills into Indian tech stocks

The Nifty IT index jumped as much as 3.2% intraday while individual stocks ran further — TCS, HCL Technologies and Coforge up around 4%, Infosys, Persistent Systems and Tech Mahindra up about 3% — lifting the Sensex 330.91 points (0.43%) to 77,264.51 and the Nifty 50 84.80 points (0.35%) to 24,175.65 on 28 August, Business Standard reported. The buying tracked a strong Wall Street close overnight after Nvidia's second-quarter results and upbeat revenue outlook renewed optimism about global AI spending, a sentiment that carried directly into India's IT services stocks given their exposure to US technology clients.

Takeaway Indian IT stocks rallying on a US chipmaker's earnings, not on anything about Indian services demand, is a reminder of how much of this sector's valuation now tracks Silicon Valley's AI capex cycle rather than its own order books.

Wall Street closes a winning week on a soft note as Fed Chair Warsh warns inflation is still too high

The Dow slipped 9.45 points (0.02%) to 53,559.99, the S&P 500 fell 0.25% to 7,711.76 and the Nasdaq dropped 0.52% to 26,402.42 on Friday 28 August, even as all three indices closed the week higher, Yahoo Finance reported. The dip came as Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, marking his 100th day in the role, used his first Jackson Hole address to say he was “impressed” by the economy's strength but concerned that underlying inflation trends “have not improved,” calling for a “quieter” central bank while leaving the door open to rate hikes rather than cuts.

Takeaway A Fed chair spending his marquee speech warning about inflation instead of signalling cuts is a different message than markets have been pricing in for months, even if Friday's modest dip barely showed it.

Tempsens Instruments lists at a 111% premium, 2026's biggest IPO debut gain

Tempsens Instruments shares opened at ₹634 on the NSE against its ₹300 issue price — a 111.33% listing-day gain — and at a 110.40% premium on the BSE, after its ₹650 crore IPO was subscribed more than 184 times during bidding, BusinessToday reported on 28 August. The debut surpasses Bharat Coking Coal's 95.7% listing gain in January to become India's largest first-day IPO pop of 2026.

Takeaway A near-185-times-subscribed IPO doubling on debut shows retail demand still chasing listing pops rather than pricing the company on fundamentals — exactly the kind of gap that eventually closes hard for whoever buys at the top.

Rupee holds near 95.54 as DIIs offset FII selling and crude stays soft near $88

The rupee closed little changed at about 95.54 to the dollar on 28 August, as domestic institutional investors bought a net ₹5,184 crore of Indian equities in the cash market while foreign institutional investors sold a net ₹5,040 crore, continuing a pattern of DII buying cushioning FII outflows through much of 2026, per Trendlyne data. Brent crude eased to around $88 a barrel, down slightly on the day and giving Indian markets some relief on the import-cost side even as the currency stayed pinned near its recent lows.

Takeaway Domestic funds absorbing foreign selling almost one-for-one, week after week, is now such a consistent pattern that FII flows have stopped being the swing factor for Indian markets they once were.

SEBI's crackdown on alleged Sensex closing-auction manipulation keeps market-structure worries alive

SEBI issued a 46-page ex-parte interim order against Copthall Mauritius Investment and Mansi Share and Stock Broking on 19 August, alleging the pair manipulated the Closing Auction Session on 13 August — a weekly Sensex options expiry day — with Copthall accounting for 99.91% of aggressive buy orders in one spike and Mansi cancelling over 99% of a large sell-order block within seconds, regulatory filings show. SEBI estimated wrongful gains of about ₹2.96 crore for Copthall and ₹71.65 lakh for Mansi; the case remains a live reference point given the same auction mechanism produced Thursday's 20-minute, since-reversed flash crash covered in Friday's edition.

Takeaway A few crore of alleged manipulation moving a multi-trillion-rupee benchmark shows how little capital it takes to distort a thinly traded closing window, which is exactly why regulators are moving fast rather than waiting for a bigger case to make an example of.

Finshots principle for today: Finshots' explainer on how traders allegedly gamed the Closing Auction Session breaks down a subtler risk than a single flash crash: a thinly traded auction window is exploitable precisely because a few large, aggressive orders can move a price that thousands of contracts then settle against, and the fix isn't avoiding the mechanism but the regulator moving fast enough that it stays trustworthy. Reading Thursday's flash crash or SEBI's action against Copthall and Mansi as one-off headlines misses the structural point — expiry-day auctions concentrate risk into a narrow window, and that's worth pricing into any options position that settles against that print, not just watching the number the day after.
