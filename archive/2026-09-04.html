AI & Tech

OpenAI, Anthropic and SpaceXAI’s Grok all go down within hours of each other as Nvidia confirms its $12.9 billion Hugging Face acquisition and the US government sides with OpenAI against The New York Times on fair use, while an independent researcher’s 4.5-billion-record TikTok scrape lands on Hugging Face, Thomson Reuters discloses a breach that hit court records across a dozen US states and Ontario, and Dell says AI server orders pushed its backlog to a record $95 billion

OpenAI, Anthropic and Grok all go down within hours of each other

On Thursday 3 September, ChatGPT and Codex hit a routing error beginning around 7:43am PT, Anthropic's Claude models — including Fable 5.1, Mythos 5.1 and Opus 5 — had issues from about 6:30am PT that were resolved by 9:16am PT, and a SpaceXAI data-center problem in Memphis knocked Grok offline for more than three hours, per The Register. SpaceXAI apologized for the Memphis outage; Anthropic hasn't said whether its issue was related, and major infrastructure providers including AWS, Azure and Cloudflare reported no outages of their own that morning.

Takeaway Three unrelated outages landing within the same few hours is either coincidence or a sign these companies now share more infrastructure and dependencies than any of them advertise.

Nvidia confirms its $12.9 billion Hugging Face acquisition

Nvidia signed a definitive agreement on 2 September to acquire Hugging Face for $12.93 billion — about $11.9 billion in cash to shareholders plus up to $1 billion in retention equity — confirming weeks of reports, per TechCrunch. The deal, Nvidia's second-largest ever after its $20 billion Groq-assets purchase, is expected to close in the first half of 2027 subject to regulatory approval; Hugging Face's platform hosts roughly three million models and half a million datasets used by more than 18 million developers, and CEO Jensen Huang said the company will keep supporting open-source and open-weight models.

Takeaway The company that sells the picks and shovels for the AI gold rush just bought the town square everyone digs from.

TechCrunch →

US government sides with OpenAI and Microsoft against The New York Times

The Justice Department filed a brief in Manhattan federal court on 2 September backing OpenAI and Microsoft's fair-use defense in The New York Times' copyright suit over AI training data, arguing the "creative possibilities and public benefits" of large language model training outweigh any competitive harm to publishers, per PYMNTS. It's reportedly the first time the US government has weighed in on the wave of AI-training copyright cases brought by authors, publishers, music labels and news outlets; a Times spokesperson said the administration is "siding with a handful of trillion-dollar AI companies at the expense of the countless American creators whose work they stole."

Takeaway A government brief isn't a ruling, but it tells every publisher suing an AI company exactly how uphill this fight just got.

PYMNTS →

Independent researcher publishes a 4.5-billion-record TikTok dataset on Hugging Face

An independent researcher who scraped TikTok's private Android API for three weeks — using anonymous device registrations, no login required — uploaded roughly 289GB across 27 compressed files, covering 4.5 billion video records with captions, engagement counts, sound and posting-time metadata, to Hugging Face, per AI Weekly. The dataset's own listing acknowledges the scrape violated TikTok's terms of service and flags that captions likely count as personal data under GDPR, placing compliance risk on whoever downloads it; the scraping reportedly cost about $950 a month to run.

Takeaway The gap between "technically public" and "legally fine to redistribute" just got measured in billions of records instead of one.

Anthropic lets regulated customers keep Claude's safety monitoring in their own cloud

Anthropic introduced Enterprise Frontier Safeguards this week, letting customers store Claude usage and misuse-detection data in their own AWS S3, Azure Blob or Google Cloud Storage buckets with customer-managed encryption keys instead of Anthropic's own infrastructure, per CNBC and Anthropic's announcement. Built with more than 100 customers including banks represented through the Analysis and Resilience Center for Systemic Risk, the offering combines zero data retention with automated misuse monitoring at no extra charge, rolling out in phases this fall across Claude Code, Claude Enterprise, Bedrock, Azure and Google's Agent Platform.

Takeaway Giving up a monitoring advantage to keep sensitive data off its own servers is Anthropic betting that enterprise trust is worth more than the visibility it's trading away.

CNBC →

Thomson Reuters discloses a breach that hit US and Canadian court records

Thomson Reuters disclosed that its C-Track case-management software was breached, with files taken as early as March but not discovered until 30 June, exposing court records across at least a dozen US jurisdictions — including Ohio, Pennsylvania and Tennessee — plus Ontario, per Help Net Security. Some exposed records may include names and other personal information, and certain sealed or redacted filings may have been affected; the company says there's no evidence its financial-transaction systems were touched or that the data has been misused so far.

Takeaway A three-month gap between the breach and its discovery is the real story here — the exposure window was longer than the investigation that followed it.

Help Net Security →

Dell says record AI server orders pushed its backlog to $95 billion

Dell booked a record $60.9 billion in AI server orders this quarter, recognized $16.4 billion in AI server revenue and exited with a record $95 billion backlog, while posting overall quarterly revenue of $47 billion, up 58% year-on-year, per Yahoo Finance. The company raised its full-year revenue guidance by $25 billion to $192 billion and now expects AI server revenue to triple to $74 billion for the year; the backlog alone is roughly 84% of Dell's entire prior-year revenue.

Takeaway An AI order backlog worth 84% of last year's total revenue is one more data point in the "AI spending bubble" debate — except this one is a contracted order book, not a valuation.

Product & Business

India’s startup week spans satellites, battery-swapping and forex as PhonePe pushes UPI to feature phones and Nirmala Sitharaman wraps a two-day G20 finance meet in North Carolina, while McKinsey finds a third of companies are now skipping software purchases to build with AI instead, and Amazon and Walmart turn fraud prevention into a retail product

Kepler Aerospace raises $8 million to build swarming ISR satellites

Bengaluru-based Kepler Aerospace raised an $8 million seed round led by Blue Ashva Capital and co-led by Finvolve, to build what it calls the world's first autonomous swarming satellite constellation for intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance, StartupTalky reported on 1 September. The company plans to use the funds to scale its avionics-as-a-service and mission-operations businesses alongside the satellite build-out.

Takeaway A defence-tech satellite startup raising from a domestic VC, not a government contract, is a sign India's private capital is now willing to fund space-based ISR on its own.

StartupTalky →

Battery-swapping startup Yuma Energy raises $35 million, acquires Grinntech

Yuma Energy raised a $35 million Series A led by Magna International and used part of the round to acquire fellow battery-swapping startup Grinntech, expanding its charging and swap-station infrastructure for electric two- and three-wheelers across India, per StartupTalky's 1 September funding roundup.

Takeaway Buying a smaller rival the same week you raise a Series A is Yuma securing market share before India's battery-swapping market settles into just one or two standards.

EV-charger maker Zenergize raises $4 million pre-Series A

Zenergize raised a $4 million pre-Series A round — a mix of equity and venture debt — led by Giraffe Studios with participation from existing investors and industrial entrepreneurs, Tech Startups reported on 1 September. The company plans to use the funds to expand manufacturing capacity, strengthen its engineering and R&D team, and build out its sales and service network.

Takeaway A four-million-dollar round barely registers next to India's IPO headlines this month, but it's exactly the kind of unglamorous hardware bet that decides whether EV-charging infrastructure actually gets built.

Niyo to acquire Capital India Finance's RemitX forex business for ₹11.4 crore

Fintech startup Niyo signed a definitive agreement to acquire the RemitX forex and cross-border payments business from listed NBFC Capital India Finance for ₹11.4 crore, through its wholly owned, RBI-licensed subsidiary Kanji Forex, Inc42 reported. The deal adds a 32-branch offline network and more than 2,500 partners to Niyo's digital-first forex business, with over 200 RemitX employees expected to join Kanji Forex; it's expected to close by 31 October, subject to regulatory approval.

Takeaway A digital-only fintech buying a 32-branch physical network is Niyo betting that forex customers still want a counter to walk into, not just an app.

PhonePe launches UPI 123Pay to bring digital payments to feature phones

PhonePe launched UPI 123Pay, an SMS-based payment service designed to work on feature phones and in areas with limited 2G connectivity, aiming to onboard what it calls "the next 300 million users" into digital payments, per MediaNama. The service will come pre-bundled on select Nokia, HMD, Lava and Itel handsets, with PhonePe exploring partnerships with other manufacturers.

Takeaway India's UPI story has mostly been a smartphone story — this is the country's biggest payments app admitting the next 300 million users won't be reached that way.

MediaNama →

Sitharaman wraps two-day G20 finance meet in Asheville with a World Bank sit-down

Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman led India's delegation at the G20 Finance Ministers and Central Bank Governors meeting in Asheville, North Carolina, on 31 August-1 September, pushing for deeper trade, investment and private-capital cooperation in bilateral talks including one with World Bank President Ajay Banga, per ETV Bharat. The meeting's broader agenda covered global growth, economic imbalances, sovereign debt and resilient supply chains.

Takeaway A finance minister spending two days on bilateral sidebars rather than the main summit stage is usually where the actual deals get shaped.

ETV Bharat →

McKinsey: a third of companies are skipping software purchases to build with AI instead

A McKinsey State of AI survey of 1,719 respondents across 97 countries found 32% of organizations have decided against buying off-the-shelf software, opting to build it internally using agentic coding tools instead, per Digital Applied's coverage of the report. The tech (41%) and healthcare (39%) sectors lead the shift, and it's most pronounced among "high performers" attributing at least 5% of EBIT to AI, where nearly half now skip software purchases entirely, compared with 31% of their peers.

Takeaway SaaS companies have spent a decade selling "buy, don't build" — agentic coding tools are the first real reason enough customers might stop listening.

Amazon and Walmart turn fraud prevention into a retail product

Amazon rolled out a new Alexa feature letting shoppers verify suspicious emails, texts and calls before acting on them, while Walmart is linking identity, transaction, returns and customer-contact data to treat fraud as a behavioral pattern rather than a single flagged payment, per PYMNTS. Separately, Walmart is opening its GoLocal delivery and Store Assist fulfilment tools to Salesforce's retail clients, extending its logistics network beyond its own stores.

Takeaway When the two biggest retailers start selling their fraud-detection systems as a feature rather than a back-office cost, it means they've decided trust itself is now a competitive product.

Sensex, Nifty close lower for a third straight session as Iran tensions weigh

The Sensex fell 417.49 points (−0.55%) to 76,152.86 and the Nifty 50 dropped 41 points (−0.17%) to 23,873.45 on 3 September, extending losses to a third straight session, per Business Standard. Nifty Realty led gains with over 2%, and Media, Private Bank, PSU Bank and Bank also outperformed, while IT, Auto, FMCG and Healthcare underperformed as renewed US strikes on Iran kept investors risk-off.

Takeaway A third losing session where banking and realty hold up while IT, auto and FMCG drag is India rotating away from anything exposed to global trade and rate uncertainty, not a broad sell-off.

Business Standard →

Hexaware Technologies falls 4% after CEO Srikrishna Ramakarthikeyan steps down

Hexaware Technologies shares fell as much as 4% to ₹522.95 after the IT services firm said CEO Srikrishna "Keech" Ramakarthikeyan will step down from his role and the board effective 28 October, per BusinessToday. The company has not yet named a successor.

Takeaway A stock repricing within minutes of a CEO-exit announcement, before any successor is even named, is markets pricing leadership risk faster than boards can communicate a plan for it.

Godrej Consumer falls 5% as brokerages turn cautious on growth

Godrej Consumer Products shares fell over 5% intraday on 3 September after brokerages turned cautious on the company's growth outlook following its business-update call, per Business Standard.

Takeaway A 5% move on a cautious brokerage note, not an earnings miss, shows how much Indian consumer-stock pricing now runs on forward guidance rather than results already in hand.

Business Standard →

Brent spikes toward $99 on fresh Iran strikes, even as Wall Street rallies on Fed's Waller

Brent crude jumped as high as $99.38 a barrel intraday on 3 September after renewed US airstrikes on Iranian missile and radar infrastructure, before settling back into the $95-97 range, per Fortune. Hours later, Wall Street rallied — the Dow gained 624 points (+1.18%) to 53,686.11, the S&P 500 rose 1.06% to 7,747.71 and the Nasdaq climbed 1.4% to 26,584.06 — after Fed Governor Christopher Waller said he's leaning toward supporting a rate hold at September's meeting if inflation data due 11 September confirms continued cooling, pulling the 10-year Treasury yield back from its recent 20-month high, per Yahoo Finance and CNBC.

Takeaway Oil spiking on a Middle East strike and US stocks rallying on a dovish Fed governor happened in the same news cycle — a reminder that a single day's market story is rarely just one story.

Gold and silver rebound in India after a week of losses

24-karat gold rose to ₹154,780 per 10 grams and silver climbed to ₹237,790 per kilogram in the Indian bullion market on 3 September, both snapping a multi-day slide that had pulled gold down more than 5% over the previous week, per StartupTalky's daily bullion update; MCX gold futures gained about 1.2% and Comex gold touched $4,490.30 an ounce.

Takeaway Bullion rebounding the same day Wall Street rallies on rate-cut hopes is gold and equities briefly agreeing on the same story — that's the part worth watching, not either move alone.

Zerodha Varsity principle for today: Hexaware fell 4% within minutes of announcing its CEO's exit — before anyone knew who'd replace him, with no fresh earnings and no new numbers behind it, just one piece of information hitting the market. That's the efficient-market idea in its most visible form: prices don't wait for confirmation, they move on any information a rational investor would find relevant, and they move on it almost immediately. Zerodha Varsity's introduction to technical analysis leans on this same assumption — that price already reflects everything currently known about a stock, which is exactly why so much of technical analysis is built on reading price and volume rather than re-deriving the news behind them. It's a useful humility check for any retail investor: by the time you've read the headline, the price has usually already moved to price it in.
