Massachusetts becomes the fault line as Anthropic backs strict AI rules and OpenAI, Google fight them

A Massachusetts Senate proposal that would require AI developers to hire independent evaluators to test their models for catastrophic risk every four months — with results made public, though the state couldn't use them to halt development — has split the industry down its usual line, per PYMNTS and Benzinga. Anthropic calls it “the clearest and strongest AI legislation in the country” and has hired lobbying firm Tremont Strategies Group to back it; OpenAI and Google are pushing instead for an Illinois-style annual third-party audit, with OpenAI warning the stricter reviews could slow releases of the very cybersecurity models meant to guard against the risks lawmakers are targeting.

Takeaway Anthropic backing the toughest state AI bill in the country while its two biggest rivals lobby against it looks less like a safety-philosophy split than a fight over whose release cycle slows down more.

Fluidstack's valuation more than doubles to $18 billion on Google and Anthropic's compute race

AI-infrastructure startup Fluidstack is in talks to raise roughly $1 billion at an $18 billion valuation — up from $7.5 billion just months ago — with Jane Street and Leopold Aschenbrenner's Situational Awareness fund discussing co-leading the round and Morgan Stanley advising, Forbes reported on 3 September. Google has already put up a $1.8 billion backstop on Fluidstack's data-center leases and is discussing an equity stake, while Fluidstack is separately building the Texas and New York sites underpinning Anthropic's roughly $50 billion compute partnership.

Takeaway A neocloud most people outside the industry have never heard of doubling its valuation in months says more about how tight AI compute supply really is than any single earnings call.

Forbes →

Anthropic's own IPO investors are pushing for more disclosure than the company has given so far

Prospective investors in Anthropic's IPO are pressing the company for revenue metrics beyond its standard financials — specifically revenue per token and per gigawatt of compute, and whether it books cloud-reseller revenue gross or net of the cloud provider's cut, per Cryptobriefing. The scrutiny follows Anthropic's annualized revenue run rate jumping from about $9 billion at the end of 2025 to roughly $47 billion in May and over $65 billion by the end of July; Amazon holds about 21% of the company and Alphabet about 15%.

Takeaway Growth this steep is exactly what invites the hardest accounting questions — investors want to know how much of that $65 billion run rate Anthropic actually keeps.

Cryptobriefing →

Google, Anthropic and OpenAI each roll out dueling cybersecurity AI models — and their own gatekeeping rules

All three frontier labs used the same week to unveil cyber-focused AI models paired with new access controls, per The Hacker News. Google's Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber, which it says beats larger rival models at autonomous vulnerability discovery, is limited to trusted defenders through a new Fairwind Program; Anthropic paired Claude Mythos 5.1 with Enterprise Frontier Safeguards, combining zero data retention with misuse detection; and OpenAI's Private Safety Processing for GPT-6 Astra ships with its own warning that the safeguards may wrongly flag legitimate security work as misuse.

Takeaway Every major lab now agrees its cyber models are too dangerous for an open API key — the disagreement is entirely about whose gatekeeping process to trust.

The Hacker News →

A maximum-severity Magento zero-day gets an emergency patch after already planting backdoors

Adobe issued an emergency hotfix on 7 September for CVE-2026-75650 — rated a maximum 10.0 on the CVSS scale — after Dutch security firm Sansec found attackers actively exploiting the flaw, dubbed StyleSmuggler, since 4 September, per BleepingComputer. The bug let unauthenticated attackers inject PHP into Magento's template system via a fake “failed payment” email to install a small Rust-based backdoor disguised as a background process; every version from 2.4.4 through the just-released 2.4.9 was affected, on a platform installed across more than 160,000 sites.

Takeaway A fully patched store getting compromised as easily as a neglected one is the detail that should worry Magento admins most — this wasn't a bug staying current would have avoided.

BleepingComputer →

Figure signs a $3.5 billion compute deal with Nscale to keep its humanoid robots fed

Robotics startup Figure said on 3 September it has signed a strategic partnership with AI cloud provider Nscale for up to 100,000 Nvidia Vera Rubin GPUs, with an initial $3.5 billion compute commitment the companies intend to scale past $6 billion, according to Figure's own announcement and Forbes. Nscale is also taking an equity stake in Figure, with first deployments targeted for the second half of 2027 in Barstow, Texas, to train the Helix model that powers Figure's 03 and upcoming 04 humanoid robots.

Takeaway A robotics company committing billions to compute before its product has scaled shows how much humanoid robotics has adopted the AI industry's habit of buying capacity ahead of demand.

Reliance's Jio Platforms readies investor roadshows for what could be India's largest-ever IPO

Jio Platforms plans to formally begin marketing its IPO to investors as early as next week, with roadshows across the US, Singapore, Hong Kong, London and the Middle East, per Bloomberg and Outlook Business, with the listing targeted for a November window around Navratri-Diwali. The offering is expected to raise around ₹37,000 crore, which would make it the largest IPO in Indian corporate history.

Takeaway Six weeks of global roadshows for a domestic listing is Reliance treating this less like an Indian IPO and more like the international debut it's actually trying to be.

Swiggy exits its B2B logistics arm, swapping it for a 3.2% stake in udaan's parent

Swiggy's subsidiary Swiggy Networks is transferring its entire stake in Lynks Logistics to Trustroot Internet, udaan's parent, in a share-swap deal valued at about ₹500 crore, per Inc42. Swiggy will end up with roughly 3.2% of udaan, including an additional ₹75 crore primary investment; Lynks generated ₹668 crore in revenue in FY26, about 2.9% of Swiggy's consolidated revenue.

Takeaway Trading a business for equity in the buyer, rather than cash, tells you Swiggy sees more upside in a udaan stake than in running a sub-3%-of-revenue logistics unit itself.

Inc42 →

Pixxel raises $100 million in India's largest-ever spacetech funding round

Bengaluru-based satellite-imaging startup Pixxel raised $100 million in a Series C round co-led by Temasek and Seraphim Space, with new investors 360 ONE Asset and IMM Investment joining existing backers Radical Ventures and growX, per Business Standard — taking its total funding to $195 million. The money will fund Pixxel's next-generation Honeybee hyperspectral satellite constellation, including new shortwave-infrared, synthetic-aperture-radar and ultra-high-resolution optical capabilities.

Takeaway A $100 million round for satellites that see in wavelengths humans can't is a bet that the next edge in earth-observation data is spectral, not just sharper pictures.

Business Standard →

Voice-AI startup Navana.ai raises ₹40 crore led by Ronnie Screwvala to go deeper into BFSI

Navana.ai, which builds a sovereign, full-stack voice-AI platform for regulated enterprises, raised ₹40 crore in a Series A round led by upGrad co-founder Ronnie Screwvala, with Antler India, Neysa.ai founder Sharad Sanghi and WestBridge Capital's Sandeep Singhal also participating, per Inc42. The startup's speech models work across 12 Indian languages and 45 dialects and have already logged more than 100 million voice-AI minutes for BFSI clients including Bajaj Finserv and Ujjivan Small Finance Bank.

Takeaway A hundred million voice-AI minutes already booked before this raise is the real signal — Navana isn't funding a product search, it's funding scale-up of something regulated clients are already paying for.

Inc42 →

Adani Power lands a letter of intent to acquire GVK Energy's 330 MW hydro plant

Adani Power received a Letter of Intent from GVK Energy's resolution professional on the morning of 7 September to acquire the company through its insolvency resolution process, per Business Today. GVK Energy owns a 330 MW hydroelectric plant in Uttarakhand through subsidiary Alaknanda Hydro Power; the deal already has Competition Commission clearance from May and now needs sign-off from the National Company Law Tribunal in Hyderabad.

Takeaway Adani picking up hydro capacity through an insolvency process rather than a fresh build is the cheaper, faster route to clean-energy capacity that India's stressed-asset market keeps making available.

Business Today →

Karamtara Engineering leads a 12-company mainboard IPO week starting September 9

Power-transmission and renewable-energy component maker Karamtara Engineering opens its ₹875 crore IPO on 9 September — ₹675 crore fresh issue plus a ₹200 crore offer for sale — with a price band of ₹241-254 a share, part of a mainboard calendar carrying a dozen companies to market this week, per PNI News. The company's revenue grew to ₹4,316 crore in FY26 from ₹3,165 crore a year earlier, with profit up to ₹229 crore from ₹139 crore.

Takeaway A component supplier growing revenue 36% and profit 64% in a year is a rarer find on this IPO calendar than the loss-making consumer names getting most of the attention.

PNI News →

Campbell's cuts 13% of its salaried workforce as budget-conscious US shoppers pull back

Soup-and-snacks maker Campbell's has cut roughly 515 salaried jobs — 13% of that workforce — through layoffs and a voluntary early-retirement program, and is closing two snack plants, as part of a plan to cut costs by $500 million, per Fox Business. The company's net sales fell 5% to $9.7 billion in the fiscal year ended 2 August, and it plans to raise prices on about 60% of its products.

Takeaway Cutting staff and raising prices in the same announcement is Campbell's admitting the easy lever — volume growth — isn't coming back anytime soon.

Kepler Aerospace raises its first outside funding — $8 million to build swarming spy satellites

Bengaluru-based Kepler Aerospace raised $8 million in a seed round led by Blue Ashva Capital with Finvolve and India Accelerator, its first external funding since founding in 2018, per Entrackr. The money will fund six swarming intelligence-surveillance-reconnaissance satellites and scale its mission-operations and avionics-as-a-service businesses; the company has also secured two iDEX prime contracts backed by roughly $4 million in defence-ministry grants.

Takeaway Eight years of bootstrapping before a first funding round, already backed by defence contracts rather than a pitch deck, is a very different startup story than most spacetech raises this year.

Nifty IT sheds 2% as Infosys leads losses on renewed Fed rate-hike bets

The Nifty IT index fell as much as 2-3% on 7 September, with Infosys down as much as 3.8% intraday and LTIMindtree, Persistent Systems, Wipro and Coforge each shedding close to 3%, per Business Standard. The trigger was Friday's stronger-than-expected US payrolls data — 162,000 jobs added against roughly 56,000 expected — which raised the odds the Fed holds or even raises rates, a scenario investors read as bad news for IT clients' discretionary tech spending.

Takeaway The same jobs report that was too good for the Fed's liking last week is now the reason IT stocks fell this week — the market still can't decide whether strong US data helps or hurts risk assets.

Business Standard →

Iran declares a “restricted zone” near Hormuz days after the US strikes three of its oil tankers

Iran's top security official said Tehran will establish a “restricted zone” extending from the US Navy's blockade line into the Persian Gulf, placing any ship that enters it on Iran's sanctions list, per the Washington Times on 7 September. The move follows US strikes that permanently disabled two Iranian crude carriers and destroyed a third near Kharg Island and the Gulf of Oman, in retaliation for Iranian ballistic-missile attacks on US Navy warships.

Takeaway A declared exclusion zone is Iran shifting from retaliating for individual strikes to trying to control who can move through the Gulf at all — a bigger escalation than another round of tanker attacks.

Washington Times →

Brent holds above $97 as the rupee climbs to a two-month high despite the same oil shock

Brent crude traded near $97.03 a barrel on 8 September, still carrying most of last week's 9%-plus surge on Gulf tensions, per Trading Economics. The rupee, meanwhile, strengthened to around 94.50 to the dollar — a more-than-two-month high — helped by persistent RBI intervention and dollar-selling flows, even as the same Hormuz risk holding crude near multi-month highs would typically be expected to pressure the currency of a major oil importer.

Takeaway A stronger rupee against a backdrop of $97 oil is the RBI actively fighting the market's natural reaction — worth watching how long that intervention can hold if crude keeps climbing.

Bitcoin cools toward $79,000 as risk appetite fades with equities

Bitcoin traded around $78,982 on 8 September, down about 1.8% on the day, after briefly touching $82,000 earlier in the week, per CaptainAltcoin. The pullback tracks the broader risk-off mood in equities, with traders watching the $78,500 level as near-term support ahead of this week's US inflation data.

Takeaway Bitcoin falling in step with IT stocks and equities, rather than moving independently, is more evidence it's currently trading as a risk asset first and a hedge a distant second.

CaptainAltcoin →

NSE's own blockbuster IPO gets closer as SEBI clears the path to a September 11 price band

The National Stock Exchange, which received SEBI's no-objection certificate on 19 August, is expected to announce its IPO price band on 11 September, targeting a valuation of roughly ₹5.2-5.3 lakh crore with an offer-for-sale of up to 14.89 crore shares, per multiple reports including Times Bull. Street estimates put the issue size above ₹30,000 crore, which would make it larger than Hyundai Motor India's ₹27,870 crore listing.

Takeaway India's own stock exchange going public at a size that could beat Hyundai's record IPO is the kind of self-referential milestone that only happens once.
