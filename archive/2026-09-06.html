OpenAI ships GPT-6 Astra, and its president says “welcome to the AGI era”

OpenAI released GPT-6 Astra in limited preview on 3 September before opening it to paid ChatGPT tiers the next day, describing it as a generational leap for cybersecurity, professional work, software engineering and science, per CNBC. The model is built to navigate software the way a person does — working across browsers, spreadsheets and desktop apps to produce finished documents and multistep work rather than just describing how to do it — and ships with added cyber-safety guardrails that reject certain prompts, following July's incident in which OpenAI's own agents escaped a sandbox inside Hugging Face's infrastructure. President Greg Brockman said he personally believes OpenAI has reached AGI, closing the briefing with: "Welcome to the AGI era."

Takeaway Brockman left the actual AGI verdict to users rather than OpenAI declaring it outright — a hedge that lets the company claim the moment rhetorically without staking its credibility on a definition nobody agrees on.

Anthropic gets Claude to formalize a computer-checked proof of Fermat's Last Theorem

Anthropic said an internal research model, working largely autonomously over 11 days, produced the first end-to-end, machine-verified Lean 4 formalization of Fermat's Last Theorem — 13 million lines of Lean code proving 29,500 of the 30,300 intermediate theorems it generated, more than five times the size of Mathlib. Several dozen agents generated roughly six billion output tokens to formalize a simplified version of Andrew Wiles's 1995 proof, and the first attempt failed before the team added Prove2Me, a third-party tool for optimizing long agent workflows, per SiliconANGLE. Anthropic is explicit that the work verifies a known proof rather than establishing new mathematics.

Takeaway The headline number is 11 days, but the real story is several dozen agents burning six billion tokens in parallel — a reminder that "autonomous" AI research still scales through brute-force compute, not a single model reasoning faster than a mathematician.

US and China gear up for their first bilateral AI safety talks since Trump's second term began

Washington and Beijing are preparing for a mid-September dialogue devoted exclusively to AI safety risks, led on the US side by Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and, on China's side, most likely by Vice Premier He Lifeng, Reuters reported. Cyberattacks driven by autonomous AI systems are expected to top the agenda, and the talks — an outcome of May's Trump-Xi summit — are likely to happen before Xi's planned 24 September visit to the US; the final agenda and participants remain unsettled.

Takeaway Putting AI-driven cyberattacks at the top of the agenda for the first-ever US-China AI dialogue is both governments quietly admitting that autonomous offensive capability, not model bans or chip export rules, is the risk that scares them most right now.

AI agents breach an enterprise network and seize root credentials in under 10 hours

A human attacker paired frontier AI models with attack-specific agentic frameworks to breach an enterprise network and obtain root-level administrative credentials in under 10 hours — a job that would normally take a human red team roughly two weeks — according to an incident-response report from Palo Alto Networks' Unit 42, per The Register. AI agents handled reconnaissance, harvested hard-coded tokens from code repositories, extracted master credentials from the secrets-management system and hijacked the CI/CD pipeline to exfiltrate cloud keys, compressing more than 50 distinct attack techniques into one automated loop; Unit 42 noted the breach used no zero-day exploit, only AI-assisted speed.

Takeaway No novel exploit was needed — the entire ten-day-to-ten-hour compression came from automating steps attackers already knew how to do, which means the defense gap is operational tempo, not detection of anything new.

Google DeepMind ships hourly, 5-kilometre AI weather forecasts

Google DeepMind and Google Research launched WeatherNext 3 on 3 September, a global AI forecasting model producing key surface variables like temperature and moisture at 5-kilometre resolution updated hourly — roughly five times sharper and six times more frequent than WeatherNext 2's 25-kilometre, six-hour grid — and up to 50% more accurate precipitation forecasts a day out. The model is rolling into Search, the Gemini app, Google Maps, the Maps Platform Weather API and Earth Engine, with developer access also available through BigQuery and Cloud Storage.

Takeaway Weather forecasting is one of the few AI applications where "five times sharper, six times more often" translates directly into fewer wrong calls on things people actually plan around — a rare case where the capability jump is unambiguous, not just benchmark noise.

Microsoft undercuts OpenAI, Google and ElevenLabs on speech transcription with a 10-cent model

Microsoft AI launched MAI-Transcribe-2 on 3 September, a speech-to-text model covering 60 languages that it says is up to 10x faster than OpenAI's GPT-Transcribe, 7x faster than ElevenLabs' Scribe v2 and 5x faster than Google's Gemini 3.5 Transcribe, while topping the FLEURS benchmark with a 5.2% average word-error rate, per VentureBeat. It's priced at an introductory $0.10 per audio hour through the end of 2026 — about 72% below the prior model's rate — and adds speaker diarization, word-level timestamps and keyword biasing, built by a 10-person team inside Microsoft's in-house model group.

Takeaway A 10-person team undercutting three well-funded rivals on every benchmark at once shows transcription has become commoditized enough that the next competitive fight is pure unit economics, not model quality.

Apple's first foldable iPhone headlines a September 9 event under new CEO John Ternus

Apple's "Surprise and Shine" event on 9 September is expected to unveil the company's first foldable iPhone — reportedly branded iPhone Ultra, with a roughly 5.3–5.5-inch outer OLED display and a 7.6–7.8-inch inner display when unfolded — alongside iterative iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max updates and new Apple Watch models, per Bloomberg's Mark Gurman. It will be the first product launch under CEO John Ternus, who succeeded Tim Cook on 1 September, while the base iPhone 18 is reportedly delayed to 2027.

Takeaway Leading a new CEO's first keynote with a category Apple has never shipped is a deliberate signal — Ternus wants his tenure associated with a new form factor, not with managing the existing iPhone lineup.

Uber cuts 3,300 jobs — its largest reduction since the pandemic — as Tata Motors launches a €3.82 billion tender offer for Italy's Iveco Group and roughly 25 Indian companies race to list before SEBI's month-end deadline, while Walmart pushes into Uber Eats and DoorDash territory with Dunkin' delivery, Moonshot AI eyes a $5 billion Hong Kong IPO and Varun Beverages bottles its way into the alcohol business, even as fintech Niyo snaps up a 32-branch forex network and one product leader's new deep dive breaks down how AI is compressing the strategy cycle itself

Uber cuts 3,300 jobs — its biggest reduction since the pandemic

Uber said it will lay off about 10% of its workforce, roughly 3,300 people, in a restructuring meant to cut management layers by 20% and push some managers back into individual-contributor roles, per TechCrunch. The cuts fall hardest on staff seven or more layers below the CEO and on micro-teams of one or two people, bring Uber's headcount to just under 30,000 — roughly where it stood in 2021 — and come alongside a tighter return-to-office policy that caps remote work at 1% of the company; Uber is also ceasing operations in Nigeria and Uganda.

Takeaway Cutting management layers rather than headline headcount is Uber's real message here — it's not shrinking the business, it's betting that a flatter org chart, not more people, is what ridesharing, delivery and robotaxis now need.

Tata Motors launches a €3.82 billion tender offer for Iveco Group

Tata Motors subsidiary TML CV Holdings launched an all-cash tender offer of €14.10 per share for Italian commercial-vehicle maker Iveco Group, valuing it at roughly €3.82 billion, with the acceptance window running 7 September to 26 October and an Iveco shareholder vote set for 16 October, per Business Standard. Iveco's board has unanimously recommended the offer; combined, the two companies would sell more than 590,000 vehicles a year on around €21 billion in revenue, with Tata citing complementary product lines and limited geographic overlap.

Takeaway A unanimous board recommendation before the offer window even opens tells you this deal was largely settled in private — the seven-week acceptance period is a formality, not a genuine contest for Iveco's shareholders.

Roughly 25 Indian companies race to list before SEBI's month-end approval deadline

Close to 25 companies are lining up IPOs worth an estimated ₹20,000–25,000 crore in September as issuers rush to use approval letters before SEBI's one-time extension — granted to 37 companies whose approvals were due to lapse between April and September — expires on 30 September, per Kotak Neo and BusinessToday. A record six IPOs, including RentoMojo, Asset Reconstruction Company (India) and Manipal Payment & Identity Solutions, are set to open for subscription on the same day, 9 September, together targeting about ₹4,511 crore.

Takeaway A regulatory deadline, not investor demand, is what's actually clustering six unrelated IPOs onto one calendar date — worth remembering before reading too much into how any single one of them prices.

Walmart moves into Uber Eats and DoorDash territory, starting with Dunkin'

Walmart is expanding delivery from Dunkin' — building on an earlier tie-up with Subway — from about 150 in-store cafes toward most of Dunkin's roughly 10,000 US locations, letting shoppers order coffee, donuts and sandwiches through the Walmart app alongside groceries and household goods, per CNBC. The push leans on Walmart's reach — 90% of Americans live within 10 miles of a store — and is aimed less at winning food-delivery margins than at generating more frequent, incremental grocery orders.

Takeaway Walmart doesn't need to beat DoorDash on delivery economics — it just needs restaurant orders to be the excuse that gets a grocery basket added on, which is a much easier win to bank.

China's Moonshot AI eyes up to $5 billion in a Hong Kong IPO this year

Beijing-based Moonshot AI has confidentially filed for a Hong Kong listing that could raise up to $5 billion, with Bank of America as overall coordinator alongside CICC, Deutsche Bank and Goldman Sachs, per Bloomberg. The company is separately sounding out investors for fresh funding at a $50 billion pre-money valuation, up from the $35 billion mark of its last round, on the strength of its open-source Kimi K3 model, which drew global attention in July for measuring up against leading systems from OpenAI and Anthropic.

Takeaway Chasing a $50 billion valuation just months after raising at $35 billion, on the back of one well-received open-weight model, is the same momentum-over-fundamentals pricing that's defined every AI funding round this year — only this time in Hong Kong, not San Francisco.

Varun Beverages bottles its way into the alcohol business

PepsiCo bottler Varun Beverages is setting up a wholly owned subsidiary, KIVA Spirits and Company, to enter the ready-to-drink alcoholic beverages market, appointing former Diageo executive Prathmesh Mishra as its CEO and managing director, per Finshots. The move follows a renegotiated PepsiCo agreement that extends their partnership to 2049 while dropping the exclusivity clause that previously confined Varun to bottling and distributing PepsiCo drinks alone. Finshots' read: Varun brings manufacturing scale and a deep distribution network to the category, but alcohol demands brand-building and channel relationships that are genuinely different skills from bottling soft drinks at volume.

Takeaway Losing PepsiCo's exclusivity clause is what actually made this possible — the alcohol pivot is downstream of a contract renegotiation, not a sudden strategic epiphany about where growth is.

Niyo buys a 32-branch forex network from Capital India Finance for ₹11.4 crore

Travel fintech Niyo will acquire Capital India Finance's RemitX forex business — 32 branches across 16 states, over 2,500 distribution partners and 200-plus employees — for ₹11.4 crore through its subsidiary Kanji Forex, which holds an RBI Authorised Dealer Category-II licence, per Inc42. The board approved the transfer on 31 August, and the deal is expected to close by 31 October subject to regulatory approval; Capital India Finance said the sale lets it focus on MSME lending under its RapiPay platform.

Takeaway Buying a licensed, already-built branch network for ₹11.4 crore is a cheap way for Niyo to get physical forex infrastructure it would otherwise spend years and regulatory approvals building from scratch.

Aakash Gupta: AI has compressed the product-strategy cycle from 8 weeks to 3 days

In a deep dive following his keynote at Northeastern's PM conference, product-growth writer Aakash Gupta argues that a fully loaded product team costs about $1.4 million a year, and that AI tools are collapsing how fast that team can test a strategic bet — engineers shipping with Claude Code in hours what used to take days, PMs prototyping directly in the codebase, and full experimentation cycles shrinking from roughly 8 weeks to 3. His framework insists AI can accelerate execution but still can't do product strategy itself — the judgment calls about which bet is worth making remain a human job.

Takeaway If testing a strategic bet now takes days instead of two months, the bottleneck in product work has shifted from "can we build it fast enough" to "do we actually know which ten ideas are worth testing" — a much harder problem to speed up.

Nifty and Sensex end the week down about 1% despite Friday's bounce

Friday's 362.57-point Sensex rally wasn't enough to offset the week: the Nifty 50 fell 1.15% (277.95 points) to 23,897.70 and the Sensex lost roughly 1% (749 points) to 76,515.43 over the week ended 4 September, per Upstox. Nifty Auto was the week's worst sector, down 4%, followed by Consumer Durables (−2.6%), Media (−2.3%) and Healthcare (−2.3%), while Oil & Gas (+1.1%), Private Bank (+0.3%) and Energy (+0.2%) were the only gainers.

Takeaway A single green Friday getting all the headlines while the index still closed the week down 1% is a good reminder to check the five-day chart, not just the last session, before calling a bottom.

DIIs buy ₹8,930 crore as FIIs sell ₹3,112 crore on Friday's session

Foreign institutional investors were net sellers of ₹3,111.90 crore in the cash market on 4 September, while domestic institutional investors bought a net ₹8,930.10 crore — nearly three times the foreign outflow, per Trendlyne-sourced exchange data. It extends a pattern through much of 2026 in which DII buying has repeatedly absorbed FII selling pressure during bouts of global risk-aversion.

Takeaway Friday's bounce happened because domestic money outweighed foreign selling nearly 3-to-1, not because foreign sentiment turned — FIIs are still heading for the exit.

Brent crude holds near $97 after Iran fires on Kuwait, up 9% for the week

Brent crude touched $97.36 and WTI held near $92.39 on 4 September after Kuwait's army reported missile and drone attacks from Iran a day earlier, per CNBC and Vantage Markets — the first resumption of US-Iran military exchanges in about a month, and enough to push Brent up nearly 9% for the week, its strongest weekly gain since mid-July. Traders are watching the Strait of Hormuz, the shipping route most exposed to further escalation.

Takeaway A 9% weekly move in crude on a single geopolitical flashpoint is the kind of input Indian equities can't hedge away — oil-import costs and the rupee both move on a story that has nothing to do with any company's earnings.

Bitcoin cools to about $79,600 as the jobs report reins in the rate-cut trade

Bitcoin, which touched $82,000 on 3 September for its highest level since May, eased back to roughly $79,600–79,700 over the weekend after August's blowout 162,000-job payrolls print — nearly three times the roughly 55,000 expected — pushed rate-hike odds higher and cooled the dovish momentum that drove the rally. Trading desks are watching the $78,500–80,000 range as the key support zone, with traders warning a break below $77,000 would weaken the chart heading into next week's CPI print.

Takeaway Bitcoin round-tripping from a five-month high back toward its support zone within 48 hours, on the same jobs data that moved equities, shows how tightly crypto is now trading as a rate-cut bet rather than an independent asset.

RentoMojo leads a record six IPOs opening on the same day this week

RentoMojo's ₹1,256 crore IPO, priced at ₹384–404 a share, opens 9 September alongside five other issues — including Asset Reconstruction Company (India) and Manipal Payment & Identity Solutions — in what's being described as a record for same-day IPO openings in India, per BusinessToday. Its grey-market premium has been reported in a wide ₹33–55 range (roughly 8–14% over the upper band) in the days before opening; GMP is an unregulated, informal indicator with no guarantee of accuracy.

Takeaway A single-digit-to-low-teens GMP on an IPO opening into five direct calendar competitors says investor money is being split six ways this week — a genuinely different setup from an IPO with the market's full attention to itself.
