AI & Tech

Hugging Face explores a sale that could value it near $13 billion and Nvidia weighs a stake in Perplexity above $30 billion, as the AI industry's memory crunch forces Amazon to quietly raise prices on Echo, Kindle and Fire TV, and the SEC opens a probe into a hedge fund that nearly imploded betting big on AI stocks

Hugging Face is reportedly exploring a sale that could value it near $13 billion

Hugging Face has hired a bank to gauge buyer interest in a potential sale that could value the AI model-hosting company at $13 billion or more, TechCrunch reported on 24 August, citing an earlier Business Insider report. Talks are described as early, with no bidder named and no deal reached. The company, founded in 2016 by CEO Clément Delangue, Julien Chaumond and Thomas Wolf, last raised outside money in 2023 — a $235 million round led by Salesforce Ventures at a $4.5 billion valuation, with Google, Amazon, Nvidia, Intel, Qualcomm and IBM also participating. Delangue said on a recent TechCrunch Equity podcast episode that the company is “close to profitability.”

Takeaway A potential buyer would be purchasing the community trust that makes Hugging Face useful — the kind of asset that can evaporate the moment it stops feeling neutral.

Nvidia discusses investing in Perplexity at a valuation above $30 billion

Nvidia is in talks to make an equity investment in Perplexity at a valuation exceeding $30 billion, The Information reported on 23 August — more than 50% above the roughly $20 billion valuation the AI search company fetched about a year ago. Perplexity's annualized revenue has climbed above $750 million, up from less than $250 million at the start of 2026, according to the report. Neither company has confirmed a completed transaction, and discussions remain ongoing.

Takeaway Nvidia backing an AI-search rival to Google while also selling Google the chips to compete shows how thoroughly the chipmaker has decided that owning a piece of every layer beats picking a side.

XPeng's humanoid-robot unit raises over $900 million at a $6.3 billion valuation

XPeng's humanoid-robot subsidiary raised more than $900 million — $600 million from outside investors including Alibaba and Tencent, $200 million from XPeng itself and $100 million from executives — valuing the unit at over $6.3 billion, Electrek reported on 24 August, citing Bloomberg. XPeng called it the largest single private funding round yet in China's “embodied intelligence” sector, led by IDG Capital with Gaorong Ventures also participating. The capital will fund mass production of XPeng's IRON humanoid robot, due to begin at year-end with initial deployment at retail and industrial sites, ahead of planned commercial sales in China and overseas in 2027.

Takeaway Chinese EV makers are increasingly funding humanoid robotics as a spinout business rather than a car-company side project, letting car profits absorb early robotics losses.

SEC probes near-collapse of an AI-focused hedge fund run by an ex-OpenAI researcher

The SEC is investigating the near-implosion of Situational Awareness, the AI-focused hedge fund run by former OpenAI researcher Leopold Aschenbrenner, TechCrunch reported on 24 August, with subpoenas sent to Wall Street banks that handled the fund's trading and financing. The fund's assets fell from roughly $45 billion to about $10 billion after a late-July downturn in AI and semiconductor stocks triggered margin calls on its reportedly highly leveraged positions, forcing distressed sales of holdings including SK Hynix and CoreWeave to Citadel. Situational Awareness has not been accused of wrongdoing; the SEC's interest centers on the fund's trades, borrowing and communications with lenders.

Takeaway A hedge fund built entirely on conviction that AI stocks would keep compounding is now the clearest evidence yet that the AI trade can move sharply in both directions.

TechCrunch →

Alibaba launches a document-to-video AI model, a day after a $10 billion share sale

Alibaba Cloud released Wan3.0, an AI model that turns documents, spreadsheets, slides and web pages into videos up to 30 seconds long, Dataconomy reported on 24 August — one day after Alibaba closed a share placement raising roughly HK$80 billion (about $10.2 billion) earmarked for AI infrastructure, per TechNode. The launch came in the same week Alibaba reported a 75% drop in quarterly net profit as capital expenditure hit 67.7 billion yuan, up 75% year-on-year, on its AI buildout; the company has already committed roughly half of a planned 380 billion yuan (about $56.4 billion) in AI investment through 2029.

Takeaway Alibaba is treating quarterly profit as the acceptable collateral damage of an AI infrastructure race it doesn't think it can afford to lose, not as a signal to slow down.

Dataconomy →

Amazon quietly raises Echo, Kindle, Fire TV and eero prices by up to 60% on memory costs

Amazon raised prices across its Echo, Kindle, Fire TV and eero device lines without a formal announcement, Fortune first reported on 21 August, with increases as steep as 60% on some models — the Echo Dot rose from $49.99 to $79.99 and the entry Kindle from $109.99 to $149.99. An Amazon spokesperson confirmed the increases stem from “significant increases” in memory and storage component costs, following similar moves by Apple, Microsoft, Dell, HP, Lenovo and Asus, as a memory-chip shortage driven by AI data-center demand squeezes global DRAM and NAND supply.

Takeaway The AI memory crunch has stopped being an abstract input-cost story for chipmakers and is now showing up directly on price tags for ordinary consumer gadgets.

Product & Business

India's capital markets have their best month on record as a $10 billion deal rush led by the LIC share sale rolls on, even as fresh funding for NeoGeoInfo, CtrlS and voice-AI startup Wispr Flow keeps AI and data-centre infrastructure the hottest category in tech, and Flipkart's senior ranks thin out over an unresolved IPO timeline

India's equity markets head for a record August on a $10 billion deal rush

Almost $10 billion worth of equity deals have been priced in India in August, putting the month on track to be the country's best on record for capital markets, Business Standard reported on 24 August, citing Bloomberg data. The rush was led by the government's $3.2 billion sale of Life Insurance Corporation of India shares, alongside Manipal Health Enterprises' $958 million IPO — which listed on 5 August roughly 11% above its issue price — plus a flurry of block trades and institutional placements. Business Standard attributed the surge to growing domestic mutual fund and insurance buying power, strong retail participation and the return of global funds; nearly all of the roughly two dozen companies that debuted on Indian exchanges in August are trading above their IPO prices.

Takeaway The record month is really a story about who's absorbing the risk — Indian domestic institutions, not foreign funds, are now deep enough to underwrite mega-offerings like LIC's, which quietly reduces India's historic dependence on FII sentiment swings.

Flipkart sees a fresh wave of senior exits as its IPO timeline stays unresolved

Flipkart is facing another round of senior leadership departures, including Gunjan Bhartia (SVP, business finance) and Amer Hussain (VP, supply chain for Grocery and Minutes), Business Standard reported on 21 August. The report ties the churn to uncertainty over the timing of the company's public listing, which weighs particularly on senior employees holding ESOPs; several other directors are said to be evaluating outside offers. Group CEO Kalyan Krishnamurthy said in July there is no defined IPO timeline, after reports that the Walmart-owned company had deprioritized listing plans in favour of profitability. The exits follow earlier departures including group CFO Sriram Venkatraman and former Myntra CEO Nandita Sinha, who left in April and resurfaced at Swiggy as Instamart CEO in August.

Takeaway When a company keeps delaying its IPO to “get profitability right first,” the ESOP-driven brain drain becomes the real cost of that patience.

Geospatial startup NeoGeoInfo raises $20 million Series A to scale GeoAI

Gurugram-based NeoGeoInfo Technologies raised $20 million in a Series A round co-led by SBI-managed Neev Fund II and Aavishkaar Capital, YourStory and Entrackr reported on 24 August. Aavishkaar invested around ₹75 crore, with the balance from Neev Fund II. Founded in 2019 by Sreeramam and Brahmam, NeoGeoInfo provides spatial-intelligence software for government and corporate clients across natural resource management, disaster management, urban governance, utilities and smart cities, and said it will split the new capital roughly equally across deepening its India business, opening international offices and building new software products.

Takeaway A geospatial-AI vendor selling primarily to government and utility clients is a reminder that India's AI investment story isn't only chatbots and coding agents — there's a quieter public-infrastructure layer being built alongside it.

Nikhil Kamath and Sreeram Reddy Vanga invest ₹250 crore in data-centre operator CtrlS

Zerodha co-founder Nikhil Kamath and entrepreneur Sreeram Reddy Vanga invested ₹250 crore in CtrlS Datacenters — ₹200 crore from Kamath and ₹50 crore from Vanga — to expand hyperscale data-centre capacity across India, Deccan Herald and Indian Startup News reported around 22 August. The capital will support CtrlS's infrastructure build-out to meet growing enterprise and hyperscaler demand as AI and cloud workloads accelerate. The investment follows a larger commitment from Canada Pension Plan Investment Board, which agreed in June to put up to ₹7,000 crore into CtrlS, including ₹4,000 crore for an 8.2% stake and up to ₹3,000 crore into a joint venture developing hyperscale data-centre campuses in India.

Takeaway When a prominent public investor like Kamath puts personal capital into data-centre real estate rather than a startup equity round, it signals that AI infrastructure itself, not just the software layered on top, is becoming its own investable asset class in India.

Atomberg files draft IPO papers for a ₹450 crore fresh issue, early backers take partial exit

Appliance maker Atomberg Technologies filed its DRHP with SEBI on 20 August for an IPO comprising a ₹450 crore fresh issue plus an offer-for-sale of up to 7.65 crore shares, Entrackr reported. A91 Partners will sell the largest chunk of shares in the OFS, with Temasek-backed V-Sciences Investments and Jungle Ventures also taking partial exits, while A91 retains roughly 21% post-issue. The filing shows Atomberg's revenue grew 34.8% year-on-year to ₹1,293.77 crore in FY26, with adjusted EBITDA turning positive at ₹37.12 crore versus a loss the year before; since 2019, early investors and founders have together sold about ₹683 crore in secondary shares, per the DRHP.

Takeaway The scale of pre-IPO secondary selling by both founders and early VCs is a useful tell that even a profitable, growing consumer brand is being priced for early-backer liquidity as much as for growth capital.

Voice-AI startup Wispr Flow raises $280 million at a $2 billion valuation

Wispr, maker of the AI dictation app Flow, raised $280 million in a Series B round led by Menlo Ventures at a $2 billion valuation, TechCrunch and Yahoo Finance reported. Existing investors Notable Capital, NEA, Neo, 8VC and MVP Ventures joined new backers including Acrew, Forerunner, Goodwater, Peak XV and Together Fund. The company said it has adoption across more than 125,000 companies and four consecutive quarters of revenue growth exceeding 150%, and used the raise to unveil Canto, its first proprietary speech model, addressing user complaints about a recent dip in Flow's accuracy. The financing brings Wispr's total capital raised to $361 million, just six months after an oversubscribed Series A2.

Takeaway A dictation app raising at a $2 billion valuation only makes sense if investors believe voice, not text, becomes the default way people interact with software — a bigger bet than the product category's name suggests.

A small AI research agent from DeepMind alumni claims to outperform Anthropic and OpenAI

London-based Inherent, founded by Google DeepMind alumni, said its AI agent Faraday — built on the 27-billion-parameter Qwen 3.6 — outperformed Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.8 and OpenAI's GPT-5.5 at independently replicating the findings of published scientific papers, TechCrunch reported on 22 August. The claim comes weeks after Inherent emerged from stealth with a $50 million seed round, positioning a small, specialized agent as competitive with far larger general-purpose frontier models on a narrow but commercially relevant task: research reproduction.

Takeaway If narrow, cheaply-run agents can match frontier models on specific enterprise workflows, it undercuts the assumption that only the biggest labs can win high-value AI product categories.

OpenAI closes the gap on Anthropic among US business users, Ramp data shows

New spend data from corporate-card firm Ramp shows OpenAI narrowing Anthropic's lead in enterprise adoption: as of July, 43.5% of Ramp-tracked US businesses paid for Anthropic subscriptions or API usage versus 39.7% for OpenAI, TechCrunch reported on 20 August. Anthropic had first overtaken OpenAI among Ramp's business customers back in May, with a 41%-to-39% split. The data reflects only spend among Ramp's own customer base — skewed toward US mid-market and growth-stage companies — rather than global market share or total revenue.

Takeaway The Ramp series is becoming the closest thing the industry has to a real-time enterprise-AI scoreboard, and its month-to-month volatility suggests business buyers are still switching vendors opportunistically rather than settling into long-term platform loyalty.

US unveils sweeping new Iran sanctions; oil slides but the rupee keeps sinking

The US rolled out what Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent called “the single greatest financial offensive ever” against Iran on Monday, 24 August, CNBC reported, prompting Tehran to threaten seizing vessels and altering transit rules in the Strait of Hormuz. Brent crude fell roughly 2.3% to about $92 a barrel and WTI dropped about 2.2% to near $85 a barrel as traders weighed whether the sanctions would actually disrupt Hormuz flows. Despite cheaper oil, the rupee still slipped to around 95.77 to the dollar, up 0.11% on the day, reflecting broader dollar strength and risk-off positioning; India imports roughly 85% of its crude and is watching the standoff closely.

Takeaway A weaker oil price should normally help the rupee and India's import bill — that it didn't on Monday shows geopolitical risk premium, not just crude levels, is driving the currency right now.

Semiconductor stocks tumble ahead of Nvidia's blockbuster earnings print

Chip and AI-linked stocks led Monday's US selloff ahead of Nvidia's earnings, due after the close on Wednesday, 26 August, Yahoo Finance reported. Micron Technology fell 5.8%, AMD dropped over 3% and Broadcom lost more than 2%, while the iShares Semiconductor ETF slid roughly 2.7–4%. Consensus expects Nvidia to post around $91–95 billion in quarterly revenue, implying 95–97% year-over-year growth, with analyst price targets clustered between $275 and $325. The pullback was concentrated in semiconductors specifically — roughly twice the decline of broader tech — marking it as pre-earnings positioning rather than a broad tech rout.

Takeaway A stumble in Nvidia's guidance this week would ripple straight into India's IT and hardware-linked names and FII flows, given how tightly Indian tech sentiment now tracks the US AI trade.

Metal stocks buck the trend as financials drag Sensex, Nifty lower

The Sensex and Nifty closed lower on Monday, down 0.22% and 0.14% respectively, as the Nifty PSU Bank index led decliners and financials including SBI Life and Adani Ports weighed on the benchmarks, Business Standard reported. But the Nifty Metal index was the day's standout, gaining roughly 1.6% on the back of JSW Steel, Hindalco Industries and Tata Steel, cushioning the broader indices from steeper losses. Broader-market breadth was mixed: the Nifty MidCap index rose 0.13% while the Nifty SmallCap index fell 0.26%.

Takeaway With global chip stocks wobbling and financials under pressure at home, it was old-economy metals — not IT or banks — doing the heavy lifting for Indian indices on Monday.

Hy-Tech Engineers IPO opens with grey-market premium pointing to a 41–47% listing pop

The ₹135.73 crore Hy-Tech Engineers IPO, a hydraulic-fittings specialist, opened for subscription on 24 August and closes 27 August, with a price band of ₹50–53, India Infoline reported. The grey market premium stood around ₹22–25 on day one, implying an estimated listing price near ₹78, or roughly 41–47% above the issue price. The company reported FY26 total income of ₹193.44 crore, up from ₹166.71 crore in FY25, and profit after tax of ₹22.59 crore, up from ₹19.62 crore; allotment is expected around 28 August with listing on the BSE and NSE around 1 September.

Takeaway Sky-high GMP on a small-cap engineering IPO, even as benchmark indices drift lower, is a reminder that India's primary-market froth is running on its own momentum, somewhat detached from the secondary market's mood.

Treasury yields ease as bond and equity markets look for clues on the Fed's path

The US 10-year Treasury yield slipped modestly on Monday, easing about 2 basis points to roughly 4.72%, even as equities fell — the move lower in yields wasn't enough to offset the tech-led selloff, Yahoo Finance reported. Bitcoin and ether both traded higher on the day, with bitcoin holding in a roughly $77,700–79,000 range through the session, as crypto investors also looked for signals ahead of this week's Federal Reserve commentary.

Takeaway With US yields easing and the Fed rate path in focus, any dovish signal this week could offer relief to rate-sensitive Indian sectors like NBFCs and realty, which were among Monday's laggards.
