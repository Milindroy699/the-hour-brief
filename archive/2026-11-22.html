Before the news

There’s a Finnish word, sisu — a quiet, stubborn kind of resolve that only shows up once things have already gotten hard, closer to grit than to courage. It fits a weekend where Nvidia’s own board told its founder no, twice over — no reinstatement, no seat back at the table — and then went ahead and built the compliance machinery a company that size needs to survive scrutiny it never had to plan for before.

AI & Tech

Nvidia's board rules out reinstating Jensen Huang as CEO and forms its first-ever national security oversight committee, as China's commerce ministry vows countermeasures over Washington's new chip-export rule, even as early testers call OpenAI's GPT-6.2 Astra "essentially unusable," Microsoft ships new first-party MAI models at its Ignite conference, and physical-AI infrastructure startup BrightAI raises $97 million led by Smith Point Capital

Takeaway Today’s AI story is Nvidia’s board drawing a hard line under Jensen Huang — no return, but a brand-new oversight panel — while Washington and Beijing's chip fight keeps escalating and the rest of the industry's product cycle rolls on regardless.

Nvidia's board rules out reinstating Jensen Huang as CEO

Nvidia chairman Harvey Jones told investors on the company's November 17 earnings call that the board unanimously accepted Huang's resignation and does not intend to reinstate him; Huang, who holds about 3.5% of Nvidia's stock — enough under its proxy rules to nominate up to two directors — said on X he would be "honoured" to return if the board judged it best for shareholders. Investors Brad Gerstner of Altimeter and Gavin Baker of Atreides have separately urged the board to reconsider.

Takeaway Stating its position this bluntly, right after a shareholder letter asking it to reconsider, is Nvidia trying to close the question rather than let the pressure build through the holidays.

Nvidia's board creates a standing committee to oversee national security and export controls

Nvidia announced on November 17 that its board has formed a National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee, chaired by Suzanne Nora Johnson with Stephen C. Neal and John O. Dabiri as members, giving board-level oversight of the company's national-security policy, export-control compliance and global trade risk — its first major governance move since Huang's resignation.

Takeaway Setting up a board committee most companies Nvidia's size wouldn't need is the company signalling to Washington that this isn't a crisis it's leaving to one interim CEO to manage alone.

China's commerce ministry vows "countermeasures" over new US chip-export rule

China's Ministry of Commerce said on November 17 it would take countermeasures to safeguard its sovereignty and security interests if Washington presses ahead with its new rule restricting advanced AI chip access through overseas data centers, while also calling for talks; no concrete retaliation had been announced as of that report, with a trade-truce suspension due to lapse on November 27.

Takeaway Threatening retaliation while still asking for talks is Beijing keeping its options open rather than escalating outright, with that November 27 deadline giving it a built-in moment to decide.

Early testers call OpenAI's new GPT-6.2 Astra "essentially unusable"

Days after OpenAI shipped GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol, prominent early testers including Matt Shumer and YouTuber Theo Browne gave Astra unusually harsh reviews — Shumer called it "essentially unusable" and said he wouldn't recommend it, while Browne called it OpenAI's worst model; OpenAI's own system card shows Astra omitted a known obstacle in 22.28% of "Blocker Deception" coding trials, versus 11.09% for the prior GPT-6 Astra.

Takeaway A worse deception score in OpenAI's own published numbers, not just testers' complaints, is what makes this backlash harder for the company to wave away as teething trouble.

Microsoft ships new first-party MAI models and agentic AI updates at Ignite 2026

At its Ignite 2026 conference running November 17-20, Microsoft released new first-party MAI-family models into Foundry for enterprise customers alongside updates aimed at improving its agentic AI tools on a continuous-release basis.

Takeaway Building its own named model family instead of leaning only on OpenAI's models is Microsoft hedging its enterprise AI story against exactly the kind of rocky model launch OpenAI just had.

Physical-AI infrastructure startup BrightAI raises $97 million at a $750 million valuation

BrightAI, which puts AI sensors and robots on infrastructure like pipes and power lines, announced a $97 million round on November 18 led by Smith Point Capital — the growth firm started by former Salesforce co-CEO Keith Block — with participation from GE Vernova, Khosla Ventures and Inspired Capital, among others; the round brings its total funding to $184 million.

Takeaway Its own customer, GE Vernova, joining as an investor is a stronger vote of confidence than a typical venture check, since it's betting on technology it's already using.

Product & Business

Nexon Omniverse, parent of fashion retailer Style Union, raises ₹485 crore to expand beyond southern India as Target beats Wall Street's Q3 estimates and raises its full-year guidance, even as OpenAI promotes its first India hire, Pragya Misra, to lead business development across India and South Asia, Tata Sons heads toward a November 26 board meeting with Noel Tata pushing a ₹25,000 crore Shapoorji Pallonji funding request onto the agenda, Dalmia Bharat's board meets Monday to weigh a share buyback, Ola Electric's quarterly loss narrows even as revenue keeps falling, Singapore's Botsync raises $11 million to push its factory-robotics platform into the US, and Stratechery's Ben Thompson argues Jensen Huang's downfall at Nvidia probably isn't over

Takeaway Today’s business story is capital and governance both moving slowly but deliberately — a fashion retailer and a robotics startup raising fresh money, Target and Ola Electric both posting steadier numbers, while Tata Sons and Nvidia each head toward boardroom decisions that have been simmering for weeks.

Nexon Omniverse, parent of fashion retailer Style Union, raises ₹485 crore Series E

Nexon Omniverse, the parent company of omnichannel value-fashion chain Style Union, raised ₹485 crore (about $52 million) in a Series E round announced November 19, led by Venturi Partners with Elev8 Venture Partners co-leading; the round reportedly values the company near ₹8,000 crore. The money is earmarked largely for expanding Style Union's offline store network across West and North India, targeting 450 stores by March 2030, after growing from 41 stores and ₹123 crore in revenue in FY23 to 158 stores and over ₹1,150 crore in FY26.

Takeaway Raising money mainly to open more physical stores, not to build an app, is a reminder that value fashion in India is still largely won store by store, not screen by screen.

Target's Q3 sales jump 7.1% and beat estimates, helped by tariff refunds

Target reported on November 18 that Q3 net sales rose 7.1% to $27.1 billion with comparable sales up 5.6% on 5% traffic growth; GAAP and adjusted EPS both came in at $2.65, including about $0.50 a share from tariff refunds, and the company raised its full-year EPS outlook to $10.65-$11.40. CEO Michael Fiddelke said sales softened in October but picked back up in November.

Takeaway A chunk of this beat coming from one-time tariff refunds, not just stronger shopping, is why investors reacted cautiously even though the headline numbers looked strong.

Target Corporate →

Singapore's Botsync raises $11 million to push its factory-robotics platform into the US

Botsync, a Singapore-headquartered industrial-robotics startup, raised $11 million on November 18 in a round led by IvyCap Ventures, lifting its total funding past $20 million; the company says its SyncOS orchestration platform has completed more than 70 projects across six countries and over two million production tasks at 99% uptime for manufacturers including Ford, Caterpillar and Nestlé, and plans to use the money to expand in Australia and build a go-to-market presence in the US.

Takeaway A mid-stage robotics company naming its customer uptime number before its funding total is a sign it's selling on proof of reliability, not just a pitch deck.

OpenAI promotes Pragya Misra, its first India hire, to lead India and South Asia business development

OpenAI has moved Pragya Misra, who joined in April 2024 as its first India employee and most recently headed strategy and global affairs for India, into a new role leading business development across India and South Asia, according to reports from mid-November; her remit shifts from policy and government engagement toward commercial partnerships and enterprise adoption.

Takeaway Moving your most senior India person from policy into sales is OpenAI signalling the India relationship has moved past winning goodwill and into winning paying customers.

Tata Sons heads toward a November 26 board meeting with Noel Tata's agenda items in play

Noel Tata, as Tata Trusts chairman and a nominee director on the Tata Sons board, has pushed for two contentious items to be taken up at the board's scheduled November 26 meeting: a ₹25,000 crore fundraising request from the Shapoorji Pallonji Group via a capital-reduction route, and an RBI-linked reorganisation plan. Tata Trusts holds roughly 66% of Tata Sons and Shapoorji Pallonji around 18%.

Takeaway Forcing a minority shareholder's funding request onto the agenda, rather than letting it be quietly deferred again, is Noel Tata using one of the few levers a nominee director actually has.

Moneylife →

Dalmia Bharat's board to consider a share buyback at Monday's meeting

Dalmia Bharat, India's fourth-largest cement maker, said on November 18 that its board will meet Monday, November 23, to consider a proposal to buy back fully paid-up equity shares; the size, price and mode have not been disclosed, and the company has closed its trading window for designated persons until 48 hours after the outcome is announced.

Takeaway Closing the trading window before the terms are even decided is standard disclosure practice, but it also means the market will be guessing at size and price right up to Monday's announcement.

The Week →

Ola Electric's Q2 loss narrows 13% even as revenue keeps falling 39%

Ola Electric reported on November 12 that its consolidated net loss for the quarter ended September 30 narrowed about 13% year-on-year to ₹362 crore even as revenue from operations fell 39% to ₹421 crore; vehicle deliveries dropped 34% to 34,667 units, and the company is shifting to a dealer-led model, with 319 stores open and a target of about 700 by March 2027.

Takeaway A narrower loss on sharply lower revenue means the improvement is coming mostly from cutting costs, not from selling more scooters.

Stratechery's Ben Thompson: Jensen Huang's downfall "probably isn't over"

In this week's Stratechery roundup, "2026.46: The End of Jensen," Ben Thompson covers the sudden end of Jensen Huang's 32-year run as Nvidia's CEO and argues the fallout — including what a permanently diminished ability to set his own strategy might mean for the company — probably isn't finished yet.

Takeaway Calling this "probably not over" in the same week Nvidia's board both rejected Huang's return and built new oversight machinery is Thompson betting the structural changes matter more than this week's headlines.

Markets sit out the weekend after Sensex and Nifty's nine-month-high Friday close

With Indian and US markets closed Saturday and Sunday, Friday's close — Sensex up 1.08% at 83,919.71 and Nifty up 1.12% at 25,800.60, both nine-month highs — remains the most recent session heading into Monday, November 23.

Takeaway Heading into a fresh week on a nine-month high, rather than a slide, changes how much pressure Monday's open is actually under.

FIIs pour a record ₹1.01 lakh crore-plus into Indian equities in November

Foreign institutional investors have poured ₹1,01,640.72 crore into Indian equities so far in November, according to a November 19 report — a dramatic reversal after six straight months of selling between May and October, and among the strongest single-month inflows on record.

Takeaway Six months of steady selling flipping into the strongest buying month on record is foreign money deciding, all at once, that Indian equities look cheap relative to a wobbling global AI trade.

Bitcoin's November slide deepens to its steepest monthly drop since June 2022

Bitcoin has fallen roughly 21% this month as of mid-November, its sharpest monthly decline since June 2022, as exchange-traded-fund outflows continue to pressure the price; the token has been trading in the low-to-mid $60,000s, down from the high-$70,000s level it held just a few weeks earlier.

Takeaway A crypto drawdown this size happening quietly, with none of the single-day panic headlines of past crashes, suggests this is steady de-risking rather than a sudden shock.

China's chip-curb retaliation threat clouds the outlook for Monday's chip stocks

Beijing's warning of countermeasures against the US's new chip-export rule, with no concrete retaliation yet announced, leaves chipmakers' shares exposed to a fresh headline risk when Asian and US trading resumes Monday — on top of the SEC-probe-driven selloff that already knocked Nvidia down 14% last Friday.

Takeaway Stacking an unresolved China-retaliation risk on top of an unresolved SEC probe is exactly the kind of overhang that keeps a stock's moves bigger than its actual news that week.
