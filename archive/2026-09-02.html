AI & Tech

Pentagon's custom ChatGPT and Grok deployment tops 1.7 million users

The Defense Department confirmed it has added tailored versions of ChatGPT and Grok to GenAI.mil, its internal platform for sensitive-but-unclassified work like planning, logistics and policy drafting, with more than 1.7 million of the department's roughly 3 million military and civilian personnel already signed on, TechCrunch and Fortune reported on 31 August and 1 September. The rollout follows an earlier custom Gemini deployment on the same platform and comes alongside defense contracts worth up to $200 million each for OpenAI and xAI.

Takeaway When the Pentagon becomes one of the largest chatbot deployments on earth, "AI safety" stops being a research-paper concept and starts being an operational one.

ChatGPT's ad business hits a $1 billion run rate, opens to India, Europe and MENA

OpenAI said on 31 August that ChatGPT advertising has crossed a $1 billion annualized revenue run rate in under 200 days, and it opened self-serve ad buying to marketers in India, Europe, the Middle East and North Africa, per OpenAI's own announcement and CNBC. Ads run against free-tier and Go-subscription users under both CPM and cost-per-click pricing, with OpenAI targeting $2.5 billion in ad revenue for the year.

Takeaway A company that sold itself on "the answer, not the ad" now has a P&L that depends on selling attention around the answer — the same tension every search engine eventually lost to.

Anthropic locks in a $35 billion compute deal with Nvidia-backed Lambda for a Texas campus

Anthropic signed a six-year, $35 billion cloud agreement with Lambda, the Nvidia-backed cloud provider, for roughly 350 megawatts of capacity at a Texas data-center campus Hut 8 is building in Nueces County, Bloomberg reported on 31 August. Nvidia plays three roles in the structure — chip supplier, Lambda equity backer and lessee of the campus — a circular-financing pattern several outlets flagged as increasingly common in AI infrastructure deals; it follows Anthropic's other recent mega-deals with Nscale and Fluidstack.

Takeaway Anthropic isn't just buying compute anymore — it's buying into a web of interlocking Nvidia-backed leases that make "AI capex bubble" arguments harder to wave off with each new deal.

Bloomberg →

FTC and 22 states sue Amazon over an alleged $20 billion advertiser overcharge scheme

The FTC and 22 state attorneys general sued Amazon on 31 August, alleging it secretly introduced an undisclosed "soft reserve price" into its Sponsored Products, Brands and Display ad auctions in 2019, inflating advertising costs by more than $20 billion since and affecting over 1.2 million advertisers, including 500,000-plus small businesses, per CNBC. The complaint also alleges Amazon raised the hidden surcharges around peak events like Prime Day and Black Friday; Amazon calls the suit "misguided" and says its ML-driven auctions actually cut average winning bids.

Takeaway As pricing shifts to opaque algorithmic auctions everywhere, this case is a preview of how regulators will handle "the algorithm decided the price" as a legal defense.

CNBC →

Berlin confirms data theft after Rhysida ransomware attack, weeks before its state election

Berlin's city administration confirmed the Rhysida ransomware gang stole data from its administrative network after the group listed the city on its leak site, claiming 5.79 TB across roughly 1.44 million files, including water-supply vulnerability assessments, payroll records and thousands of personnel files, BleepingComputer and Help Net Security reported on 1 September. Rhysida is demanding 30 bitcoin, around €2 million; Mayor Kai Wegner says the city won't pay, and officials say no election data was taken ahead of Berlin's 20 September state election.

Takeaway The real story isn't the ransom theater — it's the gap between breach and full network isolation that let the exfiltration happen at all.

BleepingComputer →

EU signs €387.8 million deal to build an AMD-powered LUMI-AI supercomputer in Finland

The EuroHPC Joint Undertaking signed a €387.8 million contract with Atos-owned Bull to build LUMI-AI — powered by AMD Instinct MI430X GPUs and sixth-generation EPYC CPUs — at CSC's data center in Kajaani, Finland, targeting roughly ten times the AI capacity of the current LUMI machine, per EuroHPC JU's official announcement and HPCwire on 31 August. Deployment is targeted for the second half of 2027, with the machine's waste heat feeding Kajaani's district heating network.

Takeaway Europe keeps picking AMD over Nvidia for its flagship public AI infrastructure — a quiet, deliberate hedge against total dependence on the chip everyone else is fighting over.

EuroHPC JU →

Meta's $18 billion child-safety settlement leans on AI to guess who's a kid

As part of its $18 billion settlement with 48 states over child mental-health harms, Meta has one year to strengthen AI-driven age verification across Instagram and Facebook, combining machine-learning signals, ID checks and third-party tools with outside accuracy audits, Fortune reported on 1 September. Notably, the settlement lets Meta retain some under-13 user data specifically to train and test its own age-detection models.

Takeaway The settlement meant to protect kids' data quietly hands Meta a fresh, court-sanctioned reason to keep collecting it — this time in the name of figuring out who's a kid in the first place.

Unacademy's edtech unwind completes: sold to upGrad for $200 million, down from a $3.4 billion peak

Unacademy Group's all-stock acquisition by Ronnie Screwvala's upGrad closed on 1 September at just over a $200 million valuation, a roughly 94% haircut from its 2021 peak, TechCrunch reported. Unacademy CEO Gaurav Munjal told press: "We raised at a peak, but sold at a fraction of that."

Takeaway This is the cleanest data point yet on how far India's 2021-vintage edtech bubble has deflated — and a reminder that "SoftBank-backed" used to be a flex, not a warning label.

Vi unveiled a refreshed brand identity — a redesigned 3D-sphere logo — and named Shah Rukh Khan as brand ambassador for a new campaign, "Vi badal raha hai… tayaar rahiye," with rollout across customer touchpoints starting 1 September, Business Standard reported.

Amazon Now plots expansion to 300 Indian cities

Amazon's quick-commerce arm, live in roughly 30 cities since its January 2025 launch, has "a path to expand to about 300 cities very, very soon," director Nishant Sardana told press on 1 September, backed by a reported $300 million investment and a new delivery-partner welfare programme called Sammaan, Business Standard reported.

Takeaway Amazon is skipping the metro land-grab and going straight for the tier-2/3 whitespace Blinkit and Zepto haven't fully claimed — a bet that quick commerce's next battle is fought in smaller cities, not denser ones.

Tata Elxsi partners with Sarla Aviation to build India's first indigenous eVTOL

Tata Elxsi signed an MoU with Sarla Aviation on 1 September to co-develop Shunya, a six-plus-one-seat electric air taxi, handling avionics, flight-control software and certification while Sarla leads design and type certification, Business Standard reported. First flight is targeted in 18-24 months.

Takeaway India's advanced air-mobility bet just got a credible systems-engineering partner — the harder problem in eVTOL was never the airframe, it's certification, and that's exactly Tata Elxsi's lane.

Battery-swapping network Yuma Energy raises $35 million from Magna, acquires Grinntech

Yuma Energy — a battery-swapping joint venture between auto supplier Magna International and Yulu Energy, delivering more than 60 million swaps across 2,500-plus charging units in 18 cities — raised a $35 million Series A led by Magna and acquired battery-technology maker Grinntech to strengthen its manufacturing base, TechCrunch reported on 31 August. The funding backs an expansion of the network and a push toward EBITDA-positive operations by FY27.

Takeaway A global auto-parts giant doubling down on Indian battery-swapping infrastructure is a bet that owning the charging layer matters as much as owning the vehicle.

Deep-tech EV-charging startup Zenergize raises $4 million pre-Series A

Zenergize Technologies, building India's first indigenous SiC-based DC fast chargers, raised roughly ₹33.5 crore ($4 million) in a pre-Series A round of equity plus venture debt led by Giraffe Studios, with strategic investors including Nikhil Sawhney of Triveni Turbine and Shyam Bagri of Bagrry's, Inc42 reported on 31 August. The company claims a ₹30 crore revenue run rate and an order book above ₹150 crore within a year of commercialisation.

Takeaway A small round, but a real signal — India's power-electronics import dependence is exactly the kind of unglamorous deep-tech gap that strategic industrial money, not just VC, is now willing to fund.

Medtronic tops Q1 earnings, raises full-year guidance on cardiovascular strength

Medtronic reported fiscal Q1 2027 adjusted EPS of $1.45 against a $1.39 estimate, on revenue of $9.8 billion, up 13.7% year-over-year, and raised its full-year organic revenue growth guidance to 7.25-7.75% from 6.75-7.25%, per the company's 1 September earnings release and Yahoo Finance. Its cardiovascular portfolio grew 18.9% organically, led by 29.1% growth in electrophysiology therapies; shares rose about 5% on the print.

Takeaway A 40-year-old medtech giant beating on the strength of a single device category is a reminder that not every growth story in this market needs an AI narrative attached.

John Cutler: most "prioritization" is just forging agreeable narratives

In a recent essay on his product-management newsletter The Beautiful Mess, John Cutler argues teams prioritize more effectively by tracking the recurring tensions and arguments that keep resurfacing in a team's day-to-day work than by running yet another scoring framework — because where the same disagreement keeps winning despite everyone's better judgment is where the real, unresolved decision actually lives. Most formal prioritization exercises, he writes, "resolve very little and produce very little real commitment."

Takeaway If your roadmap review keeps re-litigating the same fight, the fight is the roadmap decision — the framework on top of it is theater.

Purple Style Labs' ₹680 crore IPO closes to a near-empty book

Purple Style Labs, which runs the luxury multi-brand platform Pernia's Pop-Up Shop, closed its ₹680 crore mainboard IPO on 2 September having drawn bids for only a small fraction of the shares on offer overall, a threadbare finish after opening at just 0.05-12% subscribed on day one, Business Standard reported. Retail was the only category showing real pulse; qualified institutional buyers barely participated. Allotment is due 3 September ahead of a tentative 7 September listing.

Takeaway A tepid open turning into a near-empty book by close is the market telling this fashion-tech IPO its growth story didn't clear the profitability bar investors wanted.

Adani stocks rebound up to 4.6% a day after MSCI-linked $15 billion wipeout

Adani group shares recovered as much as 3.5-4.6% intraday on 1 September, a day after MSCI's index rebalancing collided with India's new Closing Auction Session to erase roughly ₹1.4 lakh crore in group value on 31 August, Business Standard reported. Adani Green Energy led the rebound at +4.6% intraday, followed by Adani Ports and Adani Power at around +3.2% each, as the one-time rebalancing-driven selling pressure eased and value buyers stepped in; all nine listed Adani companies had closed lower on Monday, with Adani Enterprises down 9.8% in its steepest single-day fall since January.

Takeaway Monday's MSCI chaos was a plumbing problem, not a fundamentals problem — the bounce proves it.

Fed rate-hike odds jump to 65%+, 10-year Treasury yield hits 19-month high

September rate-hike odds surged to roughly 65-68% on CME FedWatch, up from about 36% before Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole address, after Warsh reiterated that 2% inflation remains a "firm and fixed" goal and said financial conditions aren't currently restrictive, per CNBC and TechTimes. Wall Street slides as US strikes Iran again, oil and yields spike

US equities fell for a second straight session on 1 September — the S&P 500 down 0.71% to 7,631.47, the Nasdaq down 1.03% to 26,099.77, and the Dow down 0.79% (419 points) to 52,766.88 — after the US launched fresh strikes on Iranian Revolutionary Guard targets following reported attacks on commercial shipping near the Strait of Hormuz, per AP wire coverage. Brent crude rose further and US crude closed above $90 for the first time in over a month, with big-cap tech names leading the index-level drag alongside rising Treasury yields.

Takeaway A six-month US-Iran conflict that markets had learned to shrug off is back to setting the tape.

Zerodha Z-Connect principle for today: Yesterday's ₹1.4-lakh-crore Adani wipeout — and today's rebound — wasn't really an Adani story, it was a market-plumbing story. Zerodha's Z-Connect explainer on India's new Closing Auction Session (CAS) breaks down why: instead of the old method, where a stock's official close was a volume-weighted average of the last 30 minutes of scattered trades, CAS pools all buy and sell orders into a single 20-minute window (3:15–3:35pm, ending at a random moment between 3:28 and 3:30pm to deter last-second gaming) and matches them at one "equilibrium price." SEBI's rationale is that concentrating orders into one visible pool produces a fairer, more manipulation-resistant close. The catch is that a single-price auction is also more sensitive to a genuine imbalance — when a huge one-directional flow like Monday's MSCI-driven selling hits the auction, there's no longer 30 minutes of trading to absorb it, so the whole imbalance clears in one sharp move. That's why Adani stocks could swing so hard on Monday and mechanically snap back on Tuesday, once there was no comparable rebalancing flow to contend with.
