AI & Tech

Stripe, Nvidia and Anthropic all place multi-billion-dollar bets on AI's next layer

Stripe finalizes over $7 billion acquisition of AI gateway OpenRouter

Bloomberg reported on 16 August that Stripe has finalized a deal to acquire OpenRouter, the AI model-routing platform used by roughly 8 million developers to reach 400+ models through a single API, for more than $7 billion. That's over 5x the $1.3 billion valuation OpenRouter set in its Series B just three months earlier in May, and roughly 30% below the ~$10 billion figure reported during July acquisition talks.

Takeaway Stripe is betting the real money in AI won't be made picking the best model but billing and routing the traffic between hundreds of interchangeable ones.

TechCrunch →

Nvidia commits up to $105 billion to finance OpenAI's Ohio data center campus

Nvidia announced on 17 August it will provide more than $105 billion in financing to secure land, power and infrastructure for SB Energy's Pike Technology Campus in Pike County, Ohio, which OpenAI will lease for 20 years. Initial capacity is 4.25 gigawatts, expandable to 8GW; Nvidia is separately investing $1.5 billion directly in SB Energy, and OpenAI has committed to using only Nvidia chips at the site.

Takeaway Nvidia is no longer just selling the GPUs that go into these buildings — it's now financing the buildings themselves, the kind of circular arrangement CEO Jensen Huang has been pressed to publicly deny.

Anthropic in talks to acquire infrastructure startup Decart for about $6 billion

Bloomberg reported on 13 August that Anthropic is in talks to acquire Decart, an Israeli startup building software to make chip training and inference more efficient, for roughly $6 billion — which would be Anthropic's largest acquisition to date. Decart was valued at about $4 billion just three months earlier in a $300 million round led by Radical Ventures; the talks are not finalized and could still fall through.

Takeaway Anthropic reaching for an efficiency and infrastructure startup, rather than a model or app company, suggests its near-term bottleneck is inference capacity to serve surging Claude demand, not frontier capability.

AI video startup Higgsfield raises $400M, quadrupling its valuation to $5.4B in eight months

Higgsfield, the AI video and image generation platform founded by ex-Snap executive Alex Mashrabov, closed a $400 million Series B led by DST Global, with Goldman Sachs Alternatives and Intel Capital among the backers, on 17 August. The company says annualized revenue has reached $700 million this month, up from $20 million a year ago, across more than 30 million users in 238 countries.

Takeaway A 35x revenue jump in a year gives Higgsfield's backers real numbers to point to as evidence generative video has found paying demand, not just hype.

TechCrunch →

Google ships Gemini 3.7 Flash just three weeks after its predecessor

Google released Gemini 3.7 Flash on 13 August, pitching it as its most capable “workhorse” model yet for coding and agent workloads, built through algorithmic refinement rather than training from scratch. Google cites gains like 43.6% versus 34.4% on the FrontierCode 1.1 benchmark, a 1-million-token context window, and introductory pricing of $0.75 per million input tokens and $3.75 per million output tokens through the end of 2026.

Takeaway A three-week refresh cycle on the cheap, high-volume tier — not the flagship — shows where the real competitive pressure is, since that's the tier most production API traffic actually runs on.

Google Blog →

An AI store manager built on Claude recommended firing a human employee

Luna, an AI agent built on Anthropic's Claude models that runs a real San Francisco boutique for AI-agent testbed startup Andon Labs, recommended dismissing a human employee after 17 of 23 missed shifts and incidents including taking the store's credit card home. Store logs show Luna had actually lost track of its own attendance policy for months and only acted after a human supervisor prompted it to review its rules; a human then approved and carried out the firing.

Takeaway The real story isn't an AI “firing” someone — a human still signed off — it's that Luna forgot its own policy for months, undercutting the pitch that AI managers are more consistent than people.

The San Francisco Standard →

Clop ransomware gang claims breaches at Shell, GE and Philips via a PTC software flaw

The Clop ransomware gang claims to have compromised 43 organizations by exploiting a critical flaw in PTC's Windchill and FlexPLM industrial software, which PTC began patching in June after exploitation had already started. Shell confirmed on 14 August it was attacked, with Clop claiming to have stolen 89GB of data including engineering drawings, while GE and Philips confirmed they are investigating separate data-theft claims tied to the same campaign.

Takeaway This is the same Clop playbook as MOVEit and Cleo — mass-exploiting one widely used enterprise vendor to breach dozens of downstream customers — a reminder that third-party software risk, not novel AI threats, remains the biggest practical exposure for manufacturers.

Product & Business

India's startups swap growth-at-all-costs for profits, paid-down debt and market share

PhysicsWallah's Q1 FY27 revenue rises 24% to ₹1,054 crore as losses narrow

PhysicsWallah reported revenue from operations up 24% year-on-year to ₹1,053.95 crore for the June quarter, while its consolidated net loss narrowed 30.5% to ₹88.28 crore and EBITDA turned positive at ₹52 crore. Online revenue grew 33.3% to ₹549 crore and offline revenue grew 14.5% to ₹490 crore, helped by a shifted NEET exam cycle that pushed peak enrolments into July–August.

Takeaway Offline centres growing nearly as fast as the app undercuts the “edtech is a pure-online play” thesis that defined the 2021 funding boom.

YourStory →

PhonePe nears 11 billion monthly UPI transactions as CRED slips to 10th place

NPCI data reported on 17 August showed PhonePe processed 10.86 billion UPI transactions in July, up from 10.48 billion in June, holding 45.89% of total UPI volume and 48.33% of value at ₹14.44 lakh crore. Total UPI volume hit an all-time high of 23.66 billion transactions worth ₹29.88 lakh crore in July, while CRED fell to 10th place among UPI apps by volume.

Takeaway CRED's slide despite its premium user base suggests its bet on being a “curated” payments app is losing to PhonePe and GPay's brute-force ubiquity.

EV mobility startup Yulu raises $93 million, plans to quadruple its fleet to 200,000 vehicles

Yulu raised $93 million on 12 August — $63 million in equity led by GEF Capital Partners plus $30 million in debt — its largest round to date, valuing the company at roughly $170 million post-money. The funding will go toward quadrupling its active EV fleet to 200,000 vehicles over two years, adding service hubs, and expanding into new intra-city logistics segments.

Takeaway Structuring nearly a third of the round as debt against physical EV assets is a capital-efficient template other asset-heavy Indian mobility startups will likely copy rather than dilute further for fleet expansion.

PharmEasy's parent API Holdings goes debt-free, frees Thyrocare shares from pledge

API Holdings repaid its outstanding ₹1,050 crore debt on 17 August, funded through internal accruals and the sale of a 9.90% stake in listed subsidiary Thyrocare Technologies by its holding company Docon. The repayment released the pledge on Docon's remaining 51.02% Thyrocare stake, leaving it fully unencumbered.

Takeaway A company once synonymous with India's most cautionary down-round story using its listed subsidiary's stock to erase debt is a quieter, more durable signal of startup-sector maturing than any splashy new funding round.

Elena Verna's survey: most people, including managers, would rather stay individual contributors

A Typeform-partnered survey of more than 300 tech professionals, published around 14 August, found 71% of respondents want at least half their time on individual-contributor work, and 27% want to be full-time ICs. Among current managers, 24% said they'd want to be 100% IC, and manager-turned-ICs reported far higher satisfaction with their choice than current managers did with theirs.

Takeaway It's current managers, not junior ICs, who are most eager to step back — a sign that management-track career ladders are becoming a retention risk rather than a reward.

John Cutler: stop debating frameworks, prioritize by following the recurring tension

In a 14 August essay, John Cutler argues most prioritization exercises just produce agreeable narratives that resolve nothing, and that real decisions surface where arguments keep recurring, where conviction keeps failing, and where momentum wins despite better judgment. He suggests teams track those recurring tensions instead of scoring frameworks like RICE or ICE.

Takeaway A team's real prioritization debt shows up in its recurring arguments, not its spreadsheets.

John Cutler, The Beautiful Mess →

Marty Cagan highlights Ben Evans' definition of what product managers actually do

In a recent post, Marty Cagan spotlights analyst Ben Evans' framing that most customers now technically have tools to build their own software — vibe-coding, agents — yet rarely try, and rarely succeed when they do, because using a tool well and building the right tool are different skills. Cagan calls it the best implicit definition of the product role he's seen.

Takeaway This reframes the “AI will let anyone build software” panic among product managers — the bottleneck was never coding ability, it was problem-definition, which AI hasn't automated.

US-Iran Hormuz shipping deal lapses, keeping oil near $88–89 a barrel

A US-Iran memorandum of understanding on Strait of Hormuz shipping expired on 17 August, with talks deadlocked over sanctions relief and reparations Iran is demanding before reopening the strait, which normally carries about a fifth of global oil supply. Brent crude was trading near $88–89 a barrel, up more than 5% on the week, and Indian markets cited elevated crude prices and lingering Middle East uncertainty as a drag on Monday's session.

Takeaway Oil-linked geopolitical risk remains the single biggest swing factor for both Indian and global equities right now.

30-year US Treasury yield tops 5.31%, its highest level since 2007

The 30-year Treasury yield rose to 5.31% on 17 August, with the 10-year at 4.72% and the 2-year at 4.18%, as heavy government debt issuance, sticky tariff-driven inflation and uncertainty around incoming Fed Chair Kevin Warsh combined to unsettle the bond market. The yield spike weighed directly on both the S&P 500 and Nasdaq during Monday's session.

Takeaway Rising long-end US yields are now competing with equities for capital — worth watching before assuming any Fed-cut rally is automatic.

FIIs sell, DIIs buy on Monday as domestic flows cushion the Sensex

Foreign institutional investors were net sellers of about ₹2,535 crore in the cash segment on 17 August, while domestic institutional investors net bought roughly ₹5,101 crore the same day, offsetting the foreign outflow and keeping the Sensex's fall to under 0.4% despite a negative global backdrop.

Takeaway Domestic mutual fund and insurance buying continues to act as a shock absorber against foreign outflows.

Rupee slides to 95.61 against the dollar as crude oil surges

The rupee fell 19 paise to close at a provisional 95.61 against the US dollar on 17 August, pressured by the crude oil spike, stalled US-Iran talks and broad dollar strength, adding to the drag on Indian equities, particularly oil-import-sensitive sectors.

Takeaway A weakening rupee alongside rising oil is a double headwind for Indian equities and import-heavy sectors to watch this week.

SanDisk, Micron and Western Digital rally as AI memory-chip demand tightens supply

SanDisk surged as much as 10% on 17 August, extending a roughly 35% rally over the prior week, while Micron gained 6% and Western Digital rose 6%, after US Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick said the US is “not in favor” of Apple sourcing DRAM and NAND from Chinese suppliers — adding to comments from Elon Musk flagging memory chips as an AI supply-chain bottleneck. The moves kept the Nasdaq 100 roughly flat even as the broader Nasdaq Composite and S&P 500 fell on bond-yield pressure.

Takeaway AI-driven memory-chip demand is now a distinct enough theme that it can decouple parts of the Nasdaq from the broader market's direction.
