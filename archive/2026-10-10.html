Before the news

There’s a Finnish word, sisu — a quiet, stubborn resolve that shows up not in grand gestures but in simply continuing, calmly, through a situation that offers no easy way out. It shows up today in Dalal Street clawing back two days of losses on nothing more dramatic than IT stocks and easing oil, in Lambda raising a record war chest the same week its biggest customer is also its biggest bet, and in a Chinese AI startup rebuilding from scratch months after its own merger fell apart.

AI & Tech

The Pentagon confirms it has stopped using Anthropic’s Claude after reportedly running it in Iran-related operations for weeks past its own deadline, as Nvidia-backed cloud firm Lambda seeks $4 billion ahead of a 2027 IPO on a backlog swollen by Anthropic’s own $35 billion compute deal, even as Chinese AI agent startup Manus raises over $500 million in its first round since Meta’s blocked buyout, OpenAI widens its newest GPT-6 model tier to ChatGPT’s free users, HPE ships AMD-powered servers built for agentic AI, and Apple reportedly preps three Siri-centered devices for an October 13 unveiling

Takeaway Whether it’s a government reluctantly cutting ties or a startup betting everything on a comeback, AI companies and their biggest customers are now too entangled to separate cleanly — walking away, or doubling down, both take months.

Pentagon confirms it has stopped using Anthropic’s Claude, months after designating it a supply-chain risk

A Defense Department official told the BBC on October 5 that the Pentagon “has ceased the use of Anthropic products,” more than a month after an August deadline following Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth’s February move to label Anthropic a supply-chain risk. Former defense officials and contractors told the BBC that Claude, embedded in Palantir’s Maven Smart System, was reportedly still in use for intelligence analysis and operations against Iran as recently as the week before the Pentagon’s statement; the Pentagon has since signed contracts with Google, xAI and OpenAI.

Takeaway A six-week gap between a deadline and an actual exit shows how hard it is to rip a single model out of a system once it’s running real operations, however politically urgent the split.

Nvidia-backed Lambda seeks up to $4 billion at a $14.5 billion valuation ahead of a planned 2027 IPO

The Wall Street Journal reported on October 6 that cloud-computing firm Lambda is raising up to $4 billion at a $14.5 billion pre-money valuation, led by Coatue Management and Blackstone, in what it called its final private round before a targeted 2027 listing. A letter to limited partners reviewed by the Journal shows Lambda’s contracted backlog grew from $15 billion in June to $50 billion in September, with much of that jump coming from a $35 billion compute commitment Anthropic signed with Lambda in late August.

Takeaway Anthropic is simultaneously one of Lambda’s biggest customers and, through that backlog, one of the reasons investors are willing to fund Lambda’s own expansion — the AI boom keeps financing itself in circles.

The Star (WSJ) →

China’s Manus raises over $500 million in its first round since its Meta acquisition was blocked

TechCrunch and CNBC reported on October 8 that Manus, the Chinese AI-agent startup whose roughly $2 billion acquisition by Meta was unwound by Chinese regulators earlier this year, has raised more than $500 million in a round backed by Boyu Capital, IDG Capital, battery maker CATL, and existing investors Tencent, HSG and ZhenFund. The company is also said to be weighing a restructuring to prepare for a future Hong Kong listing.

Takeaway Raising half a billion dollars months after a blocked buyout is Manus betting that independence, not a Big Tech parent, is what investors actually wanted to fund.

CNBC →

Apple reportedly plans an iMac G4-style Siri hub, codenamed J490, for an October 13 unveiling

Bloomberg’s Mark Gurman reported this week that Apple’s planned October 13 event centers on a Siri-powered smart home hub with a roughly 6-inch display, internally codenamed J490 (a wall-mounted version is J491), alongside an updated HomePod mini and a refreshed Apple TV. The hub is said to recognize individual household members and show each person their own content, but reportedly has no App Store at launch, leaning instead on Apple’s own apps for calendar, messages and photos.

Takeaway Skipping an App Store at launch suggests Apple would rather ship a narrower, more reliable Siri experience first than repeat the feature-creep delays that have dogged Siri AI already.

OpenAI widens its newest GPT-6 model tier to ChatGPT’s free and Go users

OpenAI began rolling out GPT-6 and a new “Intelligent UI” — which adds charts, buttons and interactive forms inside chat replies — to paid ChatGPT tiers on October 7, with Free and Go-tier users reportedly getting access the following day. Paid tiers use a version called GPT-6 Sol, while Free and Go users get a lighter GPT-6 Luna, extending the new model family toward ChatGPT’s reported 1.2 billion weekly users.

Takeaway Giving free users a lighter version of the same model family, rather than holding it back entirely, is OpenAI choosing reach over exclusivity as the model itself becomes the product.

Let’s Data Science →

HPE launches AMD-powered ProLiant Gen13 servers built for agentic AI inference

HPE announced on October 7 the first wave of ProLiant Gen13 servers built on AMD’s sixth-generation EPYC “Venice” processors, aimed at AI inference and agentic-AI workloads. The flagship DL585a, due in March 2027, supports up to eight GPUs from Nvidia, AMD or Intel, while a compact single-socket model arrives in November 2026; one analyst questioned whether 8-GPU boxes are yet mainstream for ordinary enterprise buyers.

Takeaway Building a server that can take GPUs from any of the three big chipmakers is HPE hedging against picking the wrong AI-hardware winner, rather than betting the line on one vendor.

Product & Business

Jio Platforms firms up India’s largest-ever IPO at a ₹1,065-1,119 price band as Axis Bank’s provisional Q2 numbers show advances up nearly 23%, even as TVS Automobile Solutions raises ₹425 crore for its Middle East push, India Mobile Congress wraps its closing day in Delhi, India’s weekly startup funding slides to $168 million, HCLTech and the big private banks line up their own results for next week, Manus weighs a Hong Kong listing, and the RBI’s fresh rate hike starts pinching festive-season EMIs

Takeaway Big-ticket capital events — Jio’s IPO, a bank’s balance sheet, a telecom congress — are all converging on the same fortnight, even as a slower funding week and costlier EMIs complicate the mood underneath them.

Jio Platforms sets a ₹1,065-1,119 price band for India’s largest-ever IPO, targets October 28 listing

Jio Platforms has fixed an indicative price band of ₹1,065 to ₹1,119 per share for its IPO, people familiar with the matter said this week, with the anchor book opening October 19, the public subscription window running October 21-23, allotment on October 26 and listing targeted for October 28 on the NSE and BSE. At the top of the band the offer would raise roughly ₹30,000 crore, making it India’s biggest-ever public issue.

Takeaway Firming up an exact price band and a listing date, after months of roadshows, is the clearest sign yet that India’s biggest IPO is now a matter of weeks, not quarters.

TVS Automobile Solutions raises ₹425 crore to expand its myTVS brand into the Middle East

TVS Automobile Solutions, which runs the myTVS automotive-aftermarket brand, said on October 5 it raised ₹425 crore (about $44-45 million) in a Series D round led by CE-Invests, the investment arm of UAE’s Crescent Enterprises — the largest single deal in a week where Indian startups overall raised roughly $168 million. The company said myTVS reached EBITDA breakeven in FY26 and will use the funds mainly for expansion into the Middle East and North Africa.

Takeaway An automotive-aftermarket brand being the week’s biggest funding story, not another AI startup, is a reminder that India’s profitable, boring businesses are still raising real money.

Axis Bank’s provisional Q2 numbers show gross advances up 22.7% year-on-year

Axis Bank released provisional, unaudited figures on October 5 ahead of its full results due October 17: gross advances of roughly ₹13,84,600 crore, up 22.7% year-on-year, and total deposits of about ₹14,52,100 crore, up 20.7%. The bank’s trading window stays closed until October 19, when the formal board-approved results follow.

Takeaway Releasing strong provisional numbers nearly two weeks before the full results is a bank getting ahead of the earnings-season narrative rather than waiting to be judged on the day.

HCLTech, Axis Bank and HDFC Bank line up their own Q2 results for the week ahead

HCLTech’s board meets October 12 to approve Q2 and first-half FY27 results and consider a third interim dividend, while Axis Bank and HDFC Bank have both fixed October 17 for their own board meetings on the September-quarter results, rounding out a busy fortnight of earnings behind TCS, which reported first on October 8.

Takeaway Spacing IT and banking results across two weeks means the market gets repeated chances to decide whether TCS’s beat was an industry signal or a one-off.

India Mobile Congress wraps its closing day in Delhi under the theme “Scale Without Boundaries”

The 10th India Mobile Congress closes today at Delhi’s Yashobhoomi after four days of sessions spanning 5G, 6G, AI and satellite connectivity. Telecom minister Jyotiraditya Scindia has cited nearly 500 million Indians connected to 5G within two-and-a-half years of rollout, and the event has pushed India’s ambitions through the Bharat 6G Alliance as a running theme across its anchor sectors.

Takeaway Closing a telecom congress by talking about 6G, before most of the world has even finished its 5G rollout, is India trying to claim a technology-leadership story earlier than usual.

RBI’s rate hike starts pinching festive-season EMIs just as Dhanteras-Diwali buying picks up

With most retail loans linked to floating benchmark rates, the RBI’s October 7 repo-rate hike to 5.50% is now flowing through to costlier EMIs on home, auto and personal loans right as festive-season borrowing for Dhanteras and Diwali ramps up. Analysts say buyers may respond by postponing big-ticket purchases, choosing cheaper models, or raising their down payments, while small businesses that lean on short-term credit to build festive inventory face added strain.

Takeaway A rate hike timed right before the one season Indian retail depends on most is a central bank choosing inflation control over consumption, and businesses are the ones absorbing that trade-off.

Manus weighs a Hong Kong listing as it resumes operations independent of Meta

Alongside its $500 million-plus funding round this week, Manus is reportedly considering a corporate restructuring to prepare for a future Hong Kong IPO, as the Chinese AI-agent startup rebuilds as an independent company following the collapse of its roughly $2 billion acquisition by Meta earlier this year.

Takeaway Going from a blocked Meta buyout to openly prepping its own listing in the same year is about as fast a reversal of fortune as a startup can manage.

India’s weekly startup funding slides to $168 million, less than half the week before

Indian startups raised a combined $168 million across 28 deals in the week of October 3-9, YourStory’s weekly roundup found, down sharply from $418 million the previous week. TVS Automobile Solutions’ ₹425 crore round was the largest single deal of the week.

Takeaway A week-on-week funding halving is less alarming than it sounds in a market this lumpy — one or two large rounds landing a week apart can swing the total by hundreds of millions either way.

Sensex, Nifty snap a two-day slide as IT and FMCG stocks lead a broad Friday rebound

The Sensex rose 879.09 points (1.23%) to close at 72,472.33 and the Nifty 50 climbed 288.65 points (1.3%) to 22,520.45 on October 9, recovering a chunk of Thursday’s ₹10-lakh-crore rout. Nifty IT gained 3.02% and Nifty FMCG rose 2.20%, with Apollo Hospitals, ITC and Eicher Motors among the top Nifty 50 gainers, as cooling bond yields and easing crude both aided the bounce.

Takeaway IT and FMCG — two sectors with little in common except being oversold on Thursday — leading the recovery is a sign this was a broad-based unwind, not a story about any one sector’s fundamentals.

Business Standard →

Wall Street’s Dow, S&P 500 and Nasdaq all close out the week higher as tech rebounds

The Nasdaq Composite rose 0.64% to 27,366.17, the S&P 500 gained 0.59% to 7,811.54 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average added 0.83% to 51,654.95 on October 9, with all three indexes finishing the week higher as tech stocks rebounded from Thursday’s AI-valuation-driven selloff. Treasury yields and oil prices both eased from earlier-week highs, with big-bank earnings due to kick off next week.

Takeaway Ending a volatile week with all three indexes higher suggests Wall Street still treats AI-valuation scares as something to buy through, not retreat from, so long as earnings season doesn’t say otherwise.

TheStreet →

Crude eases after Trump signals no US strike on Iran before the midterms

Oil prices pulled back in Friday trading after President Trump indicated the US would not move against Iran before the November 3 midterm elections, easing a geopolitical risk premium that had been pushing crude toward multi-week highs earlier in the week and giving both Indian and US equities one less headwind into the weekend.

Takeaway A single political signal on timing, not any change in the underlying standoff, was enough to move oil — a reminder how much of this year’s crude premium is sentiment rather than supply.

Markets shut for the weekend; HCLTech, bank earnings and Jio’s IPO window headline the week ahead

Indian markets are closed Saturday and Sunday after Friday’s rebound, with HCLTech’s Q2 results due October 12 and Axis Bank and HDFC Bank both reporting October 17. Jio Platforms’ now-firmed IPO price band and October 21-23 subscription window, alongside the RBI rate hike’s slow pass-through into EMIs, give the market plenty to digest before Monday’s reopen.

Takeaway A weekend pause right after a rebound leaves Monday’s open as the real test of whether Friday’s buying was conviction or just relief.
