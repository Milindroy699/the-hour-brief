Capital discipline, in Silicon Valley and in Delhi

Stratechery: "The CapEx Train Keeps Rolling"

Ben Thompson's core worry isn't compute or power — it's capital. "If AI is as valuable as it seems, then it should pay for itself, but that hasn't happened yet." Nvidia is building vendor-financing mechanisms so customers can spread infrastructure costs over time, quietly spreading leverage through the ecosystem; Google is tapping equity markets directly. The open question: does AI revenue show up before this debt comes due?

Elena Verna goes back to individual contributor — on purpose

After leading growth at Dropbox, Miro, Amplitude and SurveyMonkey, Verna moved to an IC role at Lovable after the org flattened, and personally shipped its enterprise pricing page to production. As AI shrinks the team size needed to ship, senior operators going hands-on is becoming a real career pattern, not a demotion.

Why foreign capital keeps avoiding India

Economist Ajay Shah traces India's post-2011 investment slowdown to a build-up of "arbitrary state power" — unpredictable regulation, weak contract enforcement, friction between political and business elites — that raises the return any foreign investor needs before India is worth the risk. His diagnosis: this needs institutional reform, not a single policy fix.

The US and Japan just staged their first joint yen intervention since 1998

The 10-year US Treasury now yields roughly 4.69% against 2.85% for Japanese government bonds — a gap wide enough that investors keep borrowing cheap yen to buy US debt regardless of intervention. Washington joined Tokyo's yen-buying because a forced Japanese sale of Treasuries to defend the currency would push US yields — and global borrowing costs — even higher. The Bank of Japan's next policy meeting, in September, is the one that actually matters.
