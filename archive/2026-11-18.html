AI & Tech

Nvidia posts a record $115.8 billion third quarter as Anthropic prices its IPO at up to a $1.4 trillion valuation ahead of Thursday's Nasdaq debut, even as CISA adds six actively exploited flaws — spanning Cisco, Barracuda, Outlook and Chrome — to its must-patch list, LangChain raises $300 million at a $3 billion valuation, and SoftBank leads a $500 million round for German robotics maker Agile Robots

Takeaway Today’s tech story is Nvidia’s numbers doing the talking a month of boardroom turmoil couldn’t, Anthropic pricing cautiously into that same jittery market, and fresh money still finding its way into AI infrastructure and robotics regardless.

Nvidia posts record $115.8 billion Q3 revenue, up 103% year-on-year, and guides Q4 to $130 billion

Nvidia reported fiscal third-quarter revenue of $115.8 billion for the quarter ended October 25, up 103% year-on-year and 20% sequentially, with Data Center revenue of $107.6 billion, up 110%, the company's earnings release said. Non-GAAP earnings per share came in at $2.68, topping the $2.47 Wall Street had expected, and Nvidia guided fourth-quarter revenue to about $130.0 billion, plus or minus 3%, while saying its new Vera Rubin NVL72 racks are already shipping to CoreWeave, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure, Oracle Cloud Infrastructure and Nebius.

Takeaway Beating a guidance number set before its CEO's abrupt exit is Nvidia's clearest signal yet that the business didn't skip a beat even if its boardroom did.

Anthropic targets up to a $1.4 trillion valuation as it prices its IPO Wednesday evening

Anthropic plans to sell 825 million shares priced at $70–$78 each, which could raise up to $64.4 billion and value the company at up to $1.4 trillion on a fully diluted basis, with pricing expected the evening of November 18 and trading to begin November 19 on Nasdaq under ticker ANT, TheNextWeb reported. The company is separately raising about $26 billion via private placement from cornerstone investors including Google, Nvidia and TSMC; the final target sits below the $1.5–$2 trillion range once floated during planning.

Takeaway Landing below the valuation range once floated, while still roughly 50% above its last private mark, is Anthropic picking a number it's confident will clear rather than one built to impress.

CISA adds six actively exploited flaws to its federal must-patch list, spanning Cisco, Barracuda, Microsoft and Chrome

CISA's November 10 bulletin added six vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog based on evidence of active exploitation: a Cisco BroadWorks flaw, two Barracuda flaws, an Openwave Messaging Webtop flaw, a Microsoft Outlook flaw requiring no user interaction, and a Google Chromium use-after-free bug, under Binding Operational Directive 26-04. CISA set remediation deadlines for federal civilian agencies and urged all organizations to patch on the same timeline.

Takeaway Six unrelated products — a telecom platform, an email gateway, Outlook and Chrome — landing in the same bulletin is less a pattern than a reminder that attackers don't specialize by vendor.

LangChain raises $300 million at a $3 billion valuation, more than doubling its price tag in just over a year

LangChain confirmed a $300 million Series C at a $3 billion post-money valuation in a November 13 blog post by CEO Harrison Chase, led by existing investor CapitalG with new backers GIC and DFJ Growth alongside returning investors IVP and Sapphire Ventures. The round follows an October 2025 Series B of $125 million at a $1.25 billion valuation, and the company says more than 7,000 organizations now use its commercial LangSmith platform, naming Harvey, Rippling, Nvidia, Toyota and Salesforce as customers.

Takeaway A 2.4x valuation jump in about a year, on an agent-tooling platform rather than a model itself, is a bet that picks-and-shovels infrastructure scales as fast as the agents it supports.

SoftBank leads a $500 million round valuing German robotics maker Agile Robots at $8.5 billion

Munich-based Agile Robots raised $500 million at an $8.5 billion valuation in a round led by SoftBank Group, with Nvidia, Hitachi and T.Capital also participating, Bloomberg reported November 12. The company says the raise brings its total funding to about $2 billion, up from a $1 billion valuation at its 2021 SoftBank-led Series C, and will fund US market entry and new industrial applications.

Takeaway Valuing a robotics maker at roughly 8.5x what SoftBank itself priced it at four years ago is SoftBank doubling down on physical AI just as hard as it has on the software kind.

Product & Business

Home Depot's Q3 comparable sales turn positive for the first time in quarters as Eicher Motors calls its own best-ever quarter on record Royal Enfield and VECV sales, even as Vodafone Idea's loss widens on a one-off charge, India's October trade deficit hits a one-year high of $39.88 billion despite record exports, and Qualcomm tells staff its global layoff notifications begin this month

Takeaway Today’s business story is steady operational wins — Home Depot's comp turn, Eicher's record quarter — landing the same week trade and jobs data remind you the wider picture is still mixed.

Home Depot's Q3 comparable sales turn positive at 2.2% as net sales rise 4.9% to $43.4 billion

Home Depot's Form 8-K, filed November 17, reported third-quarter fiscal 2026 net sales of $43.4 billion, up $2.0 billion or 4.9% year-on-year, with comparable sales up 2.2% (US comparable sales also up 2.2%) after several quarters of renovation-spending weakness. Net earnings were about $3.7 billion, or $3.72 per diluted share.

Takeaway A return to positive comps after quarters of softness is Home Depot's clearest signal yet that the US home-improvement slump has bottomed.

India's October trade deficit widens to a one-year high of $39.88 billion even as exports hit a record $49.47 billion

Government data released November 13 showed India's October merchandise trade deficit rose to $39.88 billion, well above the $32.25 billion economists polled by Reuters had expected, even as merchandise exports hit a record $49.47 billion (versus $44.40 billion in September) on a 140.21% jump in petroleum product exports to $8.89 billion, Deccan Herald reported. Imports totalled $89.35 billion.

Takeaway Record exports and a record deficit in the same month means import growth — not export weakness — is now the bigger swing factor in India's trade math.

Eicher Motors posts its best-ever quarter as Q2 profit rises 15% to ₹1,573 crore on record Royal Enfield and VECV sales

Eicher Motors reported September-quarter consolidated profit up about 15% year-on-year to roughly ₹1,573 crore, on revenue up about 20% to roughly ₹7,422 crore, with operating margin near 24.3%, Free Press Journal and Autocar Pro reported. Management called it the company's best-ever quarter, with both Royal Enfield and VE Commercial Vehicles contributing.

Takeaway A profit print below one analyst estimate didn't stop Eicher from calling it a record quarter — a reminder that "best-ever" and "beat estimates" aren't the same claim.

Vodafone Idea's Q2 loss widens to ₹5,975 crore, mostly on a one-off exceptional charge

Vodafone Idea reported a widening consolidated net loss of ₹5,975 crore for the July–September quarter, driven largely by an exceptional loss of ₹462 crore, Business Standard reported.

Takeaway A widening loss driven mostly by a one-time charge is a different story than a widening loss from the core business — worth separating out before reading it as deterioration.

Qualcomm tells staff global "performance-focused" layoff notifications begin this month

CEO Cristiano Amon told staff in an internal memo, reported by Reuters, that Qualcomm will cut jobs worldwide following annual performance reviews, with employee notifications starting in November. The memo gives no headcount target and frames the cuts as addressing staff who have "consistently fallen short of expectations," against a backdrop of weak smartphone demand and Qualcomm's pivot toward AI data-center chips.

Takeaway An unsized global layoff with a start-this-month date but no number attached is a company managing the headline before it has to manage the math.

Nvidia's record $115.8 billion quarter sends shares up in after-hours trading, following weeks of selling into the report

Nvidia closed its Tuesday regular session at $155.46, down 0.4% on the day and well off its October 6 closing high, as investors had grown cautious heading into its fiscal third-quarter report. After the bell, Nvidia posted revenue of $115.8 billion, above Wall Street's estimate, and guided fourth-quarter revenue to about $130 billion; shares rose in after-hours trading, CNBC and NBC News reported.

Takeaway Rising only after a stretch of selling into the report is about as clean an answer as a stock price can give to a month of boardroom turmoil.

Nvidia announces a $150 billion buyback as its board signals Jensen Huang won't be reinstated

Alongside its Q3 results, Nvidia's board authorized a $150 billion share buyback, and NBC News reported the board does not intend to bring Jensen Huang back as CEO after his November 12 resignation, with interim CEO Colette Kress telling analysts on the call it is "business as usual."

Takeaway Announcing the buyback and the no-reinstatement signal in the same breath is Nvidia trying to let the balance sheet do the reassuring the boardroom itself won't.

GIFT Nifty signals a gap-down Wednesday open even as Nvidia's beat lifts global chip sentiment

GIFT Nifty futures traded around 25,258, down roughly 93 points or 0.36%, pointing to a gap-down start for the Nifty 50 on Wednesday even though Nvidia's earnings beat lifted sentiment for global chip stocks overnight, Business Standard's live blog reported, describing Asian markets as mixed in response. Brent crude eased slightly to $92.70.

Takeaway A chip-stock-friendly Nvidia beat failing to produce a green GIFT Nifty is Dalal Street drawing its own line between Wall Street's AI relief and its own, separately priced, worries.

Sensex and Nifty close at a nine-month high Tuesday as Bank Nifty hits a record

India's Nifty 50 closed Tuesday, November 17 at 25,387.40, up 96.30 points (0.38%), while the Sensex finished at 82,532.19, up 427.40 points (0.52%), its second straight day of gains. Bank Nifty hit a record high of 62,273.25, up 697.35 points, with banking, realty and PSU bank stocks leading, HDFC Sky and India Infoline reported.

Takeaway A Bank Nifty record landing the same week Wall Street's AI trade wobbles is a clear sign Indian money is treating this month's chip-stock anxiety as someone else's problem.

On why compounding doesn't move in a straight line, via Zerodha Varsity: Varsity's piece on non-linear compounding uses the Nifty 50 Total Returns Index to make the point — the index delivered a 10.36% CAGR between March 2008 and March 2023, enough to turn ₹1 lakh into roughly ₹4.4 lakh, but almost none of the months along that path actually returned close to 10.36%. A steady, straight-line path at that rate would have reached only about ₹1.64 lakh by March 2013; the real index trailed that line for most of the period and closed the gap through a handful of outsized rallies. The lesson: most of a long-term return comes from a small number of unusually good stretches, not steady gains every year.
