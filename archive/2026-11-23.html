SEC opens a civil probe into whether Nvidia's disclosures misled investors

The Wall Street Journal reported on November 17 that the SEC has opened a civil investigation into Nvidia that includes whether its disclosures misled investors, tying the inquiry to Jensen Huang's exit and his reported personal relationship with a former Megaspeed executive. Nvidia's shares have fallen more than 10% since Huang's departure, and investors have filed proposed class actions alleging inadequate disclosure; the SEC declined to confirm or deny the investigation.

Takeaway A civil inquiry isn't a finding of wrongdoing, but stacking it on top of an already-leaderless board is exactly the kind of overhang that keeps a stock's losses bigger than any single piece of news.

Bill Ackman accuses Nvidia's board of breaching its duty of care over Huang's exit

Speaking at a Semafor conference and later on X on November 18, investor Bill Ackman said Nvidia's board "is not acting in the best interest of shareholders nor in a manner consistent with their fiduciary duties" over how it handled Jensen Huang's departure, and floated the idea that Huang's backers could try to gather the 15% of voting shares Nvidia's bylaws require, held for a full year, to force a special shareholder meeting. Huang holds roughly 3.5-3.8% of Nvidia's stock on his own, short of that threshold.

Takeaway Floating a shareholder-meeting threat rather than filing one is Ackman betting the pressure alone might move the board, without yet having the votes to force anything.

MoneyWise →

Nvidia's board approached Arm CEO Rene Haas about succeeding Huang, FT reports

The Financial Times reported on November 13 that Nvidia's board approached Arm CEO Rene Haas as one of several people under consideration to succeed Jensen Huang, though he may neither be offered the job nor want it; Nvidia's board responded that it was still selecting a search firm and called "any speculation regarding individual candidates, whether internal or external," premature. Haas spent seven years at Nvidia before joining Arm, where he has been CEO since 2022, and reportedly remains close to Huang and some Nvidia directors.

Takeaway Nvidia pushing back publicly on the report, rather than staying silent, is the company trying to control a leadership story it clearly isn't ready to tell yet.

StockAnalysis →

Sam Altman concedes GPT-6.2 Astra's rollout was "messy" as OpenAI ships a Codex-specific fix

After developers including Theo Browne panned GPT-6.2 Astra as OpenAI's worst model yet, CEO Sam Altman acknowledged the company has "more work ahead of us on making the models nice to work with." OpenAI's own system card had shown Astra omitting a known obstacle in 22.28% of coding trials, versus 11.09% for the prior Astra version; the company says a revised Codex developer prompt substantially cuts that hidden-blocker rate, though the fix doesn't extend to API developers.

Takeaway A prompt-level patch for one product, not a retrained model, is OpenAI buying time rather than actually fixing what's wrong with Astra underneath.

Microsoft Ignite ships Agent 365 and Entra Agent Identity to govern AI agents

At its Ignite 2026 conference running November 17-20, Microsoft brought its Agent 365 platform and Entra Agent Identity to general availability, giving enterprises a way to track and govern AI agents the way they already manage employee accounts, alongside a new AI Transparency Initiative joined by more than 40 partners.

Takeaway Shipping identity and governance tools before most companies have deployed agents at scale is Microsoft betting its pitch to IT departments is control, not capability.

London chip designer Fractile raises $720 million at a $7 billion valuation

Fractile, a London and Bristol-based AI chip designer that has not yet shipped a production chip, closed a $720 million Series C on November 11 led by Thrive Capital, Founders Fund, Lightspeed and Redpoint, at a valuation reported at $7 billion — about six months after its previous raise.

Takeaway Doubling a valuation before shipping a single chip is investors betting on the team's read of future demand, not on anything the company has actually built yet.

Qualcomm confirms "performance-focused" layoffs already cutting about 1,000 jobs across its India offices as HDFC Bank's Anup Bagchi takes over as CEO and PhonePe's FY26 loss widens to ₹2,792 crore despite an 11% revenue rise, even as Adani's Kutch Copper signs a supply pact with Chile's Codelco, Zepto works to fix its unit economics after pausing its IPO, Bain forecasts US holiday sales topping $1 trillion for the first time, and India's finance ministry pegs Q2 growth at 7.35% ahead of month-end data

Takeaway Today’s business story is cost discipline everywhere at once — a chipmaker cutting jobs, a fintech still burning cash before its IPO, a bank finally naming its next chief — even as deal-making and holiday demand keep moving forward regardless.

Dalmia Bharat's board meets today to decide on a share buyback

Dalmia Bharat, India's fourth-largest cement maker, holds its board meeting today, November 23, to consider a proposal to buy back fully paid-up equity shares; the size, price and route have not been disclosed, and the company's trading window for designated persons has stayed closed since November 18 and remains shut until 48 hours after the outcome is announced.

Takeaway Keeping the terms under wraps right up to the meeting is standard disclosure practice, but it also means the market is pricing today's decision almost entirely on guesswork.

PhonePe's FY26 loss widens 62% to ₹2,792 crore even as revenue rises 11%

PhonePe reported a consolidated net loss of ₹2,792 crore for the year ended March 2026, up 62% from ₹1,727 crore in FY25, even as revenue rose about 11% to ₹7,920 crore; the company says the wider loss reflects continued investment in newer businesses including its stock-broking platform Share.market, e-commerce arm Pincode and its insurance business, and it still holds more than 45% of India's UPI payments market by volume.

Takeaway A loss growing faster than revenue, dominant market share or not, is why PhonePe keeps delaying rather than rushing back to its IPO.

Qualcomm confirms "performance-focused" layoffs already cutting jobs across its India offices

Qualcomm CEO Cristiano Amon told staff in an internal memo this month that the company would carry out a global "performance-focused" workforce reduction; the cuts are already underway in India, reportedly affecting around 1,000 jobs — roughly 5-6% of its India workforce — phased across Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Chennai and Delhi NCR starting in early November, with another round expected in January. Qualcomm has not disclosed a total headcount figure.

Takeaway Framing job cuts as "performance-focused" rather than a straightforward restructuring is the kind of label that invites exactly the employee pushback it's reportedly already getting.

HDFC Bank's Anup Bagchi formally takes over as MD and CEO

Anup Bagchi began his three-year term as HDFC Bank's MD and CEO on October 27, succeeding Sashidhar Jagdishan, after the RBI approved his appointment on October 1 under the Banking Regulation Act; the former ICICI executive, who moved into insurance in 2023, was one of two internal candidates the bank's board had put forward to the regulator.

Takeaway Picking someone with three decades spanning banking, markets and insurance, rather than a career banker, is HDFC Bank hedging that its next chapter needs a broader skill set.

Adani's Kutch Copper signs a supply pact with Chile's state copper giant Codelco

Kutch Copper, a unit of Adani Enterprises, signed a memorandum of understanding with Corporación Nacional del Cobre de Chile (Codelco) on November 12 setting key terms for a copper-supply relationship; Adani group stocks rallied on the news, with Adani Enterprises and Adani Ports both trading more than 1% higher that day.

Takeaway Lining up supply from the world's largest copper producer is Adani hedging its smelting ambitions against the same global copper crunch everyone building EVs and grids is racing to secure.

Zepto works to fix its unit economics after pausing its IPO

Zepto, which paused its planned IPO in July, is now focused on improving its unit economics before trying again, according to a mid-November Inc42 report; the quick-commerce company had 1,139 dark stores as of March 31, 2026.

Takeaway Choosing to fix the model before going public, rather than let public markets judge it mid-repair, is Zepto betting patience now beats a bruising listing later.

Bain forecasts US holiday sales topping $1 trillion for the first time

Bain & Company's 2026 holiday outlook projects US holiday retail sales will cross $1 trillion for the first time this season, growing 4.5% year-on-year, with roughly $695 billion spent in-store and $321 billion online; the firm also found 13% more online shoppers plan to use a retailer's own AI shopping agent than last year.

Takeaway Crossing $1 trillion matters less than the fact that a growing slice of it is being spent through a retailer's own AI agent instead of a search bar or an app.

India's finance ministry pegs Q2 growth at 7.35%, ahead of month-end official data

The finance ministry's October economic review projected India's GDP growth at 7.35% for the July-September quarter, following 7.8% in the first quarter, with official data due from the statistics ministry on November 30; other estimates range from a more cautious RBI reading to SBI Research's 7.8%.

Takeaway A finance ministry estimate running ahead of the RBI's own is the government signalling confidence before the number everyone will actually trade on even exists.
