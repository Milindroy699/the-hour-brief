AI & Tech

Trump creates a White House “Super Intelligence Force” chaired by intelligence chief Jay Clayton as OpenAI fires three researchers for sharing sensitive information outside the company, even as Meta’s Muse Spark chatbot co-authors six math papers and Anthropic’s own Mythos model uncovers a critical bug exploited within a day of disclosure, while Google squeezes free Gemini users onto its weakest model, Amazon pledges $1 billion to data-center host communities, and an Arizona court vacates a sentence after a judge leaned on an AI-generated “victim” video

Takeaway Today’s AI stories split into two piles — institutions formalizing their grip on the technology (a new federal task force, a bug-hunting model) and institutions being forced to answer for how it’s already being used against people.

Trump creates a White House “Super Intelligence Force,” names intelligence chief Jay Clayton to lead it

President Trump announced a new federal task force dubbed the Super Intelligence Force, chaired by Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton, per NPR, with reporting corroborated by CBS News and PBS NewsHour. Vice chairs include FTC Chair Andrew Ferguson and a Pentagon technology official, with Vice President JD Vance, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent also serving; Clayton told the Wall Street Journal a report on AI’s risks and opportunities is due within 120 days.

Takeaway Putting the intelligence chief in charge of a formal task force, not just an advisory title, is Washington moving from statements to a standing body with a deadline.

OpenAI dismisses three researchers for sharing sensitive information outside the company

OpenAI parted ways with researchers Jasmine Wang, Tomek Korbak and Mikita Balesni after an internal investigation found they shared confidential company information with an outside AI-safety advocacy group outside approved channels, per TechCrunch, citing the Wall Street Journal. An OpenAI spokesperson confirmed the company “parted ways with 3 individuals for violating our policies on accessing and handling sensitive company information.”

Takeaway Drawing a hard line against staff who route concerns to outside watchdogs rather than internal process makes safety whistleblowing a live tension inside the company, not just an industry debate.

TechCrunch →

Meta’s Muse Spark chatbot co-authors six math papers, five resolving previously open questions

Mathematicians working with Meta’s Muse Spark model, using its standard chat “Thinking Mode” rather than a specialized research system, produced six papers spanning probability, differential equations and group theory, per Meta’s own research blog, with five said to resolve questions previously considered open. In one case the model wrote a search program that found a 384-element counterexample disproving a 2024 conjecture, which human mathematicians then verified; Meta has not disclosed whether the papers underwent formal peer review.

Takeaway A general-purpose consumer chatbot contributing to research-grade proofs is notable, but “not yet peer-reviewed” is doing a lot of work in that sentence.

Meta AI Research →

Anthropic’s Mythos model finds a critical file-server flaw — exploited in the wild within a day

Researcher Zach Hanley of Horizon3 used Anthropic’s Mythos model to discover CVE-2026-61500, a critical authentication-bypass bug in Rejetto HTTP File Server that lets attackers forge admin sessions by reversing the app’s session-cookie key generation, per The Register. Within roughly 24 hours of disclosure, security firm VulnCheck observed a China-based IP actively exploiting the flaw against US and Japanese servers; Rejetto has patched the issue in version 3.2.1.

Takeaway AI-assisted vulnerability research is now fast enough that the gap between disclosure and real-world exploitation is shrinking to hours, not weeks.

The Register →

Google will cut free Gemini users down to its weakest model starting October 9

Google is restructuring Gemini access tiers effective October 9, per The Decoder: free users will be limited to the lightweight “Flash-Lite” model, losing access to Flash and Pro entirely, while even $4.99/month “AI Plus” subscribers lose Pro access. Full access to Pro, Flash and Flash-Lite is now reserved for $19.99/month “AI Pro” tiers and above, with Google citing rising compute costs as the rationale.

Takeaway Free-tier AI access is getting squeezed as inference costs bite — the “free model good enough” era of chatbots may be ending.

The Decoder →

AWS ends government data-center NDAs, pledges over $1 billion to host communities

AWS CEO Matt Garman announced the company will stop using NDAs with government agencies on data-center permitting and launched “Built Together,” a five-year, $1 billion-plus commitment funding free community-college and trades training plus energy-efficiency upgrades in towns hosting AWS facilities, per GeekWire. Garman framed the move as a response to more than 100 local moratoriums under consideration nationwide that he said could threaten the US AI lead.

Takeaway Amazon is trying to defuse a growing local backlash against AI data centers before it slows its own buildout — a sign the opposition is already biting.

GeekWire →

Arizona court vacates a sentence after a judge relied on an AI-generated “victim” video

The Arizona Court of Appeals ruled that a sentencing judge’s reliance on an AI-generated video depicting deceased road-rage victim Christopher Pelkey “speaking” at sentencing was a “fundamental error,” per NBC News, vacating Gabriel Horcasitas’s 10.5-year sentence (though not his conviction) and ordering resentencing. The court noted the sentencing judge had said he “loved” the video and felt it “was genuine.”

Takeaway Believed to be the first US case of an AI-recreated victim statement ruled prejudicial enough to overturn a sentence — a preview of fights courts will keep having as synthetic media becomes routine.

Product & Business

Jio Platforms’ IPO timeline firms up for a late-October listing as Snapdeal parent AceVector lists on the NSE and BSE today, even as Ather Energy extends its lead over a fading Ola Electric, Flipkart sets October 9 for its Big Fashion Festival rival Big Billion Days, fresh funding lands for interiors platform Gravity and paediatric-health startup Primerry, and India’s overall IPO fundraising slides 21% for the year

Takeaway Big-ticket India IPOs keep edging toward the finish line and festive-season sales keep stacking up, even as the broader IPO market and one of the EV sector’s former leaders both show real strain underneath.

Jio Platforms’ IPO timeline firms up: anchor book around October 19, listing around October 28

Trade reports suggest Jio Platforms could open its anchor book around October 19, run public bidding October 21-23, and list around October 28, at a base valuation of roughly ₹13-15 lakh crore, per a report carried by Indian News Network. Reliance has not confirmed the dates or a price band, and reports disagree on the exact band; the issue remains a 100% fresh issue of about 27 crore shares (roughly $3.8 billion) with proceeds earmarked largely to repay about ₹27,500 crore of Jio’s borrowings.

Takeaway India’s largest-ever IPO is entering its final pre-launch stretch, but with the dates and price still unofficial, nothing here should be treated as locked in yet.

Indian News Network →

Snapdeal parent AceVector lists on the NSE and BSE today after a 5.07x-subscribed IPO

AceVector Limited, Snapdeal’s parent, lists on the NSE and BSE today after its ₹420 crore IPO — priced at ₹30-32 a share, comprising a ₹287 crore fresh issue and a ₹133 crore offer for sale — closed subscribed 5.07 times. The listing price was still to be determined as of this morning.

Takeaway A 5x-subscribed book is a respectable, not blockbuster, send-off for a company whose brand name was once synonymous with Indian e-commerce.

HDFC Sky →

Ather Energy extends its lead as Ola Electric’s registration share collapses to 7.7%

Ather Energy registered 30,477 electric two-wheelers in September 2026, up 57% year-on-year, per Vahan registration data reported by Autocar Pro, even as its own market share slipped slightly to about 15% as the overall category grew faster. Ola Electric’s H1 FY27 registrations fell 23% year-on-year to 85,634 units, with its market share collapsing to about 7.7% from 17.4% a year earlier; industry-wide e-2W registrations surged 85% year-on-year in September to over 2 lakh units.

Takeaway Ola Electric’s slide isn’t a quarterly blip — its retail market share has more than halved in a year while Ather and the legacy two-wheeler makers consolidate the gains.

Autocar Professional →

Flipkart sets October 9 for Big Billion Days, with early access from October 8

Flipkart confirmed its flagship festive sale, Big Billion Days, will run from October 9, with early access for Flipkart VIP, Black and credit-card members starting October 8, per The CSR Journal; bank offers include up to 10% instant discount with Axis Bank and ICICI Bank cards.

Takeaway With Myntra’s festival on October 7 and Flipkart’s on October 9, India’s two biggest e-commerce players have converged their festive calendars almost exactly this year, intensifying the fight for the same shopping week.

The CSR Journal →

Home-interiors platform Gravity raises $15 million led by 3one4 Capital and Info Edge Ventures

Gravity, a home-interior-materials platform founded by former Livspace executives Saurabh Jain and Lalit Mittal, raised $15 million in equity and debt led by 3one4 Capital and Info Edge Ventures, with Alteria Capital, Genesis and a group of angels also participating, per Pulse2. The company says it already has a multi-hundred-crore revenue base and positive EBITDA, and plans to expand into doors, windows, lighting and wall surfaces.

Takeaway Raising growth capital on top of an already-profitable base, rather than to reach profitability, is a different kind of startup bet than most of this year’s funding rounds.

Pulse2 →

Paediatric health-tech startup Primerry raises ₹7.1 crore in a pre-seed round

Primerry, which is building everyday healthcare products for children aged 2-14, raised ₹7.1 crore in a pre-seed round backed by investor Ashish Kacholia, Lashit Sanghvi and a group of angels, per BioSpectrum India. The company plans to use the funds to strengthen its R&D pipeline and expand distribution of its eight SKUs, priced ₹499-1,499, through its own D2C platform and a planned quick-commerce rollout.

Takeaway A pre-seed round this small is a reminder that not every startup story this week is a mega-round — India’s funding market still has room for genuinely early bets.

BioSpectrum India →

India’s IPO fundraising has fallen 21% in 2026 as companies cut deal sizes

India’s IPO boom has lost steam this year, with total fundraising down 21% as companies trim issue sizes, accept lower valuations and delay listings amid cautious investor sentiment and volatile markets, per Outlook Business.

Takeaway The headline mega-IPOs (Jio, AceVector) are the exceptions holding the market’s attention — the broader pipeline behind them is actually shrinking.

Markets reopen after their longest losing run in 25 years; GIFT Nifty signals a positive start

Indian benchmarks enter Monday’s reopen carrying an eighth straight weekly loss — the longest such streak since the seven-week slide that ended in September 2001 — after persistent FII selling, elevated crude and rising global bond yields battered sentiment through late September. GIFT Nifty was trading above Thursday’s Nifty close this morning, pointing to a positive open, per HDFC Sky.

Takeaway An eight-week losing streak has drained sentiment to a 25-year low, so this morning’s GIFT Nifty uptick is a relief signal, not yet a trend reversal.

HDFC Sky →

Wall Street closes the week at fresh records as a soft jobs report cools rate-hike bets

The S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite both closed at records Friday — up 0.73% and 1.19% respectively, with the Dow up 0.49% — after September nonfarm payrolls showed just 29,000 jobs added versus roughly 84,000 expected and unemployment rose to 4.2%, per CNBC and NBC News. Markets read the miss as reducing the odds of an October Fed hike to about 20% from roughly 26%.

Takeaway Wall Street cheering a weak jobs report is the same trade it’s been running all year — bad labor data read as good news for the path of rates.

NBC News →

RBI’s rate call is no longer a formality — a Reuters poll now favors a hike, not a hold

The RBI’s Monetary Policy Committee meets October 5-7, with its decision due Wednesday, and a Reuters poll found nearly 60% of surveyed economists (35 of 61) now expect a 25-basis-point hike to 5.50% — which would be the RBI’s first hike since 2023 — as retail inflation has moved back above the 4% target and global bond yields have risen, per coverage of the poll carried by Telangana Today. The remaining panelists still expect a hold at 5.25%.

Takeaway Don’t treat Wednesday’s decision as a done-deal hold — the market is genuinely split between that and the RBI’s first hike in three years.

Telangana Today →

Brent holds near $102 as a third US carrier group heads to the Middle East

Brent crude jumped 4.4% to settle at $102.31 a barrel on Thursday after reports that the USS Theodore Roosevelt was deployed as a third carrier strike group to the Middle East, joining the USS George H.W. Bush and USS George Washington, per CNBC; Brent held near $102.25 through Friday.

Takeaway A third carrier group, not a new supply disruption, is what's keeping oil elevated — a geopolitical risk premium rather than an actual shortage.

CNBC →

Bajaj Auto slumps over 7% as September two-wheeler sales badly miss expectations

Bajaj Auto shares slumped more than 7% after September 2026 sales data showed domestic two-wheeler sales down 12% year-on-year (2,39,771 units versus 2,73,188 a year earlier), with total sales growth of just 5% badly missing Street hopes of 11-12%, even as exports rose 34% year-on-year, per Business Today. The miss revived demand worries just ahead of the Dussehra and Diwali festive season.

Takeaway A weak festive-season preview from India’s bellwether two-wheeler maker is a concrete read-through for the whole auto and consumption trade, not just a single-stock story.
