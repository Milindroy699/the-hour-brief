AI & Tech

Anthropic's IPO prospectus will name public backlash against AI and data centers as a formal risk factor as Nvidia warns its biggest customers of AI server price hikes above 15% on soaring memory costs, while Hugging Face explores a sale that could value the open-model hub at $13 billion or more

Anthropic's IPO prospectus will name public backlash against AI as a formal risk factor

Anthropic plans to list negative public sentiment toward artificial intelligence and data-center construction as a standalone risk factor in its IPO prospectus, CNBC reported on 21 August, citing people familiar with the filing. The disclosure would mark one of the first times a major AI developer has explicitly tied societal pushback to its financial outlook, warning that opposition could slow data-center construction, delay deployments and raise project costs through litigation and community concessions. Anthropic, which confidentially filed for its IPO in June, carries a private-market valuation near $1 trillion and could list at roughly $2 trillion — a debut expected to rank among the largest on record. CFO Krishna Rao has already fielded investor questions on competition, shrinking margins from open-source models, and the impact of a data-center slowdown during pre-IPO meetings in San Francisco.

Takeaway When a company built on AI has to formally warn its own investors that AI backlash is a liability, that's the clearest sign yet the industry's honeymoon with public opinion is over.

Nvidia warns its biggest customers of AI server price hikes above 15%

Nvidia has told the contract manufacturers that build AI servers for Microsoft, Google and Oracle that prices on systems built around its Vera Rubin and Grace Blackwell platforms will rise more than 15% for units shipping in early 2027, Bloomberg reported on 22 August, a figure independently confirmed by CNBC, Fortune and Tom's Hardware. The size of the increase will vary by chip generation and memory configuration, driven by soaring DRAM and HBM costs from Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron. On Nvidia's upcoming Vera Rubin NVL72 rack, memory now accounts for roughly 26% of total system cost, up from about 9% on Grace Blackwell, with the memory bill alone reportedly swelling past $2 million per rack.

Takeaway Nvidia is passing its supply chain's memory squeeze straight through to the hyperscalers, a sign the AI buildout's cost pressure is shifting from GPU scarcity to the memory market sitting underneath it.

Hugging Face is reportedly exploring a sale that could value it near $13 billion

Hugging Face has hired a bank to gauge buyer interest in a potential sale that could value the AI model-hosting company at $13 billion or more, Business Insider first reported, with the story confirmed and expanded by SiliconANGLE on 23 August. Talks are described as early, with no bidder named. The New York-based company, founded in 2016 by CEO Clément Delangue, Julien Chaumond and Thomas Wolf, last raised outside money in 2023 — a $235 million round led by Salesforce Ventures at a $4.5 billion valuation, with Google, Amazon, Nvidia, Intel, Qualcomm and IBM also participating. Hugging Face operates the Hub, the leading repository where developers publish, download and fine-tune open AI models.

Takeaway Nearly tripling Hugging Face's valuation in three years shows how central open-model infrastructure has become, even though the company itself has stayed out of the foundation-model arms race it services.

A free, anonymous AI coding model called Ox Alpha has the industry guessing who built it

An anonymous “stealth” AI model called Ox Alpha appeared on the OpenRouter marketplace on 20 August, offering a 1-million-token context window, multimodal input and free access for a roughly week-long promotional window, TechCrunch reported on 23 August. No company has claimed responsibility, but community investigation — including tokenizer probes matching Z.ai's GLM models and a leaked stack trace referencing Zhipu AI's internal API class names — has pointed toward the Chinese lab Z.ai as the likely source. AI analyst Andrew Curran, who initially flagged GLM as the top candidate, posted that “this morning people seem less sure of anything” as the identification effort continued. Developers who benchmarked the model, including one who ran it through the DeepSWE coding-agent test, reported results competitive with top-tier frontier models.

Takeaway Giving away a frontier-grade coding model for free and anonymously is either a marketing stunt or a pricing weapon — either way it raises the pressure on labs already cutting prices to hold onto developers.

TechCrunch →

A Chinese humanoid robot broke Usain Bolt's 100-metre record, then crashed into a stop mat

Tiangong Ultra, a humanoid robot developed by the Beijing Humanoid Robot Innovation Center (X-Humanoid), ran the 100 metres in 9.39 seconds during a preliminary heat at the World Humanoid Robot Games in Beijing on 22 August, beating Usain Bolt's human world record of 9.58 seconds, CBS News and Fox News reported. The robot lost stability near the finish and veered off the track into a padded stopping barrier rather than completing a controlled stop; a rival robot named Lightning, which had clocked an unofficial 9.32 seconds in pre-race testing, finished second in the official race at 9.47 seconds and collapsed, requiring removal from the track on a stretcher. The Games, the event's second edition, feature 51 events and more than 2,000 robots from 666 teams.

Takeaway The sprint time makes the headline, but robots crashing into barriers and collapsing at the finish line is the more honest measure of how far humanoid balance and control still lag raw actuator speed.

CBS News →

OpenAI, which once opposed it, now says California's AI safety law should be tougher

OpenAI's global affairs team said California's SB 53 AI safety law “should be amended to expand safeguards,” including monitoring frontier models during training and evaluating them for potential serious incidents, and strengthening cybersecurity protections across the model-development lifecycle, according to a company post reported by TechCrunch on 22 August. The reversal is notable because OpenAI previously opposed SB 53, which imposes transparency requirements and whistleblower protections on large AI developers and was signed by Governor Gavin Newsom in September 2025. OpenAI said that absent significant federal legislation, it now favors what it called “reverse federalism,” in which states move in a compatible direction on core protections that could become the foundation for a national standard, citing “recent incidents” as underscoring the need to update safeguards.

Takeaway OpenAI isn't turning cautious out of principle so much as trying to get ahead of a wave of state-level rules it can no longer count on a weaker federal law preempting.

Product & Business

Sachin Bansal's Navi raises its first-ever institutional capital — a $100 million Prosus round at a $1.3 billion valuation — as Nvidia pays $6 billion to license AI coding startup Poolside's model-building software and hire over 100 of its staff, while Battery Smart, Cashfree Payments and a Nobel Hygiene IPO filing round out a busy week for Indian fintech, EV infrastructure and hygiene-products manufacturing

Sachin Bansal's Navi raises its first outside institutional capital — $100 million from Prosus at a $1.3 billion valuation

Bengaluru-based fintech Navi, founded in 2018 by Flipkart co-founder Sachin Bansal, agreed to a $100 million investment from MIH Payments Holdings BV, an indirect wholly-owned subsidiary of Prosus, TechCrunch and Prosus reported on 19 August. It is the first institutional capital Navi has raised in its eight-year history, having operated largely on founder capital until now, and values the company at roughly $1.3 billion — below the roughly $2 billion valuation it reportedly sought when it explored institutional funding in 2024. The deal remains subject to customary closing conditions and Competition Commission of India clearance. It comes as Navi — which offers UPI payments, lending through its NBFC arm Navi Finserv, insurance and mutual funds — prepares an IPO targeting around ₹3,000 crore (about $314 million).

Takeaway A round priced below where Navi once hoped to raise, arriving via a Bansal–Naspers relationship rather than a competitive process, suggests institutional investors are marking down Indian consumer-fintech valuations just as the company heads toward public markets.

EV battery-swapping startup Battery Smart raises $19.5 million Series C at a $430 million valuation

Battery Smart raised ₹185.5 crore (about $19.5 million) in a Series C round led by existing investor Rising Tide Ventures, which contributed ₹112 crore, with Ecosystem Integrity Fund (₹49 crore) and Blume Ventures (₹25 crore) also participating, Entrackr reported around 21 August. The round values the company at approximately ₹4,075 crore (about $430 million) post-money. It follows a ₹66 crore pre-Series C tranche the company raised in March and a $15 million debt facility from Mirova in April. Founded in 2020, Battery Smart operates a swapping network for electric two- and three-wheelers and said proceeds will fund expansion, capex and working capital.

Takeaway Stitching together a Series C from existing backers rather than new marquee names points to a cautious late-stage funding environment for EV infrastructure, even as the company signals IPO ambitions.

Cashfree Payments narrows FY26 loss to ₹118.5 crore as revenue jumps 51% to ₹967 crore

Fintech payments firm Cashfree Payments India narrowed its consolidated net loss to ₹118.5 crore in the year ended March 2026, down from ₹154 crore in FY25, while operating revenue grew more than 51% to ₹967.4 crore from ₹640.1 crore, Business Standard reported on 20 August, citing regulatory filings. Payment gateway commissions were the largest revenue driver at ₹889.7 crore, up 85% from ₹480.9 crore a year earlier. The company said it achieved EBITDA profitability in March 2026 and is targeting full-year EBITDA profitability through FY27.

Takeaway Crossing into monthly EBITDA profitability just as revenue nears the ₹1,000 crore mark gives Cashfree a cleaner financial story than most Indian fintechs heading into a crowded payments IPO pipeline.

Fleetx.ai acquires logistics-tech rival Pando.ai, eyes IPO prep within 18-24 months

AI-native fleet and logistics technology company Fleetx.ai acquired transportation-management-system provider Pando.ai for an undisclosed sum, the companies announced on 20 August. Pando.ai — whose enterprise clients include Sun Pharma, Honda, Castrol and Godrej — will continue operating as a distinct brand, while Fleetx.ai, which counts UltraTech, Adani, Maersk, Unilever and Mondelez among its customers, builds a combined platform spanning fleet visibility to freight execution. The combined entity said it will begin preparing for a potential public listing over the next 18 to 24 months, targeting combined revenue of ₹300–400 crore on a profitable basis by the time of any listing.

Takeaway Bundling two enterprise logistics-AI vendors into one IPO-track entity is a bet that scale and a unified customer roster matter more to public investors than either company's standalone growth story.

Symbiotec Pharmalab's ₹1,757 crore IPO opens today, price band set at ₹938–988

Pharmaceutical-ingredients maker Symbiotec Pharmalab's initial public offering opens for subscription today, 24 August, and closes 27 August, with a price band of ₹938–988 per share, according to IPO-tracking platform Upstox. The issue comprises a fresh issue of up to ₹150 crore and an offer-for-sale of up to ₹1,607 crore, taking the total issue size to approximately ₹1,757 crore. Allotment is scheduled for 28 August.

Takeaway With an offer-for-sale more than ten times the size of the fresh issue, the raise is overwhelmingly an exit for existing shareholders rather than new growth capital for the company.

Hygiene-products maker Nobel Hygiene files DRHP for a ₹150 crore IPO, Quadria Capital to lead the sell-down

Quadria Capital-backed Nobel Hygiene filed its draft red herring prospectus with SEBI for an IPO comprising a fresh issue of up to ₹150 crore and an offer-for-sale of up to 1.55 crore equity shares by promoters and existing investors, Entrackr and DealStreetAsia reported around 21 August. Quadria Capital, via Orbit Investment Holdings, is Nobel Hygiene's largest shareholder with a 36.71% stake and will offload 95.7 lakh shares in the OFS, followed by Sixth Sense Ventures with 50 lakh shares. Proceeds from the fresh issue will go toward debt repayment and expanding manufacturing capacity at Halol, Gujarat, including a new adult-diaper production line. ICICI Securities, Motilal Oswal Investment Advisors and SBI Capital Markets are the book-running lead managers.

Takeaway A DRHP weighted toward paying down debt and adding an adult-diaper line, rather than funding a new category, shows Nobel Hygiene betting its growth is in deepening an ageing-India product line, not chasing a new one.

Nvidia pays AI startup Poolside $6 billion to license its model-building software and hire over 100 staff

Nvidia agreed to pay $6 billion for a non-exclusive license to Poolside's “Model Factory” — the system Poolside uses to build its open-weight Laguna family of coding models — and will extend job offers to more than 100 Poolside employees, according to reporting first published by Newcomer on 20 August and subsequently corroborated by Bloomberg and The Information. Nvidia separately invested $1 billion in Poolside at a $12 billion valuation. Poolside's three co-founders remain in place and the company continues operating independently, structured as a licensing-and-hiring deal rather than an outright acquisition.

Takeaway Structuring the deal as a license-plus-hire rather than a merger lets Nvidia absorb a rival model-builder's core talent and technology while sidestepping the antitrust scrutiny a straight acquisition would invite.

OpenAI cuts GPT-5.6 Sol API prices by more than 20% as Anthropic competition intensifies

OpenAI cut developer pricing for its frontier GPT-5.6 Sol model by more than 20% for the next three months, Reuters reported on 21 August. Standard short-context pricing dropped to $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens, from $5 and $30 respectively. OpenAI also cut prices for its mid-tier GPT-5.6 Terra model by 20% and its lower-cost Luna model by 80%; the cuts apply to the API and to credits for its ChatGPT Work and Codex products, while Pro, Plus and Business subscription pricing is unchanged. It was OpenAI's third self-initiated price cut in about four weeks, amid growing competition from Anthropic and Chinese AI labs.

Takeaway Three unprompted price cuts in a month reads less like routine cost optimization and more like OpenAI defending API market share as enterprise buyers openly flip between labs based on whichever just shipped a better model.

Markets brace for Kevin Warsh's first Jackson Hole address as Fed chair as Treasury yields stay elevated

The US 10-year Treasury yield held near 4.73% and the 30-year yield stood around 5.27% heading into the Federal Reserve's Jackson Hole Economic Policy Symposium (27–29 August), Saxo Bank's market commentary noted on 21 August. It will be the first Jackson Hole appearance for Kevin Warsh, who was confirmed by the US Senate 54-45 in May and sworn in shortly after as the Fed's new chair, succeeding Jerome Powell; Warsh is scheduled to deliver his keynote address on 28 August. Renewed pressure in global bond yields, alongside stalled Iran talks, kept risk sentiment cautious into the weekend even as US stocks rose Friday to trim a weekly decline.

Takeaway A hawkish-leaning new Fed chair facing already-elevated yields is a global cost-of-capital story that filters straight into FII positioning on emerging markets like India.

Brent crude holds near $94 as Iran signals openness to ending the standoff, US threatens tougher sanctions

Brent crude futures added 61 cents to close at $94.39 a barrel on 21 August, little changed as markets weighed conflicting signals from Washington and Tehran, CNBC reported. Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian said Iran would prefer to end the conflict with the US while in “a position of strength,” calling the existing memorandum with Washington a victory for Iran. At the same time, US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Washington would impose its “toughest-ever” sanctions on Tehran and intensify economic pressure. Oil had risen more than 5% for a second straight week; the US military said it had helped move over 660 million barrels of crude through the Strait of Hormuz since early May despite the standoff.

Takeaway Elevated, geopolitically-driven crude prices remain a direct inflation and current-account risk for oil-importing India, keeping FIIs cautious on the rupee and domestic equities.

Bitcoin claws back to $78,000 as a broader crypto rally resumes after Saturday's flash crash

Bitcoin climbed to $78,027 on Saturday 22 August, a 5.9% gain over 24 hours that pushed its market capitalisation to $1.57 trillion, recovering much of the ground lost in the flash crash that had briefly wiped out over $100 billion hours earlier, the Rio Times reported. The rally broadened beyond Bitcoin: Ethereum rose 8.12% to $2,515, XRP surged 14.61% to $1.4538 and Solana gained 6.87% to $93.65. BlackRock alone accounted for 83% of a $606 million single-day spot Bitcoin ETF inflow, a move also linked to a weaker US dollar and a White House meeting where President Trump and crypto executives discussed the CLARITY Act.

Takeaway A broad, ETF-flow-driven crypto recovery alongside a weaker dollar is another sign of risk-on positioning globally, a cross-current domestic equity investors will watch against India's own cautious FII stance.

Tempsens Instruments' ₹650 crore IPO closes today after one of the year's strongest subscription runs

Tempsens Instruments' ₹650 crore IPO, priced in a band of ₹285–300 per share, closes for subscription today after opening 20 August, according to Bajaj Broking and StockGro. The issue was subscribed more than 21 times by its second day, with its grey-market premium climbing steadily through the week to around ₹305 by 22 August — close to a 102% premium over the upper price band. Allotment is scheduled for 25 August and listing for 28 August on the BSE and NSE.

Takeaway A grey-market premium that roughly doubled the issue price within days of opening shows IPO demand is still wildly outpacing the secondary market's own mood — investors are chasing a listing pop, not making a longer-term call on the company.
