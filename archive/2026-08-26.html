AI & Tech

OpenAI dismantles a Russian ChatGPT propaganda network and Taiwan indicts nine people, including Nvidia and Super Micro staff, over a chip-smuggling scheme to China, as the SEC subpoenas four Wall Street banks over an AI hedge fund's near-collapse and Apple redesigns its cheapest Mac around on-device AI ahead of Nvidia's make-or-break earnings

OpenAI dismantles Russian ChatGPT propaganda network built around a fake "expert institute"

OpenAI disclosed on 25 August that it banned a cluster of ChatGPT accounts, operated from Russia via VPNs, used to generate content promoting the “International Burke Institute” (IBI) — a self-styled expert community whose website falsely listed academics including Francis Fukuyama and Noam Chomsky as contributors. A review of 36 articles attributed to IBI “experts,” published between September 2025 and May 2026, found 34 were copied from elsewhere online, often with false attribution. The network also produced a “sovereignty index” ranking countries to flatter Russia and criticize Western governments, with Telegram channels drawing 10,000–20,000 followers each.

Takeaway The audience size barely matters here — a few thousand Telegram followers is nothing — the real function was laundering pro-Kremlin framing through invented “expert” credibility a casual reader has no easy way to check.

Taiwan indicts nine, including Nvidia and Super Micro staff, over AI-server smuggling to China

Taiwanese prosecutors charged nine people — including a distribution manager at Nvidia's Taiwan office, two sales managers at Super Micro's Taiwan branch, and the CEO of Super Micro distributor Albatron Technology — with breach of trust and document forgery for illegally routing 74 AI servers containing restricted Nvidia B300 chips to mainland Chinese customers, PBS NewsHour reported on 25 August, citing the Associated Press. The servers were transshipped through Japan, Indonesia and Hong Kong to evade US export controls, according to the indictment announced Monday, 24 August.

Takeaway Naming individual Nvidia and Super Micro employees, not just shell-company middlemen, is Taiwan signaling it's willing to prosecute inside chipmakers' own distribution staff, not only the downstream resellers who usually take the fall.

SEC subpoenas Goldman, JPMorgan, Citi and Bank of America over AI hedge fund's near-collapse

The SEC sent subpoenas to Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan, Citigroup and Bank of America seeking records on their dealings with Situational Awareness, the AI-focused hedge fund run by 24-year-old former OpenAI researcher Leopold Aschenbrenner, US News reported on 24 August, citing Reuters, with CNBC confirming further detail on 25 August. The fund reportedly used up to 400% leverage and saw its assets fall from roughly $45 billion to about $10 billion during a late-July tech selloff, forcing a distressed sale of its public-equity portfolio to Citadel; the fund itself has not been accused of wrongdoing.

Takeaway The SEC isn't questioning Aschenbrenner's trading calls — it's questioning whether Goldman, JPMorgan and BofA improperly wore three hats at once as lender, margin-call trigger and seller-side broker, the more systemically dangerous question for how leveraged AI-stock bets get unwound.

Apple unveils M6 Mac mini and M5 Ultra Mac Studio, its bet on local AI compute

Apple introduced its M6 chip — its first built on a 2-nanometer process — in a redesigned Mac mini on 25 August, alongside an M5 Ultra Mac Studio, positioning both as machines for running and fine-tuning AI models locally rather than in the cloud, CNBC reported. Apple says the M6 Mac mini delivers up to 4x faster AI performance and 40% faster CPU performance than its predecessor; the new Mac mini starts at $899, $100 more than the outgoing model, and ships from 22 September.

Takeaway A $100 price increase on Apple's cheapest Mac, framed entirely around a chip upgrade, is doing quietly what Amazon's device price hikes did loudly this week — passing AI-era silicon costs on to ordinary buyers.

CNBC →

Nvidia's Groq 3 LPX inference chip enters full production

Nvidia said on 24 August that Groq 3 LPX, a dedicated chip for the “decode” phase of AI inference built on its Vera Rubin platform, has entered full production, citing benchmarks of 3,400 output tokens per second on 100,000-token long-context workloads — about four times faster than the nearest alternative platform, per Nvidia's own blog and corroborated by SiliconANGLE and Digitimes. The chip brings to market technology from Nvidia's roughly $20 billion acquisition of chip startup Groq's assets in December, its largest deal to date.

Takeaway Splitting inference into a dedicated “decode” chip separate from general-purpose GPUs is Nvidia's clearest admission yet that agentic AI's economics run on token-generation speed, not raw training compute.

Lambda in talks for up to $3 billion pre-IPO round at a $12 billion-plus valuation

AI cloud provider Lambda is negotiating a pre-IPO financing round of up to $3 billion that could value the company at $12 billion or more — roughly double its approximately $6 billion valuation from a Series E round in November 2025 — Bloomberg reported on 24 August. Lambda's 2026 revenue is on track to exceed $1.5 billion, and people close to the company said the round could position it for a public listing as soon as next year, following a separate $917 million debt facility it raised to finance chip purchases.

Takeaway Doubling its valuation in nine months while stacking nearly $1 billion in chip-purchase debt on top makes Lambda a test case for whether “neocloud” GPU renters can go public on leveraged growth before a correction catches up with them.

Investing.com (Bloomberg) →

Quintessent raises $40 million to fix AI data centers' laser shortage

Quintessent, a UC Santa Barbara spinout, raised $40 million in an oversubscribed Series A led by Cycle Capital, with Goldman Sachs' XIG-Industry Ventures, Susquehanna International Group and Ciena among the new investors, the company said on 24 August. Quintessent began customer sampling of a single-chip quantum-dot comb laser that consolidates many lasers into one for AI data-center optical interconnects, built on gallium arsenide rather than the capacity-constrained indium phosphide supply chain, which it says can cut data-movement power by up to 40% versus conventional designs.

Takeaway A laser-chip startup raising serious capital is a reminder that AI infrastructure's next bottleneck isn't compute or even power — it's the optical components needed to move data between racks fast enough to keep GPUs fed.

Tata Consultancy Services strikes a $1.5 billion AI pact with Porsche while Germany's Infineon acquires Bengaluru chip startup C2i Semiconductors, as Karnataka suspends the food licences of Amazon, Swiggy Instamart and BigBasket over toxic Datura listings and a fresh run of IPO paper — from Purple Style Labs' ₹680 crore RHP to Shankesh Jewellers' strong debut — keeps India's primary markets busy

TCS buys Porsche's MHP consulting unit in a $1.5 billion AI pact

Tata Consultancy Services agreed to acquire 100% of MHP Management- und IT-Beratung, Porsche's German IT-consulting subsidiary, for an enterprise value of about €320 million (roughly $373 million), alongside a separate five-year strategic partnership worth about €1.25 billion (roughly $1.46 billion) under which TCS will deploy AI across Porsche's manufacturing, engineering and customer-experience operations, CNBC reported on 25 August, following Bloomberg's initial report. MHP posted €742 million in 2025 revenue and employs about 4,500 people across Germany, India and other markets, and will keep operating under its existing brand.

Takeaway The €320 million price tag is almost incidental next to the €1.25 billion services contract it unlocks — TCS is buying its way into being Europe's default AI-integration vendor for a legacy manufacturer, not just buying a consultancy.

CNBC →

Infineon acquires Bengaluru's C2i Semiconductors to bolster AI data-center power chips

Germany's Infineon Technologies is acquiring C2i Semiconductors, a Bengaluru fabless startup founded in 2024 that designs power-delivery chips for large-scale AI data centers, Inc42 reported on 25 August. C2i had raised a $15 million Series A in February 2026 backed by Peak XV; financial terms of the acquisition were not disclosed, and the deal is expected to close in the fourth quarter. Infineon already employs roughly 2,800 people in India and said the deal deepens both its power-semiconductor portfolio and its Indian engineering footprint.

Takeaway A two-year-old startup getting bought by a global chipmaker within months of its Series A shows how compressed the AI-infrastructure supply chain's acquisition timeline has become.

Airbound raises $37 million to take on trucking with autonomous cargo drones

Bengaluru-based aerospace startup Airbound raised $37 million in a Series A led by Greenoaks, with DoorDash, Lachy Groom, Lightspeed and Humba Ventures also participating, taking its total funding to nearly $50 million since launching in 2023, TechCrunch reported on 24 August. The company builds lightweight autonomous “tail-sitter” aircraft that can carry roughly 100kg of cargo and has signed a deployment agreement with the Andhra Pradesh government to build a drone-delivery network across three cities, with a stated goal of scaling to 10,000 daily flights.

Takeaway Airbound is explicitly pitching itself against trucking economics rather than other drone startups — a bet that autonomous flight can now undercut ground logistics on cost, not just speed.

Karnataka suspends food licences of Amazon, Swiggy Instamart and BigBasket over toxic Datura listings

Karnataka's Food Safety and Drug Administration suspended the food-business licences of Amazon, Swiggy Instamart and BigBasket after poisonous Datura (thorn apple) seeds and fruit were found listed for sale as food on their platforms, Inc42 reported on 25 August. The order, dated 24 August, directed all three to immediately remove the listings; BigBasket said it had already stopped selling the item, while regulators called the companies' responses unsatisfactory. The action follows a similar suspension of 14 licences tied to Blinkit, Zepto and Instamart by Maharashtra's food regulator earlier in August.

Takeaway Quick-commerce's marketplace model, where platforms host third-party food listings with light vetting, is now facing state-by-state regulatory crackdowns rather than a single national standard.

Purple Style Labs raises IPO size to ₹680 crore, files RHP

Purple Style Labs, parent of luxury fashion platform Pernia's Pop-Up Shop, filed its Red Herring Prospectus and raised its IPO size by ₹20 crore to ₹680 crore, entirely a fresh issue with no offer-for-sale component, Inc42 reported on 25 August. The issue opens 31 August and closes 2 September; of the proceeds, ₹371.1 crore is earmarked for lease liabilities at its experience centres and back-end offices, and ₹138.9 crore for sales and marketing through FY30. The company counts Shah Rukh Khan and Sachin Tendulkar among its disclosed investors.

Takeaway A celebrity-investor-heavy, loss-making luxury e-tailer choosing an all-primary IPO structure signals it needs the cash for real estate and marketing burn, not an exit ramp for early backers.

Shankesh Jewellers lists at an 11% premium, beating grey-market expectations

Shankesh Jewellers, a Rajkot-based gold jewellery manufacturer, listed on 25 August at ₹103.30 on the NSE, an 11.08% premium over its ₹93 issue price, comfortably beating a grey-market premium that had implied only about 2% upside, Business Standard reported. The ₹367 crore IPO, comprising a ₹274 crore fresh issue and ₹93 crore offer-for-sale, was subscribed 2.8 times overall when bidding closed on 20 August.

Takeaway A modest gold-jewellery manufacturer outperforming its grey-market signal by roughly 5x suggests retail appetite for India's IPO pipeline currently exceeds even the pre-listing hype.

Coffee chain Blank Street raises $105 million at a $650 million valuation

US/UK coffee-and-matcha chain Blank Street raised $105 million in total, including $75 million in new Series C capital led by General Atlantic plus $30 million in secondary sales to existing shareholders, at a $650 million valuation, Axios reported on 24 August. Existing investors Left Lane Capital, General Catalyst and Tiger Global also participated; the company plans to use the funds to expand into affluent Southern California markets including Beverly Hills and Malibu.

Takeaway A quarter of the new round going straight to insiders as secondary liquidity, alongside fresh growth capital, is now the standard structure for keeping late-stage consumer-brand backers patient without forcing an IPO.

Climate-tech startup CarbonStrong raises ₹12.5 crore seed for a low-carbon cement substitute

Bengaluru-based CarbonStrong, founded in 2022, raised a ₹12.5 crore seed round co-led by IAN Angel Fund and Rainmatter, with Social Alpha and Full Circle Ventures also participating, Entrackr reported on 25 August. The company produces low-carbon binders from upcycled industrial waste that can replace up to 50% of cement in concrete and has run customer trials in Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Chennai; the funds will go toward its first production facility.

Takeaway A ₹12.5 crore seed round is a small check by fintech-era standards, but it's roughly the size hard-tech materials science in India runs at before proof-of-scale funding kicks in.

Nvidia's make-or-break earnings loom as chip stocks rally into the print

Nvidia reports fiscal second-quarter results after Wall Street's close on Wednesday, 26 August — landing in the early hours of Thursday in India — with consensus calling for adjusted earnings of $2.09 a share on revenue of roughly $91–92 billion, a jump of about 96% year-on-year, and the data-center segment alone expected near $75 billion, per The Motley Fool. Ahead of the print, semiconductor stocks rallied hard on the Nasdaq Tuesday, with Marvell up 5.54% and AMD up 4.63%, as traders positioned for a beat.

Takeaway A single earnings print from one US company is effectively holding the entire global risk-on trade — Tuesday's India rally included — in suspended animation until Thursday morning IST.

Oil tumbles 4% as markets shrug off Washington's toughest-yet Iran sanctions

The US Treasury unveiled a sweeping new round of sanctions on Iran-linked shipping, oil trading and petrochemical networks on 24 August, but instead of spiking, Brent crude fell roughly 4% to a one-week low on Tuesday as traders judged the measures less supply-threatening than a military escalation would be, CNBC reported. The sanctions carried no explicit deadline, spared Chinese banks, and came as a Pakistani intermediary was reported to be carrying a sanctions-relief proposal to Tehran.

Takeaway Falling crude on the back of a sanctions escalation is unambiguously good news for India's import bill and the rupee — a rare case where the geopolitical headline and the India-market-friendly headline are the same story.

Rupee holds near 95.73/dollar as RBI intervention caps volatility

The rupee traded little changed at about 95.7350 to the dollar on Tuesday, in a sub-one-paisa range, with state-run banks seen offering dollars in what traders read as RBI intervention to offset elevated crude prices, corporate dollar demand and derivative-contract maturities, Business Recorder reported.

Takeaway The RBI is actively fighting to keep the rupee boring right now, which, combined with Tuesday's oil price drop, removes one headwind for Nifty-listed importers and inflation-sensitive sectors.

Fed's preferred inflation gauge lands the same day as Nvidia's earnings

The July Personal Consumption Expenditures report, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, is due Wednesday, 26 August at 8:30am ET, the same day as Nvidia's results, per Kiplinger's economic calendar. Economists cited in the report expect a modest 0.2% month-on-month rise in core PCE, consistent with the Fed holding its 3.50–3.75% target range for the rest of 2026 rather than moving in September.

Takeaway A soft PCE print paired with a Nvidia beat would be the cleanest possible setup for a global risk rally spilling into Thursday's India session; a miss on either does the opposite.

Tempsens Instruments IPO closes with a 184x subscription

The ₹650 crore Tempsens Instruments India IPO, priced at ₹285–300 a share, closed subscription on 24 August at 184.07 times overall demand, driven by non-institutional and qualified institutional investors, Zee Business reported. Allotment was finalised on 25 August, with listing on the BSE and NSE set for 28 August.

Varsity principle for today: implied volatility is the market's forward-looking price of uncertainty, and it isn't the same as knowing which way things will break. Zerodha Varsity's “What Volatility Really Means” draws the distinction using Nifty constituents like Wipro and Infosys: realized volatility looks backward at how much a price has already moved, while implied volatility, priced into options, looks forward at how much the market expects it to move next — and it usually sits a notch above realized volatility as a normal premium for uncertainty that hasn't happened yet. Tuesday's session lived this out twice over: Brent crude priced in a large implied move ahead of the Iran sanctions announcement and then fell anyway, and chip stocks are doing the same dance into Wednesday's Nvidia print, with implied volatility elevated simply because nobody yet knows which way the number breaks.
