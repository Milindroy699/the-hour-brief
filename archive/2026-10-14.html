AI & Tech

Google ships its flagship Gemini 4 Argon model worldwide to challenge OpenAI and Anthropic as Apple’s long-delayed smart-home push finally ships a $299 HomeView hub, a $299 Apple TV 4K and a $149 HomePod mini 2, even as Anthropic holds a private investor day today ahead of a possible IPO some investors value near $2 trillion, NTT Data nears a $6.3 billion deal to build Anthropic’s first Japan data center in Osaka, China’s CXMT chip advances help drag Wall Street’s chip stocks lower, and AI-evaluation startup Arena raises $200 million at a $3.1 billion valuation

Takeaway Google and Apple both finally shipped products they’d spent months previewing, while Anthropic’s investor day and NTT’s data-center talks are reminders that the AI buildout itself is still running well ahead of anything a user can actually touch.

Google ships its flagship Gemini 4 Argon model worldwide to developers and businesses

Google released Gemini 4 Argon, its new flagship AI model, to developers and businesses worldwide on October 13, Reuters reported, as the company tries to regain ground against OpenAI and Anthropic after months of delays and a leadership shake-up at its DeepMind lab. Google says the model is built for “deep work” on complex, multi-step tasks in coding, finance, law, cybersecurity and science, and it launches at an introductory price of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens through the Gemini API, AI Studio and Google Cloud; it’s also rolling out to Gemini app subscribers on the AI Pro and Ultra tiers and to AI Mode in Search.

Takeaway Pricing the model before most people have even tried it is Google betting developers will decide Argon’s worth faster than reviewers will.

Google →

Apple’s “Welcome Home” event ships a $299 HomeView hub, a $299 Apple TV 4K and a $149 HomePod mini 2

Apple’s October 13 event in Tribeca confirmed the three products 9to5Mac had flagged the day before: HomeView, a new $299 smart-home display hub that serves up to six people with apps, widgets and personalised content; a fourth-generation Apple TV 4K, also $299 — $100 more than the model it replaces; and a HomePod mini 2 at $149, up $50 from the original’s price. Pre-orders opened the same day; the HomePod mini 2 ships October 30, while HomeView and the new Apple TV 4K ship November 13.

Takeaway Raising the price on every device in the refresh at once is Apple betting loyalty, not a sharper number, is what actually sells a smart home.

MacRumors →

Anthropic holds a private investor day today as backers float a valuation near $2 trillion

Anthropic is holding a private investor day for institutional backers at its San Francisco headquarters today, October 14, ahead of a possible IPO, according to Bloomberg. Investors cited in that reporting put a fair-value range of $1.8 trillion to $2 trillion on the company, well above the $965 billion post-money valuation set by its last priced funding round; a formal IPO marketing push could follow as early as the week of November 9, positioning Anthropic to list before Thanksgiving.

Takeaway Doubling your own valuation estimate before a single share trades is the surest sign an IPO is being sold on a story investors want, not one that’s been priced yet.

NTT Data nears a $6.3 billion deal to build Anthropic’s first Japan data center, in Osaka

NTT Data and Anthropic are in final-stage talks to build a data center in Osaka prefecture capable of drawing up to 500 megawatts, Nikkei Asia reported on October 13, with construction costs potentially reaching ¥1 trillion (about $6.3 billion). The two sides are reportedly aiming to sign a contract within the current fiscal year, with the facility — among the largest processing sites in Japan if built — possibly operating as early as 2028. NTT declined to comment on the report.

Takeaway Anthropic's first Japan data center landing in Osaka rather than Tokyo is the AI buildout following cheap land and power, the same way heavy industry always has.

China’s CXMT chip advances help drag Wall Street’s chip stocks lower on Tuesday

Chip stocks weighed on Wall Street on October 13 as renewed concern about China’s memory-chip maker CXMT closing the gap with Micron and other Western suppliers fed into a broader sector sell-off, contributing to the Nasdaq Composite’s decline even as the Dow and S&P 500 were little changed. CXMT, which listed in Shanghai earlier this year, has been expanding DRAM output rapidly and has said it plans to begin producing stacked high-bandwidth memory by the end of 2026.

Takeaway A Chinese memory-chip maker moving stock prices in New York shows the AI chip race now has a second front that has nothing to do with Nvidia.

TheStreet →

AI-evaluation startup Arena raises $200 million at a $3.1 billion valuation

Arena, the AI model-benchmarking platform formerly known as LMArena, closed a $200 million Series B on October 8 at a $3.1 billion valuation — nearly double the $1.7 billion mark set by its January Series A — co-led by Lightspeed Venture Partners and Khosla Ventures, with Salesforce Ventures, Dell Technologies Capital and existing backers also participating. The company, which grew out of a UC Berkeley research project crowdsourcing AI model rankings, said it reached $100 million in annualised revenue in June, up from $30 million at the time of its Series A.

Takeaway A benchmarking site growing its own valuation faster than most of the models it ranks shows how much money is chasing a referee's job in this race, not just the runners.

Product & Business

Wipro heads into Wednesday’s board meeting guided to roughly flat revenue as Zetwerk fixes its IPO price band at ₹143-151 a share and opens anchor bidding today, even as RPG Group chairman Harsh Goenka says the Tata Sons dispute has created anxiety across India’s family-run businesses, a stalled NPCI meeting leaves UPI’s new merchant fee unresolved just a day before its scheduled start, D2C brand DailyObjects raises ₹332 crore to fund 150 new stores, Goldman Sachs beats Wall Street estimates with a 23% revenue jump, and more than 70 companies report Q2 results this week including HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank and Axis Bank

Takeaway An earnings season just getting into its busiest week, an IPO market still issuing fresh paper, and a governance fight spreading anxiety well beyond the Tata group show how much of India’s corporate calendar this month is being set by other people’s deadlines.

Wipro heads into Wednesday’s board meeting guided to roughly flat revenue

Wipro’s board meets October 14-15 to approve Q2 FY27 results, with the company’s own guidance pointing to IT-services revenue of $2.574-2.627 billion — implying sequential growth of anywhere from a 1.5% decline to a 0.5% gain in constant-currency terms. That follows a Q1 FY27 in which revenue rose 1% sequentially to ₹24,480 crore but net profit fell 4.7% quarter-on-quarter to ₹3,360 crore; analysts expect this quarter’s focus to fall on margins and deal conversion rather than the topline.

Takeaway Guiding to a range that spans a decline and a gain before results land is Wipro keeping its options open on a quarter it isn’t yet sure which way will break.

Zetwerk fixes its IPO price band at ₹143-151 a share and opens anchor bidding today

Manufacturing platform Zetwerk has fixed its IPO price band at ₹143-151 per share, targeting a valuation of about ₹25,583 crore at the top end, Entrackr reported. The ₹5,012 crore issue — a ₹2,830 crore fresh issue plus an offer for sale of 14.45 crore shares — opens to anchor investors today, October 14, with public bidding running October 15-19, allotment around October 21 and a listing targeted for October 23 on the BSE and NSE.

Takeaway A price band landing just a day before anchor bidding opens is Zetwerk compressing the window investors have to actually weigh the number.

RPG Group chairman Harsh Goenka says the Tata dispute has created anxiety in family businesses

Harsh Goenka said on Tuesday, October 13, that the ongoing dispute between Tata Trusts and Tata Sons has created anxiety among India’s family-owned businesses, BusinessToday reported, with some firms now reviewing their board composition and the number of independent directors they carry. Goenka argued the trend was itself a problem, calling for stronger governance rather than fewer independent voices, as the underlying fight over chairman N. Chandrasekaran’s reappointment and a possible Tata Sons listing continues.

Takeaway A rival conglomerate’s own chairman publicly worrying about board composition shows the Tata rift has become a governance scare well beyond the Tata group itself.

BusinessToday →

A stalled NPCI meeting leaves UPI’s new merchant fee unresolved a day before its scheduled start

NPCI’s steering committee was due to meet on October 9 to decide whether to defer the 0.4% merchant discount rate due to take effect October 15 on UPI payments above ₹2,000, but the meeting didn’t take place, the Free Press Journal reported. No revised date or decision has been announced since, leaving merchants, fintechs and payment firms — who had asked for a delay to January 2027 to upgrade their systems ahead of the festive season — without clarity on whether the fee starts tomorrow as originally planned.

Takeaway A meeting that simply doesn’t happen, with the rollout date one day away, is its own kind of answer about how ready the system actually is.

Free Press Journal →

D2C brand DailyObjects raises ₹332 crore to fund 150 new stores

Lifestyle-accessories brand DailyObjects raised about ₹332 crore in a Series C round combining primary and secondary transactions, led by Xponentia Capital Partners, Anicut Capital and Axiom Asia Private Capital, YourStory reported. The round values the company at roughly ₹1,050 crore and lets early investor Roots Ventures partially exit at an 18x return; DailyObjects plans to open 150 new exclusive stores over five years on top of its existing network of about 350 retail touchpoints, after FY25 revenue grew 31% to ₹110 crore.

Takeaway Funding 150 new stores off FY25 revenue of just ₹110 crore is DailyObjects betting physical retail, not its online base, is where its next decade of growth sits.

YourStory →

Goldman Sachs beats Wall Street estimates with a 23% revenue jump

Goldman Sachs reported third-quarter 2026 net revenues of $18.68 billion and net earnings of $5.29 billion, with diluted earnings per share of $17.01 versus $12.25 a year earlier — a 23% revenue increase from the third quarter of 2025, according to the bank’s own earnings release. The result, published October 13 alongside other major US banks reporting the same week, came in well ahead of what analysts had pencilled in.

Takeaway A 23% revenue jump at Goldman is Wall Street’s dealmaking rebound showing up in hard numbers before it shows up in the broader economy.

More than 70 companies report Q2 FY27 results this week, including HDFC Bank and ICICI Bank

Over 70 companies are due to report July-September quarter results in the week starting October 12, Upstox reported, with HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, Axis Bank, Yes Bank and RBL Bank among the banks on the calendar alongside Wipro, Nestlé India, BHEL and Tech Mahindra. The banking results will be closely watched for credit growth and asset-quality trends after a mixed set of IT and FMCG earnings so far this season.

Takeaway A single week carrying this many results is the real test of this earnings season, not any one company’s number on its own.

Sensex and Nifty rally more than 1.5% as HCLTech’s earnings beat lifts IT stocks

The Sensex closed Tuesday, October 13 at 73,033.78, up 1,109.10 points (1.54%), and the Nifty 50 ended at 22,721.95, up 358.60 points (1.60%), snapping Monday’s decline. Kotak Neo tied the rally to a rebound in IT stocks, with HCLTech and TCS among the Nifty’s biggest gainers after HCLTech’s better-than-expected results, while easing crude prices from the previous session’s spike also helped sentiment.

Takeaway A single earnings beat lifting the whole IT index enough to flip the entire market's day is a sign of just how much weight that sector still carries on Dalal Street.

GIFT Nifty signals another gap-up for Wednesday after Tuesday’s IT-led rally

GIFT Nifty’s October 27 contract was trading near 22,850.50, up 429 points (1.91%), in late-afternoon trade on Tuesday — about 128.55 points above the Nifty 50’s own close of 22,721.95 — pointing to a further gap-up open for Indian benchmarks on Wednesday, according to a Business Standard report carried by HDFC Sky. The signal extends the same IT-led optimism that drove Tuesday’s session.

Takeaway A pre-open signal pointing higher after a day the market already rallied hard is the kind of momentum that usually invites its own reversal.

Wall Street closes little changed as a chip-sector selloff offsets Goldman Sachs’ beat

The S&P 500 closed barely changed at 7,805.45 (down 0.02%) on Tuesday, the Dow Jones fell 180.45 points (0.35%) to 51,670.10, and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 0.15% to 27,204.38, according to the Associated Press, as renewed worry about China’s CXMT closing the memory-chip gap weighed on chip stocks even as Goldman Sachs and other major banks posted strong quarterly results.

Takeaway A strong bank-earnings day that still can’t lift the index shows how much a handful of chip stocks now decide Wall Street’s direction on any given day.
