Sony Music and Warner Chappell sue Anthropic, naming founders Amodei and Mann personally

Sony Music Publishing and Warner Chappell Music filed suit against Anthropic on 28 August in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California, naming co-founders Dario Amodei and Benjamin Mann as individual defendants alongside the company, TechCrunch and Axios reported. The complaint accuses Anthropic of “a brazen campaign of illegally torrenting, scraping, and downloading” tens of thousands of copyrighted songs and lyrics to train Claude, and seeks statutory damages of up to $150,000 per infringed work plus up to $25,000 for each stripped copyright notice — a total that could reach into the billions. The suit follows Anthropic's roughly $1.5 billion settlement earlier this year with book publishers in the separate Bartz case.

Takeaway Naming the founders personally, not just the company, is publishers signaling they want this settled fast — before discovery reaches deeper into how Claude was actually trained.

Anthropic's IPO could target a record $2 trillion valuation, underwriters tell investors

Anthropic's underwriters have been telling prospective investors the company could seek a valuation around $2 trillion and raise more than $100 billion in its upcoming IPO, which would surpass SpaceX's record private-market benchmarks, the New York Times reported, as relayed by Yahoo Finance. That target is more than double Anthropic's roughly $965 billion valuation from its June funding round, and is reportedly being pitched on investor projections that revenue could reach $100–120 billion by the end of 2026; Anthropic confidentially submitted a draft S-1 to the SEC on 1 June and has not yet set a share price, count or listing date.

Takeaway Pricing an IPO off next year's hoped-for revenue rather than this year's actual numbers is exactly the kind of bet that makes a listing thrilling for early backers and risky for whoever buys in at the top.

Yahoo Finance →

Waymo publicly rejects Tesla's camera-only self-driving approach as a “false summit”

Waymo VP of onboard software Srikanth Thirumalai argued in an Axios interview published 26 August that after more than 200 million fully autonomous miles, “pure end-to-end systems are not able to meet our safety bar,” directly rejecting Tesla's camera-only, vision-based approach in favor of Waymo's sensor-heavy vehicles, which carry 13 cameras, four lidar units and six radars. New Street Research analyst Pierre Ferragu pushed back publicly, calling Waymo's defense of extra sensors “cognitive dissonance” and arguing improving vision AI will eventually make the sensor-heavy approach obsolete.

Takeaway This is the clearest public marker yet of a real technical fork in the robotaxi race — Alphabet betting on sensor redundancy, Tesla betting purely on scaled neural nets — and neither side is conceding the other might be right.

Axios →

OpenAI switches on ChatGPT ads across 31 European markets

OpenAI began showing ads inside ChatGPT for Free and Go-tier users across 31 European markets — including Germany, France, Spain, Italy, Poland and the Nordics — starting 24 August, Digiday reported, marking the six-month anniversary of its U.S. ad pilot after earlier rollouts in Canada, the U.K., Australia, Mexico, Brazil, Japan and South Korea. OpenAI VP of Ads Dave Dugan said ads are visually separated from chat responses and that advertisers get no access to users' chat histories; Plus and Pro subscribers remain ad-free.

Takeaway Running a real ad business inside a chatbot across most of the developed world puts OpenAI in more direct competition with Google's ad model than with any rival chatbot maker.

Apple's first foldable iPhone reportedly lands 9 September at a $1,999 starting price

Apple is preparing to unveil a foldable iPhone, said to be branded “iPhone Ultra,” alongside the iPhone 18 Pro line at an event around 9 September, Fortune reported, citing Bloomberg's Mark Gurman. The device is expected to feature a roughly 7.8-inch foldable display, an A20 Pro chip and Touch ID rather than Face ID, starting near $1,999 — below the $2,399 figure that had circulated in earlier reports.

Takeaway Apple entering foldables years after Samsung, at a price undercutting its own earlier leaks, looks less like catching up and more like waiting for the category's early mistakes to be made by someone else first.

Alibaba Cloud opens its first data centers in Brazil, entering South America

Alibaba Cloud launched its first cloud region in Brazil on 27 August, with two data centers in São Paulo run in partnership with local provider Ascenty, marking the Chinese company's first infrastructure footprint in South America, BNamericas reported. The launch follows Alibaba's Mexico region debut in 2025, with the company saying local hosting will give Brazilian companies lower latency, in-country data residency and compliance with Brazilian cybersecurity rules.

Takeaway Chinese cloud providers are racing to plant infrastructure in Latin America's fastest-growing digital economy well before Washington or Brussels get around to writing rules for where AI infrastructure is allowed to live.

NCLT set to decide today whether Byju's insolvency bidding can resume

India's National Company Law Tribunal in Bengaluru is due to hear arguments on 31 August on a challenge by Byju's founders Byju Raveendran and Riju Ravindran to a ₹11,433 crore claim filed by GLAS Trust, the US agent representing lenders on Byju's $1.2 billion term loan, Business Standard reported. NCLT had paused the Form G process — the formal invitation for buyers to bid for parent company Think & Learn — pending this hearing, after the founders argued the claim, left unchallenged, would command over 99% of the voting power on the committee of creditors.

Takeaway A single disputed loan claim, larger than any resolution plan likely to emerge, is now the hinge on which control of India's most storied edtech collapse turns.

Business Standard →

Swiggy agrees to refund Bengaluru restaurants after boycott threat forces a climbdown

More than 250 Bengaluru restaurants organised under the Bruhat Bengaluru Hoteliers Association, National Restaurant Association of India and FHRAI had threatened to delist from Swiggy and Zomato over inflated commissions and promotional charges levied without consent, Inc42 reported. Following talks with Swiggy CEO Rohit Kapoor, chief business officer Sidharth Bhakoo and Dineout head Swapnil Bajpai, Swiggy agreed to refund the unauthorised charges, while Zomato separately committed to making promotional campaigns opt-in rather than automatic; the restaurant bodies have since pushed their delisting deadline into early September to let the refunds go through.

Takeaway A WhatsApp-organised restaurant coalition just extracted a public refund commitment from a listed food-delivery giant — proof that supply-side leverage in a two-sided marketplace hasn't disappeared, it just went quiet for a few years.

ESDS Software Solution's ₹720 crore IPO draws strong retail demand, cool institutional bids

The cloud-infrastructure and AI-data-centre services provider's mainboard IPO, priced at ₹408–429 a share, was subscribed 2.10 times overall on day one, with retail investors bidding 2.69 times their quota and non-institutional investors 3.51 times — while qualified institutional buyers had barely covered 0.01 times their allocation, Business Standard reported. The issue stays open through 1 September, with listing tentatively set for 4 September on the BSE and NSE.

Takeaway Retail investors are already pricing in India's AI-data-centre boom; institutional money is waiting to see the balance sheet first.

Solar manufacturer Avaada Electro files updated papers for a ₹7,600 crore IPO

Avaada Electro, the Brookfield Renewable-backed solar cell and module maker, filed an updated draft red herring prospectus with SEBI for a ₹7,600 crore IPO — a ₹1,600 crore fresh issue plus a ₹6,000 crore offer for sale by promoter Avaada Ventures — Business Standard reported. The company currently runs 8.5 GW of solar-module and 3 GW of TOPCon cell manufacturing capacity across plants in Dadri and Nagpur, and plans to scale module capacity to 13.6 GW by FY28.

Takeaway Every large Indian solar manufacturer is racing to list before the PLI-subsidy math and a looming global panel glut change the story investors are being sold.

Space-servicing startup InspeCity raises ₹100 crore to refuel satellites in orbit

IIT Bombay-incubated InspeCity closed a ₹100 crore pre-Series A round on 27 August, led by veteran investor Ashish Kacholia and Speciale Invest, with Antler Elevate, Antler India and Shastra VC also participating, Deccan Herald reported. Founded in 2022 by Arindrajit Chowdhury, the company builds in-space servicing, robotics and docking technology to refuel and repair satellites, and plans four in-orbit missions over the next 12–18 months to validate its propulsion, sensing and docking systems.

Takeaway Indian deep-tech investors are now willing to back genuinely hard, multi-year space-hardware bets, not just the software-layer startups that dominated the last funding cycle.

Ben Thompson: AI's offense-defense asymmetry means attackers keep beating incumbents

In “Autonomy and Innovation” (Stratechery, 24 August), Ben Thompson argues agentic AI inherently favors attackers over defenders — attackers can let agents act fully autonomously because breaking things is the point, while defenders stay structurally biased toward keeping a human in the loop to avoid costly mistakes. He extends the idea beyond cybersecurity: incumbents will keep bolting AI onto existing workflows cautiously, while true AI-native competitors emerge mainly as new startups willing to hand agents full autonomy — which is why he argues “AI's takeover of the economy will take longer than people think.”

Takeaway A sharp reframe of the AI-disruption debate: incumbents keep losing to AI-native startups not for lack of capability, but from a rational unwillingness to trust agents with full autonomy.

Sensex, Nifty snap two-day slide as IT stocks rally and DIIs blunt an FII sell-off

India's benchmarks bounced back on Friday, with the Sensex climbing 330.91 points (0.43%) to close at 77,264.51 and the Nifty50 adding 84.80 points (0.35%) to settle at 24,175.65, Business Standard reported. TCS led gainers with a 4.16% surge, followed by Tech Mahindra (3.53%), while ICICI Bank (−1.40%) and Shriram Finance (−1.28%) topped the laggards. Foreign institutional investors sold a net ₹5,039.8 crore in the cash segment even as domestic institutional investors bought a net ₹5,183.93 crore, keeping the index afloat despite the foreign outflow.

Takeaway DIIs are once again doing the heavy lifting Dalal Street needs while foreign money heads for the exits.

MSCI's India rebalance sets up $2.3 billion in passive buying for Laurus Labs, Lenskart, Adani Energy

MSCI's August review, effective after Friday's close and live from 1 September, adds Laurus Labs, Lenskart Solutions, Adani Energy Solutions and Groww-parent Billionbrains Garage Ventures to its India index while dropping Astral, Balkrishna Industries and SBI Cards, Business Standard reported. Nuvama Alternative & Quantitative Research estimates passive inflows of roughly $598 million for Laurus Labs, $352 million for Lenskart and $310 million for Adani Energy Solutions, with the broader review pegged at close to $2.3 billion into Indian equities; the changes also nudge India's weight in the MSCI benchmark up to 11.9% from 11.8%.

Takeaway Four stock tickers just got a guaranteed multi-hundred-million-dollar buyer, whether or not the fundamentals agree.

Asian markets slide Monday as chip stocks fall and Brent tops $91 on a fresh US-Iran flare-up

South Korea's KOSPI fell 2.1%, Japan's Nikkei 225 dropped 1.7% and the TOPIX slipped 0.7% in Monday trade as technology stocks led a region-wide retreat, Investing.com reported, with Brent crude climbing past $91 a barrel after renewed US-Iran tension reignited Strait of Hormuz risk. GIFT Nifty signalled a cautious, muted start for Indian markets, with Nifty futures consolidating near the 24,200 pivot as elevated crude, rising global bond yields and West Asia tensions weighed on sentiment.

Takeaway India heads into Monday sandwiched between a hawkish Fed and a jumpy oil market — not the ideal setup for a breakout.

Purple Style Labs' ₹680 crore IPO opens for bidding after bagging ₹306 crore from anchors

Purple Style Labs, which runs the luxury multi-brand platform Pernia's Pop-Up Shop, opened its ₹680 crore mainboard IPO for public subscription on 31 August after raising ₹306 crore from anchor investors on 28 August, Business Standard reported. The issue is priced in a band of ₹546–575 a share and will close 2 September ahead of a tentative 7 September listing on the NSE and BSE.

Zerodha Varsity principle for today: Varsity's chapter on index funds explains that passive funds don't pick stocks on merit — they mechanically track whatever a benchmark includes, buying and selling on the day of a reconstitution regardless of price. That's exactly what's playing out in today's MSCI story: Laurus Labs, Lenskart and Adani Energy Solutions aren't being bought because of fresh company news, they're being bought because every fund tracking the MSCI India index is contractually required to own them from 1 September. It's a useful reminder that not every stock move is a verdict on the business — sometimes it's just index plumbing.
