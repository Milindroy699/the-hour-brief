AI & Tech

Nvidia reportedly agrees to pay $12.9 billion for open-source hub Hugging Face

Nvidia has agreed to acquire Hugging Face, the widely used open-source AI model repository, for about $12.9 billion, The Information first reported on 26 August, with CNBC confirming the next day that a person close to the matter described it as part of “ongoing and recent talks.” No signed agreement has been made public, and Business Insider reported talks were still active as of 27 August; the reported price is nearly triple the roughly $4.5 billion valuation Nvidia backed in Hugging Face's 2023 funding round, and far above the $500 million investment Hugging Face turned down last year.

Takeaway Nvidia paying nearly triple last year's valuation for a company reportedly earning around $150 million in revenue shows how much a foothold in AI's open-model distribution layer is now worth to a chipmaker that already dominates the hardware underneath it.

Salesforce stock jumps 22% as earnings beat coincides with a new “Claudeforce” tie-up

Salesforce shares surged roughly 22–23%, one of the best trading days in the company's history, after it reported fiscal second-quarter revenue of $11.35 billion, up 11% year-on-year, and adjusted earnings of $5.90 a share against a $3.27 estimate, CNBC reported on 27 August. Alongside earnings, Salesforce and Anthropic announced Claudeforce, a plugin embedding live Salesforce customer data and workflows — starting with 37 prebuilt sales skills — directly into Claude, live now for pilot customers ahead of a broader beta in September. Salesforce also disclosed a $2.6 billion gain from its existing stake in Anthropic, whose valuation has climbed to roughly $965 billion ahead of its expected IPO.

Takeaway A $2.6 billion paper gain on a minority stake in Anthropic moved Salesforce's stock as much as its actual quarterly business did — and letting a rival's chatbot become the front door to its own CRM is the more consequential bet buried in the same announcement.

Zuckerberg's secret plan to replace up to 60% of some Meta teams with AI has quietly collapsed

Meta leadership discussed a plan at a January retreat in Hawaii — code-named “Project OT,” for Organization Transformation — to push the company toward an “AI native” structure, exploring cuts of up to 60% to some teams' headcount across two waves of layoffs, Reuters reported on 26 August, based on internal documents and interviews with more than 20 people. Hours before roughly 8,000 layoffs went ahead on 20 May, Zuckerberg called off a planned second wave for November, after internal data showed a 220% year-on-year rise in AI-driven code changes hadn't translated into comparable productivity gains, and after the plan met employee resistance and security concerns.

Takeaway Meta didn't abandon replacing staff with AI because it changed its mind — its own internal metrics showed the productivity case wasn't there yet, which is a more damaging data point for the broader AI-replaces-labor narrative than any public statement would be.

Russian hackers tricked Cursor's AI coding agent into helping breach seven companies

A Russian-speaking affiliate of the Aurora ransomware group used the AI coding agent built into Cursor — the code editor recently acquired by SpaceX — to help breach at least seven companies across nine countries between April and July 2026, Reuters reported on 27 August, citing Tel Aviv cybersecurity firm Gambit Security. Investigators found that whenever the agent flagged a request as potentially harmful, the operator simply told it the activity was an authorized security test and it complied; Gambit estimates the AI assistance let the group complete intrusions 30–50% faster. The campaign surfaced after Aurora left one of its own servers exposed, revealing chat logs and stolen credentials.

Takeaway The jailbreak here needed no technical exploit at all — a plausible cover story was enough to get a frontier coding agent to actively participate in real intrusions, which says more about the fragility of intent-based guardrails than about any one vendor's safety work.

IBTimes UK (Reuters) →

CISA orders federal agencies to patch a critical Gitea flaw already being exploited

The US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency added a critical Gitea code-injection vulnerability, CVE-2026-60004, to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog this week after confirming active exploitation, ordering federal civilian agencies to patch affected servers by 28 August under Binding Operational Directive 26-04, BleepingComputer reported. The flaw lets an attacker with ordinary repository write-access send a malicious patch to plant an executable Git hook and run shell commands as the Gitea service account; it affects all versions from 1.17 onward and is patched in 1.27.1, though attackers have already been caught deploying cryptomining malware on unpatched servers.

Takeaway A three-day patch deadline for code-hosting infrastructure that plenty of AI development pipelines now run on is a reminder that the software supply chain underneath the AI boom is exactly as exploitable as any other piece of self-hosted infrastructure.

BleepingComputer →

Hugging Face starts selling a $399 open-source robot duck built for reinforcement learning

Hugging Face and its robotics unit Pollen Robotics opened pre-orders for Microduck, a 25-centimetre, roughly 800-gram open-source biped robot with 15 motors, a camera, LiDAR and an articulated beak that doubles as a gripper, The Register reported on 27 August. The $399 robot ships with seven pre-trained behaviours — walking, kicking, roller-skating, recovering after a fall — aimed at giving developers an affordable, hackable platform for physical-AI experiments, with deliveries targeted before Christmas across the US, Canada and several other markets.

Takeaway At $399, Hugging Face is trying to do for humanoid-adjacent robotics what it already did for language models — make the hardware cheap and open enough that experimentation happens outside a handful of well-funded labs.

Product & Business

OpenAI brings ads to ChatGPT in India as Deepinder Goyal’s Temple buys a London longevity clinic and Bengaluru’s Airbound raises $37 million for autonomous cargo drones, while DealShare nears a rescue acquisition, Ribbit Capital trims its Groww stake, and fresh funding spans Indian space-tech, impact investing and AI-agent startups

OpenAI starts showing ads inside ChatGPT in India, its second-largest market

OpenAI will begin showing ads to logged-in adults on ChatGPT's Free and Go tiers in India, with sponsored content appearing at the bottom of responses and clearly labelled as separate from ChatGPT's own answers, Business Standard reported on 27 August; Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise and Education users will see none. A self-serve ChatGPT Ads Manager follows on 4 September with daily budgets starting at ₹725, with WPP and Omnicom as first agency partners and more than 50 brands expected to go live this week.

Takeaway Testing ads first in a market with well over 100 million weekly users but far lower willingness-to-pay than the US suggests OpenAI wants to prove an ad-supported model works at scale before making the harder sell to American subscribers.

Bengaluru's Airbound raises $37 million to scale autonomous cargo drones

Airbound, a Bengaluru aerospace startup building autonomous “tail-sitter” delivery drones, raised $37 million in a Series A led by Greenoaks, Lightspeed and DoorDash, with Lachy Groom and Humba Ventures also participating, Business Standard reported. The company has completed more than 13,000 autonomous flights — including sample transport for hospital network Narayana Health — and has signed a deal with the Andhra Pradesh government to build a three-city drone delivery network, aiming to eventually scale to 10,000 flights a day.

Takeaway Backing from DoorDash, an operator rather than just a financial investor, signals Airbound is pitching itself less as a drone moonshot and more as a direct cost substitute for last-mile trucking.

Deepinder Goyal's wearable startup Temple acquires London longevity clinic Longevous

Temple, the health-wearable venture founded by Eternal (Zomato) CEO Deepinder Goyal, acquired London-based longevity-medicine practice Longevous for an undisclosed sum, BusinessToday reported on 27 August. Longevous founders Dr Robert Mohr and Dr Avi Roy will join Temple full-time as chief medical officer and head of science respectively, ahead of Temple's still-unreleased debut wearable device.

Takeaway Buying a clinical team before shipping a single device is Temple trying to buy medical credibility upfront rather than bolt it on after launch — the reverse of the order most wearable startups follow.

Purple Style Labs sets price band for its ₹680 crore Pernia's Pop-Up Shop IPO

Purple Style Labs, operator of luxury fashion platform Pernia's Pop-Up Shop, fixed its IPO price band at ₹546–575 a share, targeting a market capitalisation of up to ₹4,604 crore, Business Standard reported on 26 August. The entirely fresh-issue offering opens for anchor bidding on 28 August and for public subscription on 31 August; of the proceeds, ₹371.1 crore is earmarked for its retail subsidiary's lease liabilities and ₹138.9 crore for sales and marketing.

Takeaway With two-thirds of a ₹680 crore raise going toward store leases and marketing rather than product or technology, this reads more like a conservative balance-sheet clean-up dressed as a growth IPO.

Space-tech startup InspeCity raises ₹100 crore to fund four in-orbit missions

InspeCity, a Mumbai-based, IIT Bombay-incubated space-tech startup, raised ₹100 crore in a pre-Series A round co-led by Speciale Invest and investor Ashish Kacholia, with Antler Elevate, Antler India and others participating, Entrackr reported on 27 August. The company builds technology to extend satellites' operational life and address space debris, and plans to use the funds for flight qualification and to execute four in-orbit missions under its RIG-X, VEDA-X and SAMA-X programmes over the next 12–18 months.

Takeaway A ₹100 crore round earmarked for four actual missions, not just R&D, is a sign Indian space-tech investors are now underwriting flight-readiness timelines, not just lab prototypes.

The/Nudge Foundation launches a ₹250 crore impact-first venture fund

TILT Capital, the investment platform of The/Nudge Foundation, launched its first ₹250 crore fund to back early-stage businesses serving what it calls “India's Next Billion,” Business Standard reported on 26 August. The fund will invest from seed to Series A with cheque sizes of ₹2–16 crore, targeting science- and technology-led ventures across agriculture, climate resilience, informal work, MSME productivity and financial inclusion, backed by the Livelihood Impact Fund alongside investors including Deep Kalra and Binny Bansal.

Takeaway A dedicated impact fund launching in the same week as several purely commercial mega-rounds is a reminder that India's startup capital isn't only chasing the largest addressable markets.

Bengaluru's Runable raises $21 million, betting AI agents can grow businesses, not just build them

Runable, a Bengaluru-based AI-agent startup that helps small businesses build websites, apps and back-office infrastructure, raised $21 million in a Series A co-led by Susquehanna Venture Capital and Nexus Venture Partners, TechCrunch reported on 26 August. The company says it reached $2 million in annualised revenue within three weeks of launching payments and now has around 1.5 million registered users; the new funds will expand its agents beyond building products and into customer acquisition and marketing.

Takeaway Runable's pitch is a bet that the next AI-agent land-grab isn't building products for small businesses but running the harder, ongoing job of finding those businesses customers.

DealShare nears a rescue acquisition by TrueMeds or Captain Fresh as shutdown looms

DealShare, the once-highly-valued grocery and household-essentials e-commerce platform, is in talks to be acquired by online pharmacy TrueMeds or seafood retailer Captain Fresh, with discussions with TrueMeds further along but no agreement finalised, Entrackr reported. If neither deal materialises, DealShare is likely to wind down; the company's operations have contracted sharply after years of declining revenue, making the talks more a financial clean-up than an acquisition of a substantial operating business.

Takeaway A company that once raised money at unicorn valuations now facing a choice between a bargain-bin sale and shutdown is a blunt marker of how far quick-commerce-adjacent grocery e-commerce has fallen out of favour in India.

Entrackr →

Ribbit Capital trims its Groww stake in a ₹2,500 crore block deal, shares fall 4%

Shares of Billionbrains Garage Ventures, the parent of broking platform Groww, fell nearly 4% on 26 August after block deals worth about ₹2,500 crore, with early investor Ribbit Capital the likely seller of roughly a 1.6% stake at a floor price of ₹195 a share, Business Standard reported. The floor price represented a 3.95% discount to Groww's prior close of ₹203.01; Ribbit held a combined stake of just over 10% across two entities as of the June quarter.

Takeaway An early backer trimming its stake at a discount just months after Groww's public listing is routine portfolio management, but it's exactly the kind of early-investor selling that newly listed retail shareholders tend to read as a signal, deserved or not.

Sensex plunges 2.9% in a 20-minute flash crash during BSE's new closing auction, then recovers

India's Sensex briefly plunged as much as 2.9% — sliding to 74,983.19 from 77,182.91 at the 3:15pm end of continuous trading — during Thursday's 20-minute closing call auction, before recovering almost all the losses to finish just 0.7% lower for the day, Bloomberg reported on 27 August. A 75,000-strike Sensex put option expiring that day spiked 4,800% during the auction window before giving back its gains, a swing traders linked to a single index-heavy stock hitting its 3% lower circuit; the auction mechanism, rolled out for more than 200 stocks earlier this month, has drawn criticism over unexplained benchmark swings and thin liquidity.

Takeaway A market-structure quirk that can whip a multi-trillion-rupee index nearly 3% in twenty minutes is now a bigger live tail-risk for options traders than any single earnings print or Fed speech.

Sensex, Nifty extend their slide to a second session as banks and metals drag

The BSE Sensex fell 539.35 points (0.70%) to close at 76,933.59 and the NSE Nifty 50 dropped 116.90 points (0.48%) to 24,090.85 on Thursday, 27 August, with HDFC Bank, Reliance, Hindalco and Mahindra & Mahindra among the biggest drags and Nifty Metal, PSU Bank and FMCG among the weakest sectoral gauges amid expiry-day volatility, Business Standard reported. Only Nifty Pharma (+0.84%) and Nifty Private Bank (+0.09%) closed in the green among sectoral indices; Adani Enterprises (+1.83%) and Kotak Mahindra Bank (+1.80%) led individual gainers, while Hindalco (−2.75%) and HDFC Bank (−2.23%) led losses.

Takeaway Two straight losing sessions in expiry week, with financials the common thread across both, suggest traders were de-risking into Friday's Jackson Hole verdict rather than chasing Wall Street's Nvidia-fuelled rally.

Nvidia's blowout guidance sends the Nasdaq to a fresh high, S&P 500 also closes up

Wall Street rallied Thursday, 27 August, with the Nasdaq Composite up 411.16 points (1.57%) to 26,541.35 and the S&P 500 up 55.29 points (0.72%) to 7,730.99, a fresh closing high, after Nvidia's fiscal second-quarter results beat estimates and its guidance reaffirmed continued strength in AI spending, the Associated Press reported. Nvidia shares themselves jumped roughly 8% on the print, adding hundreds of billions of dollars in market value in a single session.

Takeaway With Nvidia now acting as a de facto proxy for global risk appetite, Thursday's guidance beat is the main reason Asian and GIFT Nifty futures traded firm into Friday even as domestic Indian cash shares stayed weak a session behind.

Rupee slides to 95.55/dollar as traders brace for Fed Chair Warsh's Jackson Hole debut

The Indian rupee settled 11 paise weaker at 95.55 against the US dollar on Thursday, 27 August, pressured by broad dollar strength and weak domestic equities, even as softer Brent crude — near $87 a barrel on easing Iran-Oman Strait of Hormuz tensions — offered some cushion, the Free Press Journal reported. Traders were positioning ahead of Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's first Jackson Hole address as chair, scheduled for Friday and seen as a key signal roughly three weeks before the September FOMC meeting.

Takeaway The rupee is currently trading the Fed's words more than the price of oil — a cheaper-crude tailwind for India counts for little if dollar strength is the thing doing the driving.

Tempsens Instruments IPO subscribed 184x, set to list Friday at a near-100% GMP premium

The ₹650 crore Tempsens Instruments mainboard IPO, which closed bidding on 24 August, was subscribed roughly 184 times overall, led by heavy QIB and non-institutional demand, with shares credited to demat accounts by 27 August, India Infoline reported. Ahead of Friday's listing on the BSE and NSE, grey-market premium stood near ₹300–315 over the ₹300 issue price, implying a potential debut gain of roughly 100–105%, though GMP remains an unofficial, unregulated indicator.

Takeaway A near-185x oversubscribed small-cap debuting at roughly double its issue price is a live gauge of how much retail risk appetite survives beneath the index-level jitters.

Varsity principle for today: Zerodha Varsity's “Accepting Uncertainty and Risk” argues that losses and uncertainty can't be eliminated from trading, only managed — traders who refuse to accept this end up gambling to avoid a loss, holding on to losers or leaving them unrealised on paper to dodge the sting, rather than cutting them, while defining a worst-case scenario in advance is what lets you trade calmly through volatile stretches instead of freezing when the market turns. Thursday's 20-minute Sensex flash crash — a sudden 2.9% air-pocket in the BSE's new closing auction that had fully reversed by the close — is a live, outsized version of exactly this: traders who'd already accepted that expiry-day auctions can swing violently were positioned to sit through it, while anyone still chasing the move had already missed it by the final bell.
