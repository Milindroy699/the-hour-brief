There's an Italian word, sprezzatura — the art of making something extremely difficult look effortless, a studied nonchalance that hides just how much work went into appearing unstudied. It isn't laziness dressed up as ease; it's the discipline of concealing the discipline, so that what took years reads as if it simply happened.

AI & Tech

Apple unveils foldable iPhone Duo in first keynote under CEO John Ternus

Apple held its "Surprise and Shine" event on 9 September at Apple Park — the first keynote under CEO John Ternus, who succeeded Tim Cook on 1 September — unveiling the iPhone Duo, its first foldable iPhone, alongside the iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max, AirPods 5, Apple Watch Series 12 and Watch Ultra 4, per CNN Business and CNBC. Duo folds out to a 7.6-inch display, about 80% larger than the iPhone 18 Pro's screen, supports Apple Pencil for the first time on an iPhone, and starts at $1,999, rising to $3,199 for the top configuration — Apple's most expensive iPhone ever. Ternus used the keynote to frame the iPhone as the best personal device for AI, pointing to Apple's privacy protections as its edge.

Takeaway A record-priced foldable as a new CEO's opening statement is Apple betting that hardware spectacle, not a headline AI feature, is still what proves it can lead.

Meta launches Muse, a paid personal AI agent that shops and books for you

Meta introduced Muse on 8 September — a proactive personal AI agent, distinct from its chatbots, that can shop online, book tickets and manage tasks through a dedicated cloud environment with a visible browser window, per Axios. It rolled out in the US via the web, the Meta AI app and WhatsApp to users 18 and older, with a free tier alongside paid "Power" ($20/month) and "Maximum" ($100/month) tiers for heavier use.

Takeaway Charging up to $100 a month for an agent that acts on your behalf is Meta's first real attempt to get consumers paying it directly, rather than funding everything through ads.

OpenAI's claimed Millennium Prize math breakthrough sparks a credit dispute

OpenAI said on 8 September that an internal swarm of roughly 10,000 coordinating AI agents produced a proposed solution to the Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem — one of the Clay Mathematics Institute's seven $1 million Millennium Prize Problems — over about 88 hours, generating some 130 billion output tokens, per Axios. NYU mathematician Tristan Buckmaster alleges OpenAI pushed to exclude Anthropic researcher Levent Alpöge from credit after learning of the pair's related unpublished work; OpenAI denies accessing their private research, and the Clay Institute has not verified the proof.

Takeaway An unverified proof tangled up in a credit fight with a rival lab's researcher previews how contentious "AI does novel science" claims get once reputation and competitive advantage are riding on them.

Axios →

US security agencies accuse six Chinese AI firms of mass-distilling American models

The NSA, CISA and FBI issued a joint advisory on 8 September naming DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, Alibaba, MiniMax, StepFun and Z.AI as running "aggressive, malicious, and targeted" campaigns since late 2024 to distill Claude, GPT, Gemini and Grok through fraudulent accounts and proxy "transfer stations," per Unite.AI. Rather than recommending outright bans, the advisory suggests US AI firms quietly degrade the outputs served to suspect accounts.

Takeaway Advising firms to quietly degrade suspect accounts rather than ban them shows Washington now treats model-IP theft as a slow leak worth managing covertly, not a breach worth advertising.

Unite.AI →

DeepMind opens a free atlas predicting 9 billion possible human gene mutations

Google DeepMind released the AlphaGenome Atlas on 8 September — a roughly 1-petabyte precomputed dataset predicting the molecular effects of about 9 billion possible single-letter DNA changes, combining its AlphaGenome and AlphaMissense models into one score, per Fortune. Access is free for academic use through a web portal and GitHub API; DeepMind is explicit that the atlas is not validated for clinical use.

Takeaway Giving away a petabyte-scale prediction atlas for free, the same way AlphaFold's structure database was, is DeepMind building scientific dependency on its models rather than charging for them directly.

Fortune →

Accenture and Google Cloud form a 1,000-engineer unit to push Gemini into enterprises

Accenture and Google Cloud launched the Accenture Gemini Enterprise Business Group on 8 September, a global unit meant to scale Gemini Enterprise deployments for corporate clients, per AIwire/HPCwire and Accenture's newsroom. The group will field a 1,000-person "forward-deployed engineer" workforce and industry-specific accelerators, drawing on Accenture's roughly 50,000 Google Cloud-trained staff; Accenture was separately named Google Cloud's Global Services Partner of the Year for a fourth consecutive year.

Takeaway Committing 1,000 engineers just to deployment work signals that selling enterprise AI is turning out to be a services and integration business as much as a model business.

Accenture Newsroom →

XPeng's humanoid robot starts rolling off an automated production line

XPeng's IRON humanoid robot autonomously walked off its new Guangzhou production line on 8 September, with the facility reporting more than 80% of its core processes automated, per Electrek. The company reaffirmed a year-end mass-production target, with commercial deliveries to Chinese and overseas retail and service customers planned to begin in 2027 — a timeline it contrasted with Tesla's stalled Optimus program.

Takeaway Treating a humanoid robot as something you mass-produce on an automotive line, not hand-build in a lab, is XPeng's bet that manufacturing discipline decides the humanoid race before any single robot's capabilities do.

NSE clears SEBI's nod for what could be India's largest-ever IPO

The National Stock Exchange received SEBI's observation letter on 4 September for a public issue estimated near ₹30,000 crore, entirely an offer-for-sale of about 14.89 crore shares at an indicative valuation near ₹5 lakh crore, per Free Press Journal. Subscription is targeted to open around 15 September with listing expected around 24-25 September — on the BSE, since NSE cannot list on its own exchange — which would surpass Hyundai Motor India's ₹27,870 crore 2024 issue as India's biggest-ever IPO.

Takeaway India's largest exchange finally going public, a decade after first seeking approval, says as much about regulatory patience paying off as it does about market appetite right now.

Ten mainboard IPOs open on the same day as India races a SEBI deadline

Ten mainboard IPOs — including Rentomojo and Manipal Payment & Identity Solutions — opened for subscription together on 9 September, seeking to raise nearly ₹6,950 crore, per Business Today. Nearly 25 companies in total are racing to raise ₹20,000-25,000 crore in September before SEBI approvals originally due to lapse between April and September expire on 30 September.

Takeaway A regulatory expiry date, more than investor demand alone, is what's compressing a quarter's worth of IPOs into a single month.

Bengaluru's QNu Labs raises ₹200 crore for quantum-safe cybersecurity

QNu Labs, an IIT Madras-incubated deep-tech firm, raised ₹200 crore in a Series A1 round co-led by the government's National Quantum Mission and Speciale Invest, with Sony Innovation Fund, Gaja Capital and Artha Ventures also participating, per Entrackr — taking its total funding to ₹375 crore. The company will use the funds to scale R&D and go-to-market in India and abroad, including work tied to India's Quantum Secure and Sensing Networks initiative.

Takeaway A sovereign quantum-mission fund co-leading a private cybersecurity round blurs the line between government industrial policy and venture capital in India's deep-tech push.

Entrackr →

Flipkart quietly tests food delivery to challenge Zomato and Swiggy

Flipkart has begun internal employee testing of a food-delivery service called "Eat In" in Bengaluru, with plans to open it to all Flipkart employees around 15 September ahead of a wider consumer launch, per Entrackr. The service currently runs inside the main Flipkart app, though a standalone app is reportedly under consideration as it scales, alongside a parallel pilot of a standalone "Minutes" quick-commerce app.

Takeaway Testing food delivery on its own staff first suggests Flipkart expects the unit-economics fight with Zomato and Swiggy to be brutal enough to want internal proof before spending on customer acquisition.

Entrackr →

Pluxee India gets an RBI licence to operate as a payment aggregator

Pluxee India has received Reserve Bank of India authorisation to operate as a Payment Aggregator for both online and physical point-of-sale transactions, adding to its existing Prepaid Payment Instrument licence, per ANI. The licence lets the employee-benefits and meal-card company directly collect, settle and refund merchant payments, as RBI's merchant-acquiring rules have tightened licensing requirements across the sector.

Takeaway RBI tightening merchant-acquiring rules is quietly forcing every fintech adjacent to payments, even benefits and meal-card players, to become fully licensed payment infrastructure companies.

ANI →

Snap's chief business officer Ajit Mohan to step down after four years

Ajit Mohan is leaving his role as Snap's Chief Business Officer effective 31 December, after four years, to spend more time with family in Singapore, per Business Standard. EMEA president Ronan Harris moves into a newly created Chief Commercial Officer role overseeing global ad sales, reporting to CEO Evan Spiegel; Snap says the departure isn't tied to any strategy disagreement.

Takeaway A high-profile Indian-origin executive's exit from a top US tech ad role, framed purely as a lifestyle choice, is worth watching for whether it's followed by a landing spot back in India's tech industry.

Business Standard →

Tata Motors presses ahead with its €3.82 billion tender for Italy's Iveco

Tata Motors' tender offer for Italian truck-and-bus maker Iveco Group runs from 7 September to 26 October, valuing the company at €3.82 billion, or €14.10 a share, per Business Today. The offer is backed by Iveco's largest shareholder, the Agnelli family's Exor (a 27.1% stake), and follows Iveco's earlier €1.7 billion sale of its defence unit to Italy's Leonardo — a deal meant to give Tata a genuinely global commercial-vehicle footprint.

Takeaway An Indian auto major buying a legacy European truck brand with Agnelli-family backing shows Indian industrials are now building global scale through acquisition, not just export volume.

Sensex, Nifty extend slide to a third session as financials and IT drag

The Sensex fell 813.35 points (−1.08%) to 74,764.23 and the Nifty 50 dropped 203.60 points (−0.86%) to 23,431.50 on 9 September — its lowest close in roughly 13 weeks — per Business Standard. Nifty IT led the fall, down 3.24% as Infosys, HCL Technologies and Tech Mahindra topped the losers' list, while rupee weakness and renewed foreign selling added to the pressure even as metal and pharma stocks offered some cushion.

Takeaway A third straight losing session with IT leading the slide is the market pricing in more than one bad day — it's starting to look like a compounding, multi-cause drawdown.

Business Standard →

Brent crude tops $100 a barrel as US strikes on Iranian tankers widen the conflict

Brent crude touched roughly $100.71-100.76 a barrel on 9 September, its highest since July, after the US destroyed five Iranian oil tankers in retaliation for an attempted attack on a US warship, and Iran-backed Houthi forces separately struck Saudi Arabia's Jazan refinery, per CNBC.

Takeaway Oil clearing $100 on a direct US-Iran naval exchange, not just sanctions rhetoric, is a materially different kind of risk than the market has been pricing for most of this year.

CNBC →

A new US visa-fee rule adds to Nifty IT's pain

A Department of Homeland Security rule imposing a $4,000 fee on H-1B extension petitions and $4,500 on L-1 extensions — removing a previous exemption for same-employer extensions — took effect on 9 September, per the Federal Register. The rule landed the same day Nifty IT fell 3.24%, compounding a sector already weighed down by a months-long freeze in US corporate technology spending.

Takeaway A visa-fee rule change hitting the same day as a broader tech-spending freeze shows Indian IT's troubles are now coming from both the demand side and the cost side at once.

Federal Register →

Gold eases off recent highs, Bitcoin drifts near $78,600

Spot gold traded around $4,399-4,400 an ounce on 9 September, a modest dip from Tuesday's roughly $4,411 close, after briefly falling further on news of the US strikes on Iranian tankers before partly recovering, per Yahoo Finance. Bitcoin held near $78,600, little changed on the day, as the same US-Iran escalation kept a lid on broader risk appetite.

Takeaway Gold dipping instead of spiking on fresh Middle East risk is a reminder that a safe-haven trade doesn't move on autopilot — it still competes with dollar strength and profit-taking.
