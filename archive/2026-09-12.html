Anthropic reveals that Chinese labs including Alibaba, Moonshot AI and DeepSeek secretly routed hundreds of millions of exchanges through Claude to distill its models, as Oracle's AI cloud backlog swells to a record $664 billion and Amazon agrees to buy up to $60 billion of Qualcomm's AI chips, even as the Pentagon opens talks to lend infrastructure startup Fluidstack $5 billion to shore up a strained supply chain, Mistral raises Europe's largest-ever tech funding round at a €21 billion valuation, and Meta scoops up an eight-month-old Swedish AI startup to power its merchant-messaging agent

Anthropic says Alibaba, Moonshot AI and DeepSeek secretly routed requests through Claude to distill its models

The same September threat-intelligence report in which Anthropic detailed a Russian espionage campaign it disrupted — covered here yesterday — also disclosed what it called its largest-ever distillation attack: operators linked to Alibaba routed more than 151 million exchanges through Claude between May and July 2026, per CNBC on 11 September. Moonshot AI silently forwarded some requests meant for its own Kimi model to Claude and returned the answers as its own, while DeepSeek generated over 12 million distillation-linked exchanges in a single 14-day window in July; Anthropic said it tracked roughly 200 million such exchanges across five campaigns and disrupted activity from seven China-based labs over the eight-month period.

Takeaway Publishing this alongside its espionage and bioweapons findings turns Anthropic's safety report into a competitive broadside as much as a security disclosure, documenting in public how rivals are cloning Claude.

Oracle's AI cloud backlog hits a record $664 billion

Oracle's remaining performance obligations reached $664 billion in fiscal Q1 2027, up $209 billion year-over-year and $26 billion from June's $638 billion, per CNBC on 10 September. Cloud infrastructure revenue jumped 121% to about $7.4 billion, net income rose 60% to roughly $4.68 billion and total revenue climbed about 30% to $19.35 billion; Oracle guided full-year 2027 revenue to at least $90 billion, with most of the quarter's $30 billion-plus in new AI contracts structured as prepayments or "bring-your-own-hardware" deals that require no incremental Oracle capital.

Takeaway Financing the AI boom off customers' balance sheets rather than its own is Oracle's structural hedge against the capex fears dogging the rest of the sector.

Amazon agrees to buy up to $60 billion of Qualcomm's AI inference chips

Qualcomm and Amazon announced a roughly decade-long deal letting AWS purchase up to $60 billion of Qualcomm's custom AI-inference chips, per CNBC on 8 September. Qualcomm granted Amazon warrants worth about $4 billion to buy shares at a fixed $161.26 apiece as purchases vest, and Amazon has already secured 15% of them; the partnership targets cost-efficient inference ASICs for AWS and follows Microsoft and Meta as earlier Qualcomm data-center customers.

Takeaway Tying the deal to warrants instead of a cash-only purchase gives Amazon a direct stake in whether Qualcomm's chip-diversification bet actually pays off.

CNBC →

Pentagon in talks to lend AI cloud startup Fluidstack $5 billion

The Defense Department's Office of Strategic Capital is discussing a roughly $5 billion loan to AI-infrastructure startup Fluidstack to reinforce the US data-center supply chain, first reported by the Wall Street Journal and carried via Reuters on 11 September. Fluidstack, founded at Oxford in 2017 and now managing more than 100,000 GPUs, would use the funds for components and manufacturing capacity rather than a specific new facility; if finalized, it would be the largest loan that Pentagon office has ever issued.

Takeaway The AI race's bottleneck has quietly moved from chips to the industrial supply chain beneath them — serious enough that the Pentagon is now underwriting it directly.

Mistral raises €3 billion at a €21 billion valuation, Europe's largest-ever private tech round

Paris-based Mistral AI raised €3 billion (about $3.5 billion) led by Samsung Electronics, valuing it at more than €21 billion — nearly double its €11.7 billion valuation a year earlier — per TechCrunch and Bloomberg on 8 September. EQT's Scaleup Europe Fund and existing investor PSG Equity co-led, with Advent, BlackRock-managed funds and the Luxembourg state also participating; Mistral called it the largest equity round ever completed by a European technology company.

Takeaway A Korean chaebol leading Europe's biggest-ever tech round shows "sovereign AI" capital is now genuinely global, not just American or Gulf-state money.

Google launches a standalone Gemini app for Windows

Google released a dedicated Gemini desktop app for Windows 10 and 11 (x64 and ARM64) on 10 September, following its earlier macOS launch, per 9to5Google. The free app is summoned via an Alt+Space shortcut and integrates with Gmail and Drive, with daily limits on the base model and a $19.99 Pro tier for expanded access.

Takeaway A keyboard-shortcut-summoned desktop overlay is Google's most direct shot yet at the always-on, OS-level assistance pattern Microsoft's Copilot currently owns.

Meta acquires eight-month-old Swedish AI startup Stilla to power its merchant-messaging agent

Meta acquired Stockholm-based Stilla.ai, closing 9 September, to accelerate Meta Business Agent, which automates merchant responses across WhatsApp, Messenger and Instagram, per Axios. Stilla, founded in 2024, had raised just $5 million in pre-seed funding when it emerged from stealth in January — meaning Meta bought it roughly eight months after launch; Meta Business Agent, launched in June, is now used by more than one million businesses.

Takeaway Folding an eight-month-old startup straight into Meta's commerce stack shows how compressed AI acquisition timelines have become — speed-to-capability now outweighs an independent track record.

Product & Business

Bengaluru space-tech firm Pixxel closes a $100 million Series C to build sovereign satellite infrastructure as restaurant chain Popo Global raises ₹532 crore from Artal Asia to expand nationally, while Flipkart Minutes marks its second anniversary with 4x year-on-year growth and Rentomojo's IPO closes more than 72 times oversubscribed ahead of a 17 September listing, even as a Not Boring essay pushes back on Silicon Valley's fashionable claim that startups no longer need a moat

Pixxel raises $100 million Series C, its largest round yet, to build sovereign satellite infrastructure

Bengaluru-based hyperspectral satellite startup Pixxel raised a $100 million Series C led by Temasek and Seraphim Space, with new investors 360 ONE Asset and IMM Invest also joining, per SpaceNews reporting around 6-7 September. The round brings Pixxel's total funding to $195 million and will fund new sensing technology, sovereign space systems for government customers, and a new satellite-manufacturing facility in the US.

Takeaway A Bengaluru deep-tech company raising from Temasek at this scale for sovereign space infrastructure shows Indian space-tech has crossed from novelty into strategic-asset status for global sovereign funds.

Popo Global raises ₹532 crore from Artal Asia, its first-ever outside funding

Bengaluru-based Popo Global — which runs The Pizza Bakery, Paris Panini and Smash Guys across 40 outlets, all currently in Bengaluru — raised ₹532 crore (about $56 million) from Artal Asia, the CAVA-backing affiliate of Artal Group, per YourStory and Entrackr on 11 September. The profitable operator logged about ₹175 crore in FY25 revenue, with the deal valuing the company at roughly ₹1,500 crore.

Takeaway A single-city, franchise-free restaurant chain pulling a ₹1,500-crore valuation from a global consumer-focused PE firm shows that kind of money is now chasing profitable regional food chains, not just growth-at-all-costs startups.

Rentomojo's IPO closes more than 72 times oversubscribed

Rentomojo's ₹1,255.57 crore IPO (price band ₹384-404) closed on 11 September subscribed roughly 72.8 times overall — qualified institutional buyers at about 177 times their portion, non-institutional investors near 68 times, and retail investors around 15 times — per 5paisa's subscription tracker, after the furniture-rental platform raised ₹376.07 crore from 41 anchor investors on 8 September. Listing on BSE and NSE is set for 17 September.

Takeaway Demand exploding from Day 1's tepid 1.97x subscription to a 72.8x close, with institutions far more enthusiastic than retail, suggests big money is more convinced by the rental-subscription model than everyday investors are.

Flipkart Minutes reports 4x year-on-year growth on its second anniversary

Flipkart's quick-commerce arm says it has grown 4x year-on-year, expanding to nearly 1,200 micro-fulfilment centres across more than 150 cities two years after launch, per Business Standard on 10-11 September. The company says tier-2-plus city customers are up roughly 25x year-on-year and Gen Z customers about 5x, with gourmet and specialty grocery orders up 8x and pet food up 5x.

Takeaway Leaning into tier-2 geography and category breadth like gourmet groceries and pet food is Flipkart fighting the quick-commerce war on different ground rather than trying to out-metro Blinkit and Zepto head-on.

Business Standard →

Provilac raises $14 million in its first-ever institutional round

Pune-based high-protein dairy brand Provilac raised $14 million from Singapore-based Panthera Growth Partners — its first institutional capital after more than a decade as a bootstrapped business — per Business Standard on 10-11 September. The funds will go toward expanding manufacturing capacity and entering Bengaluru.

Takeaway A decade-old, cash-flow-positive brand only now taking its first outside check is a sign growth investors are chasing already-proven unit economics rather than pre-profit growth stories.

Business Standard →

Indian startups raise $392 million across 27 deals as weekly VC inflow roughly doubles

Per YourStory's weekly funding roundup for 5-11 September, Indian startups raised $392 million across 27 deals — roughly double the prior week's $196 million — with spacetech, restaurants and women's wellness drawing the most interest and Series A rounds alone accounting for $104 million. Auto-parts marketplace Piston raised $15 million from FPV Ventures, Spark Capital and Pear VC in one of the week's notable Series A rounds.

Takeaway A week's funding total roughly doubling on the back of unglamorous categories like restaurants and auto parts, not just AI, points to renewed investor appetite for ordinary Indian consumer businesses.

Not Boring pushes back on the claim that startups no longer need a moat

In an essay posted around 11 September, Packy McCormick's Not Boring argues that "counter-positioning" — one of Hamilton Helmer's 7 Powers, where an incumbent's own incentives stop it from copying a challenger — still matters, pushing back on the popular founder claim that shipping faster than incumbents is itself a durable moat in the AI era.

Takeaway Without a structural reason incumbents can't follow, "we just move faster" stops being a moat the moment a big player decides to try.

Middle East oil shock rattles Dalal Street before a late recovery

The Sensex fell 120.83 points (-0.16%) to 74,781.76 and the Nifty 50 dropped 79.70 points (-0.34%) to 23,398.10 on 11 September, after Brent crude surged toward $108-110 intraday on escalating Middle East tensions and India's VIX jumped 4.66% to 12.42, per Business Standard. The Sensex had tumbled as much as 742 points to an intraday low of 74,160.16 before recovering 622 points into the close, with Nifty Metal and Nifty Realty each down roughly 2.3% at the close while IT, FMCG and select banking stocks cushioned the fall.

Takeaway The 622-point recovery from the day's low mattered more than the headline red close, signalling dip-buying is still intact even amid an oil-driven risk-off scare.

HDFC Bank's Bahrain court sweep offsets the metals rout

HDFC Bank shares rose 2.08% on 11 September after Bahrain's High Civil Court rejected two more investor claims linked to Credit Suisse's written-down AT-1 bonds — its seventh straight favourable ruling in cases stemming from the 2023 UBS takeover of Credit Suisse, per Business Today. The court found investors failed to produce sufficient evidence or prove any loss attributable to the bank.

Takeaway A single stock-specific legal catalyst was enough to make India's largest private bank the day's standout gainer even as the broader index closed in the red.

Business Today →

Wall Street snaps a four-day losing streak on an in-line CPI print

The S&P 500 rose 0.9% to 7,656.98, the Nasdaq Composite gained 1.0% to 26,333.04 and the Dow added 1.0% to 52,573.29 on 11 September, ending a four-session losing run after August CPI came in at 3.4% annually (0.4% month-on-month) — in line with estimates — and Brent crude eased from its weekly highs, per TheStreet. Core CPI's 0.3% monthly rise still kept the odds of a near-term Fed rate move elevated near 70%, per CBS News.

Takeaway Markets rallied not because inflation was good news, but because it wasn't worse than feared — a relief rally, not a good-data rally.

TheStreet →

Brent crude falls Friday but still posts an 8.7% weekly surge as Iran-Gulf diplomacy emerges

Brent crude futures settled down 2.8% at $104.61 a barrel on 11 September, even after a week that saw prices punch through $108-110 intraday on Middle East escalation, leaving the week's gain at 8.7%. The Friday pullback followed Iranian state media reports that Tehran would meet Gulf Cooperation Council states in Oman to discuss easing the Strait of Hormuz standoff, per Fortune.

Takeaway Oil's swing showed that diplomatic signalling — even a single meeting announcement — can now move crude several percentage points faster than any actual change in supply.

Fortune →

Bitcoin slides below $77,000, gold near a one-month low ahead of CPI

Bitcoin traded at $77,665.57 as of 9:15am ET on 11 September, down about 2.05% over 24 hours after opening 2.2% below Thursday's level, per Fortune. Gold spot opened near a one-month low ahead of the CPI release before edging up slightly to around $4,385 an ounce, still well below its January 2026 record high of $5,597.23, per Yahoo Finance.

Takeaway Both crypto and gold sold off ahead of the CPI print, a sign traders were positioning for a hawkish surprise rather than treating gold as a reliable inflation hedge that day.
