Before the news

There’s a Japanese art called kintsugi — repairing broken pottery with gold-dusted lacquer, so the crack becomes part of the piece’s value instead of something to hide. It fits today’s edition: Nvidia’s stock cracked 14% on a reported SEC probe into its own disclosures and Wall Street’s AI trade broke into a real correction, yet the money didn’t simply vanish — it moved into Indian banks and IT stocks, which closed at nine-month highs on the very day the global AI trade came apart.

AI & Tech

Washington bars advanced AI chips from foreign China-serving data centers and Nvidia halts its own licensed Hopper shipments to China hours later, as the SEC opens a civil probe into Nvidia's disclosures following Jensen Huang's ouster as CEO, even as OpenAI ships GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol and discloses its Q3 revenue crossed $10 billion for the first time, and AI-compute reseller Corvex signs a $103 million take-or-pay deal for Nvidia Blackwell capacity

Takeaway Today’s AI story is Nvidia’s leadership crisis turning into real regulatory exposure — a fresh export clampdown, a halted China chip pipeline and a federal probe landing in the same week — even as the rest of the industry’s growth numbers keep climbing regardless.

US bars advanced AI chips from foreign data centers that serve Chinese customers

On November 17, the US issued new rules barring advanced American AI chips from being used in foreign data centers that serve Chinese customers, tightening export controls just as Nvidia's own Hopper-chip sales to China come under fresh scrutiny.

Takeaway Closing off the foreign-data-center route is Washington making sure a licensed chip can't reach Chinese customers by a side door, even if the direct one stays shut too.

Bloomberg Government →

Nvidia halts shipments of licensed Hopper (H200) chips to China despite earlier US approval

Interim CEO Colette Kress said on Nvidia's November 18 earnings call that "we are no longer shipping licensed Hopper data center products to China," leaving roughly 600,000 H200 chips reportedly stuck in Hong Kong awaiting shipment after Beijing withheld approval for large-scale imports; Nvidia's latest revenue guidance excludes China data-center sales entirely.

Takeaway Halting chips that Washington had already approved, rather than ones still under review, suggests Nvidia itself isn't confident the politics around this trade are stable right now.

Fox Business →

SEC opens a civil probe into Nvidia's disclosures following Jensen Huang's ouster as CEO

The Wall Street Journal reported, and multiple outlets confirmed, that the SEC has opened a civil inquiry into whether Nvidia's disclosures misled investors, following Huang's resignation as CEO earlier in November; Nvidia and the SEC have not confirmed the inquiry, and an SEC spokesperson said the regulator does not comment on whether a possible investigation exists.

Takeaway A probe into what Nvidia told investors, arriving in the same week its own chips get cut off from China, turns a leadership scandal into a question about the company's numbers themselves.

PYMNTS →

OpenAI ships GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol, a frontier model and a cheaper everyday one

OpenAI released GPT-6.2 Astra, its frontier model for the hardest tasks, and GPT-6.2 Sol, a cheaper everyday model, on November 17, available to Plus, Pro, Enterprise and Edu subscribers through ChatGPT Work and Codex and to developers via the API; OpenAI priced Astra's API access 20% below GPT-6 Astra's, at $8 input and $40 output per million tokens.

Takeaway Pricing the new flagship model below its predecessor, rather than above it, is OpenAI competing on cost as much as capability this round.

OpenAI →

OpenAI's quarterly revenue crosses $10 billion for the first time

OpenAI CFO Sarah Friar told investors on November 10 that third-quarter revenue rose nearly 50% from the second quarter to about $10 billion, an annual run rate of roughly $50 billion, with enterprise revenue overtaking consumer ChatGPT revenue for the first time.

Takeaway Enterprise revenue overtaking consumer ChatGPT revenue is a sign OpenAI's growth is increasingly coming from businesses paying for work done, not just people paying for chat.

TNW →

Corvex signs a $103 million deal for Nvidia Blackwell AI compute capacity

Data-center operator Corvex signed a three-year, $103 million take-or-pay agreement on November 16 to supply Nvidia Blackwell computing capacity — more than 1,000 NVIDIA HGX B200 units — at its Mid-Atlantic 1 site, with service expected in Q1 2027; the company says roughly 80% of the deployment cost is covered by a customer prepayment and non-recourse debt, without issuing new equity.

Takeaway Financing most of a new data hall with a customer's prepayment and debt, not fresh equity, is how a smaller AI-infrastructure player tries to grow without diluting itself.

Product & Business

HCLTech partners with Cohere on sovereign AI as Fairfax-linked FIH Mauritius reportedly exits a roughly 13% IIFL Finance stake via a ₹3,454 crore block deal to help fund its IDBI Bank buy, even as POWERGRID sells its entire CTUIL stake to GRID-INDIA for ₹73.46 crore, Bengaluru space-tech startup SatSure raises a fresh funding tranche, Ross Stores and Gap both beat Wall Street's Q3 estimates heading into the holidays, and Stratechery's Ben Thompson argues Nvidia's governance crisis is becoming a real Washington story

Takeaway Today’s business story is money moving for very different reasons at once — an Indian NBFC’s largest shareholder cashing out to fund a bigger bet elsewhere, US retailers quietly having a good start to the holidays, and a reminder that Nvidia’s boardroom mess is now a regulatory one too.

HCLTech partners with Cohere to build and deliver "sovereign AI" for enterprises and governments

HCLTech and Cohere announced a partnership on November 17 under which HCLTech becomes one of Cohere's preferred global systems-integrator partners, initially focused on Canada and Germany, pairing Cohere's North platform with HCLTech's services in physical and industry AI; both companies hold ISO 42001 certification for AI management systems.

Takeaway Leading with Canada and Germany, not the US, is Cohere and HCLTech chasing governments that want AI infrastructure they don't have to trust to an American company.

HCLTech →

Fairfax-linked investor reportedly sells a roughly 13% stake in IIFL Finance for about ₹3,454 crore

FIH Mauritius Investments, linked to Fairfax Financial, sold 5.61 crore IIFL Finance shares — about 13% of the company — in a block deal on November 18 at an average price of roughly ₹617, worth about ₹3,454 crore; reports tie the exit to Fairfax's funding needs for its separate, roughly ₹52,878 crore proposed acquisition of IDBI Bank. IIFL Finance shares rose about 6% on the news.

Takeaway Selling a profitable stake in one Indian financial company to help fund buying another is Fairfax treating IIFL Finance as a source of capital, not just a long-term holding.

Kotak Neo →

POWERGRID sells its entire CTUIL stake to GRID-INDIA for ₹73.46 crore

Power Grid Corporation of India signed a share purchase agreement on November 19 to sell its entire stake in Central Transmission Utility of India Limited (CTUIL) to GRID-INDIA for an initial ₹73.46 crore, subject to a final valuation-based adjustment at closing; the board had approved the sale on November 1, and completion is expected within three months.

Takeaway Handing the grid-planning entity it helped build over to a separate state-run operator is India tidying up who runs the national grid versus who owns the wires.

Indian Masterminds →

Bengaluru space-tech startup SatSure raises a fresh $21 million Series B tranche

SatSure, a Bengaluru earth-observation and space-data startup, raised about $21 million — including roughly $17.6 million in equity, $2.7 million in venture debt and $1.1 million in secondary transactions — as a tranche of its Series B, announced November 20; the Antariksh Venture Capital Fund led with a $6.4 million investment, and the company expects to close the full ₹200 crore round by December.

Takeaway A government-backed space fund leading the check, not a traditional VC, shows India's own state is becoming a direct investor in the sector it wants to grow.

Via Satellite →

Ross Stores beats Q3 guidance and raises its full-year outlook

Ross Stores reported November 19 that fiscal third-quarter total sales rose 12% year-on-year with comparable store sales up 8%, and earnings per share of $1.91 beat its own guidance of $1.75 to $1.83; operating margin of 12.3% topped its planned 11.7% to 12.0% range, and the company raised its full-year outlook.

Takeaway Beating guidance it set only a quarter ago, rather than just beating Wall Street's estimates, is the stronger signal that an off-price retailer's demand is actually accelerating into the holidays.

SEC filing →

Gap's Q3 earnings per share jump over 30% even as comparable sales stay flat

Gap Inc. reported November 19 that fiscal third-quarter net sales rose 2% with comparable sales flat, while earnings per share rose more than 30% as operating margin exceeded expectations; CEO Richard Dickson said demand softened in October on warm weather but improved as temperatures cooled, and the company named Ashley Kechter as Athleta's next president and CEO.

Takeaway Profit growing much faster than sales is Gap getting more disciplined on costs and markdowns, not selling meaningfully more than it did a year ago.

SEC filing →

Stratechery's Ben Thompson: Nvidia's governance mess is becoming a Washington story

In a November 10 Stratechery podcast, Ben Thompson argued that Commerce Department suspicions over Megaspeed's alleged role in smuggling Nvidia chips into China — tangled up with Jensen Huang's own ouster — mean Nvidia's leadership crisis is no longer just a boardroom problem, but one with real regulatory consequences, and one Anthropic could end up benefiting from.

Takeaway A chip-smuggling suspicion tangled up with a CEO's personal conduct is exactly the kind of mess that turns a corporate scandal into a regulatory one, which is what this week's SEC probe and export rule bear out.

Stock Market

Wall Street's S&P 500 and Dow fall into correction and the Nasdaq erases all of its 2026 gains as Nvidia plunges 14% on a reported SEC probe into its disclosures, dragging Nikkei and Kospi down more than 4% each in Friday trading, even as Sensex and Nifty shrug off their own gap-down open to close at nine-month highs, up 1.08% and 1.12%, as banks and IT lead a rotation into Indian equities

Takeaway Today’s market story is the AI trade finally cracking hard on Wall Street — a seventh straight losing session, a correction, and Nvidia’s worst day in years — while Dalal Street does the opposite, shrugging off the same selloff to close at nine-month highs on bank and IT buying.

Wall Street's S&P 500 and Dow enter correction as the Nasdaq erases all of its 2026 gains

The S&P 500 fell 3.7% to 6,910.39 on Friday — a seventh straight daily loss and a weekly drop of about 8.6%, its worst week since April 2025 — while the Dow fell 949.73 points (1.93%) to 48,147.33, putting both indexes in correction territory, and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 5.28% to 22,617.73, erasing its gains for the year.

Takeaway Wiping out an entire year of Nasdaq gains in one selloff is the market deciding, all at once, that months of AI-driven optimism got ahead of itself.

UPI →

Nvidia drops 14% as a reported SEC probe into its disclosures deepens the AI-stock selloff

Nvidia shares fell 14% on Friday, its worst day in years, as the reported SEC inquiry into its disclosures compounded doubts about AI spending; AMD and Broadcom fell more than 13% and 14% respectively the same session.

Takeaway Nvidia dragging AMD and Broadcom down by a similar margin shows investors are repricing the whole AI-chip trade on this news, not just Nvidia's own stock.

24/7 Wall St →

Nikkei and Kospi each slide more than 4% as Wall Street's selloff spills into Asian trading

Japan's Nikkei and South Korea's Kospi each slid more than 4% in Friday trading, tracking Thursday's Wall Street rout, dragging GIFT Nifty to signal a gap-down open for Indian markets before the session even began.

Takeaway A gap-down open telegraphed by Asian markets before Indian trading even starts is exactly why Friday's recovery on Dalal Street is the more interesting story than the open itself.

Trade Brains →

Sensex and Nifty shrug off a gap-down open to close at nine-month highs on bank and IT buying

The Sensex climbed 897.96 points (1.08%) to 83,919.71 and the Nifty 50 rose 286.30 points (1.12%) to 25,800.60 on Friday, recovering from an open more than 460 points lower, as Nifty Bank rallied 2.49% and Nifty IT gained 1.30%; the Nifty Midcap 100 ended 0.66% higher while the Smallcap 100 slipped 0.13%.

Takeaway Recovering more than 460 points from the open to close at a nine-month high, on the same day Wall Street fell into correction, is Indian equities decoupling from the global AI selloff for at least one session.
