Before the news

There’s a Japanese idea called ikigai — a sense of purpose that comes from the overlap of what you’re good at, what the world needs, and what keeps you going even when no one is keeping score. It shows up today in Reliance and ExxonMobil’s quietly consistent results, built on steady operations rather than one lucky swing; in Intel’s own engineers delivering a real earnings beat that the market still punished over next quarter’s guidance; and in Anthropic’s IPO filing, which reads like a company staking its entire reason for being on a decade of compute spending still to come.

AI & Tech

Intel's stock posts its worst day since 1974 on weak guidance and drags Dell, AMD and Nvidia down in sympathy, as Reddit's Q3 beat gets swept into the same Friday selloff over slipping US user growth, Anthropic's IPO filing discloses compute commitments topping $600 billion and a valuation target above $2 trillion, OpenAI tightens how ChatGPT handles weapons questions from teenagers, and Apple heads toward a November 2 earnings call under new CEO John Ternus

Takeaway Today’s AI story is the market punishing guidance and user trends over headline beats — Intel and Reddit both reported strong numbers and still got sold off, while Anthropic’s IPO math shows just how much its own growth now has to keep compounding.

Intel's stock plunges nearly 29% in its worst day since 1974 despite a Q3 earnings beat

Intel shares closed down nearly 29% at $65.76 on Friday, October 30 — erasing close to $140 billion in market value in its worst single session since at least 1974 — after the company's fourth-quarter guidance overshadowed a Q3 beat, according to Yahoo Finance's market-close coverage. Adjusted EPS of $0.52 beat the roughly $0.40 analysts expected, with Data Center and AI revenue up 78% to $7.3 billion, but Intel guided fourth-quarter revenue to $15–16 billion against a $17.08 billion consensus and adjusted EPS of $0.25 versus $0.44 expected, citing a PC slowdown CFO David Zinsner said would continue into at least the first half of next year.

Takeaway Beating decisively on the quarter wasn’t enough once Intel's own forecast confirmed the PC slump analysts had hoped was temporary — a reminder that guidance, not the past quarter, is what this market is pricing.

Reddit beats on Q3 revenue and profit, but US user growth keeps slipping

Reddit reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of $895 million, up 53% year-over-year, with net income of $283 million and adjusted EBITDA of $402 million, according to the company's October 29 SEC filing. Weekly active uniques grew 16% to 515.2 million and international daily actives rose 24%, but US daily active uniques fell 2% year-over-year even as the stock was swept into Friday's broader tech-earnings selloff.

Takeaway Beating on nearly every headline number while its home market keeps shrinking is the same tension that has dogged Reddit's stock through past earnings — growth now has to come from everywhere except the US.

Dell, AMD and Nvidia slide in sympathy with Intel's post-earnings plunge

Dell Technologies fell about 13%, AMD sank roughly 10% and Nvidia dropped about 3% on Friday, October 30, as Intel's weak fourth-quarter guidance dragged down chip and PC-adjacent stocks across the board, according to 24/7 Wall St's market coverage. None of the three had reported their own results that day.

Takeaway Stocks with no earnings of their own getting pulled down this hard shows how much of the chip sector's pricing now runs on a single company's guidance rather than each company's own numbers.

24/7 Wall St →

Anthropic's IPO filing shows a $2 trillion-plus valuation target and over $600 billion in compute deals

Anthropic's IPO prospectus points to a valuation target above $2 trillion and compute commitments that now top $600 billion, according to InvestingLive's reporting on the S-1 as of October 30. The filing also discloses that two customers generated nearly a quarter of Anthropic's 2025 revenue, and that roughly 80% of that revenue is usage-based even though most of its compute costs are fixed, take-or-pay commitments.

Takeaway A company whose biggest costs are locked in for years while its biggest revenue source can swing with usage is betting that demand keeps climbing in a straight line — exactly the assumption this IPO now asks investors to price.

OpenAI tightens how ChatGPT handles weapons questions from teenagers

OpenAI said on October 30 it is restricting what ChatGPT will tell users it believes are under 18 about weapons, extending the limits even to questions that might seem harmless on their own, according to AI Weekly's coverage of the announcement. The company said it has strengthened its age-prediction system, will let ChatGPT automatically end chats where a user appears fixated on violence, and made more than 200 referrals to law enforcement over potential violence threats in the third quarter of 2026; it also committed to a twice-yearly Community Safety Report starting in early 2027.

Takeaway Restricting answers for a whole category of question rather than judging each one individually shows OpenAI choosing to over-block for teenagers rather than trying to read intent in the moment.

Apple heads toward a November 2 earnings call under new CEO John Ternus

Apple will report fiscal fourth-quarter 2026 results on Monday, November 2, with a call led by CEO John Ternus and CFO Kevan Parekh, according to MacRumors' preview of the release. Apple had guided to 9–11% year-over-year revenue growth for the quarter, implying roughly $111.7–113.7 billion, with the quarter including only about nine days of iPhone 18 Pro sales and none of the foldable iPhone Duo, which falls in the December quarter.

Takeaway A report built mostly on the old iPhone lineup makes this call more about Ternus's first impression on investors than about the Duo, which won't show up in the numbers until next quarter.

Product & Business

Reliance Industries' profit jumps 41% in its first results since Jio's record listing as ExxonMobil and Colgate-Palmolive both beat Wall Street's Q3 estimates, even as SoftBank keeps trimming its stake in Ola Electric, global tech layoffs near 196,000 for the year, and Indian startup funding keeps cooling week on week

Takeaway Today’s business story is large, established companies delivering real operating strength — Reliance, ExxonMobil and Colgate all beat on their own numbers — even as the edges stay weaker, from SoftBank’s continued exit to a cooling week for Indian startup funding.

Reliance Industries' profit jumps 41% in its first results since Jio's listing

Reliance Industries reported consolidated net profit of ₹25,548 crore for Q2 FY27, up 41% from ₹18,165 crore a year earlier, with revenue up 29%, driven by its oil-to-chemicals and digital-services businesses, according to results reported October 30. The quarter is the company's first since Jio Platforms' stock-market debut lifted Reliance's group value past the Tata Group's.

Takeaway A profit jump this size landing in the same quarter Jio went public gives Reliance two separate reasons to be the story of India's markets this week, not just one.

ExxonMobil beats Q3 estimates on record refining performance

ExxonMobil reported third-quarter 2026 GAAP earnings of $15.4 billion ($3.72 per share) and adjusted earnings of $17.5 billion ($4.23 per share), beating a $4.09 consensus estimate, according to the company's October 30 results. The release cited record year-to-date refining performance and diesel yields, production milestones of 1 billion cumulative barrels each at Guyana and Kearl, and an increased working interest in LNG projects at Papua.

Takeaway Exxon had flagged a one-time GAAP hit two weeks ago specifically so this beat wouldn't get lost in a confusing headline number — and the adjusted figure landing above consensus suggests that bet paid off.

SEC filing →

Colgate-Palmolive's Q3 sales and margins both improve

Colgate-Palmolive reported third-quarter 2026 net sales of $5,369 million, up 4.6% year-over-year, with GAAP diluted EPS of $0.94, up 3%, and base-business EPS of $0.97, up 7%, according to the company's October 30 SEC filing. Organic sales rose 3.6%, gross margin improved 80 basis points to 60.2%, and the company said its global toothpaste share reached 41.4% year to date.

Takeaway Growth split fairly evenly between volume and price, rather than price increases doing all the work, is the detail that makes this look like a genuinely healthy quarter rather than one propped up by charging more.

SEC filing →

SoftBank keeps trimming its Ola Electric stake as the EV maker's rights issue closes

SoftBank's investment vehicle, SVF II Ostrich, sold about 1.21 crore Ola Electric rights entitlements on October 22 and 23, at ₹3.64 and ₹2.28 respectively, as part of a broader series of open-market sales that has cut its stake to roughly 11.7% from 12.9% earlier this year, according to Inc42's reporting. The sales come as Ola Electric's ₹1,000 crore rights issue, offering shares at ₹27 each, was scheduled to close October 30.

Takeaway Selling rights entitlements rather than subscribing to them, right as the company it backs asks shareholders for more capital, is as clear a signal as SoftBank can send without saying outright that it isn't putting in fresh money.

Global tech layoffs near 196,000 for 2026 as Amazon, Meta and Microsoft keep cutting

Worldwide technology-sector layoffs are approaching 196,000 workers for 2026, with Amazon, Meta and Microsoft among the companies still cutting jobs late in the year, according to TechRepublic's running tally. Other trackers cited in the same roundup put US-headquartered firms at roughly 134,000 of a 165,000 global total, with Oracle alone accounting for over 25,000 cuts across several rounds this year.

Takeaway Layoffs this large still running in the same year these companies are posting record AI capital spending shows the cuts and the buildout aren't opposing forces — they're the same reallocation of money and headcount.

Indian startup funding falls 22% for the week as AI companies take half the money

Indian startups raised more than $184 million (about ₹1,747 crore) across 17 deals in the week of October 19–23, down 22% from the prior week, with AI companies taking roughly half the total at ₹892 crore, according to Inc42's weekly tally. Even Healthcare's $50 million Series C, led by Khosla Ventures, was the week's largest round.

Takeaway AI taking half of a shrinking weekly total, rather than half of a growing one, shows investor appetite concentrating into AI even as overall risk capital for Indian startups keeps thinning out.

Fintech startup Cent raises $15 million as India's daily deal flow stays busy

Fintech startup Cent raised $15 million in fresh funding, according to StartupTalky's October 27 daily roundup of Indian startup funding and key news, which also noted SoftBank's continued stake sale in Ola Electric the same day.

Takeaway A $15 million round barely registering against the week's bigger stories is itself the story — mid-sized fintech raises are still happening, just without the attention the mega-rounds get.

Sensex and Nifty rise for a second straight session as IT and healthcare lead

The Sensex closed up 308.49 points (0.42%) at 74,459.53 and the Nifty 50 gained 116.35 points (0.51%) to settle at 23,053.10 on Friday, October 30, according to Kotak Neo's market-wrap report. IT and healthcare stocks led the gains while metals and consumer names lagged, and midcap and smallcap indices fell even as the benchmarks rose, with the session swinging between an intraday high of 74,742.59 and a low of 74,082.03.

Takeaway Benchmarks rising while midcaps and smallcaps fall on the same day shows the gain was concentrated in a handful of large names rather than a broad market move.

Kotak Neo →

Wall Street's Nasdaq sinks nearly 3% as Intel and Reddit become the week's latest tech-earnings casualties

The Nasdaq Composite closed down 2.86% at 25,437.00 and the S&P 500 fell 1.82% to 7,580.07 on Friday, October 30, as Intel's weak guidance and Reddit's user-growth concerns dragged down tech stocks broadly, according to TheStreet's market-close coverage. The monthly decline left the Nasdaq down 5.3% for October, its worst month since March 2025, with the index and the S&P 500 both logging a third straight weekly loss.

Takeaway Closing out the month on its worst day of the week, driven by guidance rather than the headline earnings numbers, shows investors are now trading on what companies say is coming rather than what they just delivered.

TheStreet →

Indian markets sit out the weekend ahead of Monday's reopen and Apple's own earnings

Indian markets are closed through the weekend after Friday's second straight gain, with trading set to resume Monday, November 2 — the same day Apple reports fiscal fourth-quarter results under new CEO John Ternus. Friday's close, Sensex up 0.42% and Nifty up 0.51%, remains the most recent Indian session heading into the reopen.

Takeaway A quiet weekend close doesn't mean a quiet Monday — Apple's first earnings call under its new CEO lands the same day Indian markets reopen, giving global sentiment a fresh data point before the week even starts.
