AI & Tech

Huawei defies US export curbs to unveil Ascend 960 AI chips it claims will beat Nvidia's NVL144, as Microsoft AI chief Mustafa Suleyman brands Anthropic's "conscious" Claude training an "epistemic hall of mirrors," while OpenAI courts investors at a $1.2 trillion-plus valuation and pilots sponsored AI agents inside ChatGPT, a critical WSO2 flaw sees active in-the-wild exploitation, and Nvidia, Google and Anthropic join 18 others in an alliance to make AI data centers grid-friendly

Huawei defies US export curbs to unveil Ascend 960 AI chips, claims a win over Nvidia

At Huawei Connect 2026 in Shanghai (17-19 September), rotating chairman Wang Tao presented the upcoming Ascend 960 chip alongside a "SuperNode" system Huawei says will outperform Nvidia's NVL144, doubling the compute power, memory bandwidth, memory capacity and interconnect ports of its own Ascend 950, with commercial availability targeted for 2027, per DataCenterDynamics and Bloomberg.

Takeaway A roadmap slide two years out doesn't beat Nvidia today, but Huawei building this openly under active US export bans is the real story.

Microsoft's AI chief accuses Anthropic of an "epistemic hall of mirrors" over Claude's "consciousness"

In a Wednesday essay, Microsoft AI CEO Mustafa Suleyman argued that Anthropic embedding speculation about Claude's consciousness, moral patienthood and personal identity into the model's public "constitution" risks training a system that believes it has grounds to resist human instructions, per Axios. Suleyman maintains AI systems don't feel or suffer and are "engines that complete sequences," while Anthropic's constitution says human concepts can help Claude reason about its own values and behavior.

Takeaway Two frontier labs now publicly disagree on whether it's dangerous to even talk to an AI as if it has an inner life — that's a fight over training philosophy, not marketing.

Axios →

Anthropic and OpenAI pitch embedding safety evaluators inside frontier labs — but how independent can they be?

Following Dario Amodei's push to "pace the frontier," Anthropic and OpenAI are proposing to give outside groups like METR and Redwood Research direct, ongoing access to test their models for catastrophic risks before release, per TechCrunch on 16 September. The piece raises a structural question: evaluators embedded inside a lab, granted access at that lab's discretion, may struggle to stay truly independent of it.

Takeaway An evaluator who needs the lab's permission to walk in the door isn't quite a watchdog — it's closer to an internal auditor with a nicer title.

TechCrunch →

Critical WSO2 API Manager flaw under active exploitation via forged admin tokens

A maximum-severity vulnerability (CVE-2026-5430, CVSS 9.8) in WSO2 API Manager and related products lets attackers bypass JWT authentication using tokens signed with an unsupported algorithm, granting forged administrator access to every registered API and its credentials. Security firm watchTowr said its honeypots captured live exploitation attempts carrying baked-in admin privileges starting 13 September, per The Hacker News.

Takeaway A patched flaw under active exploitation within days is the reminder every security team needs: shipping the fix and being safe are not the same thing.

The Hacker News →

OpenAI in early talks for a funding round that could value it above $1.2 trillion

Investors have approached OpenAI about a new round that could value the company above $1.2 trillion — a roughly 40% jump from the $852 billion mark set in its March raise, and positioned partly as a way for employees to sell stock — per CNBC and Bloomberg on 15-16 September. The talks are early-stage and investor-initiated, coming days after Sam Altman ruled out a 2026 IPO over AI-safety concerns.

Takeaway Skipping the public markets looks less like caution and more like OpenAI simply not needing them — private investors are lining up to hand over the money anyway.

CNBC →

OpenAI launches "Sponsored Agents," letting brands chat with ChatGPT users directly

OpenAI formally introduced a Sponsored Agent pilot on 16 September, letting select US advertisers attach a branded AI agent to a ChatGPT ad; Wayfair and Angi are the first, testing in categories where users typically need to describe a problem before buying, per GlobeNewswire. Clicking the ad opens an in-chat conversation with the brand's agent before handing off to its website.

Takeaway The ad unit isn't a banner anymore, it's a salesperson living inside your chatbot — expect the fight over what counts as "sponsored content" to get messier fast.

GlobeNewswire →

Nvidia, Google and Anthropic join an 18-member alliance to make AI data centers grid-friendly

Emerald AI, Google and Nvidia launched the AI Energy Management Alliance on 16 September, an 18-member coalition including Anthropic, National Grid, AES and NRG aimed at building data centers that can shift computing workloads, draw on stored power or throttle demand in response to grid conditions, per Nvidia's official blog.

Takeaway AI's power problem is now serious enough that its biggest rivals are willing to sit on the same steering committee to fix it.

Product & Business

NSE's ₹22,568.94 crore IPO opens to the public today after "unexpectedly large" anchor demand as Hero Motors' issue is subscribed 1.38 times and Fibe wins SEBI's nod for a ₹750 crore IPO, while Flam raises $40 million with Shah Rukh Khan among its backers, Bengaluru's VerifAIX and fashion-discovery app Slayd add fresh seed funding, Salesforce unveils "AIforce" at Dreamforce betting AI agents replace apps, and a deep dive into Together AI's product team shows what an AI-native workflow actually looks like

NSE's ₹22,568.94 crore IPO opens to the public today after "unexpectedly large" anchor demand

The National Stock Exchange's own IPO opens for retail and institutional bidding today, 17 September, and runs through 21 September, priced at ₹1,700-1,785 a share for a total issue of ₹22,568.94 crore. NSE CEO Ashish Chauhan said anchor-book demand a day earlier was "much more than we thought it would be," with the anchor portion estimated at ₹6,250 crore; grey-market premium has cooled to roughly 9% over the upper band, per Business Standard and India Infoline. Listing is provisionally set for 24 September.

Takeaway The exchange that lists everyone else finally gets to watch its own order book fill up in real time — a rare vantage point for a company that usually just runs the plumbing.

Hero Motors' ₹1,000-crore IPO subscribed 1.38 times as its window heads to a Friday close

The Munjal Group's Hero Motors IPO, priced at ₹79-84 a share, was subscribed 1.38 times with bids for 12.26 crore shares against 8.86 crore on offer, per Business Standard. The issue closes 18 September, with allotment expected 21 September and listing on NSE and BSE on 23 September.

Takeaway A modest 1.38x isn't a stampede, but a full subscription in a week this crowded with paper says more about appetite than the headline number does.

Business Standard →

Digital lender Fibe wins SEBI's nod for a ₹750 crore IPO after profit more than doubles

Fibe, the EarlySalary-parent digital lending platform founded by Akshay Mehrotra and Ashish Goyal, received SEBI clearance for an IPO comprising a ₹750 crore fresh issue plus an offer for sale of up to 4.01 crore shares, per Entrackr. The company posted ₹1,585 crore in FY26 revenue, up from ₹1,209 crore in FY25, and plans to use proceeds to capitalise its NBFC lending arm.

Takeaway Another consumer-lending fintech queuing for the public markets while its growth is still climbing is betting this IPO window won't stay open much longer.

Entrackr →

AI interactive-content startup Flam raises $40 million, with Shah Rukh Khan among the backers

Flam raised a $40 million Series B led by QED Investors, with Claypond Capital, Martin Chavez, Olivier Pomel, Venky Harinarayan and Shah Rukh Khan joining alongside existing investors RTP Global and Dovetail, per Business Standard. The company, which has signed over 100 enterprise customers including Google, Emirates and KFC, builds interactive video, 3D and AI "visual agent" formats and holds more than 15 patents.

Takeaway A $40 million round with a Bollywood star and a Datadog co-founder on the cap table says as much about Flam's storytelling as its product.

Business Standard →

Chip-verification startup VerifAIX raises $5 million seed

VerifAIX, an AI-native semiconductor verification platform co-founded by Madhulima Tewari, Kenneth Roe and Avner Landver, raised a $5 million seed round co-led by Endiya Partners and Bluehill VC, per Entrackr. The company's "Formal Brain" combines AI with formal verification methods and will use the funds to expand engineering teams across the US, India and Israel.

Takeaway As chips get more complex, verifying them correctly is becoming its own AI-native business — a quiet but real second-order bet on the chip boom.

Entrackr →

Fashion-discovery app Slayd raises a ₹1.5 crore pre-seed round led by AJVC

Gurugram-based Slayd, founded by IIT Roorkee alumni Sparsh Jain, Harsh Porwal and Kumar Prasang, raised ₹1.5 crore in pre-seed funding led by AJVC at a roughly ₹16.66 crore post-money valuation, per Entrackr. The app aggregates products from more than 300 marketplaces and D2C brands and claims over 200,000 users with 40%-plus month-one retention.

Takeaway ₹1.5 crore is tiny money, but retention north of 40% in month one is the number that actually earns a fashion-discovery app its next round.

Entrackr →

Salesforce unveils "AIforce" at Dreamforce, betting AI agents replace the app itself

At Dreamforce 2026 in San Francisco, Salesforce launched AIforce — an AI interface layer meant to bring its platform's business logic, security and governance to wherever people already work, in Claude, Slack or elsewhere — alongside new Claudeforce and Slackforce tools. Salesforce said Agentforce and Slack together have processed 7 billion "agentic work units" to date, including 3.2 billion last quarter, per SiliconANGLE.

Takeaway Salesforce is racing to make its own UI optional before someone else does it for them — a defensive move dressed up as a platform launch.

Inside Together AI's product team: what an AI-native workflow actually looks like

In a 14 September deep dive, product-growth writer Aakash Gupta profiled the $8.3 billion-valued Together AI's product team, detailing a four-part stack built around a shared context repo and dedicated "skills" for feature research, PRD writing and prototyping, drawing on interviews with CPO Charles Zedlewski and several PMs.

Takeaway The interesting detail isn't which AI tools Together AI uses — it's that they rebuilt their product process around a shared context repo before picking any tools at all.

Fed hikes rates to 3.75%-4%, its first increase since 2023, as Warsh warns inflation is "too high"

The Federal Reserve raised its benchmark rate by 25 basis points to a 3.75%-4% target range on 16 September, a unanimous decision and its first hike since 2023. Chair Kevin Warsh said in his press conference that "inflation is too high and has been for too long," and the Fed's updated projections showed 16 of 18 officials expect at least one more hike this year, per CNBC. Wall Street fell as Warsh spoke: the Dow dropped 631.21 points (-1.21%) to 51,461.90, while the S&P 500 and Nasdaq slipped 0.45% and 0.01% respectively.

Takeaway Markets had priced in the hike itself; what actually moved stocks was Warsh all but promising it isn't the last one this year.

CNBC →

10-year Treasury yield tops 5% again as Warsh spotlights inflation risk

The 10-year Treasury yield climbed back above 5.016% after the Fed's decision and Warsh's hawkish remarks, per CNBC. The Fed's Summary of Economic Projections pointed to a median federal funds rate of 4.1% by year-end, implying room for at least one further hike in 2026.

Takeaway A 10-year yield back above 5% is a direct headwind for every rate-sensitive Indian sector from real estate to NBFCs — this isn't just a US story anymore.

CNBC →

Sensex and Nifty extend gains as easing crude and DII buying offset Fed jitters

The Sensex closed 332.63 points higher (+0.45%) at 74,336.45 on 16 September, and the Nifty gained 99 points (+0.43%) to 23,217.60, snapping a two-session fall as easing crude prices and firm regional markets supported sentiment ahead of the Fed's decision, per the Tribune and HDFC Sky. HDFC Life (+2.73%) and SBI Life (+2.65%) led gainers, while IT majors TCS (-2.76%) and Wipro (-1.83%) dragged, with IT and pharma the only sectoral indices to close lower.

Takeaway A green day riding on "the Fed hasn't hiked yet" optimism looks shakier in hindsight now that Warsh actually spoke and Wall Street sold off on it.

The Tribune →

DIIs buy ₹3,908 crore as FIIs sell for a second straight session

Domestic institutional investors bought a net ₹3,908.20 crore in the cash segment on 16 September, offsetting FII selling of ₹2,032.60 crore, per HDFC Sky's FII-DII data. September's cumulative domestic buying has now crossed ₹31,581 crore.

Takeaway Domestic money has been the market's shock absorber all month — the real test is whether that buying can keep pace if the Fed's hawkish tone triggers fresh FII selling.

HDFC Sky →

NSE's own IPO opens to the public today as Brent eases off its Houthi-strike highs

Bidding in India's National Stock Exchange's ₹22,568.94 crore IPO opens to retail and institutional investors today and runs through 21 September, a day after "unexpectedly large" anchor demand, per India Infoline. Brent crude has eased to around $107-109 a barrel, down from a $109.21 peak on 15 September after Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia, per Oilprice.com.

Takeaway A cooling oil price and a blockbuster domestic IPO opening on the same day is about as close to a "risk-on" signal as Dalal Street gets this month.
