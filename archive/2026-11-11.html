AI & Tech

Anthropic locks in a $70–$78 IPO price range valuing it at up to $1.4 trillion ahead of a November 19 Nasdaq debut, even as Jensen Huang faces a November 11 deadline to even propose an alternate date for a congressional briefing over his alleged Chinese-intelligence contact, Microsoft winds down its 2023 Oracle AI-cloud-rental deal, OpenAI hires the security researchers who once breached it to red-team its own systems, and Qualcomm’s soft guidance overshadows an 89% jump in its automotive revenue

Takeaway Today’s AI story is speculation turning into settlement — Anthropic finally has a real IPO number and Qualcomm’s guidance gets real scrutiny, even as OpenAI hires its one-time attacker and Jensen Huang’s deadline keeps one more story hanging.

Anthropic sets its IPO price range at $70–$78 a share, valuing it at up to $1.4 trillion

Anthropic’s amended filing on November 10 sets a proposed price range of $70 to $78 a share for roughly 825 million Class A shares on the Nasdaq under ticker “ANT,” implying a fully diluted valuation of up to about $1.4 trillion; the offering is expected to price on November 18 and begin trading November 19, according to CNBC and TechCrunch. The range is not final, and the top end would raise the company roughly $64 billion.

Takeaway Landing on a concrete, bankable number after months of $2 trillion speculation is itself the story — the market finally has something to actually price.

Jensen Huang faces a November 11 deadline to even propose an alternate date for a congressional briefing

Three House Republican committee chairs asked Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang for a voluntary, closed-door briefing on November 18 over his contacts with Alice Huang, a Megaspeed executive reportedly alleged to be a Chinese-intelligence contact, giving him until November 11 to accept that date or propose another. The request is voluntary rather than a subpoena, and Nvidia has not said whether Huang will attend.

Takeaway A deadline to merely propose a date, not to answer for anything specific yet, is still enough to keep a story alive for another news cycle.

Implicator.ai →

Microsoft winds down its 2023 Oracle deal to rent AI cloud capacity

Microsoft has been turning off rented AI computing capacity from Oracle in recent months after declining to renew a 2023 multi-year agreement that originally powered the AI-assisted version of Bing, Bloomberg reported on November 6, with the hardware described as an older generation of Nvidia chips that no longer matches Microsoft’s needs. Oracle said demand for its AI infrastructure, including earlier-generation GPUs, remains strong.

Takeaway Even a multi-year AI-capacity deal can look outdated within a few years when the chips underneath it don’t keep pace with demand.

Bloomberg, via The Star →

OpenAI partners with Hacktron, the security researchers who once breached its systems, to red-team its own products

OpenAI announced on November 6 that it is partnering with security startup Hacktron to jointly find vulnerabilities in its systems and third-party integrations, expanding Hacktron’s access to its frontier models through a program called Daybreak Red. OpenAI said it first noticed Hacktron through its bug-bounty program, after researchers there used Claude models in an exploit chain that compromised several OpenAI employees’ ChatGPT accounts and reached internal code repositories — a flaw OpenAI has since fixed.

Takeaway Hiring the team that just proved it could break in is a more candid response to a security scare than most companies manage.

OpenAI →

OpenAI says ChatGPT’s search volume is now roughly a fifth of Google’s

OpenAI executive Adam Fry said around November 3 that ChatGPT’s search-like query volume has reached about one-fifth of Google’s, using OpenAI’s own definition of a search as any query that triggers a web lookup — a broader measure than simple how-to or informational queries. The figure is self-reported and hasn’t been independently verified against third-party search-share trackers, which use different methodologies and generally show Google with a far larger lead.

Takeaway A one-fifth figure sounds impressive only until you remember OpenAI picked both the yardstick and the moment to publish it.

Gracker →

Qualcomm beats Q4 estimates but soft guidance sends shares lower, even as automotive revenue jumps 89%

Qualcomm reported fiscal Q4 non-GAAP revenue of about $10.4 billion and EPS of $2.17 on November 4, both slightly ahead of consensus, but handset-chip revenue fell 26% to $5.14 billion and its fiscal Q1 2027 guidance of $10.4–$11.2 billion in revenue came in below what investors wanted, sending shares lower in extended trading. Automotive revenue rose 89% to $2 billion and IoT revenue rose 4% to $1.9 billion.

Takeaway A chipmaker can beat the quarter just reported and still get punished, if the guidance for the next one isn’t generous enough.

Product & Business

PhysicsWallah’s headcount shrinks as it credits AI efficiencies for part of the cut, even as MakeMyTrip’s India unit reportedly clears SEBI’s hurdle for an IPO and buys into ride-pooling startup Quick Ride, EloElo Group raises $80 million to take its microdrama app global, JSW Steel’s profit more than triples while Rentomojo’s falls 62% despite a 53% revenue jump, and India Inc’s Q2 earnings season nears its finish with median profit growth near 24%

Takeaway Today’s business story is efficiency and expansion pulling apart — PhysicsWallah and Rentomojo both let growth and AI squeeze profit differently, while GIVA, EloElo and JSW Steel raise or report strongly, proof this season rewards selective bets over blanket growth.

PhysicsWallah’s headcount falls in Q2 as it credits AI efficiencies, even as its offline business racks up a ₹108 crore H1 loss

PhysicsWallah’s total employee count (around 19,100) and faculty count (around 7,100) both fell sequentially in Q2 FY27, with management citing AI-driven efficiencies across content creation, quality checks and evaluation, and saying AI now writes roughly 90% of the company’s code, according to Entrackr. Employee expenses fell 2.3% year-on-year excluding ESOP costs, even as its offline segment posted a pre-Ind AS EBITDA loss of ₹108 crore for H1 FY27 on weaker NEET enrolments.

Takeaway Crediting AI for a shrinking headcount is becoming as routine a line in an Indian startup’s earnings call as revenue growth itself.

Entrackr →

MakeMyTrip’s India unit reportedly clears SEBI’s hurdle for its IPO, as the company also buys into ride-pooling startup Quick Ride

SEBI has cleared the IPO of MakeMyTrip (India) Ltd, which filed confidentially in July 2026 for what press reports have put at a $1 billion-plus listing, according to StartupTalky’s November 9 roundup; MakeMyTrip has not itself confirmed the clearance or the issue size. Separately, MakeMyTrip acquired a majority stake in Bengaluru-based ride-pooling company Quick Ride for an undisclosed amount, entering the shared-mobility segment.

Takeaway A 15-year-old Nasdaq listing quietly building toward a second, Indian one is its own kind of long game.

StartupTalky →

EloElo Group raises $80 million to take its Story TV microdrama app beyond India

EloElo Group, the Bengaluru company behind microdrama app Story TV, raised $80 million in a Series C round co-led by Hornbill Capital and NBCUniversal, with MIT Investment Co. and existing backers Play Ventures, Kalaari Capital, Waterbridge Ventures and Griffin Gaming Partners also joining, the company said on November 4. The round brings its total capital raised to about $130 million, and NBCUniversal says the money will fund expansion into the US and other markets, plus original content and AI tools; the group’s apps reach more than 40 million monthly users.

Takeaway A Hollywood studio co-leading a round for an Indian microdrama app is a sign of where NBCUniversal thinks short-form video is actually headed.

YourStory →

GIVA details plans for 160 new stores in FY27 after its ₹975 crore raise

Jewellery brand GIVA, which runs about 425 stores today, plans to open roughly 160 more in FY27 and scale past 1,000 outlets within three to five years, funded by the ₹975 crore round led by Multiples Alternate Asset Management that closed last week, according to Inc42 and Lapaas Voice. The company says its new valuation is higher than its roughly ₹4,500–4,600 crore previous round, though the exact figure wasn’t disclosed.

Takeaway Going from 425 stores to over 1,000 is the kind of plan that only makes sense if the new money is treated as a down payment on real estate, not a cushion.

Inc42 →

JSW Steel’s Q2 profit more than triples to ₹5,279 crore on strong volumes

JSW Steel’s net profit rose 225% year-on-year to about ₹5,279 crore for the quarter ended September 2026, as revenue grew 20.7% to ₹54,486 crore, with the company crediting stronger volumes even as raw-material costs rose. The results were announced on October 23.

Takeaway A 225% profit jump against rising input costs says the volume growth did almost all of the work this quarter.

Digg, via Economic Times →

Furniture-rental startup Rentomojo’s Q2 profit falls 62% even as revenue jumps 53%

Rentomojo’s net profit fell 62.2% year-on-year to ₹18.2 crore for the quarter ended September 2026, even as revenue rose 52.8% to ₹142.1 crore and EBITDA grew 50.3% to ₹22.3 crore, though EBITDA margin slipped 30 basis points to 15.7%, according to BusinessToday.

Takeaway Revenue up more than half and profit down nearly two-thirds in the same quarter is what reinvesting hard for growth actually looks like on paper.

BusinessToday →

India Inc’s Q2 FY27 earnings season nears its finish, with median profit growth near 24%

With 1,331 of about 1,806 expected companies having reported, median year-on-year profit growth for Q2 FY27 stands at roughly 23.6%, up from an early-season reading near 21% with fewer companies in, according to earnings tracker Tapetide; the remaining results are due by the November 14 filing deadline.

Takeaway A median that keeps climbing as more companies report suggests the early numbers understated the season, not overstated it.

Tapetide →

Elena Verna on Lenny’s Podcast: why “minimum lovable product” is replacing the MVP

Lovable head of growth Elena Verna argues that only about 30–40% of her 15–20 years of growth playbook still applies in the AI era, because AI-native products need bigger bets and new growth loops rather than incremental activation tweaks; she credits building in public, combined founder-and-employee social posting, and giving the product away widely for helping Lovable reach $200 million in annual recurring revenue within a year with about 100 employees. Her reframe: it shouldn’t be “minimum viable product” anymore, but “minimum lovable product.”

Takeaway If even a veteran growth operator says two-thirds of her old playbook doesn’t transfer, that’s a bigger signal than any single AI product launch.

GIFT Nifty signals a roughly 300-point gap-up open as crude cools and FIIs revive buying

GIFT Nifty futures traded about 304 points (1.21%) above the Nifty 50’s last close ahead of Wednesday’s reopen — the first regular session since Monday, after Tuesday’s Diwali-Balipratipada holiday — with BusinessToday citing a moderation in crude oil prices and a revival of FII inflows into domestic equities as the drivers. India’s volatility index had already eased 5.48% to 17.42 as of Monday’s close.

Takeaway A pre-market signal is still just a signal, but a 300-point gap-up after a holiday pause is a confident way to restart the week.

BusinessToday →

Wall Street closes lower for a second day as a hot October inflation print raises the stakes for the Fed

The S&P 500 fell 0.38% to 7,520.93, the Dow fell 0.30% to 52,231.89, and the Nasdaq fell 0.74% to 25,065.87 on Tuesday, reversing part of Monday’s rally, after October consumer prices rose more than economists expected and raised the odds of another Fed rate move in December, according to multiple market reports. Tech stocks led the decline.

Takeaway One hotter-than-expected inflation number undoing a chunk of Monday’s best-session-since-April rally shows how little cushion this market currently has.

StockTitan →

Foreign funds pile into India as the global AI trade falters

Foreign institutional investors pumped roughly $1.6 billion into Indian equities on November 5 alone — reportedly the highest single-day total in nearly six years — on top of about $1.9 billion over the four days through November 4, as global fund managers rotated out of AI-exposed North Asian markets and into sectors with less direct exposure to AI infrastructure, according to Bloomberg reporting cited by Forklog and Investing.com. Indian IT-services stocks outperformed, while AI-enabler stocks lagged the Nifty.

Takeaway India drawing record foreign money specifically because it has the least AI exposure is a strange kind of compliment for a market trying to pitch its own tech story.
