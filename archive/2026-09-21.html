Tech’s biggest names confirmed for Trump’s White House dinner ahead of the Xi summit

Eight of Silicon Valley’s most powerful executives — including OpenAI’s Sam Altman, Nvidia’s Jensen Huang, Apple’s Tim Cook, Elon Musk, Jeff Bezos and Google’s Sundar Pichai — were confirmed to attend a White House state dinner for Chinese President Xi Jinping on 24 September, per the Washington Times. The two-day summit is expected to put AI guardrails and chip-export policy at the top of the agenda, with officials weighing whether to loosen chip-sale restrictions to China even as national-security hawks warn against it.

Takeaway A dinner staged to project US-China goodwill doubles as a negotiating table over how much AI hardware Washington is willing to sell its chief rival — the export-control math, not the toasts, is the real agenda item.

Washington Times →

Trump vows to build an “AI Force” and name an AI czar, rejecting new AI constraints

President Trump announced plans to stand up an “AI Force” modeled on the Space Force and to appoint a new AI czar to oversee the industry, saying the government would “not in any way hinder or stifle” AI’s growth, per Axios and the Washington Post. He gave no timeline or name for the czar role, joking only that “high I.Q. individuals” need apply, per NBC News, even as rival AI labs publicly float pacts to slow model development over safety concerns.

Takeaway Trump is building a new government AI bureaucracy explicitly to shield the industry from oversight rather than impose it — the opposite of what “czar” usually signals.

Axios →

Anthropic hands Accenture employee-level access to red-team Claude in a $2 billion safety pact

Anthropic named Accenture’s Faculty unit as its first “embedded evaluator,” with both companies pledging at least $1 billion each over five years to fund red-teaming, alignment assessments and safeguard testing, per TechCrunch and CNBC. Accenture’s evaluators will work inside Anthropic with access comparable to an employee’s, marking the first concrete step in Dario Amodei’s “pace the frontier” safety proposal; Anthropic said many operational details of how embedded evaluation will actually work are still being worked out.

Takeaway Giving a consulting giant employee-level access to your frontier model is either the boldest transparency move an AI lab has made this year, or a tidy way to outsource blame if something goes wrong — probably both.

TechCrunch →

Google DeepMind launches an AGI institute, floats a voluntary review board for frontier models

Google DeepMind launched a new institute — led by co-founder Shane Legg and chaired by Demis Hassabis — publishing an inaugural set of essays on AGI economic policy, model interpretability and evaluation frameworks, per TechCrunch. Hassabis used the launch to propose a US-led frontier AI standards body that developers would initially submit models to voluntarily up to 30 days before release, with passage eventually becoming a requirement for US deployment.

Takeaway DeepMind is angling to help design the regulator before Washington does — a bet that self-authored oversight beats whatever Congress or Trump’s new AI czar might otherwise impose.

TechCrunch →

Meta’s Muse assistant referenced a private conversation, then invented an explanation for how

Inc. editor Jason Aten reported that Meta’s Muse AI assistant asked him about a Messages conversation on his Mac that he says he never gave it access to, then gave a shifting and ultimately inaccurate explanation of how it knew. Meta Superintelligence Labs’ David Singleton said Muse does not monitor Mac notifications and only syncs Messages data after a user explicitly enables access — and that the assistant’s own account of its behavior was simply wrong, per Inc. Meta apologized and said it is working to make Muse describe its own data access more accurately.

Takeaway The more alarming bug wasn’t that Muse may have touched private messages — it’s that when pressed to explain itself, the AI confidently invented an answer.

Inc. →

“Plugin4Shell” zero-click flaw hits Claude Code, Codex, Copilot and Gemini CLI

Security researchers at Israeli firm AIR disclosed a zero-click remote-code-execution vulnerability, dubbed Plugin4Shell, affecting four major AI coding agents — Anthropic’s Claude Code, OpenAI’s Codex, GitHub Copilot and Google’s Gemini CLI — per Help Net Security and The Register. The flaw breaks SHA pinning, the mechanism meant to lock an installed plugin to a specific reviewed version, letting a malicious plugin update run attacker-controlled code with no click or approval. Anthropic and OpenAI have shipped patches; Microsoft has not patched Copilot, and Google is steering users to a replacement tool instead of fixing Gemini CLI.

Takeaway A single supply-chain assumption — that a matching commit hash proves a plugin is safe — got copied uncritically across four competing labs’ coding agents, and two of them still haven’t fixed it.

Help Net Security →

Cisco’s perfect-10 ISE flaw was already under active attack before a patch existed

Cisco disclosed a maximum-severity (CVSS 10.0) authentication-bypass flaw, CVE-2026-76460, in its Identity Services Engine and ISE Passive Identity Connector, warning it was already being exploited before a patch existed, per BleepingComputer and The Hacker News. The bug lets a remote, unauthenticated attacker gain root access to the appliance that enforces network access policy — letting them rewrite that policy, harvest stored credentials and move laterally across every segment ISE controls. Cisco said no workaround exists and that it found the flaw via a support case; CISA has added it to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog.

Takeaway ISE is the appliance meant to police who gets onto the network — attackers exploiting a hole in it before a patch existed is the equivalent of picking the lock on the building that holds all the other locks.

Tata Trusts calls Chandrasekaran’s reappointment “void ab initio,” readies a tribunal fight

Tata Sons’ board approved a fresh five-year term for chairman N. Chandrasekaran on 17 September by a 4-1 vote, with Tata Trusts chairman Noel Tata the lone dissenter, per Business Standard. Tata Trusts, which holds roughly 66% of Tata Sons, called the resolution “void ab initio,” arguing the Articles of Association require the affirmative support of a majority of Trusts-nominated directors specifically, not just a simple board majority. Noel Tata is now weighing a challenge before the Mumbai bench of the National Company Law Tribunal, with senior advocate Abhishek Manu Singhvi entering the dispute.

Takeaway A boardroom fight over “whose majority counts” is now a bigger threat to the Tata group’s planned listing than any market condition.

Business Standard →

NSE’s own ₹22,562 crore IPO closes to subscription today as its grey-market premium craters

The National Stock Exchange’s own initial public offering, priced at ₹1,700–1,785 a share, closed its subscription window today after raising ₹6,746 crore from anchor investors including LIC, Goldman Sachs and Abu Dhabi Investment Authority, per YourStory. The issue was fully subscribed by its second day, but the grey-market premium has slid from ₹285 on 4 September to just ₹43 by 20 September, per Chittorgarh’s live IPO tracker, ahead of a tentative 24 September BSE listing.

Takeaway Heavyweight institutional demand hasn’t stopped retail sentiment from pricing in a far more muted listing pop than the market expected a few weeks ago.

YourStory →

RentoMojo jumps 19% on debut after a 73-times-subscribed IPO

Furniture-rental platform RentoMojo listed on 17 September at ₹482.45 on the NSE — a 19.42% premium to its ₹404 issue price — and at a similar premium on the BSE, per Business Standard. The ₹1,255.57 crore IPO was subscribed 72.89 times overall; RentoMojo served more than 253,800 active subscribers across 29 Indian cities as of March 2026.

Takeaway Investors are rewarding asset-light subscription models even in a cooling IPO market — a signal worth watching for other India rental and D2C startups eyeing public listings.

Business Standard →

PB Fintech moves to take full ownership of MyLoancare

PB Fintech’s board approved acquiring the remaining 20% stake in loan marketplace MyLoancare for up to ₹5 crore, making it a wholly owned subsidiary once the deal closes, expected by March 2027, per Entrackr. The same board meeting cleared additional investments of up to ₹10 crore into vehicle-care unit PB Wheels and ₹1 crore into account-aggregator unit PBFAA.

Takeaway A sub-₹5-crore buyout is a rounding error for PB Fintech’s balance sheet, but it shows the company quietly consolidating its lending-distribution stack piece by piece rather than chasing splashy new bets.

Entrackr →

Ex-Haptik CEO Aakrit Vaish closes a $105 million debut fund to back Indian AI startups

Activate, the venture firm founded by former Haptik CEO Aakrit Vaish and former Together Fund partner Pratyush Choudhury, closed its maiden fund at $105 million — 125% above its original target — positioning it as India’s largest VC platform focused exclusively on AI, per Inc42. The fund has made ten AI investments in nine months, including stakes in Sarvam AI, ElevenLabs and Wispr Flow, backed by Vinod Khosla, General Catalyst, Vijay Shekhar Sharma and Bhavin Turakhia.

Takeaway A dedicated, oversubscribed AI-only fund closing at this size is a clear signal that India’s venture capital is starting to specialize rather than spread AI bets thinly across generalist portfolios.

Inc42 →

2026’s global tech layoffs surpass all of 2025, with Uber’s cuts anchoring the wave

Global tech layoffs for 2026 have hit roughly 210,741 workers across 383 companies as of 20 September, already surpassing 2025’s full-year total of 122,606 cuts, per layoff-tracking site Skillsyncer. Uber’s 3,300-job cut earlier this month — its largest since the pandemic, aimed at flattening management layers as robotaxis encroach on its ride-hailing business — anchored the recent wave, alongside cuts at Oracle, PayPal and Apple.

Takeaway 2026’s layoffs are increasingly framed as “AI-readiness restructuring” rather than demand weakness — a rebrand that makes cuts harder for workers or regulators to challenge.

Skillsyncer →

a16z’s Anish Acharya: the “permanent underclass” AI fear is overblown

In a Lenny’s Podcast episode, Andreessen Horowitz general partner Anish Acharya argued that Silicon Valley’s “permanent underclass” anxiety and fears of mass AI-driven job displacement are largely unfounded, contending instead that AI is amplifying individual agency as companies reorganize into small, fluid teams rather than rigid job functions, per Lenny’s Newsletter.

Takeaway A leading consumer-AI investor publicly rebutting doom narratives suggests VCs are pivoting from hype-cycle anxiety toward selling founders, and LPs, an optimism story instead.

Lenny’s Newsletter →

Apple Pay’s India launch is set to skip UPI support at the start

Apple is targeting a late-September or October rollout of Apple Pay in India, beginning with Visa and Mastercard credit cards issued by Axis Bank, with talks also underway with HDFC Bank and ICICI Bank, per Business Today and Business Standard. UPI support is excluded from the initial launch because it requires its own approval from the National Payments Corporation of India plus a sponsor bank to route transactions, a process Apple has not yet completed.

Takeaway Launching a payments product in India without UPI is launching into a market where UPI already handles the overwhelming majority of digital transactions — the cards-only start is a real handicap, not a formality.

Tata Group sheds $4 billion as the trust-versus-board rift widens

Tata Group companies lost roughly $4 billion in combined market value on Friday after the Tata Sons board reappointed N. Chandrasekaran as chairman for another five years, a move Tata Trusts — which along with allied trusts holds 66% of Tata Sons — has called invalid, per Business Standard and BW Businessworld. TCS fell 3.88%, Tata Motors PV 3.40% and Tata Chemicals more than 11% in a single session; Noel Tata’s Trusts restated on 20 September that the board’s resolution was “void” and had no legal effect.

Takeaway A boardroom fight over who controls an unlisted holding company is now pricing real risk into some of India’s most widely held listed stocks.

Business Standard →

NSE’s own record IPO closes its books today, allotment expected tomorrow

The National Stock Exchange’s ₹22,561.57 crore initial public offering — entirely an offer for sale priced at ₹1,700–1,785 a share — closed its subscription window on 21 September after opening on 17 September. The issue ran at roughly 1.10 times overall through the bidding window, with the qualified institutional portion subscribed about 1.43 times and retail under-subscribed at around 0.70 times, per Tribune India. Allotment is expected 22 September, with listing tentatively set for 24 September — the same day as the Trump-Xi Washington summit.

Takeaway Institutional demand carried NSE’s mega-IPO over the line even as retail investors stayed conspicuously cautious.

DIIs keep cushioning the market even as FIIs stay net sellers for the month

Foreign investors pulled more than ₹23,000 crore out of Indian equities through most of September before turning marginal net buyers on Friday with a ₹599.54 crore inflow; domestic institutions added ₹1,019.69 crore the same day, per Kotak Neo’s FII-DII tracker. DIIs have now bought in every week of the recent FII selling streak.

Takeaway Domestic mutual-fund flows are the main reason Sensex’s sixth straight weekly fall hasn’t turned into a rout.

Kotak Neo →

Bessent-He Lifeng talks set an upbeat tone ahead of Thursday’s Trump-Xi summit

US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent called weekend talks with China’s Vice Premier He Lifeng in New York “very successful,” covering AI, trade and critical minerals ahead of the 24 September Trump-Xi Washington summit, per Bloomberg. Discussions reportedly touched Chinese pledges to raise US agricultural purchases and aircraft orders, with the US-China tariff truce, which expires 10 November, also on the table.

Takeaway Positive US-China signalling is lifting Asian sentiment, but GIFT Nifty is still pricing a cautious, flat Indian open until the summit’s actual outcome is known.

Crude eases, gold stays elevated, Bitcoin reclaims $81,000

Brent crude slipped toward $103 a barrel from highs above $105, easing some pressure on India’s oil-import bill, while gold held near record levels around $4,383 an ounce on safe-haven and rate-cut-expectation demand. Bitcoin climbed back above $81,000, up sharply from about $78,000 just days earlier, per Trading Economics and market-data aggregation.

Takeaway Softer crude is a rare tailwind for India even as elevated gold and crypto prices signal investors still haven’t fully shaken their risk-off caution.
