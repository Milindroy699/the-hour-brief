AI & Tech

Nvidia beats and raises but its stock whipsaws on margin worries as OpenAI's Jalapeño chip claims an edge over Nvidia's own Blackwell, Anthropic commits $45 billion to Nscale for compute, and Amazon shutters Mechanical Turk after 21 years while SpaceX unveils a $100 billion Starship complex in Louisiana

Nvidia tops Q2 estimates and guides higher, but the stock only rallies after the earnings call

Nvidia reported fiscal Q2 2027 revenue of $96.2 billion, up 106% year-on-year from $46.7 billion, and adjusted earnings of $2.22 a share, up 111% from $1.05 a year ago — both ahead of Wall Street's $92.3 billion and $2.09 estimates — and guided to $105.8–110.1 billion in third-quarter revenue, Yahoo Finance reported on 26 August. The stock initially extended an after-hours decline of as much as 1.3% on concerns about gross margins as memory costs rise, then reversed to trade up as much as 4% at $218.77 once management's call pointed to roughly 70% revenue growth for fiscal 2028.

Takeaway The beat itself barely moved the stock — it took a specific forward number, next year's growth rate, to flip a nervous after-hours sell-off into a rally, which is a sign investors have priced in Nvidia's present and are now trading purely on its future.

OpenAI's Jalapeño chip claims a speed edge over Nvidia's Blackwell at Hot Chips

OpenAI presented benchmarks for Jalapeño, its first custom AI accelerator built with Broadcom and system integrator Celestica, at the Hot Chips conference on 25 August, claiming 1.5–1.9x more AI work at peak throughput and 1.7–3.6x lower end-to-end latency than Nvidia's Blackwell-generation systems across models including GPT-OSS-120B, DeepSeek R1 and Kimi K2.5, CNBC reported. The chip targets inference only, not training, and is aimed at low-volume production in late 2026; OpenAI has not benchmarked it against Nvidia's upcoming Vera Rubin generation.

Takeaway OpenAI is now simultaneously Nvidia's largest customer and a chip designer claiming to beat it on inference — a dual role that only makes sense if OpenAI expects inference costs, not training runs, to decide who wins the next phase of the AI business.

Anthropic commits $45 billion in compute to two-year-old British infrastructure firm Nscale

Anthropic signed a deal to rent about $45 billion in AI compute from Nscale, a British AI-infrastructure company founded in 2024 that already supplies Microsoft, with capacity delivered via Nvidia's next-generation Vera Rubin chips, TechCrunch reported on 26 August. The compute is expected to start powering Anthropic's services in late 2027; separately, Anthropic is said to be pitching investors a total addressable market above $30 trillion ahead of a potential public listing.

Takeaway A two-year-old infrastructure company landing a $45 billion commitment from Anthropic shows compute supply, not model quality, is now the scarcer resource labs are racing to lock down years in advance.

Amazon shutters Mechanical Turk, the platform Bezos once called "artificial artificial intelligence"

Amazon will close Mechanical Turk, its crowdsourced task marketplace, on 30 September, ending a 21-year-old service that at its peak served more than 500,000 workers completing small digital jobs like data labeling and transcription, CNBC reported. The company had already stopped accepting new customers in July; workers have 35 days to migrate workflows and retrieve data, with newer data-labeling startups such as Scale AI, Mercor and Prolific now recruiting workers for AI-training tasks instead.

Takeaway The platform that quietly generated the human-labeled data behind a generation of machine-learning systems is being made obsolete by the AI training pipelines it helped seed.

CNBC →

SpaceX unveils a $100 billion Starship complex on Louisiana wetlands

SpaceX announced plans on 25 August to build "Starbase Louisiana," a $100 billion, 125,000-acre launch complex on Pecan Island intended to eventually support thousands of Starship flights a year, president Gwynne Shotwell said, per US News, citing the Associated Press. Construction begins next year with a first launch targeted for 2029, expanding SpaceX's Starship operations beyond its existing Texas site and one planned in Florida, with the company framing the buildout partly around future AI-satellite launch demand.

Takeaway AI's infrastructure race has expanded to include literal rocket real estate for satellite constellations, not just the data centers and chips that usually dominate this conversation.

US News (AP) →

China's Z.ai unmasks Ox Alpha, the anonymous model that surged past DeepSeek on OpenRouter

Z.ai confirmed on 26 August that "Ox Alpha" — a reasoning and coding model that appeared anonymously on AI marketplace OpenRouter over the preceding weekend in what the platform called its biggest launch ever — is a new entry in its GLM series, now named GLM-5.3-Flash, Bloomberg reported. The free, 1-million-token-context model surged to OpenRouter's top spot, more than doubling DeepSeek's usage, and Z.ai plans to release its weights openly; it runs on Chinese AI chips.

Takeaway Releasing a strong model anonymously and letting real usage numbers build before revealing the maker is itself a new marketing tactic in China's AI race, distinct from the benchmark-chart approach US labs favor.

Product & Business

Meta agrees to pay up to $16.68 billion to settle a multistate child-safety lawsuit as India's Infra.Market plots a ₹10,545 crore reverse merger into Shalimar Paints and Honasa calls off its Fluence Pharma buyout, while Flipkart Minutes overtakes Swiggy Instamart in dark-store count and voice-AI startup Ringg, temple-services platform Utsav and EV motorcycle maker MATTER all raise fresh funding

Meta to pay up to $16.68 billion to settle child-safety claims from 29 states

Meta agreed to pay up to $16.68 billion to resolve a multistate lawsuit alleging it designed Facebook and Instagram to addict children, misled the public about platform safety, and improperly collected minors' data, reached during a California federal trial, Axios and Yahoo Finance reported on 26 August. Meta must add a midnight-to-6am usage block and a default two-hour daily cap for teen accounts across its apps, strengthen age verification, and expand parental controls; the company denied wrongdoing, and its stock rose 4.4% in premarket trading on the news.

Takeaway A stock rallying on news of a $16.68 billion liability only makes sense if investors were pricing in something worse — the settlement's real value to Meta is ending years of open-ended trial risk, not the number attached to it.

Infra.Market plots a ₹10,545 crore reverse merger into listed Shalimar Paints

Shalimar Paints approved a roughly ₹10,545 crore share-swap deal to acquire Hella Infra Market, the parent of construction-materials unicorn Infra.Market, issuing about 41.70 crore equity shares and 81.12 crore compulsorily convertible preference shares at ₹85 apiece, which would hand Hella Infra Market's shareholders more than 77% of the enlarged, listed entity, TradingView (Moody's) reported. A source close to the deal said it gives Infra.Market a stock-exchange presence without an IPO at a time when "new-age IPOs are not being treated well" in the current market.

Takeaway A little-known paints company becomes the backdoor listing vehicle for one of India's largest unicorns, a sign some startups now see a friendly reverse merger as a safer route to the public markets than an actual IPO.

Quadria-backed Nobel Hygiene files DRHP on the back of a near-10x profit jump

Nobel Hygiene, the maker of hygiene brands Friends, B-Fit, Teddyy, Snuggy and RIO, filed draft papers with SEBI for an IPO comprising a fresh issue of up to ₹150 crore plus an offer for sale of 1.55 crore shares by promoters and investors including Quadria Capital, DealStreetAsia reported. The company's FY26 revenue rose 14.6% to ₹846.75 crore while profit jumped to ₹18.91 crore from ₹2.28 crore a year earlier; ICICI Securities, Motilal Oswal Investment Advisors and SBI Capital Markets are managing the issue.

Takeaway A 20-year-old hygiene-products maker turning a near-tenfold profit jump into an IPO pitch is unglamorous compared with most of this year's tech listings, but it's exactly the kind of steady, profitable business India's primary market has room for right now.

Honasa calls off its ₹135 crore Fluence Pharma acquisition

Mamaearth parent Honasa Consumer terminated its proposed acquisition of a 58% stake in nutraceutical maker Fluence Pharma for ₹135 crore, citing "non-fulfilment of closing conditions" under the share purchase agreement, BW Marketing World reported on 25 August. The deal, first announced on 23 June, also included a plan to acquire the remaining 42% stake over five to seven years, which is now cancelled alongside it; Honasa said its nutraceutical ambitions remain intact and it will evaluate alternative opportunities.

Takeaway Walking away over unspecified "closing conditions," two months after announcing the deal, is Honasa's quiet way of saying the numbers stopped adding up without spelling out exactly why.

Flipkart Minutes overtakes Swiggy Instamart in dark-store count across top 10 cities

Flipkart Minutes has edged ahead of Swiggy Instamart in dark-store count and pincode coverage across India's top 10 quick-commerce cities, per a CLSA report cited by Entrackr: Blinkit leads with 969 stores, followed by Zepto's 828, Flipkart Minutes' 627, Instamart's 615 and BigBasket's 497. Blinkit leads in six of the ten cities, Zepto leads in Hyderabad, Mumbai and Chennai, and Flipkart Minutes has the largest footprint in Kolkata.

Takeaway Quick commerce's pecking order is now decided city by city and store by store, not by any single national market-share number, which is exactly why every player keeps racing to open more dark stores rather than consolidate the ones they have.

Entrackr →

Voice-AI startup Ringg raises $10 million Series A extension led by Peak XV

Bengaluru-based Ringg, which builds AI voice, WhatsApp and browser agents for enterprises, raised a $10 million Series A extension led by Peak XV Partners with Arkam Ventures and Capital 2b participating, taking its total raised to $15.5 million, TechCrunch reported on 25 August. The company says it now processes around 20 million call attempts a month for customers including Flipkart, Practo, Groww and PolicyBazaar, and will use the funds to deepen its proprietary models and expand internationally.

Takeaway Twenty million monthly call attempts, not the fresh $10 million, is Ringg's real evidence of product-market fit — the round is just catching up to usage that was already there.

TechCrunch →

Temple-services platform Utsav raises ₹36 crore Series A

Kolkata-based Utsav, which connects users with temple priests and scholars for puja bookings, consultations and e-prasad, raised ₹36 crore in a Series A round led by Atomic Capital with existing investors India Quotient and Equanimity Investments, Entrackr reported on 25 August. The round takes Utsav's total funding to ₹42 crore; the company plans to expand its temple network and technology, and is also exploring remote ritual services for the Indian diaspora abroad.

Takeaway Digitising India's temple economy one puja booking at a time is a slower, odder bet than most venture-backed consumer apps, but it's targeting a genuinely underserved market that has largely run on word of mouth until now.

Entrackr →

Electric motorcycle maker MATTER raises $25 million to scale manufacturing

Ahmedabad-based EV motorcycle maker MATTER Motor Works raised $25 million in growth capital from existing investors including Helena, Capital 2B, a Japan Airlines and TransLink corporate venture fund, and the Shakopee Mdewakanton Sioux Community's investment arm, YourStory reported on 25 August. The round takes MATTER's total funding to roughly ₹1,000 crore; the company plans to expand manufacturing and its supplier network and launch four new motorcycles over the next 12 to 24 months.

Takeaway A cap table that spans a Japanese airline's venture arm and a Native American tribal government's investment fund shows how globally eclectic the money backing Indian EV manufacturing has become.

Nvidia's beat-and-raise quarter whipsaws late, closing well off its post-earnings low

Nvidia beat Wall Street's estimates on both revenue ($96.2 billion) and adjusted earnings ($2.22 a share) for its fiscal second quarter, and guided third-quarter revenue to $105.8–110.1 billion, but the stock initially fell as much as 1.3% in after-hours trading on gross-margin concerns tied to rising memory costs before reversing to trade up as much as 4% once management pointed to roughly 70% revenue growth for fiscal 2028, per Yahoo Finance. The swing happened after Wednesday's Indian session had already closed.

Takeaway Because Nvidia's after-hours reversal landed too late for Wednesday's Nifty IT pack to react, Thursday's opening trade in India inherits a verdict on the quarter that US markets only settled on overnight.

Sensex, Nifty snap their winning streak as IT and FMCG drag

The Sensex fell 183.15 points, or 0.24%, to 77,472.94 and the Nifty 50 dropped 126.80 points, or 0.52%, to 24,207.75 on Wednesday, with the Nifty IT index down more than 1% and FMCG and auto stocks also weighing on the benchmarks, Business Standard reported. Kotak Mahindra Bank led gainers, up 3.76%, while Bharti Airtel led losers, down 2.31%, as investors turned cautious ahead of Nvidia's earnings.

Takeaway Wednesday's dip looks like pre-Nvidia caution rather than an India-specific trigger — the real test is whether Thursday's session reacts to the after-hours reversal that followed.

Business Standard →

Crude extends its slide to a two-week low, rupee set for a firmer open

Brent crude eased further to around $86.80 a barrel and WTI to about $80.87 on Wednesday, a two-week low, as renewed Iran-Oman talks raised hopes the Strait of Hormuz would stay open, while the rupee firmed 0.35% to near 95.40 against the dollar on the softer oil prices, FXStreet reported, adding that the currency was positioned for a firmer opening on Thursday.

Takeaway It's the mere possibility of an Iran-Oman de-escalation moving oil and the rupee here, not an actual resolution — a reminder how much of this market is trading hope rather than fact right now.

Symbiotec Pharmalab's ₹1,757 crore IPO subscribed 5.60x ahead of Thursday's close

The Symbiotec Pharmalab IPO, comprising a ₹150 crore fresh issue and a ₹1,607 crore offer for sale priced at ₹938–988 a share, was subscribed 5.60 times overall by the end of its third day on 26 August, led by non-institutional investors at 12.83x (up sharply from 3.16x a day earlier), employees at 6.21x and retail at 5.34x, while the institutional (QIB) portion excluding anchor allocation stood at 0.62x, HDFC Sky reported. The issue closes on 27 August, with listing on the NSE and BSE expected 1 September.

Takeaway Non-institutional and retail demand carrying the book while institutional interest still lags is the same pattern showing up across nearly every recent Indian IPO — enthusiasm arriving well ahead of institutional conviction.
