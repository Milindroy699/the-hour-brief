Before the news

There’s a Japanese idea called wabi-sabi — finding worth in what’s incomplete, imperfect, or still becoming, rather than waiting for a flawless version. It shows up today in Boeing’s record $745 billion backlog sitting right alongside a quarterly loss; in India’s biggest private lenders all posting double-digit profit growth while every one of their margins quietly narrows; and in Nvidia and AMD investing in the very AI buildout they’re still supplying the parts for, rather than waiting for it to be finished before buying in.

AI & Tech

Nvidia and AMD each commit to buy into Meta’s $95 billion stock offering as Microsoft and Alphabet head into today’s earnings after the close with Amazon to follow tomorrow, even as SK Hynix chases a sixth straight record profit on HBM4 demand ahead of Thursday’s report, and Apple schedules November 2 for new CEO John Ternus’s first earnings call

Takeaway Today’s AI story is capital circling back on itself: chipmakers buying stakes in the very company they supply, hyperscalers about to face a $725 billion spending verdict, and a new generation — a first-time CEO, a record-chasing memory maker — stepping into that same test.

Nvidia commits $3 billion and AMD $1 billion to Meta’s new stock offering

Nvidia said October 27 it will invest $3 billion in Meta’s newly launched equity offering, and AMD said it will invest $1 billion, a day after Meta disclosed that Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom had together indicated interest in buying up to $5 billion of the shares, according to CNBC. Nvidia tied its commitment to an expanded partnership covering its Vera Rubin systems and Meta’s Muse AI-agent family; Broadcom’s individual amount wasn’t disclosed. The raise — Meta’s first new stock issuance since 2013 — comprises about $22.5 billion in Class A shares and $12.5 billion in mandatory convertible preferred shares now, with a further $60 billion at-the-market program planned.

Takeaway Two of Meta’s own chip suppliers now literally own a slice of the company they sell to, blurring the line between customer and investor in the AI buildout.

Microsoft and Alphabet report earnings after today’s close, with Amazon following Thursday

Microsoft and Alphabet are both due to report quarterly results after the market closes today, October 28, with Amazon following Thursday, according to a Benzinga preview. Combined 2026 AI capital-spending guidance across the four largest hyperscalers has climbed to roughly $725 billion, with Alphabet alone guiding to $195-205 billion for the year; investors are watching whether cloud-revenue growth is keeping pace with that spending.

Takeaway Three hyperscalers reporting in the same 48 hours turns this week into a single concentrated verdict on whether $725 billion of AI spending is paying off.

SK Hynix heads toward Thursday’s report chasing a sixth straight profit record on HBM4 demand

SK Hynix is scheduled to report third-quarter 2026 results on October 29, with analyst consensus compiled by FnGuide pointing to operating profit of roughly 76.9 trillion won — a sixth consecutive quarterly record — as HBM4 memory revenue is fully reflected for the first time, according to South Korean financial outlet mt.co.kr. Meritz Securities estimates the company’s DRAM and NAND selling prices rose 22% and 15% respectively in the quarter, and the securities industry expects SK Hynix to announce additional shareholder returns alongside the results.

Takeaway A memory maker chasing its sixth straight record quarter, in the same week hyperscalers report their own capex, shows how far AI’s winnings have spread beyond the chip designers themselves.

Apple schedules its first earnings call under new CEO John Ternus for November 2

Apple confirmed it will report fiscal fourth-quarter 2026 results on Monday, November 2 — the first earnings call led by new CEO John Ternus, alongside CFO Kevan Parekh, according to MacRumors and the company’s own announcement. The quarter, which ran from late June through late September, doesn’t yet include the newly launched iPhone Duo, which falls into the current December quarter instead; Apple has guided to 9-11% year-over-year revenue growth.

Takeaway Handing a brand-new CEO his first earnings call in the same week hyperscalers report their own AI spending puts Ternus’s debut squarely inside this week’s broader test of tech earnings.

Product & Business

Boeing posts a third-quarter GAAP loss even as its backlog hits a record $745 billion, as UPS beats estimates and raises full-year guidance despite a $279 million restructuring and impairment charge, even as General Motors beats Q3 estimates and authorizes a $20 billion buyback only to see its shares slip on weaker truck-heavy Q4 guidance, and India’s top private lenders — ICICI, Axis, Kotak Mahindra and Bajaj Finance — all post double-digit profit growth while margins compress across the board

Takeaway Today’s business story is headline numbers hiding softer undercurrents — a record backlog beside a loss, a guidance raise beside an impairment charge, a profit beat that still sent a stock down, and loan growth outrunning margins at every major Indian lender.

Boeing posts a third-quarter GAAP loss even as its backlog hits a record $745 billion

Boeing reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of $24.2 billion and a GAAP loss per share of $0.26 (a core loss per share of $0.34), with operating cash flow of $1.5 billion and free cash flow of $0.5 billion, according to its October 27 results. The company delivered 161 commercial aircraft during the quarter — 475 for the year to date — and said its backlog reached a record $745 billion, up from $715 billion at the end of the prior quarter.

Takeaway A record $745 billion backlog sitting alongside a GAAP loss shows Boeing’s near-term costs and its long-term demand are moving in opposite directions.

UPS beats estimates and raises full-year guidance despite a $279 million charge

UPS reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of $22.9 billion, up 6.7%, with GAAP diluted EPS of $1.37 and non-GAAP adjusted diluted EPS of $1.69, according to its October 27 results. The GAAP figure absorbs a $279 million net charge — mostly a $182 million goodwill impairment in its Supply Chain Solutions unit plus transformation costs — yet UPS still raised full-year guidance to roughly $92.6 billion in revenue, $8.75 billion in adjusted operating profit and $7.28 in adjusted diluted EPS, up from the outlook it gave with Q2 results in July.

Takeaway Raising full-year guidance in the same release that discloses a goodwill impairment is UPS betting investors will look past the write-down to the underlying trend.

General Motors beats Q3 estimates and authorizes a $20 billion buyback, but shares slip on Q4 guidance

General Motors reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of $50.0 billion, up 2.9% year over year, with adjusted EPS of $3.70 against a $3.63 analyst estimate and adjusted EBIT up 21.6% to $4.11 billion, helped by a heavier mix of large pickup trucks and fewer lower-margin EVs, according to the company’s October 20 results. GM narrowed its 2026 adjusted EPS guidance to $12.75-13.75 and authorized a new $20 billion share buyback, even as U.S. unit sales fell about 6% in the quarter and the stock declined on signs that fourth-quarter profit will run well below the third quarter’s pace.

Takeaway Beating estimates and announcing a $20 billion buyback wasn’t enough to stop the stock falling — investors are pricing the softer quarter ahead, not the one GM just reported.

India’s top private lenders post double-digit Q2 profit growth as margins compress across the board

ICICI Bank, Axis Bank, Kotak Mahindra Bank and Bajaj Finance have all now reported September-quarter results, with profit growth ranging from about 22% at ICICI (consolidated net profit to ₹16,333 crore) to roughly 38% at Axis (standalone profit to ₹7,003 crore, still below the roughly ₹7,180 crore analysts had expected), 29% at Kotak Mahindra (standalone profit to ₹4,212 crore) and 29% at Bajaj Finance (consolidated profit to ₹6,367 crore), according to each bank’s own results reported between October 17 and 24. Every lender’s net interest margin narrowed from a year earlier — ICICI’s fell to 4.18% from 4.36%, Axis’s to 3.36%, and Kotak’s to 4.31% from 4.54% — even as loan growth and lower provisioning kept profit climbing.

Takeaway Profit climbing at every one of India’s big private lenders while margins shrink at every one of them is the same quarter telling two different stories depending which number you read.

Sensex and Nifty end flat on October’s F&O expiry day as smallcaps and media stocks rally

The Sensex closed at 73,598.67, down 8.08 points, and the Nifty 50 ended at 22,749.75, down 3.45 points, on Tuesday, October 27 — the monthly derivatives expiry session — according to Business Standard and BusinessWorld. Broader markets outperformed the headline indices: the Nifty Smallcap 100 rose 2.69% and Nifty Media jumped roughly 3.5%, while Nifty Bank fell 0.72% and pharma, realty and consumer durables led sector gains, as traders weighed elevated crude prices against Wednesday’s Fed decision.

Takeaway A flat headline close hid a real move underneath — smallcaps and media rallying while banks dragged — a split market waiting on the Fed rather than a quiet one.

Business Standard →

Wall Street’s Nasdaq jumps 1.18% as the Dow ekes out a third straight gain ahead of today’s Fed decision

The Nasdaq Composite closed up 1.18% at 26,477.81 and the S&P 500 rose 0.6% to 7,722.60 on Tuesday, October 27, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average added just 15.99 points (0.03%) to 52,377.57, according to TheStreet. Chipmakers and AI-linked names led the gains as investors positioned ahead of Wednesday’s Fed decision and a wave of hyperscaler earnings, including Microsoft and Alphabet later today.

Takeaway Tech leading Wall Street’s gains the same week three hyperscalers report shows investors betting on AI momentum before the earnings that will actually test it.

TheStreet →

Jio Platforms is set to list on the NSE and BSE today, India’s largest-ever IPO

Shares of Jio Platforms — raised through a record ₹30,213 crore IPO subscribed 22.19 times — are scheduled to begin trading on the NSE and BSE today, October 28, under the symbol JPL, with trading expected to start around 10 AM IST, according to Upstox. The issue was priced at ₹1,119 per share at the top of its ₹1,065-1,119 band; grey-market indicators ahead of listing pointed to a possible debut in the ₹1,290-1,363 range, though grey-market premiums are unofficial and don’t guarantee the actual listing price.

Takeaway India’s largest IPO finally trades today — everything until now was paperwork and grey-market guesswork; this is the first real price the market sets.

Upstox →

The Fed’s rate decision lands today as markets lean toward a hold

The Federal Reserve’s FOMC concludes its two-day October meeting today, October 28, with the rate decision due at 2 PM ET (roughly 11:30 PM IST), followed by a press conference, according to FOMC calendar trackers. The Fed raised rates a quarter point to 3.75%-4.00% in September — its first hike in over three years; as of the most recent market-implied pricing, investors see a majority probability the Fed holds steady this time rather than hiking again.

Takeaway A decision markets are leaning toward calling “boring” is still the single biggest swing factor hanging over every other story in today’s edition.
