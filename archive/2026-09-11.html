AI & Tech

OpenAI throws open its Agents API to every developer as Microsoft plans to triple its data-center fleet to 38 gigawatts by 2032, while Anthropic's own threat report reveals it disrupted a Russian state-linked espionage campaign and blocked bioweapons-research attempts even as it hands the EU's cybersecurity agency access to its Mythos model, a maximum-severity flaw surfaces in Google's Agent Development Kit, and chip startup Positron raises $875 million to challenge Nvidia on AI inference

OpenAI opens its Agents API to every developer, exposing the Codex harness

OpenAI launched the Agents API in public beta on 10 September, giving all developers direct access to the same orchestration harness that powers Codex — handling sessions, context compaction, tool search and recovery so developers only supply tools and pick an execution environment, per OpenAI's own announcement and MarkTechPost. The API adds no separate fee beyond standard token and tool usage, and lets teams run agents in an OpenAI-managed sandbox, their own infrastructure, or a partner's environment.

Takeaway Turning its own internal agent harness into a usage-priced product suggests OpenAI now sees the plumbing behind Codex as a business in its own right, not just infrastructure for its own tools.

Microsoft plans to triple its data-center fleet to 38 gigawatts by 2032

Microsoft aims to expand total data-center capacity from roughly 12 gigawatts today to more than 38 gigawatts by 2032 across owned and leased sites, Bloomberg reported on 10 September citing people familiar with the plans. Only about 2 of the current 12 gigawatts is AI-chip-specific capacity, a share expected to grow to roughly a third of the expanded total; the buildout excludes capacity rented from neoclouds like CoreWeave and comes after hardware constraints reportedly forced Microsoft to turn away some cloud and AI customers.

Takeaway Planning to more than triple capacity while still capacity-constrained today shows Microsoft believes the compute shortage, not demand, is what's actually capping its AI business.

Anthropic says it disrupted a Russian espionage campaign and blocked bioweapons-research attempts

Anthropic's September threat intelligence report, published 10 September and covering activity disrupted between December 2025 and August 2026, details a Russian-speaking operator (tracked as GTG-20006, consistent with Midnight Blizzard) that used Claude for AI-driven reconnaissance, phishing and command-and-control against Ukrainian and European government and defence targets, alongside five separate cases where researchers tried to use Claude for biological-weapons research, including a May 2026 attempt tied to a military institute studying the chikungunya virus.

Takeaway Publishing specifics on a nation-state espionage group in the same report as blocked bioweapons attempts is Anthropic treating its own misuse data as a public accountability record, not just an internal safety metric.

Anthropic hands the EU's cybersecurity agency access to Mythos, three months after promising it

Anthropic has given ENISA, the European Union's cybersecurity agency, access to test its Mythos 5 model under Project Glasswing, more than three months after first signalling the bloc would get it and following a direct request from European Commission officials who travelled to San Francisco, per Bloomberg and Silicon Republic on 10 September.

Takeaway A three-month gap between promising access and actually delivering it is a reminder that frontier labs still set their own timeline for letting outside regulators test their most capable models.

Silicon Republic →

A maximum-severity flaw in Google's Agent Development Kit exposes AI-agent deployments

A code-injection vulnerability (CVE-2026-79696, CVSS 10.0) in Google Cloud's Agent Development Kit for Python, versions 2.0.0 through 2.6.0, let an unauthenticated attacker execute arbitrary code via a crafted test-session replay on any deployment with pytest installed, per a public disclosure on 9 September. The open-source toolkit has more than 90 million downloads and is widely used to build and deploy AI agents on Cloud Run and GKE; Google has since fixed the underlying issue.

Takeaway A perfect-10 flaw in the toolkit developers use to build AI agents is a sharper worry than a flaw in any single agent, since it undermines the foundation a huge number of separate deployments were built on.

TheHackerWire →

Chip startup Positron raises $875 million to challenge Nvidia on AI inference

Positron AI raised an $875 million Series C on 10 September at a $5 billion post-money valuation — five times its February figure — co-led by NEA, Atreides Management, Valor Equity Partners and SemiAnalysis's Dylan Patel, per SiliconANGLE. The financing, split across a $375 million Series C and a $500 million Series C-1, funds the tapeout of Positron's Asimov chip on TSMC's N3P process (production targeted for H2 2027) and a production ramp of its Titan inference system, which pairs the chip with 288GB to 2,304GB of memory in place of pricier HBM.

Takeaway Betting on cheaper consumer-grade memory instead of HBM is Positron's attempt to win on inference economics rather than try to out-engineer Nvidia on raw performance.

SiliconANGLE →

OpenAI ends its $1-a-year government pricing pilot, moves to a 50% discount

OpenAI is winding down the pilot that let US federal agencies use its models for $1 a year — a deal used by roughly 3.5 million federal employees and credited with $1.4 billion in claimed cost savings — replacing it from 1 October with usage-based pricing at a 50% discount off standard rates, extended to state, local and tribal governments too, per Bloomberg (via MobileWorldLive) on 10 September.

Takeaway Swapping a headline-grabbing $1 deal for a smaller but permanent discount is OpenAI trading a one-time PR win for a pricing relationship with government that can actually scale.

Product & Business

India's IPO frenzy grinds on as Pranav Constructions closes 121 times oversubscribed and Rentomojo's debut draws strong Day 1 demand, while 10-minute food delivery startup Swish raises $24 million and Bengaluru's Mesa School lines up a ₹100 crore Series A, even as Nifty IT stocks buckle under a pile-up of visa fees and AI-disruption fears and SoftBank courts bond investors to keep funding its own AI bets

Rentomojo's ₹1,256 crore IPO draws steady Day 1 demand

Rentomojo's ₹1,256 crore initial public offering was subscribed 1.97 times on its first day of bidding on 9 September, with non-institutional investors leading at 3.87 times and retail investors at 2.05 times, even as qualified institutional buyers came in at just 0.41 times, per Entrackr. The furniture-and-appliance rental platform's issue, priced at ₹384-404 a share, closes on 11 September.

Takeaway Retail and NII investors turning up while institutions stay cautious on Day 1 is worth watching through book-building, since QIB demand usually decides how an IPO actually prices.

Entrackr →

Pranav Constructions' ₹351 crore IPO closes 121 times oversubscribed

Mumbai-based Pranav Constructions' ₹351.03 crore IPO closed on 9 September subscribed 121 times overall, drawing more than 44.83 lakh applications and bids worth nearly ₹33,700 crore — institutional investors bid 258.71 times their portion and retail investors 43.33 times, per Business Standard. Allotment was finalised on 10 September, ahead of a tentative 15 September listing.

Takeaway A small construction company pulling in ₹33,700 crore of demand for a ₹351 crore issue shows how much oversubscribed money is chasing India's crowded September IPO calendar, not just interest in the company itself.

Glass Wall Systems' ₹428 crore IPO books 81.65 times overall

Glass Wall Systems (India)'s ₹427.89 crore IPO closed its three-day subscription window on 10 September booked 81.65 times overall, with qualified institutional buyers subscribing 167.93 times their portion, non-institutional investors 79.71 times and retail investors 33.17 times. Allotment is expected 11 September, with listing on NSE and BSE tentatively set for 16 September.

Takeaway Institutional demand running well ahead of retail on this issue is the opposite pattern from Rentomojo's, a reminder that "IPO frenzy" hides very different investor bases from one issue to the next.

10-minute food delivery startup Swish raises $24 million as it crosses 1 million monthly orders

Swish, which runs neighbourhood "dark kitchens" promising delivery within 10 minutes across Bengaluru, Gurugram, Noida, Delhi and Ghaziabad, raised $24 million led by Bertelsmann India Investments with existing backers Accel, Bain Capital Ventures and Hara Global, per Entrackr on 10 September. The company says monthly orders have crossed 1 million, tripling since March, with over 80% delivered within 15 minutes; it has now raised roughly $78 million in total and plans to grow past 1,000 kitchens over five years.

Takeaway A tripling of order volume in six months is the clearest sign yet that ultra-fast food delivery, not just quick-commerce groceries, has found real repeat demand in India.

Entrackr →

Bengaluru's Mesa School lines up a ₹100 crore Series A

Mesa School, the Bengaluru-based business school for founders and product leaders started by Ankit Kumar and Varun Limaye, is in talks to raise about ₹100 crore in a Series A round — roughly ₹84 crore in equity and ₹16 crore in venture debt — with Limaye also looking to sell a 2.9% stake worth about ₹3 crore, Entrackr reported on 10 September, citing sources. The round has yet to close; Mesa previously raised about ₹34 crore in a 2023 seed round backed by Elevation Capital and angels including CRED's Kunal Shah.

Takeaway A business school raising venture-style Series A capital, complete with a founder secondary sale, treats education-for-founders as a scalable startup rather than a boutique cohort business.

Entrackr →

Nifty IT stocks buckle under a pile-up of visa fees, AI fears and frozen budgets

Infosys, TCS, HCL Technologies, Tech Mahindra and Wipro all extended recent losses through the week to 9 September, with TCS down roughly 30% year-to-date, as a new US visa-extension fee, a months-long freeze in US corporate tech spending, valuation compression on AI-disruption worries, and hyperscalers redirecting their own capex toward in-house data centres and GPUs all weighed at once, per Business Today and Nomura commentary.

Takeaway Four distinct headwinds landing together is why Nifty IT's slide looks less like a single bad news cycle and more like a structural re-rating of what the sector is worth.

Business Today →

SoftBank plans a dollar bond sale to keep funding its AI bets

SoftBank Group will meet investors in New York from 14-17 September to gauge appetite for a potential US dollar junk-bond sale, as it presses ahead with a borrowing spree to finance its AI wagers, per The Japan Times on 9 September, citing people familiar with the plans. Chief financial officer Yoshimitsu Goto and other executives are leading the roadshow, days after SoftBank moved to retire a separate $40 billion bridge loan tied to its OpenAI stake.

Takeaway Going back to bond investors so soon after retiring one AI-linked loan shows SoftBank's AI ambitions are still outrunning what its own balance sheet can fund without fresh borrowing.

Sensex and Nifty snap a three-day losing streak as financials lead a late recovery

The Sensex rose 138.36 points (+0.19%) to 74,902.59 and the Nifty 50 gained 46.30 points (+0.20%) to 23,477.80 on 10 September, snapping a three-day losing run, per Business Standard. Financial services, PSU banks and private banks outperformed while auto and metal stocks lagged; HDFC Life (+2.03%) and Power Grid (+1.94%) led gainers, while ITC (-0.58%) and ICICI Bank (-0.49%) were among the laggards even as the broader market stayed weak, with 36 of 50 Nifty constituents closing lower.

Takeaway A benchmark-level bounce built on just a handful of large financials, while two-thirds of the index still fell, is a thinner recovery than the headline numbers suggest.

Business Standard →

Brent crude pushes toward $105 a barrel as the US-Iran conflict widens

Brent crude climbed to around $104-105 a barrel on 10 September, extending its rise past the $100 mark hit a day earlier, as investors weighed an intensifying conflict between the US and Iran with no resolution in sight, per Trading Economics and Yahoo Finance market coverage. US equities fell for a fourth straight session on the same worry, with the Dow, S&P 500 and Nasdaq all in the red.

Takeaway Oil grinding higher for a second straight day, not just spiking once, is what's now translating Middle East risk directly into US equity losses rather than staying contained to energy stocks.

Yahoo Finance →

Wall Street falls for a fourth straight session as oil-driven inflation fears build

The S&P 500 fell 44.66 points (-0.58%) to 7,591.70 and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 171.62 points (-0.65%) to 26,081.72 on 10 September, while the Dow lost 316.56 points (-0.6%) to 52,064.10 — a fourth consecutive daily decline — as crude above $100 a barrel stoked concern about inflation heading into next week's Fed meeting, per TheStreet.

Takeaway Four straight down days on a single, well-understood cause is a more orderly kind of sell-off than markets usually see, which is its own signal that investors think this could run for a while.

TheStreet →

Gold holds near record highs, Bitcoin drifts below $78,000

Gold traded around $4,406 an ounce on 10 September, up slightly on the day and still close to its recent highs, per Trading Economics, as the same US-Iran tensions pushing oil higher kept safe-haven demand firm. Bitcoin held near $77,900-78,300, little changed, as crypto traders stayed cautious ahead of the week's US inflation data and the Fed's upcoming rate decision, per Yahoo Finance.

Takeaway Gold holding firm while Bitcoin just drifts sideways on the same worries is a reminder the two assets still don't trade as reliable substitutes for each other despite years of "digital gold" framing.
