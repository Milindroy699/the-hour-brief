AI & Tech

New models arrive as AI's talent and security cracks show

xAI ships Grok 4.6, undercutting rivals by 60% on price

xAI released Grok 4.6 on 12 August, matching GPT-5.6 Sol on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index while expanding context to 500K tokens. It launched simultaneously on Cursor, Grok Build and the API at roughly 60% below Claude Opus 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol list pricing — $2/$6 per million input/output tokens, doubling past 200K tokens — with a 2x usage promo for its first week.

Takeaway Frontier-model pricing is becoming as competitive a battleground as benchmark scores — xAI is betting developers will switch labs for cost before they switch for capability.

VentureBeat →

Alibaba open-sources its largest model yet, a 2.4-trillion-parameter Qwen3.8-Max

Alibaba unveiled Qwen3.8-Max on 3 August — a 2.4 trillion-parameter Mixture-of-Experts model activating 95 billion parameters per query, with a 1-million-token context window and text/image/video input. It's the first Max-class Qwen model Alibaba has open-sourced, with weights for it and a smaller Qwen3.8-27B variant now on Hugging Face and ModelScope, and Alibaba claims benchmark parity with leading systems from OpenAI and Anthropic.

Takeaway Every time a lab open-sources a frontier-class model, it gets harder for closed labs to charge a premium for capability alone.

MarkTechPost →

Jeff Dean and three Google AI veterans leave to start a rival research lab

Google confirmed on 5 August that Jeff Dean, Sanjay Ghemawat, Oriol Vinyals and Google Brain co-founder Quoc Le are departing to co-found Discovery Loop, a startup aimed at automating scientific and machine-learning research — with Google staying on as a founding investor and cloud partner. The news landed alongside Demis Hassabis stepping back from day-to-day leadership of DeepMind to become Alphabet's chief scientist, and Alphabet shares fell 4–5% on the announcements.

Takeaway Losing four of its most senior AI researchers to a spin-out in a single day is a reminder that even Google's compute and talent moat has limits.

GeekWire →

Zuckerberg publishes a 6,500-word AI manifesto alongside Meta's open-weight Muse Glimmer

Mark Zuckerberg published “The Future is for Everyone” on 10 August, arguing for AI-driven individual empowerment and reaffirming Meta's commitment to open models. Alongside the essay, Meta released Muse Glimmer — a new family of open-weight models built to run on laptops — which Meta is positioning as a direct challenge to OpenAI's and Anthropic's closed-model approach.

Takeaway Meta is betting its long-term edge is philosophical — open, on-device AI everyone can run — rather than chasing benchmark leadership against closed frontier labs.

CNBC →

OpenAI launches GPT-5.6-Cyber, its first model to hit a “high” cyber-risk rating

OpenAI launched GPT-5.6-Cyber on 10 August and restructured its Daybreak program into “Daybreak Blue” and “Daybreak Red” access tiers for vetted security researchers and defenders. OpenAI says the model completes roughly 95% of advanced exploit-chain and privilege-escalation tasks, versus 1.5% for standard GPT-5.6 Sol, and researchers used it to uncover two previously unknown vulnerabilities in Chrome's V8 engine; hardware security keys become mandatory for every Daybreak account from 1 September.

Takeaway A model this good at finding exploits is a double-edged tool — the same capability that surfaces real Chrome bugs could just as easily be misused if access controls ever slip.

VentureBeat →

AI notetaker tl;dv left 181,874 meetings — including government calls — exposed

Researcher BobDaHacker disclosed that tl;dv's Firestore database lacked tenant isolation, letting any authenticated user query metadata for 181,874 meetings across 84,312 users and 35,003 email domains, including government meetings from 23 countries. Exposed live conference IDs let the researcher join active calls by impersonating the AI notetaker bot, succeeding in roughly 80% of tests; the flaw was first reported on 28 January and remained unfixed as of Dark Reading's 4 August report.

Takeaway AI notetakers now sit inside sensitive meetings by default — this is a good prompt to actually check what access you've granted one before your next call.

Dark Reading →

Anthropic investors reportedly target a record $2 trillion IPO valuation for October

Investors briefed on Anthropic's plans said on 13 August that the Claude maker is targeting an October IPO at a valuation of $2 trillion or more — which would make it the largest public offering in history, surpassing SpaceX's June 2026 debut. Backers cited annualized revenue projected to reach $100–120 billion by year-end, more than tenfold the $47 billion figure reported in May, with Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan named as lead underwriters.

Takeaway A $2 trillion target assumes enterprise AI spend keeps compounding at nearly 10x a year — a bet public investors haven't had to underwrite at this scale before.

Product & Business

IPO season peaks as Molbio and Shiprocket hit the tape

Svatantra Microfin files DRHP for a ₹3,000 crore IPO

Svatantra Microfin, India's second-largest NBFC-microfinance institution by AUM, filed its draft red herring prospectus with SEBI on 14 August for a ₹3,000 crore IPO — a ₹1,500 crore fresh issue plus a ₹1,500 crore offer-for-sale from investors Multiples Private Equity and Violicina Limited. Its microfinance AUM grew to ₹21,093 crore as of March 2026, up from ₹14,438 crore two years earlier; fresh-issue proceeds will bolster Tier-I capital for further lending.

Takeaway A second large microfinance NBFC filing for an IPO in as many months suggests investor appetite for financial-inclusion lending is recovering, even as sector-stress headlines from 2024–25 still linger.

Business Standard →

Shiprocket's ₹1,617 crore IPO sails through at 102x subscription

E-commerce enablement platform Shiprocket closed its ₹1,617.48 crore IPO (price band ₹92–97) on 14 August with overall subscription of 102.28 times — QIBs bid 125.2x, NIIs 92.58x and retail 48.38x. Allotment is being finalised on 17 August, with listing tentatively set for 19 August; the company posted FY26 revenue of ₹2,024 crore, up from ₹1,632 crore in FY25, across 214,769 active merchants.

Takeaway A 102x-subscribed IPO for an e-commerce logistics enabler is a strong read on how much investors are betting on India's D2C and quick-commerce ecosystem continuing to scale.

Molbio Diagnostics debuts on Dalal Street today after 70x subscription

Point-of-care molecular diagnostics maker Molbio Diagnostics — known for its portable Truenat PCR platform — lists on NSE and BSE today, 17 August, following a ₹940 crore IPO (final price ₹807, band ₹768–807) that closed 12 August subscribed 70.27 times overall (186.39x QIB, 49.80x NII, 12.97x retail). The issue comprised a ₹200 crore fresh issue and ₹739.7 crore offer-for-sale.

Takeaway A diagnostics-hardware maker listing at 70x subscription, rather than another consumer app, is a reminder that India's IPO boom isn't purely a software story.

AstroTalk becomes India's 8th unicorn of 2026 via a profit-funded ESOP buyback

Noida-based astrology and spiritual-tech platform AstroTalk crossed a $1 billion valuation on 12 August through an ESOP buyback covering more than 100 employees — funded entirely from internal profits, not a fresh funding round. The valuation is up 3.3x from $300 million in June 2024, on total lifetime funding of just $34 million; FY25 revenue reached ₹1,176 crore, up from ₹651 crore, with ₹285 crore in pre-tax profit.

Takeaway A unicorn valuation funded entirely by profits rather than new investor capital is rare enough in Indian startup land to be worth flagging on its own.

Electric two-wheeler maker River Mobility raises $120M Series C

Bengaluru-based electric two-wheeler maker River Mobility raised $120 million in a Series C led by Elev8 Venture Partners and Claypond Capital — its first major backing from Indian institutional investors, alongside existing global backers Yamaha Motor, Toyota Ventures and Mitsui & Co. The round funds a new manufacturing plant and retail expansion from 75 to over 350 stores by March 2028.

Takeaway Global auto majors backing a homegrown EV two-wheeler maker signals India's EV supply chain is maturing enough to attract strategic, not just financial, capital.

Entrackr →

Ola Electric's battery unit wins an extended PLI timeline, ₹7,240 crore at stake

The Ministry of Heavy Industries granted Ola Electric's battery subsidiary, Ola Cell Technologies, a revised five-year ACC PLI window running through 2031, pushing out original milestones by two years. The company is now eligible for cumulative incentives of up to ₹7,240 crore against its 20 GWh allocation; it currently has 2.5 GWh of installed cell capacity and expects to hit its first 6 GWh milestone by the September quarter.

Takeaway A two-year timeline extension buys Ola Electric's battery unit breathing room, but it also raises the question of whether the original PLI milestones were realistic in the first place.

Inc42 →

SoftBank-backed OfBusiness revives an $800M India IPO plan

B2B commerce and SME-financing platform OfBusiness is reviving IPO plans targeting roughly $800 million, including a fresh issue of around $200–260 million, at a mooted $5–6 billion valuation; Axis Capital, Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan and Citigroup have been engaged, with a DRHP filing targeted for mid-November. For FY26, OfBusiness reported net profit up 21% year-on-year to ₹724 crore on largely flat revenue of ₹20,645 crore.

Takeaway Profit growth on flat revenue is an unusual growth story to take public — it will test how much investors reward margin discipline over top-line growth in this IPO cycle.

Stratechery: capital, not chips or power, may be the AI buildout's real constraint

In Stratechery's free Friday roundup (14 August), Ben Thompson examines whether capital could become the binding constraint on the AI buildout, pointing to Nvidia's new financing mechanism designed to help customers tap long-duration capital and Google leaning on equity financing as a bridge to sustainable AI revenue. The edition also flags the EU's mandate that AI providers label AI-generated outputs.

Takeaway If capital becomes the real bottleneck on AI infrastructure, financing structures like Nvidia's residual-value guarantees stop looking like marketing and start looking like necessity.

Lenny's Newsletter: the three founder archetypes that shape your story

Ghostwriter and narrative strategist Kristen Lowe published a guest essay on Lenny's Newsletter (11 August) arguing founders fall into three archetypes — “Problem,” “Insight” and “Vision” — each evoking a different audience reaction: identification, trust or followship. She maps each to a distinct communications strategy, with Insight founders better served by long-form essays and podcasts, and Vision founders by video formats like keynotes.

Takeaway How a founder tells their own story may matter as much to fundraising and hiring as the metrics do — narrative strategy is becoming its own product-leadership skill.

FPIs turn net buyers, pumping ₹16,621 crore into Indian equities in early August

Foreign portfolio investors injected ₹16,621 crore into Indian equities in the first fortnight of August — building on July's ₹20,200 crore inflow after four straight months of heavy selling — though FPIs remain net sellers for 2026 overall, having pulled out roughly ₹2.4 trillion year-to-date, already exceeding all of 2025's ₹1.66 trillion outflow. Indian and US markets are shut through the weekend into Monday morning IST, so the figures above are still Friday's close.

Takeaway A fortnight of buying doesn't erase eight months of selling — worth watching whether this is the start of a real reversal or just a pause.

Business Standard →

S&P 500 slips off a fresh record high but logs a third straight weekly gain

US stocks pulled back on Friday, 14 August, with the S&P 500 closing down 0.17% at 7,785.76 and the Nasdaq Composite down 0.28% at 26,729.16 — a day after the S&P touched a fresh intraday record. The Dow Jones Industrial Average also slipped roughly 107 points (0.2%) to 53,732.41 as softer consumer-sentiment data weighed on the session, even as the S&P 500 still closed out its third consecutive weekly gain.

Takeaway A single soft data point can dent a record-setting week without breaking its underlying trend — this pullback looks like profit-taking, not a reversal.

Yahoo Finance →

Brent crude climbs past $88 a barrel as Middle East supply risk builds

Brent crude rose to roughly $88.52/barrel on 14 August, up 1.67% on the day, after Iran and Oman failed to agree on reopening the Strait of Hormuz and Iran-backed Houthi militants targeted Saudi Arabia's Jazan refinery in the Red Sea. Analysts flagged that global oil supply could fall by roughly 4.3 million barrels per day this year if hostilities continue, pushing the market deeper into deficit.

Takeaway Rising crude is a tax on Indian import costs and inflation alike — worth watching alongside equities, not as a separate story.

Nvidia's 26 August earnings shape up as a bellwether for the whole AI trade

Nvidia shares were trading near $219 in mid-August, up about 17.7% year-to-date but lagging the S&P 500's roughly 7% gain over the same stretch. Wall Street expects Q2 FY27 revenue of $93–95 billion — about 96% year-over-year growth — driven by AI accelerator demand and the ramp of Blackwell-based products, with GAAP EPS estimated at $2.06, nearly double the year-ago figure.

Takeaway With so much of the market's AI narrative riding on one company, Nvidia's 26 August print could move far more than just Nvidia's own stock.
